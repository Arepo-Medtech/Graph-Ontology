# Gastroenteritis in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** assessment, investigation, rehydration, antiemetics and disposition for acute gastroenteritis in children. Related: `dehydration-children`, `vomiting` (RCH) and `diarrhoea` (AMH, mostly adult).

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 24 of 24 claims quoted verbatim from the **RCH Melbourne Clinical Practice Guideline *Gastroenteritis*** (Last updated August 2025); **36 fragments re-checked by machine. 3 quoted doses.**

> ⚠️ **Red flags or vomiting without diarrhoea: think of another diagnosis first.** Rehydrate by the enteral route (oral or NG) wherever possible.

## Recognise it

- ⚠️ **With red flag features, or vomiting without diarrhoea, consider alternative diagnoses.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Red flags:** age under 6 months · bilious vomiting · haematemesis · diarrhoea for more than 10 days · severe abdominal pain · refusal to walk · past GI/surgical history · complex medical history · post organ transplant · immunocompromise · re-presentation or failure to respond to standard therapy. [S1]
- **Vomiting may precede diarrhoea by 24–48 hours**, but a vomiting child without diarrhoea needs other causes considered. [S1]
- **Blood or mucus in the stool** suggests significant inflammation, as with bacterial infection or inflammatory bowel conditions. [S1]
- **Assess the degree of dehydration** — it sets the route and rate of rehydration. Weigh the child (bare in infants). [S1]
- **Knowing the cause rarely changes management**, whether viral or bacterial. [S1]

## Investigate

- **Most children with presumed, uncomplicated gastroenteritis need no investigations**, including stool testing. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Every child on IV fluids: check serum electrolytes and glucose before starting** (typically at cannulation) **and again within 24 hours** if IV therapy continues. [S1]
- **BGL, ketones and electrolytes may also be needed** with severe dehydration, profuse or prolonged losses, altered conscious state, hypertonic or prolonged hypotonic fluid intake, complex comorbidities or diuretic use. **Ketotic hypoglycaemia can follow vomiting**, more often in younger children. [S1]
- **Stool culture or viral testing is not recommended**, except in unwell children or young infants with bloody diarrhoea, returned travellers with symptoms over 10 days, and immunocompromised patients with fever. [S1]

## Treat

- **Many cases are self-limiting** and need only encouragement of fluid intake and monitoring of hydration. **Failure of initial management should prompt a rethink of the diagnosis.** [S1]
- **Oral rehydration: aim for 10 mL/kg/hr of oral rehydration solution (ORS).** [S1]
- **Mildly dehydrated child refusing ORS:** a short-term flavoured ice block, or apple juice diluted 1:1 with water. [S1]
- **Stop any feed fortification.** **Continue breastfeeding, more often**, with extra ORS if appropriate. [S1]
- **If oral rehydration fails, use rapid or slow NG rehydration**; if NG is not tolerated in a clinically dehydrated child, **move to IV**. IV may be preferred in older children if NG is not tolerated or appropriate. [S1]
- **Ondansetron** may support hydration in children and infants **over 6 months** with persistent nausea or vomiting. [S1]
- **Initial oral ondansetron by weight:** 8–15 kg **2 mg** · 15–30 kg **4 mg** · over 30 kg **8 mg**. **Ongoing dosing is 0.15 mg/kg.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **A response to anti-emetics does not confirm the diagnosis.** Other anti-emetics are not routinely recommended because of side effects. [S1]
- **No anti-spasmodics or anti-diarrhoeals.** Evidence is insufficient to support probiotics. [S1]
- **Reserve antibiotics** for enteritis-associated sepsis, specific bacterial pathogens in selected cases (eg *Salmonella* Typhi; non-typhoidal salmonella under 3 months, immunocompromised or septic) or severe *C. difficile* infection. [S1]
- **Once rehydrated, restart a normal diet.** Do not routinely restrict lactose; consider it only if stool frequency increases. Diarrhoea may persist. [S1]

## Disposition

- **Discharge when** vomiting has reduced and the child is tolerating adequate oral fluids, with nil or mild dehydration. [S1]
- **Involve the paediatric team** if initial rehydration fails or losses are significant, with complex comorbidities, electrolyte abnormalities, an unclear diagnosis or red flags. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Consider transfer** for severe electrolyte disturbance or **shock needing 40 mL/kg of fluid boluses**. [S1]
