## The three device families [R1]

| | How it works | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| **Pressurised MDI (pMDI)** | **inert propellant gas rapidly delivers drug to the lungs**; most activated by pushing the canister while breathing in | ⚠️ **GOOD HAND–BREATH COORDINATION** |
| **Mist MDI (Respimat)** | **NON-pressurised; a compressed spring produces the aerosol** | ⚠️ **does NOT depend on inspiratory flow, hand–breath coordination OR a spacer** |
| **Dry powder inhaler (DPI)** | **breath-activated powder** | ⚠️ **adequate INSPIRATORY FLOW; no spacer needed** |

> ⚠️ **WHEN A DPI IS THE WRONG CHOICE** [R1]: **low inspiratory flow — some children under 8 years,
> elderly patients with poor lung function, and DURING ACUTE ASTHMA ATTACKS.** **In these, pMDI with a
> spacer is preferred.**

> ⚠️ **UNLESS IT HAS A DOSE COUNTER, THE ONLY RELIABLE WAY TO KNOW A pMDI IS EMPTY IS TO COUNT THE DOSES
> ADMINISTERED** [R1]. **Shaking it, floating it, and "it still sprays" are not methods.**

**AMH's practice point: ⚠️ TO REDUCE CONFUSION, AVOID USING DIFFERENT INHALER TYPES IF POSSIBLE** [R1] —
which matters given the count below. **Assess inhaler technique regularly**, using National Asthma Council
and Lung Foundation resources [R1].

## ⚠️ How to decide: the bedside test [S1]

> **"THE BEST INHALER FOR ANY PATIENT IS ONE THAT THEY CAN AND WILL USE AS PRESCRIBED"** [S1].

> ⚠️ **THE RULE OF THUMB.** Formal inspiratory flow measurement is not usually available in general
> practice, **but see if the patient, AFTER EXHALING, CAN TAKE A QUICK DEEP BREATH IN LESS THAN THREE
> SECONDS. If so, inspiratory ability is considered sufficient for a DPI** [S1].
>
> **DPIs are NOT recommended in children under six years or people with poor inspiratory flow** [S1] —
> ⚠️ **note this differs from AMH's "some children <8 years" for the spacer rule** [R1]; **the two sources
> use different age anchors for related but distinct decisions, and both are stated.**

**In appropriate patients, DPIs are AT LEAST AS EFFECTIVE as pMDIs for clinical outcomes in asthma, with
LEVEL 1 EVIDENCE showing no difference in randomised trials for both beta-agonists and inhaled
corticosteroids** [S1]. **A recent trial in England found asthma control IMPROVED in patients moved from
pMDIs to a DPI** [S1].

**There are non-MDI inhaler options at EVERY treatment step in the current Australian asthma guidelines**
[S1].

## Spacers [R1]

**Used with pMDIs, EXCEPT the Autohaler.** **A spacer requires less hand–breath coordination, improves the
pulmonary deposition pattern, reduces the risk of triggering a cough reflex and oropharyngeal deposition,
and gives fewer local adverse effects and less systemic absorption** [R1].

| ⚠️ Recommended for [R1] |
|---|
| **Children — SMALL-VOLUME spacer if under 5 years, with a face mask if necessary** |
| **Adults with poor hand–breath coordination** |
| ⚠️ **ACUTE ASTHMA ATTACKS** |
| **Patients on inhaled corticosteroids, particularly at higher doses** |
| **Reducing oropharyngeal adverse effects — candidiasis, dysphonia, frequent cough reflex** |
| **Most children under 8 years, and people with poor dexterity, using a pMDI** |

**Large-volume spacers (650–850 mL) are MORE EFFICIENT than small-volume (60–160 mL) but less
convenient**, and ⚠️ **are UNSUITABLE for children under 5 years because of their insufficient tidal
volume** [R1].

### ⚠️ Cleaning — and the step people get wrong

> **Wash BEFORE FIRST USE, then ONCE A MONTH:**
> - **wash in warm water and dish-washing detergent** — ⚠️ **DO NOT RINSE**
> - ⚠️ **ALLOW TO AIR DRY. Do NOT dry with a cloth — this can make medication stick to the spacer and make
>   it less effective**
> - **check for cracks and that the valve is functioning** [R1]

## ⚠️ Nebulisers: the default answer is no [R2]

> **USE OF pMDIs WITH A SPACER FOR SHORT-ACTING BETA2 AGONISTS IS AT LEAST AS EFFECTIVE AS NEBULISERS in
> acute exacerbations of asthma or COPD, and is generally preferred** [R2].
>
> **Nebulisers may be used for severe exacerbations IN HOSPITAL, where large doses may be required over
> long periods.** ⚠️ **RESTRICT HOME USE TO SPECIAL CASES** [R2].

- **Higher doses are required by nebuliser than by pMDI** because of decreased efficiency from lower
  pulmonary deposition [R2].
- ⚠️ **NEBULISERS INCREASE THE RISK OF TRANSMITTING VIRAL RESPIRATORY INFECTIONS via droplets and
  aerosols — AVOID USE IF POSSIBLE** [R2].
- **To reduce eye and skin reactions, consider a MOUTHPIECE rather than a mask, or wear eye protection and
  wash the face afterwards** [R2].
- **Risk of MICROBIAL CONTAMINATION — correct cleaning and maintenance including REGULAR REPLACEMENT OF
  THE BOWL AND FILTER** [R2].

### Mixing solutions [R2]
⚠️ **EVIDENCE FOR COMPATIBILITY IS LIMITED.** **If solutions are combined, MIX JUST BEFORE USE and INSPECT
for cloudiness or precipitation.** **Any TWO of salbutamol, ipratropium and budesonide appear
compatible**; for three or more, AMH refers to the UK Specialist Pharmacy Service [R2].

## ⚠️ Two label traps

> ⚠️ **STRENGTH IS EXPRESSED TWO WAYS, AND AMH CALLS IT CONFUSING** [R1]:
> **METERED (ex-valve) dose** = the amount intended for release on activation.
> **DELIVERED (ex-actuator, ex-device) dose** = the amount actually expelled — the metered dose minus a
> small quantity deposited on the mouthpiece.
> **IN AUSTRALIA THE METERED DOSE IS MORE COMMONLY USED unless the product information says otherwise**
> [R1]. **Two products can carry different numbers for the same delivered drug.**

> ⚠️ **FLUTICASONE FUROATE IS NOT INTERCHANGEABLE WITH FLUTICASONE PROPIONATE** — AMH's device table
> carries this as an explicit footnote [R3]. **Similar names, different molecules, different potencies,
> different devices.**

## The Australian device landscape [R3]

**AMH's table lists at least sixteen distinct DPI designs and trade names — Turbuhaler, Accuhaler, Ellipta,
Breezhaler, Handihaler, Zonda, Genuair, Aerolizer, Easyhaler, Spiromax, LupinHaler among them — alongside
pMDIs and the Respimat mist MDI** [R3].

| Device quirk [R3] | Products |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ **BREATH-ACTIVATED pMDIs** (no spacer needed) | **Airomir Autohaler** (salbutamol) · **Qvar Autohaler** (beclometasone) |
| ⚠️ **REQUIRE A CAPSULE LOADED BEFORE EACH DOSE** | **Foradile Aerolizer · Onbrez Breezhaler · Seebri Breezhaler · Braltus Zonda · Spiriva Handihaler · Tiotropium LupinHaler · Ultibro Breezhaler · Atectura Breezhaler · Enerzair Breezhaler** |
| **Triple ICS+LABA+LAMA** | **pMDI: Trimbow, Breztri Aerosphere** · **DPI: Trelegy Ellipta, Enerzair Breezhaler** |

> ⚠️ **ALL FOUR SALBUTAMOL PRODUCTS in AMH's table — Airomir Autohaler, Asmol, Ventolin, Zempreon — ARE
> pMDIs.** **The ONLY short-acting beta2 agonist listed as a DPI is TERBUTALINE (Bricanyl Turbuhaler)**
> [R3]. **S1 says the same from the other direction: ALL salbutamol inhalers in Australia are pMDIs** [S1].

## ⚠️ The environmental dimension — and why it is a clinical one

**AMH gives this one line: the hydrofluorocarbon propellants in pMDIs are greenhouse gases contributing to
global warming, although they do NOT deplete the ozone layer** [R1]. **S1 does the rest.**

| [S1] | |
|---|---|
| **Scale of the gas** | ⚠️ **HFCs are ozone-safe but POTENT greenhouse gases, with an effect THOUSANDS OF TIMES GREATER than the equivalent volume of CO₂** |
| **Current practice** | **in 2021, pMDI versions of fluticasone/salmeterol were dispensed APPROXIMATELY TWICE as commonly as the DPI versions** |
| **Size of the switch** | **pMDI → DPI is approximately 420 kg CO₂e annually** — similar to **switching a petrol car to a hybrid (~500 kg)** or **becoming vegetarian (~660)** |
| **Cost** | **terbutaline aside, DPIs and pMDIs have SIMILAR COSTS to patients** |
| ⚠️ **The barrier** | **restricted PBS availability of TERBUTALINE, the sole SABA available as a DPI** |

### ⚠️ Where the clinical and environmental answers coincide [S1]

> **Good disease control prevents excessive SABA use, and most SABA inhalers are the polluting pMDI
> variety — including ALL salbutamol inhalers in Australia** [S1].
> **SABAs are easily available OVER THE COUNTER here, and significant proportions of people with asthma
> report FREQUENT SABA USE WITH LITTLE OR NO PREVENTER MEDICINE** [S1].
> **So the prescription that reduces emissions is the same one that reduces exacerbations.**

> ⚠️ **AND THE FIRST STEP IS DIAGNOSIS.** **Research from a large Canadian community sample found that
> APPROXIMATELY ONE-THIRD of people diagnosed with asthma, and APPROXIMATELY HALF of those diagnosed with
> COPD, CANNOT HAVE THEIR DIAGNOSES PROVEN after careful evaluation, even after withdrawal of treatment**
> [S1]. **The greenest and safest inhaler is the one not needed.**

**One dosing consequence worth knowing:** **budesonide 400 micrograms ONCE daily has equivalent clinical
outcomes to 200 micrograms twice daily** [S1] — **halving plastic waste, cost and inconvenience.**
