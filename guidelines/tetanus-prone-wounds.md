# Tetanus-prone wounds in children

## Key points

- ⚠️ **Treat every wound other than a clean minor one as tetanus-prone.** [S1]
- **Check the immunisation history and immunise if it is incomplete or uncertain.** TIG may be needed if the primary course is incomplete or the tetanus history is uncertain. [S1]
- Tetanus causes jaw spasm, trouble swallowing, painful muscle spasms and stiffness, seizures, headache and difficulty swallowing or breathing; it may need sedation or prolonged ventilation in ICU, and can be fatal. [S1]
- **Schedule:** tetanus doses at 2, 4, 6 and 18 months and 4 years, with a booster at 12–13 years. **A complete primary course is 3 or more doses.** [S1]
- In Australia, tetanus-toxoid vaccines come only as **combination vaccines**, with pertussis, diphtheria and other antigens. [S1]

## Assessment

- **Tetanus-prone wounds:** any wound other than a clean minor cut · compound fractures · bites · deep penetrating wounds · foreign bodies (especially wood splinters) · pyogenic infection · extensive tissue damage (contusions, burns) · obvious soil, dust or horse-manure contamination (especially if topical disinfection is delayed more than 4 hours) · **reimplantation of an avulsed tooth**. [S1]
- **Take an immunisation history** and discuss catch-up. Records: myGov (parents, or children over 14), My Health Record, or the Australian Immunisation Register via HPOS or the AIR enquiries line (1800 653 809). [S1]

## Tetanus prophylaxis

- **Clean, disinfect and treat the wound surgically if appropriate.** [S1]
- **3 or more doses — clean minor wound:** vaccine only if due for the routine booster or more than 10 years since the last booster. **Other wounds:** vaccine if more than 5 years since the last booster. **No TIG** in either case. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Fewer than 3 doses, uncertain, or unimmunised:** vaccine **and complete the course** for any wound; **add TIG for every wound other than a clean minor one.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **TIG 250 IU IM, as soon as practicable after the injury; 500 IU if more than 24 hours have elapsed.** [S1]
- TIG is viscous: **give it slowly through a 23 gauge needle**, split large volumes across different sites, and **use the opposite limb** if giving the vaccine at the same time. [S1]
- TIG reactions are very uncommon: injection-site pain, redness and stiffness; rarely mild fever, chills, drowsiness or itchy rash. **Seek expert advice if there are known antibodies to IgA or a previous allergy to immunoglobulin products.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Significant humoral immune deficiency or HIV: TIG for all tetanus-prone wounds**. Discuss with a senior doctor or the immunology team. [S1]

## Vaccines

- **Complete the tetanus schedule with the same vaccine brand if available**; otherwise use another brand of the same antigen combination. [S1]
- **dTpa** has less diphtheria toxoid and pertussis antigen than DTPa, and is **for use over 10 years of age**. [S1]
- ATAGI recommends Infanrix hexa and Vaxelis up to 10 years of age; some centres use them up to 18 years when multiple vaccines are needed (e.g. catch-up, after chemotherapy or HSCT). [S1]
- **Side effects:** common — mild fever, unsettled behaviour and drowsiness in infants and young children; injection-site pain, redness and swelling in older children. Very rare — anaphylaxis, urticaria, peripheral neuropathy. [S1]

## Discharge

- **Discharge when** the wound has been treated, tetanus prevention given, and follow-up for catch-up immunisations arranged. [S1]
