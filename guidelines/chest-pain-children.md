# Chest pain in children

> ⚠️ **Most paediatric chest pain is benign** — but trauma, critical illness or cardiorespiratory compromise needs **early senior involvement**.

## Key points

- **Most paediatric chest pain is unexplained or self-limiting** (e.g. musculoskeletal). A serious cardiac or respiratory cause is **highly unlikely** in a previously well child with new-onset pain. [S1]
- **The goal is to rule out serious causes.** Reassurance, with or without simple analgesia, is often all that is needed. [S1]
- **Cardiac causes account for as few as 1%.** Without risk factors, the common causes are musculoskeletal pain/costochondritis, precordial catch, respiratory infection or asthma, upper GI or biliary causes, and anxiety. [S1]
- **Reserve ECG, CXR and bloods for children with risk factors** on history and examination. Discuss echocardiogram or CT pulmonary angiogram with a senior clinician. [S1]

## History and examination

- **Take a thorough history** — acute onset is more likely to have an identifiable cause. Ask about recent illness or vaccination (e.g. myopericarditis after COVID-19 mRNA vaccine), underlying conditions such as Duchenne muscular dystrophy, and a family history of arrhythmia, cardiomyopathy, pulmonary hypertension or sudden death. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Risk factors for a serious or life-threatening cause include:** first episode of severe chest pain · pain radiating to arm or back · dizziness or syncope · congenital/acquired heart disease · family history of sudden cardiac death or arrhythmia · Kawasaki disease · connective tissue disease · longstanding diabetes. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Further listed risk factors:** immobility, recent surgery, hypercoagulability, a central venous catheter, haemoptysis; chronic respiratory disease; chest pain after vomiting, odynophagia, dysphagia, food impaction, recent heavy alcohol, an ingested foreign body — and **suspected button battery ingestion**. [S1]
- **Examine for cardiorespiratory distress:** tachypnoea and increased work of breathing, hypoxia, abnormal pulse or BP, distended neck veins, muffled heart sounds, absent breath sounds, altered conscious state. [S1]

## Management

- ⚠️ **Involve senior staff early** for trauma patients, the critically unwell, and anyone with clear cardiorespiratory compromise. [S1]
- **Offer simple analgesia (paracetamol and ibuprofen) while you assess.** Treatment is cause-specific. [S1]

## Referral, transfer and discharge

- **Consult cardiology** if an acute cardiac condition or abnormal investigation is found, if investigations are normal but concern persists, or if a child with known heart disease has chest pain. [S1]
- **Consult the relevant specialist team** for diagnosed acute pulmonary embolism, or PE risk factors needing shared decision-making. [S1]
- **Consider discharge when** the pain has resolved or improved with simple analgesia and no serious cause is found, vital signs are normal, and GP or specialist follow-up is arranged. [S1]

## Serious causes to recognise

- ⚠️ **Myocarditis** can mimic respiratory disease or sepsis. Look for a recent viral illness or an **mRNA vaccine 1–14 days earlier (higher risk in adolescent males and after the 2nd dose)**, dizziness, syncope or palpitations. [S1]
- **Pericarditis:** retrosternal pain **improved by sitting upright or leaning forward**, tachycardia, pericardial rub; ECG may show diffuse ST elevation with PR depression. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Aortic dissection:** acute 'tearing' pain radiating to the back, in a child with connective tissue disease (e.g. Marfan, Ehlers-Danlos), Kawasaki disease or known aortic root dilatation; check for a **difference in upper-limb BPs**. [S1]
- **Cardiac ischaemia:** pain radiating to arm or neck, blunt chest trauma, **cocaine, methamphetamine or synthetic cannabinoid use**, known heart disease or Kawasaki disease; ECG ST/T changes and raised troponin. [S1]
- **Arrhythmia:** palpitations, **exertional chest pain**, syncope, dizziness, family history of arrhythmia (e.g. Brugada) or sudden cardiac death. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Pulmonary embolus:** recent surgery or immobility, malignancy, hypercoagulability, a CVAD, with pleuritic pain, haemoptysis, dyspnoea, or hypoxia, tachycardia or tachypnoea with no other cause. [S1]
- **Pneumothorax:** tall, thin adolescents; acute severe stabbing pain or dyspnoea after cough or Valsalva; the examination can be normal. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Acute chest syndrome** in known sickle cell disease — with current or recent infection, dehydration, fever, hypoxia, sedatives or surgery. [S1]
- **Inhaled foreign body:** high suspicion under 4 years and in older children with developmental impairment; it may present days to weeks later, and the CXR can look normal — ask for inspiratory, expiratory and lateral decubitus views. [S1]

## Benign causes

- **Precordial catch:** sudden sharp pain at the left lower sternal border or apex, never during sleep, **intense but brief (30 seconds to 3 minutes)**; normal examination, no investigations. [S1]
- **Costochondritis:** pinpoint pain over 2 or 3 adjacent ribs, reproducible on palpation, movement or deep breathing, usually unilateral, usually ribs 2 to 5, no swelling. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Bony pain that wakes the child at night**, or persists after minor trauma, raises **osteomyelitis or bone neoplasm**. [S1]
- **Psychogenic:** consider a HEEADSSS screen; transient hyperventilation with or without tachycardia, normal examination and normal ECG. [S1]
