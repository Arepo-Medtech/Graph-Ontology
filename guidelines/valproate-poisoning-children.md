# Sodium valproate poisoning in children

> ⚠️ **Serious toxicity is unlikely under 400 mg/kg; over 1 g/kg or a level over 6000 micromol/L is life-threatening — transfer to an ICU with dialysis.** Enteric-coated tablets: observe at least 12 hours.

---

## Key points

- ⚠️ **Toxicity is dose-dependent; serious symptoms are unlikely below 400 mg/kg.** [S1]
- **Valproate levels and an ECG help assessment. Consider transfer to a tertiary centre early** — intensive care support such as haemodialysis may be needed. [S1]
- **Onset: within 4 hours for standard release, up to 12 hours for enteric-coated.** ⚠️ **Bone marrow suppression can appear 3 to 5 days after massive ingestion.** [S1]

## Who needs assessment

- **Assess:** any deliberate self-poisoning · accidental ingestion over 200 mg/kg in a drug-naïve child (or 50 mg/kg more than the usual single dose) · any symptomatic child · unknown quantity. **Consider non-accidental poisoning** if developmental age doesn't fit. [S1]
- **Dose–response:** under 200 mg/kg — no more than mild sedation · 200–400 mg/kg — moderate toxicity with CNS depression · ⚠️ **over 400 mg/kg — multi-organ toxicity · over 1000 mg/kg — coma, multi-organ failure, cerebral oedema, potentially life-threatening.** [S1]
- **Examine for:** drowsiness, ataxia, seizures, coma · nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain · hypotension, QT prolongation, tachycardia · **metabolic: hypernatraemia, raised lactate, metabolic acidosis, hypocalcaemia, hypoglycaemia, hyperammonaemia, deranged LFTs** · late myelosuppression. [S1]

## Investigations

- **ECG at presentation and repeat at 6 hours until normal. Valproate level every 6 hours while conscious state is altered**, until normal. [S1]
- **Therapeutic range 350 to 700 micromol/L (50 to 100 mg/L); ⚠️ over 6000 micromol/L (850 mg/L) indicates severe poisoning.** [S1]
- **Also:** blood glucose, electrolytes, LFTs, blood gas, ammonia, calcium, and paracetamol in all intentional overdoses. [S1]

## Treatment

- **Discuss every ingestion over 400 mg/kg with a toxicologist early** about decontamination. [S1]
- **Support blood pressure and electrolytes: IV fluid 20 mL/kg for hypovolaemia or hypotension;** potassium and glucose as needed. [S1]
- **Consider charcoal for over 200 mg/kg presenting within 4 hours**, after discussion with toxicology. ⚠️ **Only if the child is not drowsy or the airway is secured (intubated).** [S1]
- **Consider multi-dose activated charcoal for over 500 mg/kg or rising levels.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Haemodialysis is considered in life-threatening poisoning: over 1 g/kg or levels over 6000 micromol/L.** [S1]
- **Carnitine lacks strong evidence but has a reasonable biological basis, is cheap and low-risk.** Consider it for poisoning complicated by significant metabolic acidosis, cerebral oedema, hyperammonaemia or hepatotoxicity. [S1]
- **Carnitine: 100 mg/kg IV, then 50 mg/kg every 8 hours until coma or acidosis resolve.** [S1]

## Observation and disposition

- **Mild symptoms (ataxia, GI upset): observe 6 hours for immediate-release, at least 12 hours for enteric-coated;** discharge if symptom-free. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Moderate–severe or persisting after 6 hours, OR any ingestion over 400 mg/kg: admit to a tertiary centre.** Take a level at 6 hours; discuss serial monitoring with a toxicologist if symptoms continue. [S1]
- **Consult the local paediatric team** for any intentional overdose (consider admission) or symptoms persisting after 6 hours. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Transfer to an ICU with dialysis if over 1 g/kg ingested or level over 6000 micromol/L**; also transfer for severe symptoms or care beyond the hospital's comfort level. [S1]
- **Discharge when:** normal GCS, normal ECG, observation period complete. [S1]
- **Adolescents with deliberate ingestion need a self-harm risk assessment and drug and alcohol assessment before discharge**, with links to services if needs are found. [S1]
