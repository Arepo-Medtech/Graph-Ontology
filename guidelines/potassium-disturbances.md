## Why the number on the report may mislead you

- Potassium is the **major intracellular cation**, mainly in skeletal muscle. ⚠️ **Only 2% of total body
  potassium is in the extracellular fluid** — so **very small changes in intracellular/extracellular
  distribution can cause major changes in serum levels**, altering the resting membrane potential of
  excitable tissues and causing **arrhythmias and muscle dysfunction** [M1].
- ⚠️ **Serum potassium may not reflect total body potassium.** **Acute changes are usually due to
  transcellular shift, not a change in total body potassium** [M1].
- **Interpret the concentration relative to arterial or venous pH** — metabolic acid–base status affects
  transcellular shift and renal loss [M1].
- ⚠️ **Serum concentrations are often slightly higher than plasma**, from in-vitro contamination by
  intracellular red cell potassium [M1].

## Hyperkalaemia

> ⚠️ **Severe hyperkalaemia — serum potassium above 6.0 mmol/L — is a medical emergency that can cause
> muscle weakness, arrhythmias and/or cardiac arrest** [M1]. **Most commonly caused by renal failure, and
> rare when renal function is normal** [M1].

**Other causes** [M1]: **drugs that raise potassium** — potassium supplements, potassium-sparing
diuretics, **ACE inhibitors, NSAIDs, trimethoprim** · **tissue damage or breakdown** — trauma, burns,
haemolysis, rhabdomyolysis · **acidosis**.

### When to start

**Start immediately if there are ECG changes consistent with severe hyperkalaemia.** ⚠️ **In the absence
of symptoms or ECG changes, first exclude spurious hyperkalaemia — a haemolysed specimen, a high platelet
count — before starting treatment** [M1].

### The sequence, and what each step does *not* do

| Step | Drug | Onset and duration |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Stabilise the cardiac cell membrane** | **Calcium gluconate or calcium chloride by IV injection** | ⚠️ **Effects begin within a few minutes and last 30–60 minutes. Repeat if necessary** [M1] |
| **2. Promote cellular uptake** | **IV glucose and insulin** | **Insulin begins within 10–20 minutes and lasts 4–6 hours.** **Glucose is not required if hyperglycaemic** [M1] |
| | **Salbutamol**, nebulised, rarely IV | ⚠️ **Usually only short term, in patients with ongoing symptoms or serious ECG changes after calcium and insulin/glucose** [M1] |
| | **IV sodium bicarbonate** in severe acidosis | ⚠️ **Caution in fluid overload or heart failure; rapid alkalinisation may cause tetany by lowering calcium** [M1] |
| **3. Promote excretion** | **IV/oral furosemide and saline** if not in renal failure; **haemofiltration or dialysis** | Once the emergency is treated [M1] |

⚠️ **Check local protocols to guide calcium dosage and administration — AMH gives no dose** [M1].
**Calcium gluconate is less irritant and contains less calcium per gram than calcium chloride** [M1].

> ### ⚠️ Two hard stops
> **Do not give calcium and sodium bicarbonate in the same IV line — precipitation may occur** [M1].
>
> **In hyperkalaemia due to digoxin toxicity, seek advice from a toxicologist or a Poisons Information
> Centre, phone 13 11 26.** ⚠️ **Manufacturers of calcium injections contraindicate use with digoxin, but
> specialists may use them in digoxin toxicity** [M1].

> ### ⚠️ The point that is easy to miss
> **None of the membrane-stabilising or cellular-uptake measures reduces total body potassium** [M1].

**Cation exchange agents** [M1]: **polystyrene sulfonate resins** slightly increase excretion ⚠️ **although
their effectiveness is debatable** — any effect takes some hours, repeated dosing may be needed, and **it
is not a substitute for emergency measures or dialysis**. **Patiromer**'s onset **may take up to 7 hours**
after oral administration; ⚠️ **it is not a substitute for emergency measures, and long-term safety and
efficacy data are lacking**.

## Hypokalaemia

| Band | Serum potassium | Clinical |
|---|---|---|
| **Mild** | **3.1–3.5 mmol/L** | **Usually asymptomatic** [M1] |
| **Severe** | **below 2.5 mmol/L** | ⚠️ **Muscle weakness, palpitations, arrhythmias and/or sudden cardiac death** [M1] |

**Establish and treat the underlying cause** [M1]:

- **Potassium loss** — GI (vomiting, diarrhoea) · renal (diuretics, hyperaldosteronism) · severe burns ·
  other drugs such as corticosteroids.
- **Intracellular shift** — often drugs: **IV insulin for hyperglycaemia, high-dose salbutamol**.
- ⚠️ **Reduced oral intake rarely causes hypokalaemia on its own in healthy people.**

> ### ⚠️ Check magnesium
> **Check serum magnesium and replace if deficient — hypokalaemia may be refractory to treatment if
> hypomagnesaemia is present** [M1].

**Replacement** [M1]:

- **Severe acute loss may require IV treatment**, especially if symptomatic. ⚠️ **Give IV slowly as a
  thoroughly mixed, dilute solution to avoid life-threatening arrhythmias.** **Monitor serum potassium
  frequently — and fluid balance if giving large volumes — and monitor ECG.**
- **In chronic loss and less urgent hypokalaemia the usual oral supplement is potassium chloride.**
- ⚠️ **Routine use of oral potassium supplements with thiazide diuretics used for hypertension is not
  recommended** — potassium loss is usually small, and **potassium-sparing agents such as ACE inhibitors
  or potassium-sparing diuretics are preferred**.
- **Take oral potassium chloride with a meal and plenty of fluid to reduce the risk of GI irritation.**
