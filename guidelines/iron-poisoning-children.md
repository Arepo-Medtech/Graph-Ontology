# Iron poisoning in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** risk assessment by elemental iron dose, investigations, whole bowel irrigation, desferrioxamine and disposition for acute iron ingestion in children. General principles are in `poisoning-initial-management-children`; the AMH decontamination counterpart is `gastrointestinal-decontamination`. Therapeutic iron (not poisoning) is `iron-deficiency`.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 24 of 24 claims quoted verbatim from the **RCH Melbourne Clinical Practice Guideline *Iron poisoning*** (Last updated June 2020); **35 fragments re-checked by machine. 5 quoted doses.**

> ⚠️ **Risk is set by ELEMENTAL iron per kg, not the salt.** **Activated charcoal does not bind iron.** A 6–24 hour latent phase can look like recovery before systemic toxicity.

---

## Key points

- ⚠️ **Calculate elemental iron, not the amount of iron salt** — the elemental content varies by salt form. [S1]
- **Iron is also in some plant fertilisers (sulphate of iron) and snail baits (iron phosphate).** [S1]
- **Elemental iron examples:** FGF 250 mg ferrous sulphate = 80 mg · Ferrogradumet/Ferrograd C 325 mg ferrous sulphate = 105 mg · Ferro-Liquid 30 mg/mL = 6 mg/mL · Maltofer tablets 370 mg iron polymaltose = 100 mg · Maltofer syrup/drops 37 mg/mL = 10 mg/mL. [S1]
- **Assess:** any ingestion over 40 mg/kg elemental iron · unknown quantity · any symptomatic child. **Calculate the maximum possible dose per kg** and consider co-ingestants such as paracetamol. [S1]

## Dose–response (elemental iron)

- **Under 20 mg/kg:** asymptomatic. **20–40 mg/kg:** GI symptoms only, usually under 6 hours. **40–60 mg/kg:** GI symptoms, systemic toxicity not expected. ⚠️ **60–120 mg/kg: potential systemic toxicity. Over 120 mg/kg: potentially lethal.** [S1]

## Clinical course

- **No symptoms in the first 6 hours makes significant toxicity unlikely.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Severe poisoning often has a 6–24 hour latent period** when GI symptoms resolve before systemic toxicity — **improvement then may precede deterioration.** [S1]
- **Later phases:** cardiogenic shock and acidosis (6–72 hours: hypotension, anion-gap metabolic acidosis, multi-organ failure, coma) · hepatic necrosis (12–96 hours: liver failure, jaundice, coagulopathy, hypoglycaemia) · bowel obstruction (2–8 weeks). [S1]

## Investigations

- **Asymptomatic, tablet ingestion: abdominal X-ray — if negative, no further investigation or observation.** Liquid ingestion: AXR not indicated. **Unknown amount or over 40 mg/kg: serum iron 4-hourly until falling.** [S1]
- **Symptomatic:** AXR (tablets), blood gas, glucose, **serum iron immediately and again at 4 hours**, FBE, UEC, LFTs, clotting, group and cross-match. **Slow-release or enteric-coated: repeat at 6–8 hours.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Levels after 4–6 hours may underestimate toxicity**, and **once desferrioxamine starts, most automated lab iron levels are inaccurate.** [S1]

## Resuscitation and decontamination

- **IV fluid 20 mL/kg for hypovolaemia or hypotension**; potassium and glucose as needed. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Activated charcoal does not bind iron and is not indicated.** **Whole bowel irrigation is the decontamination of choice** — indicated when AXR shows tablets **and** more than 60 mg/kg was ingested; discuss with a toxicologist first. [S1]
- **WBI: nasogastric colonic lavage solution 30 mL/kg/hr until rectal effluent is clear.** ⚠️ **Contraindicated with bowel obstruction or haemorrhage.** [S1]
- **Endoscopic removal** may be indicated for a potentially lethal ingestion (over 120 mg/kg) when WBI has failed or is impractical. [S1]

## Antidote: desferrioxamine

- **Consider desferrioxamine (with a toxicologist) if:** serum iron over 90 micromol/L · 60–90 micromol/L with tablets on AXR or symptoms · ⚠️ **altered conscious state, hypotension, tachycardia, tachypnoea or worsening symptoms, whatever the dose or level.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Don't wait for a level if there is altered conscious state, shock, severe acidosis (pH under 7.1) or worsening symptoms** — start without delay. A falling bicarbonate is a reasonable surrogate when iron levels aren't available. [S1]
- **Desferrioxamine 15 mg/kg/hr IV**, reduced after four hours so the total **does not exceed 80 mg/kg/24 hours.** [S1]
- **Usually 12–16 hours; continue until** asymptomatic, decontamination complete, anion-gap acidosis resolved and **serum iron under 60 micromol/L.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Pulmonary toxicity:** use with caution beyond 24 hours, on toxicologist advice. If oliguria or anuria develop, dialysis may be needed to remove ferrioxamine. [S1]

## Disposition

- **Consult/admit locally:** over 40 mg/kg or unknown quantity · intentional overdose · desferrioxamine being considered or worsening symptoms. [S1]
- **Transfer** when desferrioxamine and/or WBI is needed, conscious state is significantly decreased or not improving, or respiratory support is needed. **PIPER: 1300 137 650.** [S1]
- **Discharge:** under 40 mg/kg, negative AXR (tablets) and asymptomatic 6 hours after ingestion; **or** over 40 mg/kg only if still asymptomatic and serum iron is falling and under 60 micromol/L on two measurements 4 hours apart. [S1]
- **Adolescents with deliberate ingestion need a self-harm risk assessment and drug and alcohol assessment before discharge.** [S1]
