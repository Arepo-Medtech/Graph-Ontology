## What the drugs do

**Topical anticholinergics aid eye examination by causing mydriasis (pupil dilation) and cycloplegia
(paralysis of accommodation)** [A1].

**Phenylephrine (a sympathomimetic) may be used as an adjunct to anticholinergics for mydriasis, eg to
ensure maximal dilation in difficult-to-dilate pupils. An ocular topical anaesthetic may be used beforehand
in some cases** [A1].

**Indications:** **mydriasis for examination of the ocular fundus; cycloplegia for diagnostic refraction;
uveitis, for pain relief and prevention or freeing of posterior adhesions** [A1].

## ⚠️ Before you dilate

> ⚠️ **Dilating the pupils may reduce ability to assess head injury** [A1].

**In significant head injury:** ⚠️ **use short-acting agents only, with care; consult the patient's
neurosurgeon or intensive care specialist. Before use, note appearance and light reaction of pupils.**
⚠️ **ALWAYS make a note that pupils were dilated intentionally** [A1].

| Also check | Why [A1] |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ **Risk factors for angle-closure glaucoma** | ⚠️ **an acute angle-closure crisis may rarely be precipitated.** Use **under specialist supervision** in patients who have had treatment for an angle-closure crisis |
| **Lenticular subluxation** | **small risk of anterior lens displacement** |
| **Diabetes** | ⚠️ **adequate mydriasis may not be achieved** — **consider phenylephrine as an adjunct** for diagnostic purposes |
| ⚠️ **Darkly pigmented irides** | ⚠️ **mydriatic and cycloplegic effects may be REDUCED and their onset DELAYED compared with light irides.** Consider **a higher concentration, a second drop, or adding phenylephrine** |

> ⚠️ **The last two matter together.** The patients most likely to need a dilated fundus examination
> include **people with diabetes**, and the drug works **less well and more slowly** in **diabetes** and in
> **darkly pigmented irides**. A failed dilation is not a reason to abandon the examination — A1 gives
> three ways to get it.

## Choosing the agent

| Drug | Mydriasis: peak / duration | Cycloplegia: peak / duration | AMH's comment [A1] |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⚠️ **Atropine 1%** | **30–40 min** / ⚠️ **7–10 DAYS** | **1–3 h** / ⚠️ **7–14 DAYS** | ⚠️ **rarely used for diagnostic indications as it can affect near vision for at least a week after use**; **may be considered for refraction when maximal cycloplegia is required** |
| **Cyclopentolate 0.5–1%** | **30–60 min** / **1 day** | **25–75 min** / **6–24 h** | ⚠️ **preferred for cycloplegic refraction**; **may be considered for mydriasis if other agents fail** |
| **Tropicamide 0.5–1%** | **20–40 min** / **6 h** | *(incomplete)* **30–40 min** / **2–6 h** | ⚠️ **preferred for mydriasis** — **may be combined with phenylephrine if additional dilation is required**; **may be considered for cycloplegic refraction, although its effects are less reliable** |

*(Figures are for a single dose.)*

⚠️ **The spread is the whole point: 6 hours against 7–14 days.** Reaching for atropine when tropicamide
would do costs the patient a week of blurred near vision.

## Reversal

> ⚠️ **Although pilocarpine (a cholinergic agent) may be used to reverse mydriasis due to phenylephrine, it
> is not routinely recommended; note that pilocarpine is NOT effective for reversal of mydriasis due to
> topical anticholinergics** [A1].

⚠️ **There is effectively no reversal for the agents you will actually have used.** Plan the duration
before instilling, not afterwards.

## Adverse effects

**Systemic effects appear to be dose-related. They are particularly rare with tropicamide** [A1].

**Common:** **intolerance to bright light (glare), stinging on instillation (especially 1% cyclopentolate),
blurred vision (especially near vision), transient intraocular pressure elevation (especially in
pre-existing ocular hypertension)** [A1].

**Infrequent:** **contact allergic blepharitis (atropine); persistent ocular irritation — mucus discharge,
severe watering discharge, superficial punctate keratopathy and characteristically NO ITCH; punctal
stenosis with prolonged use (years); insomnia; drowsiness (especially cyclopentolate)** [A1].

⚠️ **Rare systemic toxicity: dryness of skin and mouth, fever, facial flushing, tachycardia, irritability,
disorientation, ataxia, visual hallucinations, incoherent speech, delirium, psychosis, seizures,
hyperactivity (children)** [A1].

> ⚠️ **An eye drop can cause delirium and seizures.** Rare, dose-related, and the reason tropicamide is the
> default.

## Counselling

**Enlarged pupils cause blurred vision and sensitivity to bright light; wearing dark glasses may help. Your
ability to judge distance may be impaired. Do not drive or operate machinery while vision is
disturbed** [A1].

**Tell your health professional if the eye becomes painful or red, or if vision deteriorates** [A1].

**Parents or carers of children: following pupil dilation, your child may not be able to perform skilled
tasks until their vision clears** [A1].

## Ocular surface anaesthesia

**Lidocaine with fluorescein, oxybuprocaine, proxymetacaine and tetracaine (eye)** — **block nerve
conduction reversibly**, indicated for **short-term ocular surface anaesthesia** [A1].

| Drug | Type | Onset | Duration | Initial sting | Punctate epithelial damage [A1] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lidocaine** *(not available as a single-ingredient product)* | **amide** | **2–5 min** | **20–30 min** | **30 s** | **least of the four** |
| **Oxybuprocaine** | **ester** | **20 s** | **10–20 min** | **10 s** | **intermediate** |
| **Proxymetacaine** | **ester** | **20 s** | **10–20 min** | **10 s** | **intermediate** |
| ⚠️ **Tetracaine** | **ester** | **20 s** | **20 min** | **30 s** | ⚠️ **greatest of the four** |

### ⚠️ The rules that matter

> ⚠️ **Topical anaesthetics mask the protective mechanism of pain** [A1].
>
> ⚠️ **Prolonged use impairs corneal epithelial healing, prevents reflex ocular protection and masks
> progression of keratopathy; use for short procedures only (<20 minutes)** [A1].
>
> ⚠️ **NEVER prescribe for home use** [A1].

**Do not use long term because of epithelial toxicity, ie acute corneal ulceration** [A1].

### Sequencing

⚠️ **Topical anaesthetics increase corneal permeability and intraocular bioavailability of other topical
drugs; they also reduce the initial stinging of other topical drugs and should be instilled FIRST** [A1].

⚠️ **That cuts both ways** — it is why the anaesthetic goes in first for comfort, **and** why the drug that
follows it is absorbed more than it otherwise would be.

**For corneal scrapings, use preservative-free drops — preservative may affect microbiological
culture** [A1].

**After instillation: close eyes and dab away tears without rubbing** [A1].
