# Community-acquired needlestick injury in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** risk stratification, first aid, testing of the child and the source, and tetanus, hepatitis B, hepatitis C and HIV post-exposure prophylaxis after a community-acquired needlestick injury (CA-NSI) in a child. Adult/other-source counterparts: `hepatitis-b`, `hepatitis-c` and `hiv` (ASHM, adult primary care). Related: `tetanus-prone-wounds`.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 17 of 17 claims quoted verbatim from the **RCH Melbourne Clinical Practice Guideline *Community acquired needle stick injury*** (Last Updated January 2021); **17 fragments re-checked by machine. 0 quoted doses.**

## Key points

- **There are no published reports of an incidental community needlestick in a child transmitting a blood-borne virus** (hepatitis B, hepatitis C or HIV). [S1]
- **Decide whether the CA-NSI is high risk, and establish the child's immunisation status.** [S1]

## Assessment

- **History:** time, date and place · body site, superficial or deep · source known or unknown · needle/syringe type · first aid given · visible blood on or in the needle · **immunisation history, specifically tetanus and hepatitis B**. [S1]
- ⚠️ **High-risk CA-NSI:** source known to be infected with a blood-borne virus · deliberate assault · deep, large-volume injection with a hollow-bore needle · adolescent with a personal history of injecting drug use · needle placed directly into a vein or artery · device visibly contaminated with blood. [S1]

## Management

- **Assess and manage associated injuries first. First aid: wash the site with soap and water**, and dispose of the needle safely. [S1]
- ⚠️ **High-risk CA-NSI: discuss with a senior clinician and consider referral to Infectious Diseases.** [S1]

## Investigations

- **Immunised child: test hepatitis B surface antibody (HBsAb), marked urgent, and follow up the result within 72 hours** (in ED or via the GP). The anti-HBs level should be over 10 mIU/mL. [S1]
- **Store serum; do not routinely test the child for hepatitis C or HIV.** [S1]
- **Source, if known:** test hepatitis B surface antigen, hepatitis C serology and HIV serology. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not send the needle or syringe for testing** — results on discarded injecting equipment are unreliable. [S1]

## Post-exposure prophylaxis

- **Tetanus: needlestick injuries are tetanus-prone wounds** — see `tetanus-prone-wounds`. [S1]
- **Hepatitis B:** follow the page's flowchart, with Australian Red Cross Lifeblood for immunoglobulin and the Immunisation Handbook for immunisation. [S1]
- **Hepatitis C: no post-exposure prophylaxis is available.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **HIV: only children with a high-risk CA-NSI are offered post-exposure prophylaxis** — discuss with Infectious Diseases. [S1]
- **PEP immunisations begin a catch-up schedule**; refer children needing further doses to their GP or local immunisation service. [S1]

## Discharge and follow-up

- **Discharge when** the necessary treatment, immunisations and a plan to follow up results are arranged. [S1]
- **Reassure parents that the risk of viral transmission is exceedingly low.** Unimmunised children: give written information on hepatitis B and tetanus catch-up doses at minimum. Consider referral to the Infectious Diseases outpatient clinic. [S1]
