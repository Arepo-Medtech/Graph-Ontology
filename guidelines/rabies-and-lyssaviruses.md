# Rabies and other lyssaviruses

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** pre-exposure vaccination, wound management and the exposure categories that decide post-exposure prophylaxis.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 11 of 11 claims quoted verbatim from the **Australian Immunisation Handbook**; **12 fragments re-checked by machine. 3 quoted doses.**

> ⚠️ **Australia is not rabies-enzootic — but Australian bat lyssavirus is here.** It has been found in **all 4 species of Australian flying fox**. **Any direct contact with a bat is a Category III exposure.**

---

## ⚠️ Why it matters

- **Rabies is caused by exposure to saliva or neural tissue from an infected animal** — a bite or scratch that breaks the skin, or **direct contact with a mucosal surface such as the nose, eye or mouth**. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Once symptomatic it is almost always fatal.** **Australia is not rabies-enzootic**, but **bats anywhere in the world are a potential source of lyssaviruses** — and **Australian bat lyssavirus is found in all 4 Australian fruit bat species and several insectivorous bat species**. [S1]

## ⚠️ Wound management — immediately

- ⚠️ **Immediately and thoroughly wash all bite wounds and scratches with soap and water, then apply a virucidal preparation such as povidone-iodine.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do NOT suture a bite from a potentially rabid animal.** Clean, debride and **infiltrate the wound with rabies immunoglobulin**. [S1]
- **Post-exposure prophylaxis = prompt wound management + rabies vaccine + in some cases human rabies immunoglobulin**, recommended for **anyone potentially exposed**. [S1]

## ⚠️ Exposure categories

- **Category I — no exposure:** touching or feeding animals, licks on intact skin, contact with blood, urine or faeces. **No prophylaxis if the contact history is reliable.** [S1]
- **Category II — exposure:** nibbling of uncovered skin; minor scratches or abrasions without bleeding. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Category III — severe exposure:** single or multiple **transdermal bites or scratches**; **saliva on mucous membrane or broken skin**; and ⚠️ **ANY direct contact with bats**. [S1]

## Pre-exposure vaccination

- **Recommended for: people who have contact with bats · travellers to rabies-enzootic regions, on a risk assessment · laboratory workers with live lyssaviruses.** [S1]
- **Four pre-exposure options exist, and there is no preference** among them — choose on circumstance and preference. [S1]
- **Travellers to enzootic areas need a risk assessment** of likely animal contact and **access to emergency care** at the destination. [S1]
