# Burns in children — acute management

## Key points

- **Manage a burn as trauma**, prioritising burns first aid. **Accurate TBSA drives the fluid decision — and TBSA excludes epidermal burns.** [S1]
- **Primary survey first — do not get distracted by the burn.** [S1]

## Primary survey

- ⚠️ **Airway burn/inhalation signs** — stridor, hoarseness, black sputum, respiratory distress, singed nasal hairs, facial swelling, soot in the mouth, intraoral oedema, significant neck burn: **consider early intubation.** Suspected airway burn or carbon monoxide: **high-flow oxygen.** [S1]
- **Full-thickness or circumferential chest/abdominal wall burns with abnormal breathing: consider escharotomy** — needs the tertiary burns service. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Early shock — look for a cause other than the burn.** Ideally 2 points of IV/IO access; bolus for haemodynamic instability; **IV fluid if over 10% TBSA**, rate by the modified Parkland formula. [S1]

## First aid

- **Remove clothing, nappy and jewellery** in contact with the burn and distal to it. **Cool as soon as possible (within 3 hours) for a total of 20 minutes with cool running water.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Never apply ice; avoid hydrogel burn products.** Cooling may be paused for hypothermia or significant distress. [S1]
- **Cling film lengthways — never wrapped circumferentially, and not on the face or a chemical burn.** Paraffin ointment for facial burns. [S1]
- **Prevent hypothermia:** remove wet clothes and dressings after cooling, cover the wound and child, warm the room or use warming devices, consider warm IV fluids. [S1]
- **Chemical burns: discuss decontamination with Poisons Information, 131126.** [S1]

## History and analgesia

- ⚠️ **Consider child abuse:** immersion scalds, patterned contact burns, a mechanism that does not fit the pattern, burns on the back or genitalia. Check tetanus status. [S1]
- **Analgesia early and throughout, especially for dressings and mobilisation. Procedural sedation is recommended for debridement and dressings.** [S1]

## Depth and extent

- **Depth is hard to judge in the first 48–72 hours.** Do not count erythema-only (epidermal) areas. [S1]
- **Depth by appearance:** epidermal — red, no blisters, brisk refill, sensation present · superficial dermal — pale pink, blisters, brisk, painful · mid dermal — dark pink, blisters, sluggish, ± sensation · deep dermal — blotchy red, ± blisters, refill and sensation absent · full thickness — white, no blisters, refill and sensation absent. [S1]
- **Baseline tests:** major burn (≥10% TBSA) — Hb, electrolytes, BGL, group and hold, VBG · suspected inhalation — VBG for CO or cyanide · electrical — ECG. [S1]

## Fluid resuscitation (burns ≥10% TBSA)

- **Count from the time of the burn, not presentation.** Resuscitation fluid is **Hartmann's**, with maintenance **sodium chloride 0.9% + glucose 5% through a separate line**; volume by the Modified Parkland Formula. [S1]
- **Give half in the first 8 hours, the rest over the next 16.** Review the rate every 1–2 hours against urine output and fluid state — the total is an estimate. **Hartmann's is first choice; sodium chloride 0.9% if unavailable.** [S1]
- **Urinary catheter; nil by mouth with a nasogastric tube** (risk of ileus). Delayed resuscitation, electrical and inhalation injury need more fluid — discuss with the specialist team. [S1]

## Wounds and special burns

- **De-roof blisters over 5 mm or crossing a joint.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Facial burns: fluorescein 2% for corneal damage.** Ocular burns: irrigate at least 30 minutes with sodium chloride 0.9%, topical chloramphenicol, **urgent ophthalmology review.** Alkali eye injury may need 2–4 hours of irrigation, until pain stops and eye pH is 7.0–7.2. [S1]
- **Suspected carbon monoxide or cyanide poisoning: 100% oxygen**, and liaise early with the burns unit, ICU and Poisons Information. [S1]
- **Electrical injury:** involve the burns service and ICU early; look for entry/exit wounds; consider spinal precautions and **24-hour ECG monitoring**; monitor CK and urine haemoglobin. [S1]

## Referral

- ⚠️ **Discuss with the state paediatric burns service** for: over 5% TBSA · over 5% full thickness · face, ears, eyes, neck, hands, feet, genitalia, perineum or a major joint even if under 5% · any inhalation injury · circumferential · chemical or electrical · associated trauma or spinal injury · **under 12 months** · suspected abuse. [S1]
- **Involve the local paediatric team** for suspected abuse or self-inflicted burn, multiple co-morbidities, or doubt about caring for the burn at home. [S1]
