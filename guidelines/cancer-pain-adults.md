## When to assess in full

- **Do a comprehensive assessment when a new patient scores pain 2 or more** on a zero-to-10 numerical rating scale, **or an existing patient reports a new pain or a sudden, unexpected change in intensity.** [S1]
- **If cognitive impairment makes self-report difficult, use the Abbey Pain Scale.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Consider an oncological emergency** as the cause: fracture (or imminent fracture), brain, epidural or leptomeningeal metastasis, infection, or an obstructed or perforated abdominal organ. [S1]

## Before and during opioids

- **Exclude causes other than cancer before starting opioid therapy for pain.** [S1]
- **Elderly or frail patients may need dose reduction and regular reassessment.** [S1]
- **Use the eviQ Opioid Conversion Calculator** for dose equivalents of opioid preparations available in Australia. [S1]
- **Tell every patient starting opioids that constipation is very common**, and teach preventive bowel care. [S1]
- **Refer for specialist review** if opioid adverse effects persist after trialling more than one opioid, or opioid-induced hyperalgesia is suspected. [S1]
- **A stable opioid dose may not affect driving**, provided the person takes no other medicines that impair driving. [S1]
- **Prescribe bisphosphonates with caution in renal impairment.** [S1]
