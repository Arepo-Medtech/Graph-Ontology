# Hepatitis A

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-21 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults, primary care.

> **Australian primary source.** ASHM *Australian STI Management Guidelines for use in primary care* —
> *Hepatitis A* [S1], modified 30 June 2026.

## The whole of management is prevention

There is no antiviral on this page. **Care is supportive** [S1] — and both auditable outcomes measure
**vaccination**:

> **100% of HIV-positive men who have sex with men are vaccinated or immune** [S1].
> **100% of close contacts receive vaccination if susceptible** [S1].

Twenty-seven guidelines in, this is the only topic whose quality measures are entirely about people who do
**not** have the disease.

**Offer hepatitis A immunisation to people at risk**, including **men who have sex with men, people who
inject drugs, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people, people in custodial settings, and sex
workers** [S1].

**Infection with hepatitis A confers lifelong immunity** [S1], and **it does not cause chronic
hepatitis** [S1].

## ⚠️ Occupational exclusion

> **If there is a risk of occupational transmission of hepatitis A — e.g. food handlers, healthcare workers,
> child-care workers — then [they] must be excluded from work for at least 1 week after the onset of
> jaundice** [S1].

The only place in the set where the guideline directs that a patient **be kept away from their job**, and
the only one phrased as **must** rather than *advise* or *consider*. It is a public health instruction
sitting inside a clinical consultation, and it has employment consequences the clinician has to raise.

**Advise no sexual contact during the acute illness and for at least 1 week after the onset of
jaundice** [S1].

## ⚠️ Do not screen for hepatitis A routinely — including before vaccinating

> **Screening for hepatitis A is not recommended during routine sexual health screening, or before
> hepatitis A immunisation, unless a previous infection is reported or considered likely** [S1].

The sixth explicit recommendation *against* in the set, and the second clause is the useful one:
**do not check immunity before vaccinating.** Serology before a vaccine is a common reflex; the source says
to skip it unless there is a specific reason.

## Diagnosis

| Specimen | Test | Meaning [S1] |
|---|---|---|
| **Blood** | **AST, ALT, bilirubin** | **Raised in acute hepatitis** |
| | **Anti-HAV IgM** | **Acute HAV infection** — **persists for 3–6 months** |
| | **Anti-HAV Ig-total** | **Previous infection or vaccination** |

The IgM persistence window matters both ways: a positive IgM may reflect an infection from months ago, and
total antibody cannot distinguish past infection from vaccination.

**Exclude other causes of acute hepatitis, including hepatitis B and hepatitis C** [S1].

## Treatment

| Situation | Recommended [S1] |
|---|---|
| **Symptomatic infection** | **Supportive care. Hospitalisation if severe illness or clinical deterioration** |

**Severe hepatitis may require hospital admission** [S1]. **In pregnancy, seek specialist advice — hepatitis
can be more severe in pregnant people** [S1].

**Comprehensive STI testing may be appropriate, depending on the patient's history** [S1].

**Notify the state or territory health department** [S1].

## Presentation

- **Incubation period 15–50 days, mean 28 days** [S1].
- **Acute hepatitis: lethargy, nausea, fever, anorexia for a few days, then jaundice, pale stools and dark
  urine** [S1].
- **Usually asymptomatic in children; more severe illness in the elderly and in pregnant people. Usually
  resolves in 1 month** [S1].
- **Liver failure is rare** [S1].

**Most infections are from contaminated food or water, but faecal-oral transmission can also occur during
sex, especially in men who have sex with men** [S1] — which is why the topic appears in an STI guideline at
all.

## Contact tracing and post-exposure prophylaxis

**Notifiable condition.** **Contact tracing is important to prevent re-infection and reduce
transmission** [S1].

> **Trace back 50 days from onset of symptoms** [S1].

> **Infectivity is for 2 weeks before the onset of prodromal symptoms, to 1 week after the onset of
> jaundice** [S1].

The infectious period **begins two weeks before the patient feels unwell**. By the time hepatitis A is
diagnosed, the transmission has already happened — which is what the 50-day trace-back and the contact
vaccination outcome are designed around.

> **The diagnosing doctor is responsible for initiating and documenting a discussion about contact
> tracing** [S1].

The same named-responsibility clause as HIV — the only two topics in the set that carry it.

> **Post-exposure prophylaxis using hepatitis A vaccine or normal human immunoglobulin (NHIG) — passive
> immunisation — can be used to prevent secondary cases in close contacts** [S1].

## Follow-up

**Review in 1 week** to confirm or support contact tracing, and — **if high risk, e.g. men who have sex with
men** — to provide further sexual health education and prevention counselling [S1].

There is no test of cure and no retesting on this page; the follow-up exists for the **contacts and the
next infection**, not for the index patient's liver.
