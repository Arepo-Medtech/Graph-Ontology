# Nitrous oxide misuse in adolescents

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognition, investigation, vitamin B12 and methionine treatment, admission and discharge for neurological toxicity from chronic recreational nitrous oxide ('nangs') use. Acute inhalant toxicity is in `recreational-drug-overdose-children`; general poisoning care is `poisoning-initial-management-children`. No adult counterpart exists in this compendium.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 17 of 17 claims quoted verbatim from the **RCH Melbourne Clinical Practice Guideline *Nitrous Oxide Misuse*** (Last updated February 2021); **23 fragments re-checked by machine. 2 quoted doses.**

> ⚠️ **A normal vitamin B12 level does not exclude toxicity.** With clinical signs and a history of nitrous oxide misuse, **give IM vitamin B12 and oral methionine anyway.** ☎️ **Poisons Information Centre, 24 hours: 13 11 26.**

---

## Recognise it

- **Chronic high-level use impairs vitamin B12 function, causing neurological dysfunction and rarely myelosuppression**; presentations often resemble **subacute combined degeneration of the spinal cord.** [S1]
- **Assess every child with deliberate self-poisoning, and any child with neurological symptoms and a history of nitrous oxide misuse.** [S1]
- **Higher-risk pattern:** regular daily use, or more than 100 bulbs per session, is often reported in cases with neurological dysfunction. [S1]
- **Symptoms progress over days or weeks:** altered sensation, muscle weakness, gait disturbance; rarely depression, irritability or personality change. [S1]
- **Examination:** sensory deficits (dermatomal or patchy), **impaired proprioception and coordination — dorsal columns first**, sensory ataxia, reduced power and reflexes. [S1]
- **Acute toxicity is rare** — brief confusion, hallucinations or falls from temporary ataxia; ⚠️ **forceful inhalation can rarely cause pneumothorax.** [S1]

## Investigate

- **FBE and blood film** for macrocytic anaemia or marrow suppression. Total and active B12 (holotranscobalamin) may be low or undetectable; **methylmalonic acid is usually raised.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not withhold vitamin B12 and methionine because levels are normal** in a patient with clinical signs and a history of nitrous oxide misuse — measured B12 does not reflect functional B12. [S1]
- **MRI brain and/or spine may show demyelination; nerve conduction studies may show impaired transmission.** [S1]

## Treat

- **Stop nitrous oxide; IM vitamin B12 and oral methionine are the mainstays.** ☎️ **Consult toxicology via the Victorian Poisons Information Centre (13 11 26).** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Admit if unable to walk independently or with haematological or psychiatric sequelae.** [S1]
- **Vitamin B12 1 mg IM daily for 2 weeks, then weekly for 4 weeks, then monthly until maximal recovery.** Higher weekly IM doses speed resolution and may improve recovery. [S1]
- **Methionine 1 g orally TDS for 2 weeks.** [S1]
- **Consult haematology if there is marrow suppression**; consider other nutritional deficiencies from poor intake. [S1]

## Escalation and discharge

- ⚠️ **Any child or adolescent with intentional overdose: consult the paediatric team and consider admission.** [S1]
- **Discharge when:** walking safely and independently · haematological dysfunction improving (if present) · safe from a mental health perspective with drug and alcohol referrals made · educated about the dangers of nitrous oxide misuse. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Before discharge, assess adolescents with deliberate ingestion for risk of further ingestion or self-harm**, and screen for other drug and alcohol use. [S1]
