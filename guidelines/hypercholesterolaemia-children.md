# Hypercholesterolaemia in children

## Recognise it

- **Suspect familial hypercholesterolaemia (FH) when LDL-C is >4 mmol/L or there is a strong family history.** Early treatment, including statins, improves long-term cardiovascular outcomes. [S1]
- **For practical purposes, childhood LDL-C ≥3.5 mmol/L is elevated.** [S1]
- **FH is autosomal dominant, about 1 in 250 children.** No universal screening, but **a one-off lipid profile is reasonable if adolescents are having other bloods.** [S1]
- **Family history that counts:** premature coronary artery disease (males <55 years, females <65 years), hypercholesterolaemia or known FH. [S1]
- **Secondary causes:** hypothyroidism, diabetes, liver disease, nephrotic syndrome; medications (steroids, atypical antipsychotics, some antiepileptics); obesity/metabolic syndrome; high saturated-fat diet. [S1]
- **Examination is typically normal. Plot BMI and blood pressure in all children**; look for tendon xanthomas, xanthelasma and corneal arcus. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Red flags for homozygous FH:** LDL-C >13 mmol/L · parental consanguinity with elevated LDL-C · tendon xanthomas. [S1]

## Investigate

- **Confirm with a fasting lipid profile**; repeat if the first was non-fasting or unexpected. [S1]
- **Screen for secondary causes:** FBE, UEC, LFTs, TFTs, HbA1C, urinalysis ± albumin:creatinine ratio. [S1]
- **If FH is suspected:** consider genetic testing if criteria are met (MBS Item 73352); baseline CK and Lp(a); **advise screening of all first-degree relatives.** [S1]

## Treat

- **Everyone:** heart-healthy diet (less saturated fat, plant sterols), regular activity, **no smoking, vaping or alcohol**, and optimise comorbidities. [S1]
- **Start a statin from age 8 years (earlier if high risk)** if LDL-C stays above target despite up to 6 months of lifestyle change. [S1]
- **LDL-C target: <4.0 mmol/L at 8-10 years; <3.5 mmol/L over 10 years.** [S1]
- **Rosuvastatin — usually first choice:** start 5 mg daily, titrate to max 20 mg; dose-adjust in renal impairment. [S1]
- **Atorvastatin:** start 10 mg daily, titrate to max 40 mg; more side effects at higher doses. [S1]
- **Pravastatin:** 10-20 mg nocte, titrate to max 40mg; less potent, but myalgia less common — an option if myalgia occurred on another statin. [S1]
- **Recheck lipids, LFTs and CK 6-12 weeks after starting or changing the dose.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Stop promptly for myopathy, rhabdomyolysis or severe hepatotoxicity** (rare). Myalgia, headache and GI upset are usually self-limiting. [S1]
- **Consider ezetimibe if a statin alone is inadequate.** ⚠️ **Advise effective contraception for adolescents starting a statin.** [S1]

## Refer

- **Local paediatric team:** suspected FH, or uncertainty about starting a statin. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Lipid team — urgent:** features of homozygous FH, or multidisciplinary care needed (genetics, cardiology, apheresis). **Routine:** LDL-C above target despite treatment, or genetic testing/cascade screening questions. [S1]
