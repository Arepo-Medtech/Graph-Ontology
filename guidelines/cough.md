# Cough

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-22 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** **acute and post-viral cough in adults and children**, and the over-the-counter products sold
for it. ⚠️ **ANTIBIOTIC CHOICE IS OUT OF SCOPE** — the antibiotic content here is about **not** prescribing
them. **Asthma, COPD, bronchiectasis, pertussis and chronic cough of established cause have their own
management and are named here only as things to look for.**

## ⚠️ First: is it just a cough?

> **Although cough is an important protective mechanism, it may also occur as a symptom of a serious
> underlying disorder.** **IDENTIFY AND TREAT ANY UNDERLYING DISORDER — asthma, GORD, postnasal drip,
> bronchitis, pertussis** [C1].
>
> **ENCOURAGE SMOKING CESSATION.** ⚠️ **CONSIDER DRUG-INDUCED COUGH — ACE INHIBITORS** [C1]. **This
> compendium's `hypertension` and `heart-failure` guidelines both make ACE inhibitors first line.**

### The examination finding that decides it [S1]

| | [S1] |
|---|---|
| **Normal lung sounds + moist cough in a healthy young adult** | ⚠️ **ACUTE BRONCHITIS IS VERY LIKELY OVER-DIAGNOSED** — normal auscultation **rules out** a lower respiratory tract infection |
| ⚠️ **Abnormal lung sounds + WET cough** (as opposed to moist) **productive of a few teaspoons of phlegm** | **suggests LOWER airway pathology, which needs specific treatment** |

**The distinction S1 draws is between mucus in the pharynx producing a moist cough — erroneously called a
"chesty cough" — and genuine lower-airway disease.**

## ⚠️ How long it actually lasts — the most useful thing to tell a patient

> **In one series of 131 patients with URTI and cough recruited in general practice, 78% COUGHED FOR AT
> LEAST ONE WEEK, 35% FOR THREE WEEKS, AND ONE FOR 10 WEEKS** [S1].
>
> **In a systematic review of the PLACEBO ARMS of controlled trials in children treated for URTIs, IT TOOK
> 25 DAYS BEFORE 90% HAD RECOVERED from acute cough** [S1].

**For context: adults have 2–4 URTIs per year and young children twice that; symptoms typically peak around
day 3 or 4 and mostly resolve by day 7 — but the cough outlasts them. URTIs make up almost 6% of
presentations to Australian general practices** [S1].

> ⚠️ **THE COMMONEST EXPLANATION for a post-viral cough in the weeks after a URTI is POSTNASAL DRIP COUGH
> (upper airway cough syndrome), secondary to acute rhinosinusitis and the initial viral pharyngitis.**
> **It can be dry or moist, WORSE IN THE EVENING AND ON ARISING FROM SLEEP, with difficulty clearing
> sticky mucus or throat discomfort** [S1].

## ⚠️ What the evidence says about the things people buy

**AMH:** ⚠️ **"Acute cough associated with a viral airways infection is self-limiting and may be managed
with fluids and humidity. MEDICATIONS HAVE QUESTIONABLE EFFICACY"** [C1].
**S1 puts numbers and trials to it.**

| Intervention | Verdict [S1] |
|---|---|
| **Honey** | ✅ **helps relieve cough and sore throat IN CHILDREN** — ⚠️ **most children in the Cochrane review got it FOR ONE NIGHT ONLY** |
| **Anti-inflammatory OR local anaesthetic lozenges/sprays** | ✅ **both show significant reduction in pain over placebo** |
| **Nasal saline irrigation** | ✅ **possibly beneficial** — ⚠️ **trials generally too small with HIGH RISK OF BIAS** |
| **Intranasal oxymetazoline** | ✅ **small but statistically significant improvement in nasal congestion** |
| ⚠️ **Oral decongestant + antihistamine + analgesic ("cold and flu tablets")** | ❌ **effect on individual symptoms probably TOO SMALL TO BE CLINICALLY RELEVANT** |
| ⚠️ **Antitussive and expectorant cough mixtures** | ❌ **"continue to be sold but have LONG FAILED to demonstrate any benefit beyond placebo"** |
| **Zinc lozenges** | ⚠️ **some evidence of reduced cough duration if given WITHIN 24 HOURS of onset — but the benefit may NOT be clinically significant** |
| ⚠️ **Intranasal corticosteroids** | ❌ **NO MORE EFFECTIVE THAN PLACEBO for URTI symptom duration or severity** |
| ⚠️ **Vitamin C, echinacea, steam inhalation, rest, analgesics, anti-inflammatory drugs** | ❌ **NO RELIABLE EVIDENCE that any reduces URTI duration** |
| ⚠️ **Increased fluid intake** | ❌ **NO randomised controlled trial evidence to recommend it** |

> ⚠️ **THE SYRUP MAY BE THE ACTIVE INGREDIENT.** **S1: "it may be that the most beneficial component of
> cough mixtures is the SYRUP DELIVERY VEHICLE, soothing the inflamed pharynx akin to the probable
> mechanism of honey"** [S1].
> **AMH independently: DEMULCENTS — sucrose and glycerol syrups — may suppress coughing by forming a
> protective layer over pharyngeal sensory receptors, and ⚠️ "THE HIGH PLACEBO RESPONSE SEEN IN TRIALS OF
> COUGH MEDICATIONS MAY BE RELATED TO USE OF SYRUP IN PRODUCTS"** [C1].
>
> **Two independent sources, the same conclusion, reached separately. Honey and syrup are the same
> mechanism, and one of them is cheaper.**

**S1's summary: HONEY, anti-inflammatory or local anaesthetic sprays and lozenges, and NASAL SALINE
probably reduce the duration of postnasal drip and post-viral cough** [S1].

## ⚠️ Antibiotics: the discoloured sputum conversation

> **It has been estimated that ANTIBIOTICS IN AUSTRALIA ARE PRESCRIBED FOR ACUTE RESPIRATORY TRACT
> INFECTIONS AT RATES FOUR TIMES GREATER than what is recommended by Therapeutic Guidelines** [S1].

> ⚠️ **"THERE IS GOOD EVIDENCE FOR THE POOR CORRELATION BETWEEN YELLOW OR GREEN SPUTUM AND PATHOLOGICAL
> BACTERIAL INFECTIONS"** [S1].
>
> **Patients producing discoloured sputum were MORE OFTEN PRESCRIBED ANTIBIOTICS — but this WAS NOT
> ASSOCIATED WITH FASTER RESOLUTION of symptoms** [S1].
>
> **The routine use of antibiotics for ACUTE PURULENT RHINITIS IS NOT RECOMMENDED**: no benefit, significant
> adverse effects, and antibiotic resistance risk [S1].

## AMH's product rules [C1]

> **SINGLE INGREDIENT PRODUCTS ARE PREFERABLE** [C1].
>
> ⚠️ **COMBINING A COUGH SUPPRESSANT WITH AN EXPECTORANT IS IRRATIONAL AND SHOULD BE AVOIDED** [C1].

| Class | AMH's position [C1] |
|---|---|
| **Demulcents** (sucrose, glycerol syrups) | **may suppress cough by coating pharyngeal receptors** |
| **Opioid cough suppressants** (codeine, dihydrocodeine, dextromethorphan) | **for NON-PRODUCTIVE cough**; **dextromethorphan may have FEWER adverse effects** |
| **Expectorants** (guaifenesin, ammonium salts, senega, sodium citrate) | **aim to promote expectoration in productive cough** |
| ⚠️ **Mucolytics** (bromhexine, acetylcysteine) | **EVIDENCE FOR EFFICACY IS LIMITED**; ⚠️ **bromhexine associated with a LOW RISK OF SEVERE SKIN REACTIONS**; ⚠️ **NOT RECOMMENDED in chronic bronchitis or COPD** |

### ⚠️ What else is in the bottle [C1]

- **Sedating antihistamines** (chlorphenamine, diphenhydramine, triprolidine): **may decrease cough and may
  suit postnasal drip** — ⚠️ **DO NOT USE IF THE COUGH IS PRODUCTIVE: risk of VISCID MUCUS PLUGS.**
- ⚠️ **PARACETAMOL: avoid fixed-dose combinations containing it.** **Doses may be insufficient for
  antipyretic or analgesic effect AND they increase the risk of OVERDOSAGE if taken with other paracetamol
  products.**
- ⚠️ **Sympathomimetic decongestants (pseudoephedrine, phenylephrine): AVOID.** **Doubtful therapeutic
  value; risk of cardiovascular and CNS stimulation.**

**Although doses in these products are often SUBOPTIMAL, a risk of adverse effects remains** [C1].

## ⚠️ Children

> **A DEMULCENT MAY HELP** [C1].
>
> ⚠️ **EXPECTORANTS, DECONGESTANTS, OPIOID COUGH SUPPRESSANTS AND SEDATING ANTIHISTAMINES SHOULD NOT BE
> USED FOR COUGH IN CHILDREN UNDER 6 YEARS** [C1].
>
> ⚠️ **CODEINE-CONTAINING PRODUCTS ARE CONTRAINDICATED UNDER 12 YEARS** — and **under 18 years if
> respiratory function is compromised, e.g. obstructive sleep apnoea** [C1].
>
> ⚠️ **DO NOT USE PRODUCTS CONTAINING ETHANOL** [C1].
>
> **Fixed-dose cough and cold combinations should be PARTICULARLY AVOIDED in children** [C1].

**CHILDREN WITH CHRONIC COUGH REQUIRE CAREFUL EVALUATION TO MINIMISE THE USE OF UNNECESSARY MEDICINES
INCLUDING INHALED CORTICOSTEROIDS** [C1].

> ⚠️ **ONE PAEDIATRIC EXCEPTION WHERE ANTIBIOTICS ARE INDICATED:** **in children younger than 14 years
> with a WET cough lasting MORE THAN 4 WEEKS without specific cough pointers, the diagnosis of PROTRACTED
> BACTERIAL BRONCHITIS is likely, and antibiotics are indicated** [S1].

## ⚠️ When to escalate

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| **Abnormal lung sounds + wet productive cough** | **lower airway pathology — needs specific treatment** [S1] |
| ⚠️ **Cough persisting MORE THAN SIX WEEKS with no clear underlying pathology** | ⚠️ **consider REFERRAL TO A SPECIALIST COUGH CLINIC** [S1] |
| **Child <14 years, wet cough >4 weeks, no specific pointers** | **protracted bacterial bronchitis likely — antibiotics indicated** [S1] |
| **Any suspicion of a serious underlying disorder** | **identify and treat it** [C1] |
