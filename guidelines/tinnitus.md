# Tinnitus

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-22 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults presenting with tinnitus in general practice.

> **Mixed sources.** **S1** is the **open** RACGP *Australian Journal of General Practice* review,
> **quoted verbatim and machine re-checkable** (42 claims). **A1** is the **Australian Medicines
> Handbook**, read under subscription and **paraphrased — no passage reproduced, no `source_text`

**AMH topic closed by this guideline:** *Tinnitus*.

> ⚠️ **No claim in this guideline carries a dose, and that is the correct outcome.** **Neither source
> recommends a drug for tinnitus.** The one drug intervention named — **steroids for sudden-onset
> sensorineural hearing loss** — is given **without a dose by both sources**, and none has been
> supplied from memory or carried from another guideline.

## The one-sentence version

> **The goal for managing primary, bothersome tinnitus is to implement strategies to allow the patient
> to habituate. The aim for secondary tinnitus is to investigate and manage the underlying
> cause** [S1]. ⚠️ **Drug treatment is not part of either.**

**Tinnitus may be defined as conscious awareness of a sound in the absence of an external auditory
stimulus** [S1]. ⚠️ **At least two-thirds of Australians have reported suffering from noticeable
tinnitus at some point in their lives** [S1].

**People with tinnitus almost always seek the attention of their general practitioner, who is best
placed to assess, investigate and provide appropriate counselling** [S1].

## Classify before you manage

**Tinnitus may be categorised as subjective, objective, primary or secondary. The assessment of
tinnitus begins by determining which of these types the patient presents with** [S1].

| Type | Definition [S1] |
|---|---|
| **Subjective** | **only the affected individual can hear the sound** |
| **Objective** | ⚠️ **the sound can also be heard by the examiner** — **crepitus of temporomandibular joint, bruit of vascular malformation** |
| **Primary** | **idiopathic, may or may not be associated with sensorineural hearing loss**; ⚠️ **the SNHL should be symmetrical** |
| **Secondary** | **associated with a specific underlying cause other than symmetrical SNHL** |
| **Acute / recent onset** | **apparent for <6 months** |
| **Chronic** | **apparent for >6 months** |

> **One must also differentiate between bothersome tinnitus and non-bothersome tinnitus because this
> will influence how active the treating general practitioner needs to be about management** [S1].

## ⚠️ Red flags — what changes the plan

| Feature | What it suggests | Action |
|---|---|---|
| ⚠️ **Sudden-onset SNHL** | a **time-critical** emergency inside a tinnitus presentation | ⚠️ **pure tone audiogram requested promptly, steroid therapy commenced, urgent otolaryngologist referral** [S1] |
| ⚠️ **Unilateral tinnitus** | **a focal lesion, such as a vestibular schwannoma** [S1] | **MRI is often part of the work-up** [S1]; ⚠️ **refer to an ENT specialist to exclude retrocochlear pathology such as acoustic neuroma** [A1] |
| ⚠️ **Pulsatile tinnitus** | **a vascular lesion, such as a glomus tumour** [S1] | **CT angiography in the general practice setting** [S1]; **auscultate the carotid and periauricular regions** [S1] |
| **Objective tinnitus** | the examiner can hear it — by definition not primary | seek the generating structure |
| **Associated otalgia, otorrhoea or vertigo** | **secondary tinnitus** [S1] | treat the cause |
| **Focal neurological deficit** | central pathology | **imaging warranted** [S1] |

⚠️ **Both sources independently single out unilateral tinnitus for specialist referral** — S1 for
imaging, A1 for ENT. It is the one feature they agree needs escalation.

## ⚠️ The two sources give different ototoxic drug lists

This is the finding a reader should not miss. **Neither list is a superset of the other.**

| | **A1 — AMH** | **S1 — AJGP** |
|---|---|---|
| **Aminoglycosides** | ✔ | ✔ **and vancomycin** |
| **Cancer drugs** | ✔ **cisplatin** | ✔ **cancer medications** (unnamed) |
| **Diuretics** | ✔ **loop diuretics** | ✔ **frusemide** |
| **Salicylates** | — | ✔ ⚠️ **aspirin or other salicylates** |
| **NSAIDs** | ✔ ⚠️ **NSAIDs** (broader) | — |

> ⚠️ **A clinician using only AMH's list will not think of vancomycin. A clinician using only AJGP's
> list will consider aspirin but not NSAIDs generally.** Both lists are reproduced here as each
> source gives them; ⚠️ **they have deliberately NOT been merged into a single list**, because a
> merged list would attribute to each source drugs it does not name.

**Other reversible causes:** ⚠️ **tinnitus may be associated with common and treatable conditions, eg
ear wax or otitis media** [A1] — **cerumen impaction, otitis externa, otosclerosis, otitis media,
cholesteatoma** [S1]; inner ear: **vestibular schwannoma, Meniere's disease, cochleitis/neuritis** [S1].
**Non-auditory: vascular anomalies, myoclonus such as stapedial myoclonus, nasopharyngeal
carcinoma** [S1].

⚠️ **Tinnitus can be a prominent feature of Ménière's disease** [A1] — see the **vertigo** guideline.

## History

**Timeline** (acute vs chronic), **character** (constant or pulsatile), **laterality**, **associated
otological symptoms**, **associated hearing loss and whether sudden in onset**, **noise exposure,
occupational or social**, **current medication**, **whether the tinnitus is bothersome**, and
**associated anxiety or depression** [S1].

> **A link between tinnitus and comorbid psychological disorder has been demonstrated, and a high
> prevalence of anxiety and depression is seen in tinnitus sufferers** [S1].

## Examination

**Routine cranial nerve and otological examination is required, further guided by the history** [S1].

**Tuning fork, 512 Hz** [S1]:

| Test | Method | Normal | Abnormal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Weber** | **fork in the middle of the forehead** | **sound heard equally in both ears** | **lateralising** → **conductive loss in that ear OR sensorineural loss in the contralateral ear** |
| **Rinne** | **fork on the mastoid, then vertically in front of the auricle** | **positive Rinne: air conduction louder than bone conduction** | **louder on the mastoid** → **conductive hearing loss** |

**Auscultate the head and neck including the carotid and periauricular regions, especially if the
patient reports pulsatile tinnitus** [S1].

## Investigation

> **A formal audiology assessment is recommended for ALL patients presenting with tinnitus. This is
> because a large proportion of patients with tinnitus have some degree of hearing loss** [S1].

**Conductive hearing loss almost always indicates a secondary cause for tinnitus. SNHL may indicate a
primary or secondary cause** [S1].

### Imaging — indicated only in five situations

**Imaging of the head and neck is indicated only if the patient has** [S1]:

1. ⚠️ **unilateral tinnitus** — a focal lesion; **imaging warranted**
2. ⚠️ **pulsatile tinnitus** — a vascular anomaly; **best investigated by CT angiography**
3. ⚠️ **asymmetrical hearing loss, defined as ≥10 dB difference between the two sides in three or more
   frequencies** — **MRI of the cerebellopontine angle and internal acoustic meatus (MRI-CPA/IAM) to
   rule out a vestibular schwannoma**
4. **focal neurological deficit**
5. **otosclerosis** — **third to fifth decade, slowly progressive hearing loss with coexisting
   tinnitus, conductive loss on tuning fork** — **CT of the temporal bone**

**CT of the temporal bone may also be indicated in the setting of trauma to the head and if
cholesteatoma is suspected** [S1].

⚠️ **These criteria are relayed by S1 from the 2014 American Academy of Otolaryngology – Head and Neck
Surgery clinical practice guideline** — see *Unresolved*.

## Management

**Management of secondary tinnitus is centred on identification of the underlying cause and, where
possible, reversal of the underlying condition** [S1].

For **primary** tinnitus: **if it is not bothersome and chronic, reassurance may be all that is
needed** [S1].

If it **is** bothersome:

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Hearing aids** | ⚠️ **even in the absence of marked hearing loss, hearing aids can provide some relief** [S1]; consider referral **in the presence of bothersome SNHL** [S1] |
| **Sound therapy** | **an external sound is provided to the auditory centre, the patient focuses on this sound and therefore has a reduced perception of tinnitus** [S1]. **Normally prescribed by an audiologist**, but **sound therapy alone can also be prescribed by the GP** [S1] |
| **Masking devices** | **produce sound to cover the tinnitus** [A1] |
| **CBT** | ⚠️ **tinnitus causes emotional disturbance, which stimulates the sympathetic nervous system and can increase the volume of tinnitus** [S1]. CBT **can be effective in treating a patient's response to tinnitus** [S1] — **an appropriately trained GP may provide it, or refer to a psychologist with an interest in tinnitus** [S1] |
| **Tinnitus retraining therapy** | listed among non-drug therapies patients may consider [A1] |

> **A combination of treatment options may be necessary to allow the patient to habituate — in other
> words, to not be bothered by the noise they hear** [S1].

### ⚠️ Drugs: both sources say no, from different directions

> ⚠️ **GPs should avoid prescribing antidepressants, anticonvulsants or anxiolytics for tinnitus, as
> there is no evidence for their effectiveness in treating tinnitus, and because of adverse
> effects** [S1].

> ⚠️ **Avoid drug treatment: although many have been tried — carbamazepine, antidepressants such as
> TCAs and SSRIs — there is little evidence of benefit and a risk of adverse effects** [A1].

**A variety of complementary medicines have been trialled in patients, but again there is no evidence
for their use** [S1].

### ⚠️ The distinction that gets lost

**A1 also says: consider the need for managing associated conditions, such as depression, anxiety and
insomnia** [A1].

> ⚠️ **This is not a contradiction of the advice above, and the difference is the whole point.**
> **Treat the comorbid depression or anxiety on its own merits, at its own threshold, with its own
> monitoring.** ⚠️ **Do not prescribe an antidepressant *for the tinnitus*.** Neither source supports
> the second, and a patient given an SSRI framed as tinnitus treatment has been given a drug with
> **no evidence for that indication** and **real adverse effects**.
