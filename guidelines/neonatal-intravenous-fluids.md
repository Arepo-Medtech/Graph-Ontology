# Intravenous fluids in neonates

## Key points

- **For neonates born after 32 weeks, outside neonatal intensive care.** Use the enteral route whenever possible and start enteral feeds as soon as possible. [S1]
- **Over 32 weeks and over 1500 g on short-term IV: glucose 10% for the first 24–48 hours of life**, then fluids containing sodium and potassium. **Parenteral nutrition for anyone needing IV fluids beyond 5 days.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Below birth weight? Use birth weight in all fluid calculations** unless specified. Expect up to 10% weight loss in the first week in a term neonate. [S1]
- **Goal: maintain hydration and biochemical balance — particularly avoid hypoglycaemia and hyponatraemia.** [S1]

## Red flags

- ⚠️ **Notify a senior clinician:** sodium under 135 or over 145 mmol/L, or changing by more than 0.5 mmol/L/hr · short gut or major GI pathology · polyuria (over 6 mL/kg/hr prompts assessment, especially preterm) · **fluid resuscitation over 20 mL/kg.** [S1]

## Resuscitation

- ⚠️ **Shock: sodium chloride 0.9% 10 mL/kg as fast as possible, then reassess** for further fluid. **Do not count the bolus in later calculations.** Packed red cells or albumin only on senior advice. [S1]

## Maintenance fluid choice

- **Over 32 weeks and over 1500 g: glucose 10% for the first 24–48 hours; then consider adding sodium and potassium.** Prefer premade bags to cut mixing errors and infection. [S1]
- **Consider parenteral nutrition** if enteral feeding will not be established by day 5, or with HIE, NEC or congenital GI conditions. [S1]
- **Special care nursery:** day 1 **10% glucose**; day 2 onwards **0.22% sodium chloride + 10% glucose ± potassium chloride 10 mmol/500 mL.** [S1]
- **Emergency department, day 2 onwards: 0.9% sodium chloride + 10% glucose.** **Paediatric ward, day 2 onwards: 0.9% sodium chloride + 10% glucose ± potassium chloride 10 mmol/500 mL.** [S1]
- **Alternative, with a senior clinician:** sodium chloride 0.45% + glucose 10% ± potassium chloride 10 mmol/500 mL — **consider the risk of hyponatraemia.** 0.22% and 0.225% sodium chloride are interchangeable. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Glucose 10% can run peripherally; above 12.5% glucose or osmolality ≥1000 mOsmol/L needs a central line** (umbilical catheter or PICC). [S1]

## How much

- **Total fluid intake by day of life (mL/kg/day), preterm 32–36 weeks / term over 36 weeks:** day 1 **60 / 60** · day 2 **80 / 90** · day 3 **100 / 100–120** · day 4+ **120 / 100–120.** [S1]
- **Consider fluid restriction** in meningitis, congenital cardiac lesions, HIE and renal impairment (seek senior advice). **Higher needs** with high insensible losses, phototherapy, or renal concentrating defects. [S1]
- **Replace NG or stoma losses over 20 mL/kg/day mL for mL with sodium chloride 0.9% + potassium chloride 10 mmol/L over 24 hours.** [S1]

## Monitor

- **Oliguria:** under 1 mL/kg/hour; in preterm neonates under 24 hours old, under 0.5 mL/kg/hour. A term neonate may take up to 24 hours to first pass urine. [S1]
- **Weigh at birth or admission and daily while on IV fluids.** Watch for oedema (periorbital, genital, sacral, peripheral) as a sign of overload. [S1]
- **Electrolytes before starting IV fluids (where possible), again at 24 hours, then every 24–48 hours.** [S1]
- **Hypernatraemia usually means more fluid is needed; hyponatraemia may mean less fluid, sodium losses, or dilution** from excess volume or poor urine output. High-volume sodium chloride can cause hyperchloraemic acidosis. [S1]

## Back to enteral feeds

- **Reduce IV fluids as enteral intake rises; the infusion can usually stop once enteral intake exceeds 90 mL/kg/day.** If at risk of hypoglycaemia, check BGL before the second enteral feed. [S1]
- **Well term neonates: halve the IV rate and offer suck feeds on demand or at least 4-hourly**; stop IV after 2 or 3 adequate feeds. [S1]
- **Preterm over 32 weeks and over 1500 g: feeds every 2–3 hours, starting at 30 mL/kg/day** and cutting IV to meet the target TFI; **increase by 30–40 mL/kg/day**, more slowly with feed intolerance or worsening breathing. [S1]
- **Involve the paediatric or neonatal team** if unsure which or how much fluid, with electrolyte abnormalities, significant comorbidity, or **resuscitation over 20 mL/kg.** [S1]
