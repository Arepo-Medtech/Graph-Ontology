# Fever with petechiae or purpura in children

## Key points

- **Most children with petechiae do not have serious bacterial infection or meningococcal disease**, and often no cause is found. ⚠️ **Seriously unwell children with petechiae/purpura need urgent management.** [S1]
- **Serious bacterial infection, including meningococcal disease, can present with a non-blanching rash with or without fever.** Pneumococcal and meningococcal bacteraemia have fallen since routine vaccination. [S1]

## Recognise it

- **Petechiae and purpura do not blanch.** Glass test: press a drinking glass firmly on the rash — if it does not disappear, it is non-blanching. **Petechiae are pinpoint; purpura are larger (over 2 mm).** [S1]
- ⚠️ **All children with fever and petechiae/purpura should be reviewed promptly by a senior clinician.** [S1]
- **Higher-risk history:** under 6 months or incompletely immunised · rapid onset or progression · **prior antibiotics may mask bacterial infection** · immunosuppression or previous invasive bacterial infection. [S1]
- ⚠️ **The child is unwell if:** abnormal vital signs (tachycardia, tachypnoea, desaturation in air) · **cold shock** (narrow pulse pressure, cold extremities, prolonged refill) · **warm shock** (wide pulse pressure, bounding pulses, flushed skin, rapid refill) · altered conscious state (inconsolable crying, lethargy) · limb tenderness or difficulty mobilising. [S1]
- **In every child, look for haematological causes:** hepatomegaly or splenomegaly, lymphadenopathy, joint swelling or erythema. [S1]

## Differential diagnosis

- **Mechanical:** vomiting or coughing (petechiae in the superior vena cava distribution, above the nipples) · local pressure (holding, tight tourniquet) · non-accidental or accidental injury. [S1]
- **Infective:** enterovirus, adenovirus, influenza; *N. meningitidis*, *S. pneumoniae*, *H. influenzae*, group A streptococcus, *S. aureus*. [S1]
- **Haematological and other:** ITP · malignancy including acute leukaemia · aplastic anaemia · DIC · HUS · Henoch-Schönlein purpura · vasculitis · drug-induced thrombocytopenia. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Any newborn with petechiae needs prompt senior review** — consider congenital CMV, toxoplasmosis and neonatal lupus as well. [S1]

## Management

- **If no senior clinician is available, manage by the flowchart** — the safest approach. A blood film must be reviewed to exclude an alternative diagnosis. [S1]
- **Ceftriaxone 100 mg/kg (max 4 g) IV daily.** ⚠️ Avoid where possible in neonates under 41 weeks' gestation, especially if jaundiced or receiving calcium-containing solutions including TPN. [S1]
- **Cefotaxime 50 mg/kg (max 2 g) IV** — 12-hourly in week 1 of life, 6–8-hourly in weeks 2–4, 6-hourly after week 4. [S1]
- **Involve the paediatric team** for any unwell child including suspected meningococcal disease, diagnostic uncertainty or follow-up, and escalation advice. [S1]
- **Discharge when a serious cause is unlikely** on assessment and/or investigations — and **always advise return if the child becomes more unwell.** [S1]
