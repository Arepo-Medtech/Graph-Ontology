# Sepsis in children — assessment and management

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-22 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognition and initial management of paediatric sepsis and septic shock.

> **Australian primary source.** The Royal Children's Hospital Melbourne. ***Clinical Practice Guidelines:
> Sepsis — assessment and management***. **Last Updated March 2020. PIC Endorsed** [S1].

**The paediatric counterpart to the ACSQHC Sepsis Clinical Care Standard already in this set**, and the
destination both the **febrile child** and **febrile seizure** guidelines route to.

## ⚠️ It is the oldest source in the compendium

**Last updated March 2020 — six and a half years old**, and **the newest work in its reference list is from
2018**. Nothing else in these 56 guidelines is older.

**That matters more here than it would elsewhere**, because sepsis is the topic where Australian practice has
moved most since 2020: the **ACSQHC Sepsis Clinical Care Standard** in this compendium postdates it, and this
guideline opens by subordinating itself to documents it does not contain:

> ⚠️ **Some state and territory health departments have [sepsis pathways]; these must be followed** [S1].

**A national-quality paediatric guideline whose first line says a state pathway overrides it.** It names
**NSW, Queensland, Western Australia** and a **National** resource [S1] — none of which were retrieved. **This
guideline therefore cannot be the operative document in those jurisdictions**, and says so itself.

## ⚠️ A sepsis guideline warning against over-diagnosis

> **It is important to diagnose sepsis quickly but also to not over-diagnose. Most children with fever (with
> or without a focus) do not have sepsis** [S1].

**This is the counterweight to every "think sepsis" campaign, stated inside the sepsis guideline.** It is the
same boundary the **febrile child** guideline draws from the other direction — *any febrile child who appears
seriously unwell should be managed as suspected sepsis, irrespective of the degree of fever*, and **most
febrile children are not that child**. The two guidelines fit together precisely.

## The five key points

> 1. **Sepsis is defined as life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to
>    infection**
> 2. **It is important to diagnose sepsis quickly but also to not over-diagnose…**
> 3. ⚠️ **Septic shock is sepsis with evidence of cardiovascular organ dysfunction; hypotension is a late
>    sign**
> 4. **Rapid vascular access, early initiation of empiric antibiotics and carefully titrated fluid
>    resuscitation is vital**
> 5. ⚠️ **Inotropes and vasopressors may be safely administered via peripheral cannula or intraosseous access
>    (IO) in children during initial resuscitation** [S1]

**Key point 5 removes a delay.** Waiting for central access before starting an inotrope is explicitly
unnecessary — peripheral or IO is safe in initial resuscitation. **Key point 3 removes a reassurance**: a
normal blood pressure does not exclude septic shock in a child.

**"Carefully titrated"** in key point 4 is doing deliberate work. The reference list carries **Maitland et al.,
*Mortality after fluid bolus in African children with severe infection*, NEJM 2011** — the FEAST trial, in
which boluses increased mortality. The phrasing is the trial's residue.

## Background

> **Care delivered in the first hour following sepsis identification is crucial** [S1].

**High risk groups** [S1]: **neonates · immunocompromised children · children with central venous access
devices · Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islanders**.

⚠️ **The same unqualified listing appears in the febrile child guideline.** Neither states a basis, a
magnitude, or any accompanying guidance on culturally safe application — unlike the ACSQHC standards elsewhere
in this set, which pair such statements with explicit cultural safety recommendations. **Across two RCH
guidelines now, this is a pattern rather than an isolated omission** (see *Unresolved*).

**Pathogens split by age** [S1]:

| Infants **<2 months** | Older children |
|---|---|
| ***Escherichia coli*** | ***Neisseria meningitidis*** |
| **Group B *Streptococcus*** | ***Streptococcus pneumoniae*** |
| ***Listeria monocytogenes* is uncommon** | ***Staphylococcus aureus* (MSSA or MRSA)** |
| **HSV infection should be considered in differential diagnosis of sepsis** | **Group A *Streptococcus* (GAS)** |

**"*Listeria* is uncommon" and "consider HSV" are both corrections to reflex teaching** — the first tempers a
classic empirical-cover argument, the second adds a virus to a bacterial differential.

## Assessment

**Sepsis or septic shock should be considered in a patient with a suspected or proven bacterial infection
**and** any of the following** [S1]:

- **Altered conscious state (lethargy, irritability, floppiness, weak cry)**
- **Unwell appearance ± non-blanching rash**
- **Features of cardiovascular dysfunction** — **reduced peripheral perfusion, pale, cool or mottled skin,
  prolonged central capillary refill time (CRT >2), tachycardia, decreased urine output (<1 mL/kg/hr) or
  narrow pulse pressure**
- **Tachypnoea ± hypoxia ± grunting (not adequately explained by a respiratory illness)**
- **Unexplained pain**
- **Fever or hypothermia (temperature may be normal in neonates or the immunocompromised)**

**Temperature is the least reliable item in its own list** — fever, hypothermia, or normal.

### ⚠️ Two shocks, and the one that gets missed

| | [S1] |
|---|---|
| **Cold shock** | **narrow pulse pressure, prolonged capillary refill** — **more common in neonates/infants** |
| **Warm shock** | **wide pulse pressure, bounding pulses, flushed skin with rapid capillary refill** — **more common in older children/adolescents and often under-recognised** |

**A child with bounding pulses, warm flushed skin and brisk capillary refill looks well perfused.** The
guideline names that appearance as shock and states plainly that it is under-recognised.

**Toxin mediated sepsis** is **caused by superantigens from toxin-producing strains of *S. aureus* or GAS**,
with **fever, vomiting, diarrhoea, myalgia, conjunctival injection, confusion, collapse and a widespread
erythematous rash** [S1].

### ⚠️ Red flags that are not physiological

> - **High level of parental concern**
> - **Representation within 48 hours**
> - **Clinical deterioration despite treatment**
> - **Recent surgery or burns** [S1]

**Two of the four are about the encounter, not the child.** *High level of parental concern* is a red flag in
its own right; so is *coming back*. This is the third RCH guideline in a row to give the parent's account
formal weight — alongside **febrile child**'s *"pallor (including parent/carer report)"* and *"multiple health
service presentations"*, and **acute seizures**' instruction to obtain a **bystander account, including video
recordings**.

## Management

**Nine key principles** [S1]:

> 1. **Early recognition/seeking senior help** · 2. **Assess airway and breathing and administer oxygen if
> required** · 3. **Rapid vascular access** · 4. **Empiric antibiotic therapy** · 5. **Carefully titrated
> fluid resuscitation** · 6. **Early initiation of inotropes** · 7. **Early involvement of critical care
> services** · 8. **Source control** · 9. **Frequent reassessment**

⚠️ **The approach to management is two flowchart images and was not retrieved.** **Antibiotic choice, fluid
volume per bolus, inotrope selection and doses, and the timing sequence are all in those diagrams.** They are
recorded as `input_unavailable` and **none is reconstructed here** — see *Unresolved*. **This guideline cannot
be used to resuscitate a child.**

**Consult the local paediatric team for any child with suspected sepsis** [S1].

## Escalation, with hard numbers

**Consider transfer to intensive care** where the **child requires escalation of care beyond the local
centre** [S1]:

| Trigger [S1] |
|---|
| **Signs of shock persisting despite 40 mL/kg fluid** |
| **Inotrope requirement** |
| **Persistent venous serum lactate >3 mmol/L** |
| **Neutropenia (<1000/mm³) unrelated to chemotherapy** |
| **Large pleural effusion (near white out of the hemithorax)** |
| **Coagulopathy (INR >1.6, APTT >60, Fib <1)** |

> **Secondary resuscitation measures (second line inotrope/vasopressor, steroids, haemofiltration, ECMO)
> should be discussed with Retrieval Services or PICU** [S1].

**40 mL/kg is the only fluid figure in the retrieved text**, and it appears as an *escalation* threshold
rather than a prescription — the volume at which persisting shock means calling ICU, not the volume to give.
