# Generalised anxiety disorder

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-21 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults with a primary diagnosis of generalised anxiety disorder.

> Every clinical sentence carries a source tag. Claims no retrieved source supports are listed under
> *Unresolved*. **No current free Australian guideline exists for GAD** — the RANZCP guideline covers mood
> disorders, not anxiety. Sources here are international; the Australian layer is PBS listing only.

## Treatment aims

Reduce symptom severity and restore function. Two treatment routes — psychological and pharmacological —
each with its own comparative evidence base, and the choice between them is often made on resourcing rather
than on evidence.

## Psychological treatment

A network meta-analysis compared **eight psychotherapies** against one another and against two control
conditions, with certainty rated by CINeMA, examining both short- and long-term effectiveness and
acceptability [S1].

The question it was designed to answer — which psychotherapy should be first line — is one the authors state
needed clarifying [S1]. That framing matters: "psychotherapy" is not one intervention, and recommending it
generically is not the same as recommending a specific one.

## Drug treatment

The comparative base is a network meta-analysis of **89 trials, 25,441 patients, 22 active drugs plus
placebo**, with efficacy measured as change in Hamilton Anxiety Scale score and acceptability as
discontinuation for any cause [S2].

Two agents with quoted effect estimates against placebo [S2]:

| Agent | Mean difference in HAM-A change | 95% credible interval |
|---|---|---|
| Duloxetine | −3.13 | −4.13 to −2.13 |
| Pregabalin | −2.79 | −3.69 to −1.91 |

The authors note that pharmacological treatment is often the clinician's first choice **because of the cost
and resource constraints of psychological alternatives**, and that comparative information across the many
available drugs has been scarce [S2]. Both halves of that sentence should be read: the drug-first default is
described as a resourcing consequence, not a clinical finding.

## Australian context

PBS Schedule 4333, checked directly [S3]:

| Agent | PBS | Benefit type |
|---|---|---|
| Sertraline, escitalopram, paroxetine | listed | restricted |
| **Duloxetine** | listed | restricted |
| Venlafaxine | listed | restricted |
| **Pregabalin** | listed | authority required (streamlined) |
| Diazepam | listed | **unrestricted** (also A) |
| Quetiapine | listed | authority required (streamlined) |
| **Buspirone** | **not on Schedule 4333** | — |

Both agents with quoted effect estimates are constrained — duloxetine restricted, pregabalin
authority-required — while **diazepam is unrestricted**. The least-restricted pharmacological option in
Australia is a benzodiazepine, which is not among the agents the comparative evidence singles out.
