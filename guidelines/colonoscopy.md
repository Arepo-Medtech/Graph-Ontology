# Colonoscopy

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-22 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults undergoing colonoscopy for screening, diagnosis, treatment or surveillance.

> **Australian primary source.** Australian Commission on Safety and Quality in Health Care (ACSQHC).
> ***Colonoscopy Clinical Care Standard*** [S1]. **Updated September 2025** — released **21 September 2025**,
> replacing the 2018 edition. **Page last updated 30 April 2026.**

**A new domain for this compendium** — endoscopy, cancer screening, and the first standard built around
**credentialing an operator** rather than managing a condition.

## ⚠️ The Standard says eight. It has nine.

> **The Colonoscopy Clinical Care Standard includes: eight quality statements describing safe and appropriate
> care** [S1]

**The document then lists nine**: 1 Initial assessment and referral · 2 Appropriate and timely colonoscopy ·
3 Informed decision making and consent · 4 Bowel preparation · 5 Sedation · 6 Clinicians · 7 Procedure ·
8 Discharge · 9 Reporting and follow-up [S1].

**Transcribed as found, not reconciled** — see *Unresolved*.

## ⚠️ Six indicators for nine statements, and they are the operator's

| Indicator [S1] | |
|---|---|
| **2a** | **Evidence of a locally approved policy that ensures the timely and appropriate provision of colonoscopy** |
| **7a** | **Proportion of patients whose bowel preparation was adequate using a validated assessment** |
| **7b** | **Proportion of patients whose entire colon was examined to the caecum and/or terminal ileum** |
| **7c** | ⚠️ **Proportion of patients whose colonoscopy detected one or more adenoma(s)** |
| **7d** | ⚠️ **Proportion of patients whose colonoscopy detected one or more sessile serrated lesion(s)** |
| **9a** | **Evidence of local arrangements to ensure information about a person's colonoscopy is recorded and shared** |

**Statements 1, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 8 carry no indicator.** Referral, consent, bowel preparation, sedation,
credentialing and discharge are unmeasured.

⚠️ **Indicators 7c and 7d are adenoma and sessile serrated lesion detection rates — a measure of how well the
operator looks**, not of whether a process was followed. A low detection rate does not mean the patients were
healthy; **it means lesions were missed.** Nothing else in this compendium audits a clinician's technical
yield this directly.

**Set that against the other standards here.** Stroke measures time; heavy menstrual bleeding and perineal
tears count how often the intervention or injury happens; **this one measures whether the clinician saw what
was there.**

## ⚠️ Credentialing as a quality statement

**Statement 6** [S1]:

> **A patient's colonoscopy is performed by a credentialed clinician working within their scope of clinical
> practice, who meets the requirements of an accepted certification and recertification process. Sedation or
> anaesthesia, and clinical support are provided by credentialed clinicians working within their scope of
> clinical practice.**

> **If you are a colonoscopist, undergo certification and participate in a recertification process that is
> accepted by your professional association and employer. Supervise trainees at a level appropriate to their
> skill and experience** [S1].

**The model behind it is unusual in Australian clinical governance** [S1]:

> **certification — and periodic recertification — of colonoscopists' performance in accordance with defined
> quality indicators and performance targets established by the Gastroenterological Society of Australia
> (GESA), and overseen by the Conjoint Committee for the Recognition of Training in Gastrointestinal Endoscopy
> (CCRTGE) and the Recertification in Colonoscopy Conjoint Committee (RCCC).**

**Recertification against performance targets, not just time served.** The compendium's only comparable
provision is the stillbirth standard's **indicator 7a**, which audits clinicians who have completed
bereavement care training — but that measures *attendance*, where this measures *performance*.

## Statement 1 — the referral, and open access

**Referrals should include** [S1]: **the indication, including presenting symptoms and the clinical concern** ·
**results and dates of previous investigations, including iFOBT (indicating whether this was through the
National Screening Program), colonoscopies and histopathology** · **all relevant medical and family history,
including of bowel and other cancers and known genetic predispositions** · **current medicines and other
medical conditions** · **previous relevant treatment**.

> **Evaluate the likelihood of the patient benefiting from the procedure, considering their overall health,
> comorbidities, likelihood of benefit from future management, the procedural risk and their willingness to
> proceed** [S1].

⚠️ **Open-access colonoscopy** is **added in the 2025 update** [S1]:

> **An open-access (sometimes called direct-access) colonoscopy service is a service which allows clinicians
> to refer patients for a colonoscopy without a prior consultation with the colonoscopist. Open-access models
> have been developed to improve access to services for suitable patients (such as following a positive
> screening iFOBT ['poo test'])** [S1].

**The safeguard is a conversation that is not with the proceduralist**:

> **provide the opportunity for contact between the patient and a suitably trained clinician (including a
> registered nurse or nurse practitioner) before the day of the procedure to enable the patient to ask
> questions about the procedure, including about potential risks, benefits, and bowel preparation; this may
> be via telehealth when appropriate** [S1].

## ⚠️ Statement 2 — a referral is not an indication

> **People might have a colonoscopy for different reasons and every person's situation is different. Just
> because you are referred to a specialist to consider having a colonoscopy does not mean that it will be the
> right thing for you** [S1].
>
> **You should only be offered a colonoscopy if the benefits outweigh any risks of the procedure for
> you** [S1].

**Written to the patient, and it undercuts the referral they are holding.** The statement also requires that
**if colonoscopy is not appropriate, the receiving clinician advises the patient and their referring clinician
of alternative recommended management** [S1] — the refusal has to be communicated in both directions.

## Statement 3 — consent before the preparation starts

**The consent must be obtained *before starting bowel preparation*** [S1] — not on the day, because by then
the patient has already fasted and purged.

**Inform the patient of** [S1]: the reason · likelihood of benefit · ⚠️ **potential adverse events, including
those related to the bowel preparation or sedation, perforation, bleeding (immediate and delayed), splenic
injury and missed pathology** · what happens during the procedure · **the financial costs** · alternatives
including **the risks of not having the colonoscopy**.

**"Missed pathology" is named as an adverse event of the procedure itself** — consistent with indicators 7c
and 7d measuring detection.

> **Even after you have given your consent, you can ask for more information or change your mind about having
> the colonoscopy at any time before the colonoscopy begins** [S1].

## Statement 4 — bowel preparation

**Split dosing** is the method, **associated with increased adenoma detection rates**: **splitting the
standard dose between the day before and the morning of the colonoscopy**, ⚠️ **ensuring some of the dose is
given on the same day of the procedure (3–6 hours before the planned start)** [S1].

⚠️ **Medicines that complicate preparation** [S1]:

> **Ensure patients on diabetes medicines, anticoagulants, antiplatelets, or other medicines are provided
> with individualised instructions about how to adjust their medicines and manage their condition as they
> undergo bowel preparation. Enquire about the patient's use of medicines to lose weight, due to the
> potential complications in endoscopic procedures.**

**The weight-loss medicine question is new and specific** — the standard's resource list names **SGLT2
inhibitors and periprocedural diabetic ketoacidosis** (TGA and Australian Diabetes Society) and
**GLP-1 receptor agonists and dual GLP-1/GIP co-agonists prior to anaesthesia or sedation** (ANZCA, GESA,
NACOS, ADS) [S1]. **Retained gastric contents and DKA are the two procedural hazards of the current
metabolic-drug era, and this 2025 standard names both.**

And a social provision: ⚠️ **consider overnight admission for patients who are unlikely to manage bowel
preparation independently** [S1].

## Statement 5 — sedation, including none

> **Provide patients with the opportunity to discuss the approach to sedation. This may include the option of
> no sedation where this is the patient's preference and is clinically appropriate** [S1].

**Sedation must be administered by a credentialed practitioner working within their scope of practice** [S1],
in accordance with **ANZCA** guidelines. **If an increased risk is identified, an anaesthetist, or other
trained and credentialed medical practitioner within their scope of practice, should assess the patient and
be present during the colonoscopy** [S1].

> **Whilst anaesthetists are specialists in the sedation–anaesthesia continuum, sedation may be administered
> and managed by clinicians from other clinical disciplines** [S1].

And a consent point that is easy to miss: **ensure that the patient understands that their awareness of the
colonoscopy will depend upon the depth of sedation** [S1].

## Statement 7 — the procedure

> **To maximise adenoma detection, intubate the caecum or terminal ileum and allow adequate time for mucosal
> inspection whenever performing colonoscopy** [S1].

**Assess the adequacy of bowel preparation using a validated tool such as the Boston Bowel Preparation Scale,
Ottawa Bowel Preparation Scale, or the Aronchick Scale** [S1].

**Document**: **the quality of the bowel preparation** · ⚠️ **whether caecal intubation was achieved (with
photodocumentation)** · **withdrawal time** · **clinical findings** · **the details of polyps removed, how they
were removed and whether they were retrieved** [S1]. **All polyps removed are retrieved, where possible, and
sent for histopathology** [S1].

**Photodocumentation is a minimum requirement added in the 2025 update** [S1] — **proof that the caecum was
reached, rather than an assertion that it was.**

**Record adverse events, including perforation, post-polypectomy bleeding and sedation-related
cardio-respiratory compromise** [S1].

### ⚠️ Artificial intelligence, named and bounded

> **Any adjunctive technologies (such as artificial intelligence [AI] polyp detection, foot pump irrigation
> and narrow band imaging) should be used in an assistive capacity and according to local policy. Clinicians
> remain accountable for delivering safe and high-quality care regardless of the technology used, and for
> ensuring their practice meets their professional obligations** [S1].

**The only clinical AI provision in this compendium.** It does three things at once: **permits** the
technology, **confines it to an assistive capacity**, and **leaves accountability with the clinician
regardless**. The standard's resource list cites AMA, AHPRA, RANZCR and the Australian Alliance for AI in
Healthcare [S1].

## ⚠️ Statement 8 — the responsible adult problem

**The patient is safely discharged into the care of a responsible adult, in accordance with ANZCA
guidelines** [S1]. And then the standard confronts what happens when there isn't one:

> **If, despite all reasonable efforts, a responsible adult is not available, the clinician responsible for
> managing sedation may exercise their judgement in deciding alternative post-sedation supervision and
> transport (excluding driving or public transport)** [S1].
>
> ⚠️ **Alternatives, including admission or scheduling of appointments to allow longer periods in recovery,
> should be explored when scheduling admissions to avoid denying colonoscopy to people due to their social
> circumstances** [S1].

**A standard that names its own rule as a potential barrier and requires services to work around it.** The
escort requirement is a real reason people do not have colonoscopies; this says the answer is to change the
scheduling, not to refuse the patient.

## Statement 9 — reporting, and the register

**The report goes to the patient, their GP and other relevant clinicians**, covering **the reason, findings,
histology results where relevant, and recommendations for follow-up based on national evidence-based
guidelines** [S1].

- ⚠️ **Ensure that both positive and negative histology findings are communicated** [S1]
- **Upload the report and results to the patient's healthcare record and My Health Record** [S1]
- ⚠️ **For National Screening Program participants, report colonoscopy outcomes, results and adverse events to
  the National Cancer Screening Register** [S1]
- ⚠️ **If presenting symptoms have not been explained by the colonoscopy, advise the patient and refer on if
  further investigation or treatment is required** [S1]

**That last line closes the loop a normal colonoscopy can otherwise leave open** — the symptom is still there
and the test was negative.

## Where this connects in the compendium

| Guideline here | Connection |
|---|---|
| **Iron deficiency** | ⚠️ the commonest route to a colonoscopy referral this compendium contains — iron deficiency anaemia as a presentation of bowel cancer |
| **Type 2 diabetes** | ⚠️ **SGLT2 inhibitors and periprocedural DKA**, and **GLP-1 receptor agonists before sedation** — both named in this standard's bowel preparation resources |
| **Atrial fibrillation** | **anticoagulants** require individualised adjustment for preparation and polypectomy |
| **Antimicrobial stewardship** · **Sepsis** | **perforation and post-polypectomy bleeding** as recorded adverse events |
| **Stillbirth** · **Third and fourth degree perineal tears** | the other ACSQHC standards that measure **clinician capability** rather than only process |

## Unresolved

| Point | Kind | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| ⚠️ **"Eight quality statements"** | **observation** | the Standard's own About section says **eight**; **nine are listed and detailed**. Transcribed as found and **not reconciled** |
| **Statements 1, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 8 carry no indicator** | **observation** | referral, consent, bowel preparation, sedation, credentialing and discharge are **unmeasured**; the six indicators concentrate on **procedure quality** |
| **Bowel preparation agents and regimens** | **input_unavailable** | **split dosing and 3–6 hours before the procedure are stated; no product, volume or regimen is named** |
| **Sedation agents and doses** | **input_unavailable** | deferred entirely to **ANZCA PG09 and PG15**; **no agent or dose** |
| **Indications and surveillance intervals** | **input_unavailable** | ⚠️ **the clinical core is deferred to Cancer Council Australia's *Clinical practice guidelines for the prevention, early detection and management of colorectal cancer* and *Clinical practice guidelines for surveillance colonoscopy*, neither retrieved.** This standard governs **how** colonoscopy is done, not **when** |
| **Target values for indicators 7a–7d** | **input_unavailable** | detection rates are measured; ⚠️ **GESA's performance targets are referenced through the certification model and are not stated here.** **No benchmarks are set by the Commission** |
| **Withdrawal time** | **input_unavailable** | required to be documented; **no minimum is specified** |
| **AI polyp detection** | **evidence_unsettled** | permitted **in an assistive capacity**, with accountability retained by the clinician; **no evidence, threshold or evaluation requirement is given** |
| **Children and adolescents under 18** | **out_of_scope** | explicitly excluded; **paediatric colonoscopy is not covered anywhere in this compendium** |
