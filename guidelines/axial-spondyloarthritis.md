# Axial spondyloarthritis (ankylosing spondylitis and non-radiographic axSpA)

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults with suspected or established axial spondyloarthritis: assessment, imaging, exercise, NSAIDs, conventional drugs, corticosteroids and biologics. The source is written for **non-radiographic** axSpA; where it draws on AS or whole-axSpA evidence it says so, and those claims carry over to ankylosing spondylitis. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Recognise and assess

- **A rheumatologist makes the diagnosis.** MRI helps, but it is neither sensitive nor specific enough to diagnose axSpA by itself. [S1]
- **Take a full history and examine:** look for spondyloarthritis features, other causes of back pain, and common co-existing problems such as non-specific back pain and fibromyalgia. [S1]
- **Inflammatory back pain is the key symptom but is non-specific** (sensitivity 74%, specificity 40% in nr-axSpA). Clues: rest does not help, pain at night, and morning stiffness that eases with activity. [S1]
- **Consider fibromyalgia**, which can mimic, accompany or follow axSpA; about 23% of men and 21% of women with nr-axSpA have it. [S1]
- **Test CRP and HLA-B27 in everyone being assessed.** A normal CRP can become raised later: 50% of placebo-arm patients with a normal baseline CRP had a high one within 16 weeks, so repeat it. [S1]
- **HLA-B27 together with a positive ASAS MRI makes nr-axSpA very likely** (specificity 99%); with neither, only 2% turn out to have axSpA. [S1]

## Imaging

- **Start with an AP pelvic X-ray**, before any sacroiliac MRI; dedicated sacroiliac views seldom add anything. Read it in clinical context, and remember a normal film does not rule out nr-axSpA. [S1]
- **Do not use CT to investigate suspected nr-axSpA.** [S1]
- **If suspicion remains, order sacroiliac MRI with T1 and STIR sequences**, no contrast needed. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Sacroiliac bone marrow oedema is not diagnostic on its own.** It appears in 23% of healthy controls and 40–64% of women after childbirth, as well as in athletes. [S1]
- **Repeating a negative MRI rarely helps if HLA-B27 is negative:** fewer than 5% convert to positive. [S1]
- **Classification criteria (ASAS) are not a diagnostic checklist** for individual patients, though their components inform the overall judgement. [S1]

## First-line management

- **Share decisions and educate.** The patient's goals and preferences drive care, and education is a core part of management. [S1]
- **Build in lifelong regular exercise;** physiotherapy may also help. [S1]
- **An NSAID is the first drug to use**, at the maximum tolerated dose in the studies; it can also help patients take part in exercise. [S1]
- **Conventional DMARDs do not treat axial disease.** Sulfasalazine is an option for peripheral arthritis. [S1]
- **Corticosteroids:** sacroiliac injection has a limited, short-lived role; oral or other systemic steroids have no clear place. [S1]

## Biologic therapy

- ⚠️ **Consider a biologic only when the diagnosis is secure** and disease stays active despite exercise and an NSAID. [S1]
- **TNF inhibitors and IL-17 inhibitors both work for axial symptoms** (ASAS40 32 to 57% with TNF inhibitors, 35–42% with IL-17 inhibitors); they have not been compared head to head. [S1]
- **Let extra-articular disease guide the class.** TNF inhibitors suit uveitis (certolizumab cut flares by 87%; etanercept may be worse), IBD and psoriasis. IL-17 inhibitors suit psoriasis but do not help IBD and lack uveitis evidence. [S1]
- **Ustekinumab does not work** in nr-axSpA or AS. [S1]
- **High ASDAS-CRP predicts response to a TNF inhibitor** better than BASDAI; MRI sacroiliitis alone does not clearly predict it. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Avoid stopping a TNF inhibitor in stable patients:** 70–87% flared within 48–68 weeks of stopping, whereas doubling the interval raised flares only from 16.3 to 21%. [S1]
- **Function, work and quality of life suffer as much as in AS** and improve in responders; fibromyalgia, depression and fatigue make them worse. [S1]
