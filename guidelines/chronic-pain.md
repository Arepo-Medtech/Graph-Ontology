# Chronic pain

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-22 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** **chronic non-cancer pain, neuropathic pain, and cancer pain** in adults, plus **review of a
patient already on long-term opioids**. ⚠️ **ACUTE PAIN AND LOW BACK PAIN HAVE THEIR OWN GUIDELINES** in
this compendium — `opioid-analgesic-stewardship-acute-pain`, `low-back-pain`.

> **Mixed and complementary.** **P1** — **AMH**'s Chronic pain topic, paraphrased (27 claims): the drug
> choices. **S1** — the **open** RACGP *AJGP* article *Reviewing long-term opioid use in patients
> experiencing chronic pain* (**July 2025**), quoted verbatim (13 claims): **the review of a patient
> already on opioids, which is the commoner general-practice problem and which AMH does not cover.**

**AMH topic closed by this guideline:** *Chronic pain*.

## Definitions and aims [P1]

> **Chronic pain is pain persisting for MORE THAN 3 MONTHS, or persisting after healing is expected to be
> complete** [P1].

| | The main aim [P1] |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ **Non-cancer pain** | **MAINTAIN OR RESTORE FUNCTION** |
| ⚠️ **Cancer pain** | **SYMPTOM RELIEF — even if function is decreased** |

**Both aim to improve quality of life.** ⚠️ **The aims are different, and so the measure of success is
different.**

### Before any analgesic [P1]

> **Establish the patient's understanding of their pain. Be CLEAR AND REALISTIC about expectations:**
> ⚠️ **"explain PAIN MAY NOT BE ELIMINATED, however, IMPROVED FUNCTIONING CAN OCCUR EVEN WITH PAIN
> PRESENT"** [P1].
>
> **Discuss the benefits of a MULTIMODAL AND MULTIDISCIPLINARY approach — usually MORE EFFECTIVE than a
> single strategy. Encourage active involvement** [P1].

## Chronic NON-CANCER pain [P1]

> **NON-DRUG TREATMENT — exercise therapy, mindfulness strategies — IS PREFERRED.** **Use drugs ONLY as
> part of an integrated, multidisciplinary strategy to improve physical and social function** [P1].

### ⚠️ Opioids: what AMH actually says

> **RESERVE OPIOIDS FOR PAIN UNRESPONSIVE TO OPTIMISED NON-DRUG AND NON-OPIOID DRUG TREATMENTS** [P1].
>
> ⚠️ **"There is LIMITED evidence that opioids improve quality of life in the SHORT term. However, in the
> LONG term, the evidence is WEAK and RISK OF HARM IS CONSIDERABLE"** [P1].

**Consider involving a SPECIALIST PAIN TEAM, particularly for patients already taking opioids** [P1]:
- ⚠️ **in high doses — e.g. >50 mg morphine equivalent daily**
- **long term — e.g. years**
- **during pregnancy**

### AMH's example opioid trial [P1]

| Step | |
|---|---|
| **1** | **continue non-drug and non-opioid treatment** |
| **2** | ⚠️ **AGREE ON CRITERIA for success or failure — function, quality of life — BEFORE starting** |
| **3** | **trial over 4–8 WEEKS; review efficacy EVERY 1–2 WEEKS** |
| **4** | **establish the LOWEST EFFECTIVE DOSE; ensure the patient understands improvement may not occur, or initial effects may not last** |
| **5** | **if no benefit: TAPER before stopping — risk of withdrawal symptoms** |
| **6** | **if continuing beyond the trial: REVIEW WEEKLY. Taper and stop if no improvement with escalating doses (>50 mg morphine equivalent daily) or unacceptable adverse effects** |
| ⚠️ **7** | ⚠️ **"treatment should not last >90 days, followed by planned tapering"** |

## ⚠️ Reviewing a patient already on long-term opioids [S1]

**This is the situation a GP meets far more often than a fresh opioid decision, and AMH does not address
it.**

> ⚠️ **AUSTRALIA PRESCRIBES MORE OPIOIDS, PER CAPITA, THAN THE UNITED STATES** [S1].
>
> **"Opioids are essential medicines, and although they play an important role in the management of severe
> pain, IT HAS BECOME CLEAR THAT THEIR ROLE IN CHRONIC PAIN IS LIMITED AND CAN CAUSE HARMS"** [S1].

**Australian policy responses: CODEINE RESCHEDULING · national REAL-TIME PRESCRIPTION MONITORING (RTPM) ·
PBS changes reducing pack sizes and restricting indications** [S1].

### ⚠️ The warning that runs against the obvious response

> ⚠️ **"Although it might be tempting to simply reduce or cease opioid prescribing, QUALITY USE OF OPIOID
> MEDICINES IS MORE THAN THIS. HARMS HAVE BEEN ASSOCIATED WITH OPIOID TAPERING, PARTICULARLY RAPID DOSE
> REDUCTION OR OPIOID CESSATION WITHOUT PATIENT CONSENT"** [S1].
>
> **"Collaborative patient care, shared decision making and careful assessment of patient outcomes are
> important to optimise chronic pain management and ELUCIDATE THE ROLE OF OPIOIDS, IF ANY, FOR EACH
> PATIENT"** [S1].

> ⚠️ **TWO SOURCES, DIFFERENT EMPHASIS — RECORDED, NOT MERGED.**
> **AMH [P1] sets a ceiling: treatment should not last more than 90 days, followed by planned tapering.**
> **S1 [S1] warns that tapering itself carries harms, particularly when rapid or without consent.**
> **They are not contradictory — AMH is describing a planned trial, S1 a patient already years in — but
> a clinician reading only one of them would act differently from one reading both.**

### The review itself [S1]

> ⚠️ **THE PBS REQUIRES AN OPIOID REVIEW from a GP colleague, pain, addiction or other appropriate
> specialist EVERY 12 MONTHS** [S1]. **Regular review is recommended and now required with long-term
> opioid prescribing.**

**The "4As" framework, first described 20 years ago:** **ANALGESIA · ACTIVITIES of daily living · ADVERSE
EFFECTS · ABERRANT drug-related behaviours** — **expanded with AFFECT (mood disorder) as a fifth domain**
[S1].

⚠️ **More recently attention has focused on RISKY AND DEPENDENT ALCOHOL USE — A COMMON CO-RISK FACTOR FOR
OVERDOSE among people living with chronic pain in Australia** [S1].

**The ROOM tool (Routine Opioid Outcome Monitoring) is a 12-ITEM patient-reported measure, VALIDATED AND
TESTED in metropolitan, regional and rural Australian general practice among chronic pain patients
prescribed opioids.** It can **indicate unmanaged pain, depression, constipation, risky alcohol use and
opioid dependence or use disorder** [S1].

### ⚠️ Take-home naloxone [S1]

> **NALOXONE is a short-acting opioid antagonist for opioid overdose and is AVAILABLE AT NO CHARGE IN
> COMMUNITY PHARMACIES IN AUSTRALIA.**
> ⚠️ **"It is recommended that naloxone is considered for EVERYONE TAKING LONG-TERM OPIOIDS"**, with
> information on recognising opioid toxicity and how to administer it [S1].
>
> **"NALOXONE IS ANALOGOUS TO A FIRE EXTINGUISHER OR EPI-PEN — SOMETHING TO HAVE IN THE HOME — BUT
> HOPEFULLY NEVER NEEDED"** [S1].

### Deprescribing [S1]

> ⚠️ **"THE MOST COMMON OUTCOME OF DEPRESCRIBING IS LESS PAIN AND BETTER FUNCTION"** [S1].
>
> **The decision to trial reducing opioids can be a LONG-TERM PROCESS and is BEST DONE IN COLLABORATION
> WITH PATIENTS** [S1].

**Withdrawal to recognise: yawning, tearing, sweating, pupillary dilatation, goose pimples, nausea,
vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, DEEP MUSCLE ACHES, INCREASED PAIN, anxiety, irritability** [S1].
⚠️ **"Increased pain" is on that list — withdrawal can be mistaken for the pain returning.**

## Neuropathic pain [P1]

**Consider non-drug treatment; use drugs as part of an integrated multidisciplinary strategy.** **Agree on
criteria for success or failure, ensure the patient understands improvement may not occur, START LOW and
titrate to the lowest effective dose, and ASSESS BENEFIT AFTER 4–8 WEEKS — cease or switch if no benefit or
unacceptable adverse effects** [P1].

> **TCAs (e.g. amitriptyline), ANTISEIZURE DRUGS (e.g. gabapentin, pregabalin) and SNRIs (e.g. duloxetine)
> are the DRUG TREATMENTS OF CHOICE.**
> ⚠️ **"However, efficacy studies are SHORT-TERM (UP TO 16 WEEKS) and provide NO EVIDENCE OF CLINICAL
> SUPERIORITY FOR AN INDIVIDUAL DRUG; comparative studies are limited and long-term data are lacking"**
> [P1].

| Agent | AMH's position [P1] |
|---|---|
| **TCAs, particularly AMITRIPTYLINE** | **the most evidence of efficacy; all appear similarly effective** |
| **DULOXETINE** | **evidence limited to painful diabetic neuropathy — but CAN BE CONSIDERED FIRST LINE REGARDLESS OF UNDERLYING DISORDER** |
| **Venlafaxine, SSRIs** | **evidence conflicting and limited** |
| ⚠️ **PREGABALIN, GABAPENTIN** | **similar efficacy — but MOST PEOPLE WILL NOT ACHIEVE EFFECTIVE PAIN RELIEF, ADVERSE EFFECTS ARE FREQUENT, and there is a RISK OF MISUSE, DEPENDENCE AND WITHDRAWAL** |
| **Valproate, oxcarbazepine, lamotrigine, topiramate** | **limited and conflicting data** |
| **CARBAMAZEPINE** | ⚠️ **drug of choice for TRIGEMINAL NEURALGIA** |
| **Opioids** | ⚠️ **LAST OPTION, SHORT TERM ONLY** — may be effective, particularly in peripheral pain syndromes |
| **Topical lidocaine or capsaicin** | **may benefit LOCALISED neuropathic pain** |

> **PATIENTS WHO FAIL TO RESPOND TO ONE DRUG MAY RESPOND TO ANOTHER — EVEN FROM WITHIN THE SAME CLASS**
> [P1]. **Specialists may combine classes, but EVIDENCE FOR COMBINATION EFFICACY IS LACKING.**
>
> ⚠️ **"ADDITIVE TOXICITY (INCLUDING DEATH) CAN OCCUR WITH CERTAIN DRUG COMBINATIONS, EG OPIOIDS AND
> PREGABALIN; use lowest effective doses and monitor closely"** [P1].
> **Both of those are drugs AMH names as treatments of choice here. The combination is foreseeable.**

## Cancer pain [P1]

> **Controllable with the WHO analgesic ladder in APPROXIMATELY 80% of patients.** For the rest, adjuvants —
> **antidepressants, antiseizure drugs, local anaesthetics, clonidine, ketamine** — and/or non-drug
> treatment are required [P1].
>
> ⚠️ **"NO PAIN IS PREDICTABLY UNRESPONSIVE TO OPIOIDS; ALL PATIENTS WITH MODERATE-TO-SEVERE CANCER PAIN
> SHOULD TRY OPIOID ANALGESIA"** [P1].

**Analgesia and adverse effects may sometimes be improved by CHANGING the opioid — specialist advice
recommended.** **Once control is achieved with conventional release products, consider conversion to
CONTROLLED RELEASE** [P1].

| Situation | Treatment [P1] |
|---|---|
| **Metastatic bone pain** | **RADIOTHERAPY with analgesics, particularly NSAIDs** · **BISPHOSPHONATES for refractory bone pain** · **corticosteroids occasionally for intractable pain** · **radioactive STRONTIUM-89 for widespread bone metastases from prostatic or breast cancer** |
| **Inflammation and oedema in confined spaces** (raised ICP, hepatic capsular pain, nerve compression or infiltration) | **a trial of DEXAMETHASONE** — ⚠️ **high doses may be required** |

### ⚠️ Breakthrough versus incident pain [P1]

> **BREAKTHROUGH pain occurs, spontaneously or on a trigger, BETWEEN doses of regular analgesia.** Managed
> primarily with **short-acting oral or buccal/sublingual opioids.** **Onset APPROXIMATELY 30 MINUTES for
> oral morphine liquid, WITHIN 15 MINUTES for a fentanyl lozenge or tablet** (more rapid onset *and*
> offset) [P1].
>
> ⚠️ **"It is important to distinguish between breakthrough pain and END-OF-DOSE FAILURE"** [P1] — the
> second is a regular-regimen problem, not a rescue-dose problem.

**INCIDENT pain occurs on, or is exacerbated by, MOVEMENT**, and is difficult because the analgesia needed
for control causes unacceptable adverse effects at rest. **When predictable (bathing, dressing)** [P1]:
- **a short-acting opioid HALF AN HOUR BEFORE the activity**
- **an NSAID HALF AN HOUR BEFORE**
- **nitrous oxide and oxygen just before or during movement**
