# Lymphogranuloma venereum (LGV)

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-21 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults, primary care.

> **Australian primary source.** ASHM *Australian STI Management Guidelines for use in primary care* —
> *Lymphogranuloma venereum* [S1], modified 30 June 2026.

## The test will not happen unless you ask for it in words

LGV is *Chlamydia trachomatis* **serovars L1–3**; ordinary genital chlamydia is serovars **D–K** [S1]. A
routine chlamydia NAAT does not distinguish them. Typing is a **second test, on the same sample, at a
reference laboratory** — and it is only performed if someone asks.

> Write on the request form: ***Proctitis: NAAT. If chlamydia positive please send for LGV testing*** [S1].

> **Ensure the laboratory has sent positive chlamydia samples from men who have sex with men with proctitis
> for LGV typing to the local reference laboratory** [S1].

Twenty-two guidelines in, this is the first where the recommended action is **a sentence written on a
pathology form**, and the first that tells the clinician to **follow up with the laboratory** to confirm the
referral actually happened. The diagnosis is lost by default, not by error.

## Testing

| Site/specimen | Test | Note [S1] |
|---|---|---|
| **Rectal swab** | **Chlamydia NAAT** — initial test, in patients with proctitis symptoms | Clinician- or self-collected |
| **Same rectal sample** | **LGV-specific NAAT** — subsequent test on a positive rectal chlamydia result in **symptomatic men who have sex with men** | Sent to the local reference laboratory |
| **Swab from ulcers** | **Chlamydia NAAT** — to investigate an ulcer | Clinician-collected **viral transport swab rolled directly over the lesion**. **Not a routine test for genital ulceration** — only where clinical suspicion of LGV is high |

**Routine screening of asymptomatic patients is not recommended**, because LGV in Australia is usually
symptomatic [S1]. But the rule is conditional, not blanket:

> **LGV testing is recommended for men who have sex with men living with HIV with a positive chlamydia
> rectal swab — even if they are asymptomatic — and all men who have sex with men with chlamydial
> proctitis** [S1].

**If proctoscopy is performed**, a **red, ulcerated, oedematous mucosa** is typical, possibly with
mucopurulent discharge; **a Gram stain showing more than 20 white cells on high-powered film is suggestive
of LGV** [S1].

**LGV is a very uncommon vaginal infection in Australia. If suspected, referral to a local Sexual Health or
Infectious Diseases clinic is advised** [S1].

## Treatment

| Situation | Recommended [S1] | Alternative [S1] |
|---|---|---|
| **Suspected or confirmed LGV** | **Doxycycline 100 mg orally twice a day for 21 days** | **Alternative regimens are not recommended due to lack of efficacy data.** If an alternative is required, **seek specialist advice** |

The second topic in the set with no usable alternative — but for a different reason than trichomoniasis.
There, no other drug class works; here, **no one has the data**. The source says so rather than listing a
plausible substitute.

**Why 21 days, stated explicitly:**

> **Studies have shown that LGV DNA can persist in the rectum for up to 16 days after initiation of
> treatment, hence a long course (21 days) is required** [S1].

**Treat empirically while you wait:**

> At initial consultation for the patient with proctitis with a suspicion of LGV, **treat also for
> gonorrhoea and HSV. LGV serovar results may take some time to return from the laboratory** [S1].

**Advise no sexual contact for 21 days while taking treatment**, and **no sex with partners from the last
3 months** until those partners have been tested and treated if necessary [S1].

**Primary care professionals do not have to notify the state or territory health departments about
LGV** [S1].

## Special situations

| Situation | Recommended [S1] |
|---|---|
| **Inguinal buboes** | **May require drainage through normal skin under ultrasound guidance.** Seek specialist advice |
| **Persistence of symptoms despite initial treatment** | **Check other STI tests were done at the initial consult.** Seek specialist advice |
| **Pregnancy** | Seek specialist advice |
| **Allergy to the principal treatment** | Seek specialist advice |

The bubo instruction is precise about a detail that matters: drainage **through normal skin**, not through
the inflamed surface.

## Co-infection is the expectation, not the exception

> LGV in men who have sex with men is **associated with a high rate of co-infection with gonorrhoea,
> syphilis, hepatitis C and HIV** [S1].

**HSV can also cause proctitis, so HSV NAAT should be taken at the time of consultation** [S1]. Tests for
these conditions should be done **at initial consultation and at follow-up** [S1]. **In addition to syphilis
serology, syphilis NAAT can be performed from any area of ulceration** [S1].

## Contact tracing — rarity raises the priority

> **LGV is rare in Australia, therefore contact tracing is of high priority and should be performed in all
> patients with confirmed infection** [S1].

That inverts the usual reasoning. For trichomoniasis, rarity in a setting is a reason not to screen; here,
rarity is precisely why every case matters — a case implies a transmission network that is otherwise
invisible.

- **All partners traced back for a minimum of 3 months** before the development of primary symptoms, **or
  since arrival from an LGV endemic area** if infection likely occurred overseas [S1].
- **If asymptomatic, trace sex partners from the last 6 months** [S1].

## Follow-up

**Review in 1 week** to review initial results, confirm adherence and symptom resolution, confirm or support
contact tracing, and educate about condoms, contraception, HIV PrEP/PEP, safe injecting, consent, cervical
screening and HAV/HBV/HPV vaccination [S1].

**Test of cure by chlamydia NAAT at 6 weeks** — three weeks after treatment completion [S1].

> If test of cure is positive, **seek specialist advice. This sample should be sent for LGV testing if
> positive, to confirm LGV persistence. If negative, there is no need to send for LGV** [S1].

**Full STI testing including syphilis, HIV (if negative initially) and hepatitis C at 3 months**, then as
required [S1].

## Auditable outcomes

- **100% of patients diagnosed with LGV have contact tracing completed** (patient or provider) [S1].
- **100% of patients are recommended to repeat HIV and hepatitis C testing at 3 months** [S1].

## Clinical stages

| Stage | Features [S1] |
|---|---|
| **Primary** | Small ulcer or nodule on penis, vulva or anus — **may go unnoticed**; proctitis |
| **Secondary** | **Inguino-femoral lymph node swelling and/or discharge (bubo)**, ± erythema |
| **Tertiary** | Chronic proctitis, fistulae, strictures, genital oedema, **scarring of the vulva (esthiomene)** |

**Long-term tertiary sequelae are rarely seen in Australia, but may occur with chronic untreated
infection** [S1].

**The site of the primary lesion depends on the site of inoculation** — genitals, perianal area, or rarely
the mouth [S1]. **Proctitis is characterised by rectal pain, bleeding, rectal discharge, tenesmus and
changed bowel habit** [S1].
