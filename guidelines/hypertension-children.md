# Hypertension in children and adolescents

## Key points

- ⚠️ **Severe hypertension needs urgent consultation; hypertension with encephalopathy is a medical emergency.** All childhood hypertension needs monitoring and follow-up. [S1]
- **Measure BP annually in healthy children.** Confirm an abnormal machine reading, preferably manually, with a correctly sized cuff. [S1]
- **Scope: ages 1–17 years, not infants.** Childhood hypertension predicts adult hypertension, cardiovascular disease and end-organ damage. [S1]
- **Over 6 years: mostly primary**, linked to obesity or family history. **Under 6 years: secondary is more common**, mostly renal, with greater risk of hypertensive emergency. [S1]

## Assessment

- **Risk factors:** overweight/obesity · male sex · family history · low birth weight/IUGR · prematurity · excess salt · inactivity · chronic disease such as CKD or diabetes. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Red-flag history:** headache/vomiting · blurred vision · altered mental state · seizures · chest pain/palpitations · breathlessness · cardiac failure · past AKI. [S1]
- **Examine:** confirm the BP · tachycardia and **four-limb BP** for upper/lower discrepancy · height and weight · end-organ damage (retinopathy, apical heave, hepatomegaly, oedema, palpable kidneys, facial nerve palsy). [S1]

## Measuring BP

- ⚠️ **Use the correct cuff, erring larger** — a small cuff creates artificial hypertension. **Cuff width = 40% of the upper-arm length** (shoulder tip to elbow). [S1]
- **Check any abnormal oscillometric reading with a manual BP.** [S1]

## Classification

- **Normal:** under the 90th centile (1–13 years); under 120/80 mmHg (13–17 years). **Elevated:** 90th to under 95th centile, or 120/80 to under the 95th centile, whichever is lower (1–13 years); 120/<80 to 129/<80 mmHg (13–17 years). [S1]
- **Stage 1:** 95th centile to under 95th + 12 mmHg, or 130/80–139/89, whichever is lower (1–13 years); 130/80–139/89 mmHg (13–17 years). [S1]
- **Stage 2:** 95th centile + 12 mmHg or more, or 140/90 or more, whichever is lower (1–13 years); 140/90 mmHg or more (13–17 years). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Hypertensive urgency:** over the 95th centile + 30 mmHg (1–13 years) or over 180/120 (13–17 years) **without** end-organ symptoms or signs. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Hypertensive emergency:** the same thresholds **with encephalopathy** — headache, vomiting, vision change, facial nerve palsy, lethargy, seizures, coma — ± end-organ damage. [S1]

## Emergency management

- ⚠️ **Severe hypertension: discuss with the renal team and retrieval/ICU team.** [S1]
- **Urgency, if medically stable:** a short-acting oral agent while investigating — **nifedipine 0.25–0.5 mg/kg/day (max 20 mg), titrated to a maximum of 3 mg/kg/day (max 120 mg).** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Emergency: IV therapy with renal and retrieval/ICU input. Lower BP gradually — by 25% of the original value every 24 hours** — towards the estimated 95th centile; slow down if symptoms develop. [S1]

## Without severe features: investigate

- **First-line:** UEC, CMP, urinalysis ± renal ultrasound. **Consider** LFT, HbA1c and fasting lipids, especially if BMI is over the 95th centile. [S1]
- **Further tests only with a general or renal paediatrician**, if under 6 years, secondary cause suspected, or first-line tests abnormal: FBE, bicarbonate, renin/aldosterone, TFT, metanephrines, cortisol, fasting glucose; urine microscopy, protein/creatinine ratio, catecholamines, drug screen; renal Doppler, DMSA, CTA/MRA; echo, sleep study. [S1]

## Without severe features: treat

- **Lifestyle:** diet rich in fruit, vegetables, legumes, fish, poultry, lean meat and low-fat dairy; limit high-sodium, fatty and sugary foods; **40 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous exercise 3–5 days a week**; consider behavioural support for weight. [S1]
- **Start medicines if:** conservative measures fail · symptomatic hypertension · stage 2 with no modifiable risk factor · CKD or diabetes. **Only with a general or renal paediatrician.** [S1]
- **First line: a long-acting calcium channel blocker such as amlodipine.** Other agents may be preferred with BMI over the 95th centile, diabetes or proteinuria. [S1]

## Escalation and discharge

- **Consult the local paediatric team** for red flags or ongoing concern, hypertensive urgency or emergency, or when medical treatment is needed. Children **without severe features can be discharged** with follow-up. [S1]
