## How to read the schedule

- The recommended vaccinations **are considered best practice by the Australian Technical Advisory Group
  on Immunisation, and most are funded by the National Immunisation Program** [M1].
- ⚠️ **The table recommends antigens rather than specific vaccines**, as some vaccines combine different
  antigens [M1].
- **Vaccines that protect against the same disease but are made by different manufacturers are usually
  interchangeable** [M1].

## The schedule [M1]

| Age | Vaccines |
|---|---|
| **Birth** | **Hepatitis B** |
| **2 and 4 months** | **DTP (child), hepatitis B, Hib, pneumococcal 20-valent, polio, rotavirus, meningococcal ACWY conjugate, meningococcal B** |
| **6 months** | **DTP (child), hepatitis B, Hib, polio, annual influenza (injectable)** · pneumococcal 20-valent *(medically or otherwise at risk, incl. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people)* · meningococcal ACWY *(medically at risk)* · meningococcal B *(at risk)* |
| **12 months** | **MMR, meningococcal ACWY conjugate, meningococcal B, pneumococcal 20-valent, annual influenza** |
| **18 months** | **DTP (child), Hib, MMR + varicella, annual influenza** · hepatitis A *(Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people at high risk; refer to state/territory public health unit)* · meningococcal ACWY and B *as first dose if not given before*. ⚠️ **A second varicella dose given to children under 14 enhances protection** |
| **4 years** | **DTP (child), polio, annual influenza (intranasal or injectable)** · hepatitis A, meningococcal ACWY and B *(Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people)* · pneumococcal 20-valent *if needed for those at risk*. ⚠️ **Intranasal influenza is for children aged 2–17 unless contraindicated** |
| **10–15 years** | **DTP (adult) at 11–13 years** and **HPV 9-valent at 12–14 years**, both **via the School Vaccination Program** · meningococcal ACWY and B, pneumococcal 20-valent *(Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people at risk)* · annual influenza |
| **15–49 years** | **Annual influenza** · **DTP (adult) including each pregnancy** · **herpes zoster** *(immunocompromised, over 18)* · **HPV 9-valent** *(unvaccinated, under 26)* · **meningococcal ACWY and B at 15–24** — ⚠️ **all aged 15–19, and those 20–24 who are current smokers or live in close contact, eg university accommodation** · **RSV (pregnancy only)** |
| **50 years and over** | **DTP (adult) if no booster in the previous 10 years** · **herpes zoster** · **pneumococcal 13-, 15- or 20-valent and 23-valent** *(Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people, if not given before)* · **RSV** *(medically or otherwise at-risk people over 60)* · **annual influenza (injectable)** |
| **65 years and over** | **DTP (adult) if no booster in 10 years** · **pneumococcal 13-, 15- or 20-valent** *(unvaccinated people over 70)* · **RSV** *(unvaccinated people over 75)* · **annual influenza (injectable)** |

## Pregnancy

**All pregnant women are recommended to receive** [M1]:

| Vaccine | Timing |
|---|---|
| **Influenza (inactivated)** | **Any stage of each pregnancy**, particularly if the second or third trimester will be in the influenza season |
| **Pertussis (as dTpa)** | **Each pregnancy, preferably between 20 and 32 weeks gestation** |
| **RSV** | **Each pregnancy, between 28 and 36 weeks gestation** |

⚠️ **Immunisation during pregnancy protects the newborn until they can receive their first vaccination —
at 6 weeks for pertussis, at 6 months for influenza** [M1].

> ### ⚠️ Live vaccines and inadvertent exposure
> **Live vaccinations such as MMR should be avoided during pregnancy** due to theoretical risk of disease
> transmission, and **women are advised to avoid pregnancy for 28 days after vaccination.**
> ⚠️ **However, inadvertent exposure just before or during pregnancy has not been shown to cause fetal
> harm and is NOT a reason to terminate the pregnancy — seek specialist advice** [M1].

## Preterm babies, children at higher risk, and catch-up

⚠️ **Provided they are healthy, immunise preterm babies according to usual chronological age without
correcting for prematurity** [M1].

**Children with chronic heart or lung disease, asplenia, immunosuppression or HIV infection, or preterm
babies, are a high priority for immunisation**, and **sometimes a vaccine outside the National Immunisation
Program should be used** [M1].

**Catch-up** [M1]:

- ⚠️ **Offer children with a poor history of vaccination the full course appropriate for their age —
  serious adverse effects are unlikely if extra vaccine doses are given.**
- ⚠️ **The immune response is unaffected by exceeding the recommended intervals — there is no need to give
  extra doses or restart the schedule.**
- **Written evidence of vaccinations is important, as recall can be faulty.**
- **A catch-up calculator is available** for children under 10 on the National Immunisation Program.

## Immunosuppression

**Inactivated vaccines are recommended, but there may be a poor immune response.** ⚠️ **Most live vaccines
are contraindicated in severely immunosuppressed patients due to the risk of infection from the vaccine
virus — infections may be serious, and death has occurred** [M1].

> ### ⚠️ Do not use these live vaccines in immunosuppressed people [M1]
> **Japanese encephalitis (Imojev) · MMR · cholera (Vaxchora) · oral typhoid · BCG · yellow fever ·
> smallpox (ACAM2000) · varicella.**
> ⚠️ **Intranasal influenza vaccine (FluMist) is a live vaccine** and is contraindicated in moderately or
> severely immunosuppressed patients.
>
> **Who counts:** oncology and transplant patients · those taking cytokine modulators · ⚠️ **infants up to
> 6 months of age exposed in utero to cytokine modulators such as TNF-alpha antagonists.**

> ### ⚠️ The corticosteroid threshold — the number a GP is most likely to need
> Live vaccines are contraindicated in those taking systemic corticosteroids equivalent to:
> **prednisolone above 2 mg/kg daily for more than 14 days**, or **above 1 mg/kg daily for more than 1
> month in children**, or **prednisolone above 20 mg daily in adults** [M1].
>
> ⚠️ **Seek specialist advice if taking chronic low-dose corticosteroids, eg 5 mg prednisolone daily, as
> the degree of immunosuppression can vary.**
> ✅ **People using inhaled corticosteroids can be safely vaccinated** [M1].

**HIV infection:** risk from live vaccines and immune response **vary with degree of immunodeficiency (CD4
count) and age**, eg vertically acquired HIV. ⚠️ **Individuals well controlled on antiretroviral therapy
are likely to respond well — seek specialist advice** [M1].

**Oncology:** **inactivated vaccines can be used during chemotherapy and for 6 months afterwards**, with a
possible poor response; **after this, if completely well and no longer immunosuppressed, revaccinate.
Discuss any immunisation with the oncologist** [M1].

⚠️ **Contacts of immunosuppressed people should be vaccinated** against **pertussis, measles, mumps,
rubella, rotavirus where indicated, coronavirus disease, chicken pox and shingles if over 50 — plus annual
influenza** [M1].

## Tetanus-prone wounds

| History of tetanus immunisation | Clean minor wounds | All other wounds |
|---|---|---|
| **3 or more doses, <5 years since last** | **No** | **No** |
| **3 or more doses, 5–10 years since last** | **No** | ⚠️ **Give booster** |
| **3 or more doses, >10 years since last** | ⚠️ **Give booster** | ⚠️ **Give booster** |
| **Uncertain or fewer than 3 doses** | **Finish course** | **Finish course** ⚠️ **+ tetanus immunoglobulin** |

*(All from [M1]. Use appropriate dT, dTpa or DTPa vaccine.)*

> ### ⚠️ The only drug quantity on the page
> **Tetanus immunoglobulin: give 250 units as soon as practicable — 500 units if more than 24 hours has
> elapsed** [M1].
>
> ⚠️ **In humoral immune deficiency, including HIV, give tetanus immunoglobulin** for all wounds other
> than clean minor ones [M1].

## Immigrants, occupations and travellers

- **Immigrants:** ⚠️ **vaccination may be incomplete and documentation may be missing.** **Child** — begin
  catch-up as appropriate. **Adult** — ⚠️ **ensure vaccination against rubella using MMR, especially in
  women of child-bearing age** [M1].
- **Healthcare workers and students** should be vaccinated against infections they may encounter
  **including hepatitis A and TB**, and be up to date with **diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis, polio,
  hepatitis B, influenza, coronavirus, varicella, measles, mumps and rubella** [M1].
- **Laboratory staff** should also be vaccinated against pathogens they work with — **Japanese
  encephalitis, meningococcal infection, typhoid, Australian bat lyssavirus, hepatitis A, Q fever, yellow
  fever, anthrax and vaccinia** [M1].
- **Travellers:** consider particularly **measles, tetanus, hepatitis B and, depending on age, influenza
  and pneumococcus**; **hepatitis A may also be useful**. AMH names **Smartraveller**, the **WHO travel
  advice pages** and the **US CDC Yellow Book** [M1].

## Practice points [M1]

- ⚠️ **Resuscitation equipment, drugs and a protocol for anaphylaxis must be available and checked before
  each vaccination session.**
- ⚠️ **Maintain the vaccine fridge and other cold chain components correctly; check before each working
  day.**
- **Vaccine, dose, route and administration must be in accordance with the Australian Immunisation
  Handbook.**
- ⚠️ **Ensure documentation — inadequate vaccination records will affect catch-up requirements.**
- **Check the vaccination status of other family members and offer catch-up where appropriate.**
- **Report any significant adverse effect.**
