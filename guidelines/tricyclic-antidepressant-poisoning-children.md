# Tricyclic antidepressant poisoning in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** risk assessment, ECG, decontamination, sodium bicarbonate, seizure care and disposition for TCA poisoning in children and adolescents. ---

## Key points

- ⚠️ **TCA poisoning can be fatal in relatively low doses**: cardiac instability (arrhythmias, reduced contractility) and neurological instability (CNS depression, seizures). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Narrow therapeutic window; single-tablet fatalities have been reported; patients can deteriorate quickly.** [S1]
- **Management is mainly supportive**, but **alkalinisation with sodium bicarbonate may be needed.** **There is no antidote.** [S1]
- **TCAs covered:** amitriptyline, imipramine, nortriptyline, doxepin, dothiepin, clomipramine. [S1]
- **Signs usually appear within an hour and most within 6 hours**, but anticholinergic slowing of gut transit can make absorption unpredictable. [S1]

## Who needs assessment

- **Assess:** all deliberate self-poisoning · any symptomatic patient · asymptomatic patients with cardiac or neurological disease. ⚠️ **Refer any child who took over 5 mg/kg to hospital.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Over 10–15 mg/kg: intubate, ventilate and give charcoal.** [S1]
- **Co-ingested serotonin reuptake inhibitors** may raise TCA levels and precipitate serotonin syndrome. [S1]

## Recognise the toxidrome

- **Sodium-channel blockade:** reduced contractility and hypotension, **widened QRS predisposing to VT and VF**, prolonged QT. **Reuptake inhibition:** CNS depression/coma, seizures. [S1]
- **Anticholinergic:** sinus tachycardia, vomiting, blurred vision, ataxia, delirium, urinary retention, ileus. **Antiadrenergic:** vasodilation. [S1]

## Investigations

- ⚠️ **ECG on admission, repeated if abnormal: QRS over 100 ms is associated with seizures; QRS over 160 ms with ventricular arrhythmias.** [S1]
- **TCA levels help only if the diagnosis is in doubt — they don't predict outcome.** Also: paracetamol level, glucose if GCS reduced, blood gas for acidosis. [S1]

## Treatment

- **Consider early intubation if GCS is reduced.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Charcoal is generally contraindicated (aspiration risk)** — except over 10–15 mg/kg, when it is given **after intubation.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Widened QRS or ventricular arrhythmia: sodium bicarbonate 2 mmol/kg bolus**, repeated as needed, with consideration of intubation and hyperventilation **to a pH of 7.5.** [S1]
- **Seizures: benzodiazepines.** ⚠️ **Avoid phenytoin** — it also blocks sodium channels. [S1]
- **Cardiac monitoring and regular ECGs.** ⚠️ **Contact ICU for altered conscious state, GCS under 12, seizures, widened QRS or arrhythmia.** [S1]

## Disposition

- **Asymptomatic: investigate as above, observe 6 hours, and discharge if the ECG stays normal.** [S1]
- **Consider admission for:** all adolescents with intentional overdose · symptomatic patients · overdose with cardiovascular or neurological disease · co-ingestion of cardio-depressant drugs or MAO inhibitors. **Intentional overdose needs mental health review.** [S1]
- **Transfer to a tertiary centre** for significant CNS depression/seizures or any cardiovascular instability. **PIPER: 1300 137 650.** [S1]
- **Discharge criteria:** normal GCS · normal ECG · 6 hours' observation if stable · for deliberate ingestion, a risk assessment showing low risk of further self-harm. [S1]
