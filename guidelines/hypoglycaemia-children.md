# Hypoglycaemia in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** definition, causes, investigation (hypoglycaemia screen), treatment and discharge for hypoglycaemia in children, including neonates. The AMH-derived, mostly adult counterpart is `hypoglycaemia`; DKA-related hypoglycaemia is in `diabetic-ketoacidosis-children`.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 20 of 20 claims quoted verbatim from the **RCH Melbourne Clinical Practice Guideline *Hypoglycaemia*** (Last updated August 2025); **30 fragments re-checked by machine. 3 quoted doses.**

> ⚠️ **Prolonged or severe hypoglycaemia can cause permanent neurological injury or death.** Treat first: diagnostic tests must not delay correction.

---

## Recognise it

- ⚠️ **Without diabetes, hypoglycaemia is a BGL under 3.0 mmol/L if symptomatic, or under 2.6 mmol/L regardless of symptoms.** In neonates under 48 hours it is under 2.6 mmol/L. Thresholds differ in diabetes. [S1]
- **In children who cannot communicate, consider treating a BGL under 3.0 mmol/L.** Neonates are often asymptomatic. [S1]
- **Severe hypoglycaemia** means serious cognitive dysfunction, eg convulsions or coma. [S1]
- **An underlying condition is more likely** in infants under 1 year and with severe, recurrent or unexplained hypoglycaemia. [S1]
- **Accelerated starvation is a diagnosis of exclusion**: a prolonged fast, low BGL with ketones, and normal BGL on feeding. [S1]
- **Symptoms:** palpitations, anxiety, hunger, paraesthesia, lethargy, irritability. **Neonates under 48 hours** may show apnoea, lethargy, seizures, hypotonia, jitteriness, poor feeding, a high-pitched cry, altered behaviour — or nothing. [S1]
- **Ask about toxins** — alcohol, oral hypoglycaemics, aspirin, beta-blockers — and diet (prolonged fasting, dairy, juice, protein). [S1]
- **Look for clues to the cause:** poor growth, syndromic features, hyperpigmentation, midline defects, hepatomegaly and/or hypotonia (inborn error of metabolism). [S1]

## Investigate

- **Take a point-of-care BGL and ketones, and keep the first urine after the event.** Confirm with a blood gas or laboratory glucose — meters can be inaccurate in the hypoglycaemic range. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Consider a hypoglycaemia screen for unexplained, severe or recurrent hypoglycaemia — but do not delay correction** if access is difficult. Infants under 48 hours who respond to feeds rarely need extensive tests. [S1]
- **Screen bloods while hypoglycaemic:** glucose, ketones (beta-hydroxybutyrate), insulin and C-peptide, cortisol, growth hormone, lactate. Send on ice immediately. [S1]
- **After treatment if blood was short:** LFTs, ammonia, free fatty acids, amino acids, acylcarnitine. **First urine:** organic and amino acids, reducing substances, glucose, ketones. [S1]
- **Any detectable insulin with a BGL under 2.6 mmol/L is inappropriate.** Consult metabolic or endocrinology for abnormal or unclear results. [S1]

## Treat

- **Enteral glucose is preferred where conscious level allows**: apple juice, honey (over 12 months) or 10% glucose polymer. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Not oral rehydration solution or icy poles** — they contain minimal glucose. Consider NGT and antiemetics if vomiting. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Severe hypoglycaemia: glucose 10% 2 mL/kg IV bolus.** No or delayed IV access: consider intraosseous glucose or IM glucagon. [S1]
- ⚠️ **IM glucagon may be ineffective after prolonged hypoglycaemia or fasting.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Always follow correction with slow-release carbohydrate (eg a sandwich) or ongoing IV fluids containing 5 or 10% glucose with normal saline.** [S1]

## Disposition

- **Discharge when** the cause is known or accelerated starvation assumed; BGL over 3.0 on two occasions 3 hours apart with the child asymptomatic and eating; parents educated; follow-up arranged if a screen was sent. [S1]
- **Involve the paediatric team** for any child not responding, **all neonates**, and unexplained hypoglycaemia or suspected underlying cause. [S1]
