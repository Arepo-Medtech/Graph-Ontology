## Part 1 — Palpitations in general practice [S1]

**Palpitations account for an estimated 16% of general practice presentations and are the second most
common presentation to cardiologists after chest pain** [S1].

| Setting | What it turned out to be [S1] |
|---|---|
| **University medical centre** | **arrhythmic 41%** · **psychological disorder such as anxiety 31%** · **no cause identified 16%** |
| **Emergency population** | **cardiac cause 34%** |

### ⚠️ The finding that should change a consultation

> ⚠️ **DO NOT LABEL PALPITATIONS AS ANXIETY OR PANIC.** **54% of that group will eventually be diagnosed
> with an arrhythmic cause, and the time delay until arrhythmia diagnosis is 3.3 YEARS** [S1].
>
> **Young patients with rapid palpitations, particularly young women, may be misdiagnosed as having panic
> attacks.** ⚠️ **Anxiety and distress may be a NORMAL RESPONSE to an episode of SVT — the history
> clarifies whether the SVT or the anxiety came first** [S1].

### Reading the story

| Pattern | Likely [S1] |
|---|---|
| **Sudden-onset, regular, sustained, rapid** | ⚠️ **SVT in the vast majority**; **classically induced by sudden movements, particularly BENDING**; **light-headedness at onset but syncope unusual** |
| **Gradual onset and offset over minutes or longer** | **sinus tachycardia** — usually benign, but **less often thyrotoxicosis or anaemia** |
| **Momentary skipped or missed beat** | **benign ectopic beats** |
| **Forceful but not fast; audible at night** | **increased awareness of NORMAL sinus rhythm** |
| **Rapid and irregular** | **atrial fibrillation** |

**Patients with SVT can frequently, but not universally, terminate an event with vagal manoeuvres — the
Valsalva technique or a cold stimulus to the face** [S1].

### ⚠️ Ectopics, and the caffeine advice that is not supported

> **Up to 100 ventricular ectopic beats in a 24-hour Holter recording are within normal limits.** **More
> frequent beats require further investigation — but even THOUSANDS per 24 hours will most usually be
> benign and not signify increased risk of adverse cardiovascular outcomes** [S1].

> ⚠️ **SOURCE DISAGREEMENT, RECORDED NOT RESOLVED.**
> **[S1]:** **"Although doctors frequently recommend patients with ectopic beats to avoid caffeine, there
> is very little evidence that this is beneficial"**, and **a recent study identified NO increased risk of
> any arrhythmia with caffeine intake, even after adjustment for confounders** [S1].
> **[B1]/[B3]:** AMH lists **caffeine or alcohol intake** among the underlying causes to seek and remove
> [B1], and says for premature ventricular ectopics that **reduction or avoidance of caffeine or alcohol
> may help** [B3].
> ⚠️ **Both are stated. The advice to cut caffeine is near-universal in practice and one current
> Australian source says the evidence for it is very little. Alcohol is NOT in dispute** — [S1] itself
> says **alcohol history is important given the association of alcohol consumption with the risk of
> atrial fibrillation**.

### What actually predicts arrhythmia [S1]

> **A systematic review identified only SIX history factors that significantly correlated with
> arrhythmia:** **age over 60 years**, **regular palpitations**, **palpitations affected by sleep**,
> **a regular pounding sensation in the neck** (atrial contraction against a closed tricuspid valve, as in
> AVNRT), **visible neck pulsations**, and **vasovagal symptoms — pallor or diaphoresis** [S1].

> ⚠️ **The ONLY clinical sign that significantly correlated with arrhythmia was an abnormal resting heart
> rate — below 60 or above 100 beats per minute** [S1].

### Investigation [S1]

**The gold standard is a 12-lead ECG recorded AT THE TIME OF SYMPTOMS** — rarely achieved. **All patients
presenting between symptoms should still have a 12-lead ECG, because the sinus rhythm ECG can sometimes
infer the arrhythmic mechanism** [S1]:

- **Pre-excitation / delta wave → WPW, AVRT**
- **Long or short QT, Brugada pattern, early repolarisation pattern → genetic arrhythmia syndromes, risk
  of sudden cardiac death**

⚠️ **Hand-held ECG devices** are limited by patient cost, the time to activate before the arrhythmia
terminates, and **NO Medicare Benefits Schedule rebate for analysis** [S1].
⚠️ **Fitness trackers are prone to error, particularly during exercise, and can erroneously give very high
or very low readings. The key clue to a genuine problem is ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS** [S1].

### ⚠️ When to send them in [S1]

> **Sustained palpitations with pre-syncope, breathlessness or chest pain → early referral.**
> ⚠️ **Syncope or ongoing chest pain → emergency department BY AMBULANCE** [S1].

**Ventricular tachycardia infrequently presents with palpitations as an isolated symptom**: it usually
occurs with **underlying structural heart disease, most commonly prior MI**, and presents with
**haemodynamic compromise** [S1].

⚠️ **Syncope in paroxysmal AF is frequently a significant SINUS PAUSE at spontaneous reversion —
tachycardia-bradycardia syndrome** [S1]. **Polyuria may accompany paroxysmal AF from atrial natriuretic
peptide release, and night-time onset may suggest a vagal mechanism or sleep-disordered breathing** [S1].

# Part 2 — Drug treatment [AMH]

⚠️ **Everything from here is paraphrased from AMH, stored without quotes, and not machine re-checkable.**

## ⚠️ The first rule is: consider not treating

> ⚠️ **ALWAYS CONSIDER THE NO-TREATMENT OPTION.** **Many antiarrhythmics have proarrhythmic activity and
> may WORSEN arrhythmias and cause sudden death. Many arrhythmias are benign** — atrial premature
> contractions, ventricular ectopic beats [B1].

**Before treating:** **make an accurate diagnosis with a 12-lead ECG together with LONG RHYTHM STRIPS FROM
MULTIPLE LEADS** [B1]. **Seek and treat or remove underlying causes: heart disease including MI or
ischaemia, electrolyte abnormalities, hypoxia, pneumonia, thyroid disease, caffeine or alcohol intake, and
proarrhythmic drugs** [B1].

**The haemodynamic consequences of most arrhythmias depend on the resulting heart rate and underlying
cardiac reserve rather than on their origin.** **Dizziness, loss of consciousness, hypotension, shortness
of breath, heart failure or angina indicate POOR TOLERANCE** [B1].

⚠️ **Implantable cardiac defibrillators are SUPERIOR to antiarrhythmic drugs in prolonging survival,
although high cost limits use** [B1].

**If the arrhythmia is secondary to a reversible event — AF after cardiac surgery or with pneumonia,
ventricular arrhythmia after MI — drugs may not be required in the long term** [B1].

## ⚠️ QT interval: the numbers [B1]

| | [B1] |
|---|---|
| **Normal** | **about 90% of adults have a QTc <440 ms; women generally have a longer QTc** |
| ⚠️ **Danger zone** | **risk rises gradually with QT; especially QTc >500 ms** |
| ⚠️ **Change from baseline** | **>20 ms is considered proarrhythmic, particularly >60 ms**; **5–20 ms, risk uncertain** |

**Causes:** **congenital long QT syndrome; hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia, hypocalcaemia; testosterone
suppression; bradycardia; structural heart disease including heart failure and coronary heart disease; and
drugs** [B1].

**Drugs AMH lists at therapeutic doses** [B1]: **amiodarone, disopyramide, sotalol**; **amisulpride,
droperidol, haloperidol, ziprasidone**; **clarithromycin, erythromycin, fluconazole, moxifloxacin, quinine,
voriconazole**; **citalopram, escitalopram, fluoxetine, methadone, domperidone, donepezil,
hydroxychloroquine, solifenacin, TCAs**.

> ⚠️ **AVOID COMBINING QT-prolonging drugs** — the risk of arrhythmia is increased [B1]. ⚠️ **Note that
> three of the antiarrhythmics in this guideline are themselves on that list.**

## Bradyarrhythmias [B2]

**Sinus bradycardia can be normal, or result from acute MI, sick sinus syndrome, drugs (beta-blockers,
digoxin, amiodarone), hypothyroidism, hypothermia or raised intracranial pressure.** ⚠️ **TREAT ONLY IF
SYMPTOMATIC — uncommon at a rate above 40–45 beats/minute** [B2].

**Sick sinus syndrome** covers **persistent sinus bradycardia, sinoatrial block, sinus arrest,
tachycardia-bradycardia syndrome and junctional or ventricular escape rhythms**; usually **idiopathic
fibrosis of the node**, but also associated with **myocardial ischaemia and DIGOXIN TOXICITY** [B2].

**AV block causes: myocardial ischaemia, infection, and drugs — digoxin, diltiazem, verapamil,
beta-blockers** [B2].

> **Atropine (short-acting) or isoprenaline (prolonged infusion) may be used where there is haemodynamic
> compromise.** ⚠️ **PACEMAKER INSERTION IS THE PREFERRED TREATMENT for persistent bradycardia** [B2].

## Tachyarrhythmias [B3]

### Sinus tachycardia
**Usually physiological — pain, hypovolaemia, fever, anxiety — but may be sympathomimetic drugs or
thyrotoxicosis. With no obvious cause, drug treatment is unnecessary unless symptoms are troublesome, when
a beta-blocker is recommended; verapamil or diltiazem are alternatives** [B3].

### SVT — AVNRT
**Usually remits spontaneously or with vagotonic manoeuvres (Valsalva, swallowing ice-cold water, carotid
sinus pressure), more effective if used EARLY** [B3].
⚠️ **AVOID CAROTID SINUS MASSAGE IN THE ELDERLY** — risk of arterial emboli from a stenosed carotid [B3].
**If it persists, use adenosine** [B3].

> **Radiofrequency catheter ablation is the TREATMENT OF CHOICE for prevention** — low risk, usually
> curative, avoids long-term antiarrhythmic exposure [B3].
> **If ablation is not possible: verapamil or a beta-blocker (except sotalol) first; diltiazem,
> flecainide, sotalol or amiodarone as alternatives.** ⚠️ **Do not combine verapamil with a beta-blocker
> unless under specialist supervision** [B3].

### ⚠️ Pre-excitation — the rule that prevents a death

> ⚠️ **DO NOT USE AV NODAL BLOCKERS — adenosine, verapamil, diltiazem, digoxin, or beta-blockers other
> than sotalol — IN WOLFF-PARKINSON-WHITE SYNDROME WITH AF. RISK OF VENTRICULAR FIBRILLATION** [B3].
>
> **For tachycardia with pre-excitation, use DC cardioversion or flecainide; catheter ablation is the
> treatment of choice for prevention** [B3].

⚠️ **Wide complex tachycardia of unknown origin requires specialist supervision, and it is SAFEST TO TREAT
AS VENTRICULAR TACHYCARDIA — avoid verapamil and diltiazem** [B3].

### Atrial tachycardia
**Multifocal atrial tachycardia usually occurs in hypoxia, electrolyte or acid–base disturbance, or with
enlarged atria in chronic pulmonary disease or chronic heart failure.** ⚠️ **ATRIAL TACHYCARDIA WITH AV
BLOCK IS TYPICALLY SEEN WITH DIGOXIN TOXICITY** [B3].

### Ventricular
**Premature ventricular ectopics without structural heart disease are usually benign and reassurance may be
sufficient; a beta-blocker may be considered if symptoms are troublesome** [B3].
**Non-sustained VT terminates spontaneously within 30 seconds and usually needs drugs only with symptoms or
haemodynamic compromise — beta-blockers or amiodarone.** ⚠️ **Management of ventricular tachycardias
requires specialist supervision** [B3].

**Torsades de pointes** is **more common in women and the elderly**, **usually self-limiting**, but **can
recur if the cause is not corrected and can deteriorate into ventricular fibrillation with sudden death**.
**Treatment: stop QT-prolonging drugs, correct electrolytes especially potassium; cardiac pacing, IV
magnesium sulfate and IV isoprenaline may be used** [B3].

## Two sources on AF

**This compendium's `atrial-fibrillation` guideline is built from the 2018 NHFA/CSANZ Australian
guideline. AMH is a separate source read in 2026. On two questions they agree, and the agreement is worth
having:**

| | AMH [B3] | This compendium's AF guideline |
|---|---|---|
| **Stroke score** | **CHA2DS2-VA is recommended in Australia** | **CHA₂DS₂-VA — the sexless score — is recommended** |
| **Rate-control target** | **resting ventricular rate <110 bpm**, lower if still symptomatic | same target |

**AMH's CHA2DS2-VA scoring** [B3]: **2 points each for age >75 years and previous stroke/TIA/systemic
thromboembolism; 1 point each for heart failure, hypertension, diabetes, vascular disease, and age 65–75
years.** **Score ≥2 → anticoagulate. Score 1 → benefit unclear, individualise. Score 0 → no
antithrombotic** [B3].

**Oral anticoagulants reduce stroke risk by about 60% in AF; NOACs are preferred to warfarin and, compared
with warfarin, reduce mortality and significantly lower the risk of intracranial haemorrhage** [B3].
⚠️ **WARFARIN IS THE ONLY oral anticoagulant for valvular AF — moderate-to-severe mitral stenosis or a
mechanical valve** [B3].
⚠️ **ANTIPLATELET TREATMENT IS NOT RECOMMENDED for stroke prevention in AF: aspirin, alone or with
clopidogrel, is much less effective than an oral anticoagulant but carries a SIMILAR risk of major
bleeding** [B3].

### AF rate and rhythm [B3]

- **Rate control: a beta-blocker, diltiazem or verapamil.** ⚠️ **If LVEF <40%, AVOID diltiazem and
  verapamil** — they may exacerbate heart failure [B3].
- **Digoxin may be added, or used alone if first-line agents are unsuitable. It is relatively ineffective
  against a rapid rate driven by sympathetic tone (exercise, hyperthyroidism, fever, hypoxia), but may
  suit older, inactive people** [B3].
- **Cardioversion: amiodarone or flecainide; amiodarone is considered the safest agent in heart failure.**
  ⚠️ **If using flecainide, RATE CONTROL IS ALSO NEEDED** to avoid conversion to atrial flutter and
  ventricular tachyarrhythmia [B3].
- **Maintenance: flecainide with rate control, amiodarone or sotalol** [B3].

### Atrial flutter [B3]
**Catheter ablation is often more effective and lower risk than in AF and may be considered before
antiarrhythmic treatment.** ⚠️ **DO NOT USE FLECAINIDE ALONE — one-to-one AV conduction may occur and
increase the ventricular rate. Add a beta-blocker, digoxin, verapamil or diltiazem** [B3].

## ⚠️ Drug-specific traps

### Amiodarone [B4]
**The least negatively inotropic antiarrhythmic, usually well tolerated in heart failure** — and the one
with the longest tail of harm.

> ⚠️ **ADVERSE EFFECTS ARE SLOW TO RESOLVE AFTER STOPPING** because of the very long half-life, and many
> are dose- and duration-related and **may not appear for weeks, months or even YEARS** [B4].

- **Check QT <500 ms before starting; avoid if QT risk factors cannot be corrected** [B4].
- **Common: nausea and vomiting (especially while loading), metallic taste or loss of taste, raised
  aminotransferases, thyroid dysfunction, photosensitivity, BLUE-GREY SKIN PIGMENTATION, corneal
  microdeposits, tremor, ataxia, paraesthesia, peripheral neuropathy, vivid dreams or nightmares,
  pulmonary toxicity, moderate bradycardia** [B4].
- **Pulmonary toxicity has two forms:** an **acute inflammatory disorder** — **reversible if withdrawn
  early, may respond to corticosteroids** — and a **chronic fibrotic form with prolonged exposure, less
  reversible** [B4].
- **Thyroid: hypothyroidism mainly within the FIRST 2 YEARS; thyrotoxicosis can occur even MONTHS AFTER
  STOPPING** [B4]. ⚠️ **Amiodarone contains iodine and affects thyroxine metabolism — tell the laboratory
  the patient is on it when requesting thyroid function tests** [B4].
- **Corneal microdeposits occur in MOST patients and rarely affect vision.** ⚠️ **STOP amiodarone if optic
  neuropathy or neuritis occurs** [B4].

| Amiodarone monitoring [B4] | |
|---|---|
| **Baseline** | **clinical status, electrolytes, thyroid and liver function, lung function including chest x-ray, ECG** |
| **Every 6 months** | **thyroid function, liver function, electrolytes** |
| **Annually** | **chest x-ray and ECG** |
| ⚠️ **After stopping** | **thyroid and liver risk persists UP TO A YEAR — continue monitoring** |

**Infuse via a large or central vein in glucose 5% — INCOMPATIBLE with sodium chloride 0.9% — with DEHP-free
giving sets and glass or rigid PVC bottles** [B4].

### Flecainide [B5]
⚠️ **CONTRAINDICATED in heart block, cardiogenic shock and a HISTORY OF MI**, and it **increases the risk
of ventricular arrhythmias in structural heart disease, particularly with impaired left ventricular
function** [B5].
⚠️ **In atrial flutter — or in AF by conversion to flutter — flecainide may cause ONE-TO-ONE AV CONDUCTION
and a ventricular arrhythmia. An AV nodal blocker lessens this risk** [B5].
**Dilute IV with glucose 5% only — incompatible with alkaline and chloride-containing solutions** [B5].

### Sotalol [B6]
**A nonselective beta-blocker that also prolongs the refractory period of atria, ventricles and bypass
tract** [B6].
⚠️ **CONTRAINDICATED if the QT interval is prolonged, in ASTHMA, in severe heart failure, in second- or
third-degree heart block or sinus node dysfunction without a pacemaker, in sinus bradycardia without a
pacemaker, and in cardiogenic shock or systolic BP <90 mmHg** [B6].
⚠️ **MONITOR THE QT INTERVAL when starting and with each dose increase — particularly in older people,
women and renal impairment** [B6]. ⚠️ **Sotalol ACCUMULATES IN RENAL IMPAIRMENT — increase the dosing
interval** [B6].
⚠️ **Avoid abrupt withdrawal in ischaemic heart disease: reduce over 8–14 days** to avoid exacerbating
angina and precipitating MI and ventricular arrhythmias [B6].

### Adenosine [B7]
⚠️ **CONTRAINDICATED in reactive airways disease such as asthma** — **bronchoconstriction LASTING UP TO 30
MINUTES** — and in **second- or third-degree heart block or sick sinus syndrome without a pacemaker** [B7].
⚠️ **May rarely ACCELERATE the ventricular rate in AF or atrial flutter, especially with an accessory
pathway** [B7].
**Dipyridamole increases and prolongs adenosine's effect: avoid, or use a quarter to a half of the usual
dose. Heart transplant recipients are more sensitive — reduce the initial dose** [B7].
**Its extremely short duration and half-life make it the TREATMENT OF CHOICE IN PREGNANCY when indicated,
and it is safe in breastfeeding** [B7].

### Digoxin [B8]
⚠️ **CONTRAINDICATED in second- or third-degree heart block without a pacemaker, SVT involving an accessory
pathway (WPW), ventricular tachycardia and fibrillation, hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy, acute or
chronic cor pulmonale, and constrictive pericarditis** [B8].
**Hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia, hypercalcaemia, acidosis and hypoxia increase sensitivity — especially
hypokalaemia — so toxicity may occur at lower concentrations** [B8].
⚠️ **Withhold for 1–2 days before DC cardioversion, or use the lowest effective energy** [B8].
**Digoxin is NOT effective at converting AF to sinus rhythm or preventing recurrence after cardioversion.**
**Routine pulse measurement before the next dose is NOT necessary** [B8].
**Check renal function and electrolytes BEFORE starting; about 70% is renally cleared, so reduce the dose
in renal impairment and in the elderly** [B8].
