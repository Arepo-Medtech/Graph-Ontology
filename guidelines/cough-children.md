# Cough in children

## Key points and definitions

- **The commonest cause is a (usually viral) URTI.** Investigations and treatment are rarely needed for well children with a short history. [S1]
- **Young children have 6–12 respiratory infections a year**; most coughs resolve without treatment in 1–3 weeks. [S1]
- **Definitions:** acute = up to 2 weeks; protracted acute = daily for 2–4 weeks; **chronic = daily for more than 4 weeks**. Chronic cough warrants further assessment. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Persistent daily cough over 4 weeks: assess and investigate with CXR and spirometry (if able).** [S1]

## History and examination

- **First establish duration and whether the cough is wet or dry**, look for red flags, and identify specific causes or recent infection. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Sudden onset without a viral prodrome, or after choking, may mean foreign body inhalation.** Onset in early infancy suggests aspiration, congenital airway abnormality or cystic fibrosis; choking with feeds suggests aspiration. [S1]
- **Cough type:** daily wet morning cough → PBB, suppurative lung disease or bronchiectasis; paroxysmal → pertussis or foreign body; staccato in infants → chlamydia; barking → croup; honking, distractible → tic or somatic cough disorder. [S1]
- **More than 3 episodes of PBB in 12 months warrants referral** or investigation for chronic suppurative lung disease. [S1]
- **Examine for** infection, work of breathing, hypoxia, stridor, chest signs, faltering growth, cardiac failure, and clubbing or chest wall deformity (suppurative lung disease). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Red flags in chronic cough:** shortness of breath · recurrent chronic/wet cough · recurrent pneumonia · chest pain · **haemoptysis** · systemic symptoms (fever, weight loss, growth failure) · neurodevelopmental abnormality · feeding difficulties · stridor · abnormal examination, CXR or lung function · co-existing chronic disease. [S1]

## What not to give

- ⚠️ **Over-the-counter cough medicines and decongestants are not recommended** — no proven efficacy and potential safety risks. [S1]
- ⚠️ **No empiric antibiotics, steroids, proton pump inhibitors or antihistamines.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Honey has no strong evidence for cough** and carries risks — **infant botulism under 12 months**, dental caries and aspiration. [S1]
- **For every child:** advise avoiding airway irritants (smoking, vaping, wood-fire heaters), explain when to return (transition to chronic cough), and acknowledge parental concerns. [S1]

## Protracted bacterial bronchitis

- **PBB is the commonest cause of chronic wet cough**: the child is otherwise well, with no alternative cause, and investigations (including CXR apart from perihilar thickening) are normal. [S1]
- **Start a 2-week course of amoxicillin/clavulanic acid 22.5 mg/kg (amoxicillin component, max 875 mg) bd.** [S1]
- **Penicillin hypersensitivity:** trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole 4 mg/kg (trimethoprim component, max 160 mg) bd. [S1]
- **Still wet after 2 weeks: continue the same antibiotic for a further 2 weeks.** Still wet after 4 weeks, or more than 3 PBB episodes a year: investigate as for suppurative lung disease and refer. [S1]

## Other chronic causes

- **Chronic suppurative lung disease / bronchiectasis** (wet cough unresponsive to PBB treatment or recurring off antibiotics): FBE, serum immunoglobulins, CXR, spirometry, sweat test, sputum culture; refer to a paediatric respiratory physician. [S1]
- **Interstitial lung disease:** chronic dry cough with diffuse inspiratory crepitations, tachypnoea, growth failure or hypoxia — refer to a paediatric respiratory physician. [S1]
- **Somatic cough or tic disorder:** honking, disappears asleep or distracted — education and reassurance; consider distraction therapies, psychological support or hypnosis. [S1]

## Referral and discharge

- **Refer to a paediatric respiratory physician for:** chronic wet cough failing 4 weeks of antibiotics · more than 3 PBB episodes a year · chronic dry cough over 6 months failing treatment (e.g. an asthma trial) · specific cough or red flags. [S1]
- **Consider discharge when** the child is stable and any investigations and follow-up are arranged. [S1]
