## Recognise it

- **Syncope** = brief, sudden loss of consciousness and postural tone with spontaneous recovery. About 15% of children have an episode by the end of adolescence. [S1]
- ⚠️ **An arrhythmia should be considered in every child with syncope**, even though most paediatric syncope is vasovagal. A thorough history is the best guide to cause and investigation. [S1]
- **Reflex (vasovagal) syncope** has a clear precipitant — tiredness, hunger, heat, dehydration, emotion. [S1]
- **Orthostatic hypotension:** within 2–3 minutes of standing, systolic falls over 20 mmHg or diastolic over 10 mmHg. Causes include hypovolaemia, anaemia, electrolyte disturbance, menorrhagia, medications. [S1]
- **POTS:** daily chronic orthostatic intolerance **and**, within 5–10 minutes of standing, a heart-rate rise over 40 bpm, or over 30 bpm without postural hypotension. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Cardiac causes:** brady/tachyarrhythmia, long QT, Brugada, CPVT, conduction disease such as WPW, and structural disease (congenital heart disease, cardiomyopathy, myocarditis). [S1]
- **Mimics:** seizure, migraine, hypoglycaemia, toxins (carbon monoxide, clonidine), functional disorder, narcolepsy. [S1]

## Syncope or seizure?

- **Unconsciousness:** usually seconds in autonomic and cardiogenic syncope; **more than a few seconds, up to minutes, in seizures.** [S1]
- **Incontinence** is absent in autonomic and cardiogenic syncope and may be present in seizures; **confusion on waking** is absent in both syncope types and **marked for 20–30 minutes after a seizure.** [S1]
- **Tonic-clonic movements** are occasional and brief in either syncope type, particularly with prolonged unconsciousness (syncopal seizure); **frequent in seizures.** [S1]

## Examine and investigate

- **Measure orthostatic heart rate and BP**, and do a complete cardiac and neurological examination. [S1]
- ⚠️ **ECG in every child at least once** — may be skipped only if done before with no new concern. [S1]
- **BGL** if seen soon after the event, **FBE** if anaemia is suspected, and consider a pregnancy test. [S1]

## Treat

- **Frequent or problematic vasovagal or orthostatic syncope:** avoid triggers, **increase fluid and salt intake**, and recognise prodromal symptoms early. [S1]

## Escalation and discharge

- ⚠️ **Suspected cardiac syncope on history, examination or ECG: refer urgently to a paediatrician or cardiologist.** [S1]
- **Refer** recurrent vasovagal/orthostatic syncope unresponsive to non-drug measures (paediatrician or cardiologist), and seizure-like presentations (paediatrician or neurologist). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Consider transfer** if persistently symptomatic, or with clustering of syncope with cardiac features. [S1]
- **Discharge** once the most likely cause is identified and follow-up is arranged. [S1]
