# Redback spider bite in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognition, analgesia, antivenom and discharge after redback (and Steatoda) spider bite in children. Funnel-web and other big black spiders are `big-black-spider-bite-children`; antivenom reactions overlap `anaphylaxis-children`; snake antivenoms are `snakebite-antivenoms`, which does not cover redback antivenom.

> ⚠️ **Redback bite: analgesia first. Ice pack only — pressure immobilisation is NOT used.** Antivenom is for pain that analgesia fails to control, after toxicologist discussion.

---

## Recognise

- **Redback bites can cause severe pain — adequate analgesia is the priority. Significant envenoming is rare.** [S1]
- **Cupboard/brown house spiders (*Steatoda*) cause similar symptoms and are treated the same way.** White-tailed spiders may cause only local irritation or inflammation. [S1]
- **Necrotising arachnidism** — small ulcers to spreading necrosis — **has been shown to be unrelated to spider bites.** [S1]
- **Assess children whose pain is not controlled by simple analgesia.** Diagnosis rests on a typical history; severity on the pain and any systemic features. [S1]
- **The bite isn't immediately painful; pain builds over minutes to hours**, radiates to regional nodes, proximal limb, abdomen, chest or back, and may last days. ⚠️ **In an infant it may present as intractable crying.** [S1]
- **Systemic features:** headache, lethargy, malaise, nausea and vomiting, myalgia and neck spasm; sweating in unusual local or regional distributions; hypertension, irritability and agitation (possibly more common in children); priapism; rarely rhabdomyolysis or myocarditis. [S1]
- **Investigations are usually unnecessary**; check CK or ECG if rhabdomyolysis or myocarditis is suspected. [S1]

## First aid and analgesia

- ⚠️ **First aid is an ice pack. Pressure immobilisation is NOT used.** [S1]
- **Analgesia:** paracetamol 15 mg/kg (max 1 g) and ibuprofen 10 mg/kg (max 400 mg), ± oxycodone 0.1 mg/kg (max 5 mg), ± fentanyl. [S1]

## Antivenom

- **Discuss antivenom with a toxicologist only if analgesia fails.** Its aim is pain relief and the evidence for it is not strong, so give adequate analgesia first and discuss risks and benefits with the family. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Acute hypersensitivity risk is under 5%; no premedication is needed.** After treating a reaction, the antivenom can be restarted slowly. **Serum sickness can follow at 5–14 days.** [S1]
- **Dose: 2 ampoules (500 units each) CSL redback spider antivenom, diluted in 10 mL/kg up to 100 mL, IV over 20–30 minutes.** If IV access is difficult, give it undiluted IM. [S1]
- **No effect in 2 hours: reconsider the diagnosis** and discuss with a clinical toxicologist. [S1]

## Disposition

- **Admission is not usually required.** Call the Poisons Information Centre if considering antivenom or if presentation is delayed. [S1]
- **Discharge once pain is controlled; after antivenom, observe at least 1 hour for recurrence** and give serum sickness information. [S1]
