# Paracetamol poisoning in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** risk assessment, paracetamol levels, activated charcoal, the two-bag acetylcysteine regimen and disposition for immediate-release, modified-release and repeated supratherapeutic ingestions in children. General decontamination principles are in `gastrointestinal-decontamination`.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 29 of 29 claims quoted verbatim from the **RCH Melbourne Clinical Practice Guideline *Paracetamol poisoning*** (Last updated July 2023); **47 fragments re-checked by machine. 13 quoted doses.**

## Recognise and assess

- ⚠️ **Suspect paracetamol in every deliberate adolescent self-poisoning**, and consider co-ingestants. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Time to acetylcysteine is crucial for protecting the liver**; delays beyond 8 hours after ingestion increase the risk of liver injury. The stated time and dose can be unreliable. [S1]
- **Acetylcysteine is a safe and effective antidote.** Write it in full — "NAC" is not an approved abbreviation. [S1]
- **Assess:** any symptomatic child · all deliberate self-poisoning · any child whose developmental age doesn't fit accidental poisoning (consider non-accidental poisoning) · unknown quantity · **acute ingestion over 200 mg/kg or 10 g, whichever is less.** [S1]
- **Repeated supratherapeutic ingestion needs assessment (paracetamol level and ALT) if:** over 200 mg/kg or 10 g in 24 hours · over 300 mg/kg or 12 g in 48 hours (whichever is less, each) · more than a daily therapeutic dose per day for over 48 hours with abdominal pain, nausea or vomiting. [S1]
- **Establish the formulation — immediate or modified release — because it dictates management.** Paracetamol is in many combination products; calculate the maximum possible dose per kg. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Significant toxicity may have no signs or symptoms.** Hepatotoxicity may take 48–72 hours to develop; fulminant hepatic failure and coagulopathy may appear after 72 hours. [S1]
- **Discuss complicated ingestions** — modified release (e.g. Panadol Osteo) or repeated supratherapeutic — **with the Poisons Information Centre or a clinical toxicologist.** [S1]

## The nomogram

- **The nomogram decides acetylcysteine for acute immediate- and modified-release ingestions; it is not for repeated supratherapeutic ingestion.** Below the line: no treatment. Above the line: acetylcysteine. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Check the lab result's units match the nomogram** (mg/L or micromol/L). [S1]

## Immediate-release ingestion

- **Paracetamol level and LFT at 4 hours after tablets (or as soon as possible after), or 2 hours after liquid.** INR if more than 24 hours after ingestion or ALT is abnormal. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Start acetylcysteine immediately if** the level won't be available within 8 hours of a potentially toxic ingestion, or the child has symptoms of hepatic injury. [S1]
- **Activated charcoal has a very limited role and only with a toxicologist**: it may be offered up to 2 hours after ingestion if the dose is over 200 mg/kg or 10 g. It is unlikely to be needed under 6 years. [S1]
- **Liquid, well child under 6 years, over 200 mg/kg:** level at 2–4 hours. **Under 150 mg/L (1000 micromol/L): no acetylcysteine.** 150 mg/L or more: repeat at 4 hours; **if still 150 mg/L or more, start acetylcysteine.** Over 4 hours or aged 6 or older: follow the acute ingestion pathway. [S1]

## Modified-release ingestion

- **Take the first level more than 4 hours after ingestion and repeat 4 hours later** to guide the acetylcysteine dose and further decontamination; baseline LFT with the first level. [S1]
- **Charcoal (with a toxicologist) should be offered for modified release** over 200 mg/kg or 10 g up to 4 hours, over 500 mg/kg or 30 g up to 24 hours, or when serial levels are rising. [S1]

## Repeated supratherapeutic ingestion

- ⚠️ **Do not use the nomogram.** **Start acetylcysteine if ALT is over 50 U/L or the paracetamol level is over 20 mg/L (132 micromol/L)**, and discuss with the Poisons Information Centre or a toxicologist. [S1]

## Acetylcysteine

- ⚠️ **Two-bag IV infusion, 300 mg/kg total: 200 mg/kg over 4 hours in 7 mL/kg crystalloid (up to 500 mL), then 100 mg/kg over the next 16 hours in 14 mL/kg (up to 1000 mL).** [S1]
- **Massive exposure: double the second-bag dose if the level is over twice the nomogram line.** Dose on actual body weight, capped at 110 kg. [S1]
- **Dilute in maintenance fluid** such as 0.9% sodium chloride in 5% glucose, and **count the acetylcysteine volume within the total** to avoid under-dosing. [S1]
- **Over 40 kg:** first bag 200 mg/kg in 500 mL total at 125 mL/hr for 4 hours; second bag 100 mg/kg in 1000 mL total at 62.5 mL/hr for 16 hours. [S1]
- **20–40 kg:** first bag 200 mg/kg in 250 mL total at 62.5 mL/hr for 4 hours; second bag 100 mg/kg in 500 mL total at 31.25 mL/hr for 16 hours. ⚠️ **The resulting 750 mL is inappropriate for smaller children.** [S1]
- **20 kg or less:** first bag 200 mg/kg in 250 mL total at 62.5 mL/hr for 4 hours; second bag 100 mg/kg in 250 mL total at 15 mL/hr for 16 hours. **Infants may need smaller volumes** (e.g. 100 mL bags, still over the specified time); ask the hospital pharmacist. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Non-immunologic anaphylaxis** (flushing, rash, itch, wheeze, breathlessness, angioedema, hypotension): **stop the infusion and give promethazine; restart after 30 minutes at half the rate**, increasing to full rate over 30 minutes. A previous reaction does not preclude future use. [S1]
- **At 18 hours (2 hours before the second bag ends), send paracetamol level and ALT**; if presentation was over 24 hours after ingestion, add INR, venous gas and urea, electrolytes and creatinine. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not stop acetylcysteine early without Toxicology**, except for an anaphylactoid reaction. **Stop only after the 20-hour course if ALT is normal, the level is under 10 mg/L (66 micromol/L) and the child is clinically well**; discuss every other case. [S1]

## Disposition

- **Consider admission for every intentional overdose.** Poisons Information Centre: 13 11 26. [S1]
- **Discharge when no further acetylcysteine is needed and investigations are normal**; after deliberate ingestion, only once a risk assessment shows low risk of further self-harm. [S1]
- **Before discharge after deliberate ingestion, assess self-harm risk and other drug and alcohol use**, and link identified mental health or drug and alcohol needs to services. [S1]
