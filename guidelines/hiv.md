# HIV

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-21 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults, primary care — **diagnosis, first response and referral. Not regimen selection.**

> **Australian primary source.** ASHM *Australian STI Management Guidelines for use in primary care* —
> *HIV* [S1], modified 30 June 2026.

## What this guideline deliberately does not contain

**No antiretroviral regimen appears below, because none appears in the source.** The ASHM primary-care page
names no drug, no dose and no combination. It says *"consider starting ART at the time of diagnosis or as
soon as possible following diagnosis, regardless of CD4+ T-cell count"* and then routes the prescriber to
the **Antiretroviral Guidelines** and to an **s100 prescriber** [S1].

That is a deliberate division of labour, and this guideline honours it. Regimens are not written here from
memory — the pages that hold them were not retrieved, so they are listed under **Unresolved** rather than
filled in.

The primary-care job on this page is: **diagnose, start the conversation, protect the contacts, notify,
refer, and follow up in days rather than weeks.**

## 72 hours is the number to hold

> **Post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) can be offered within 72 hours of potential HIV exposure** [S1].

It appears **three times** on one page — under complications, under prevention, and under contact tracing —
which is the source's way of saying it is the one time-critical action a primary-care clinician can take
without specialist input.

And it applies to the **contact**, not just the index patient:

> If there is potential for transmission within the last 72 hours (e.g. condomless sex),
> **offer post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) to that contact** [S1].

## U = U

> People with HIV who **consistently take ART daily as prescribed and who achieve and maintain an
> undetectable HIV viral load cannot sexually transmit the virus to an HIV-negative partner**
> (U=U, undetectable = untransmissible) **and have a near normal life expectancy** [S1].

The source lists discussing this as the **first** item of immediate management — before treatment options,
before contact tracing. Transmission is defined in the same terms: it requires fluids **from a person with
HIV who has a detectable viral load** [S1].

## Diagnosis

**Follow the ASHM *Decision making in HIV* tool** [S1]. A second tool, *Could It Be HIV? — HIV Clinical
Indicator Testing Tool 2021*, is provided for deciding whom to test [S1].

> **A confirmatory test following a reactive HIV self-test must be completed** [S1].

People increasingly present having already tested themselves. A reactive self-test is not a diagnosis.

**Acute infection is symptomatic in 70% of patients** — fever, rash, lymphadenopathy, pharyngitis, myalgia,
diarrhoea — and **this seroconversion illness often occurs about 2 weeks after exposure** [S1]. Asymptomatic
infection can then follow for several years.

**Untreated, HIV progresses to AIDS after a variable period of 2–20 years, on average 10 years after
infection** [S1].

## How transmission works, as the source defines it

Contact with **blood, semen or anogenital fluid** from a person with HIV **who has a detectable viral load**.
Those fluids **must enter an HIV-negative person through a mucous membrane (rectum, vagina, tip of penis),
open wounds, or direct injection** [S1].

**Vertical transmission** can occur if the pregnant person **has a detectable viral load during pregnancy or
when breastfeeding** [S1].

## Immediate management at diagnosis

The order on the page is the order of the consultation [S1]:

1. **Discuss U=U and near-normal life expectancy.**
2. **Discuss the support available** — including **peer navigation** for people wanting support,
   information or services.
3. **Urgent referral to an experienced counsellor or psychologist may be needed.**
4. > **Avoid overemphasis of technical discussions about treatment options in the initial discussion** [S1].
5. **Discuss informing sexual contacts** and support contact tracing; **offer PEP to any contact exposed in
   the last 72 hours**.
6. **Complete comprehensive STI testing** — syphilis, chlamydia, gonorrhoea — if not done at first
   presentation, or retest past the window period.
7. **Notify the state or territory health department.**

Point 4 is the one most easily lost. The page explicitly instructs the clinician **not** to lead with
pharmacology on the day of diagnosis — a rare instruction about what *not* to say.

## Special situations

| Situation | Recommended [S1] |
|---|---|
| **Patient unwell** | **Consider urgent referral to hospital or a specialist centre for assessment** |
| **HIV primary illness** | **Urgent commencement of ART** |
| **CD4 < 200 cells/μL** | **High risk for opportunistic infection, needs urgent treatment.** May need chemoprophylaxis with **co-trimoxazole / fluconazole** — seek specialist advice |
| **Allergy to treatment** | **Check HLA-B57 status before use of abacavir** |
| **Pregnancy** | **Should start ART.** Seek specialist advice |
| **Co-infection with hepatitis B or hepatitis C** | May need to modify treatment choice. Seek specialist advice |

**All complex situations should be managed in collaboration with a specialist** [S1].

## Complications, including the ones this compendium already covers

**AIDS:** opportunistic infections such as ***Pneumocystis (carinii) jiroveci* pneumonia**, oesophageal
candidiasis, cerebral toxoplasmosis; and cancers such as **Kaposi sarcoma** [S1].

**Non-infectious comorbidities:** people with HIV are at **increased risk for cardiovascular disease,
chronic kidney disease, osteoporosis and some non-AIDS malignancies** [S1].

Three of those four already have guidelines in this set — cardiovascular disease risk, chronic kidney
disease, osteoporosis. HIV is a risk multiplier across them, which is the sort of link a
per-condition library loses.

## Contact tracing

**Notifiable condition** [S1]. **Contact tracing is important to prevent re-infection and reduce
transmission** [S1].

> **The diagnosing doctor is responsible for initiating and documenting a discussion about contact
> tracing** [S1].

Named responsibility and a documentation requirement — stated in no other topic in the set.

**Start with recent sexual or needle-sharing partners; the outer limit is the onset of risk behaviour, or
the last known negative HIV test result if known** [S1]. Unlike trichomoniasis or *M. genitalium*, the window
is defined — by the patient's own history rather than by a fixed number of weeks.

## Follow-up

- **Close follow-up within a few days of diagnosis** — psychosocial wellbeing, baseline investigations, and
  response to ART if started [S1].
- **Long-term regular review by a practitioner experienced in HIV care**: specialist clinic, sexual health
  service, or **s100 GP prescriber** [S1].
- **The patient should also see their GP for ongoing primary care** [S1] — HIV care does not replace it.
- **Once stable on ART, follow-up can be every 3–6 months** [S1].

## Auditable outcomes

- **100% of patients should have contact tracing performed** [S1].
- **100% of patients should be offered treatment at the time of diagnosis** [S1].
