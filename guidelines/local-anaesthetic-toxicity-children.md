# Local anaesthetic toxicity in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognition, resuscitation, antidotes (sodium bicarbonate, methylene blue, 20% lipid emulsion) and disposition for local anaesthetic systemic toxicity and ingestion in children. > ⚠️ **Stop the local anaesthetic and call for help. Cardiac arrest can be the first sign after massive IV overdose.** Severe cardiovascular toxicity: 20% lipid emulsion 1.5 mL/kg bolus then 0.25 mL/kg/minute (max cumulative 12 mL/kg).

---

## Key points

- ⚠️ **Onset is usually rapid, with potentially fatal neurological and cardiovascular effects** — but toxicity can appear later after oral or topical use. [S1]
- **Management is largely supportive, with IV lipid emulsion in severe cases.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Methaemoglobinaemia** is more likely with benzocaine, lignocaine or prilocaine; it is **not dose-related and is more likely in children, including after topical use.** [S1]
- **Allergic reactions including anaphylaxis are possible** — treat along conventional lines. [S1]

## Background

- **Usually therapeutic error.** ⚠️ **Fatalities are reported after ingesting lignocaine-containing local and topical preparations**, despite first-pass metabolism. [S1]
- **Most agents have a 2-hour elimination half-life; bupivacaine's is 5 hours or longer.** [S1]
- **Maximum recommended doses (mg/kg):** bupivacaine 1–2.5 · lignocaine 4–5 · mepivacaine 4–5 · prilocaine 5–7 · ropivacaine 2.5–3. ⚠️ **Toxicity can occur below these after IV or intra-arterial injection**; larger doses can be given with adrenaline. **Bupivacaine is particularly cardiotoxic.** [S1]
- **Assess:** any deliberate self-poisoning, significant accidental overdose, or inadvertent venous or arterial injection · any symptomatic patient. [S1]

## Recognise it

- **Early features:** tinnitus, difficulty focusing, dizziness, anxiety, agitation, confusion, drowsiness, perioral or tongue numbness, metallic taste. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Severe:** seizures, coma · bradycardia, hypotension, dysrhythmias, conduction block, collapse, asystole · respiratory depression, apnoea. **Cardiac arrest may be the first sign after massive IV overdose.** [S1]
- **Investigate:** UEC, arterial blood gas, methaemoglobin; **serial ECGs for sodium channel blockade** (prolonged PR and QRS, large terminal R in aVR). [S1]

## Resuscitation

- ⚠️ **Immediately stop the local anaesthetic and call for help.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Any cardiotoxicity: intubate and ventilate immediately** to prevent hypoxaemia, hypercarbia and acidosis. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Ventricular dysrhythmias: sodium bicarbonate 1–2 mmol/kg (max 100 mmol) IV every 1–2 minutes until a perfusing rhythm**; amiodarone can be used. **AVOID calcium channel blockers, beta blockers and local anaesthetics.** [S1]
- **Seizures: benzodiazepines. Hypotension: normal saline 20 mL/kg IV, then inotropes if needed; AVOID vasopressin.** [S1]
- **Recovery from local-anaesthetic cardiac arrest may take over 1 hour.** Decontamination is not indicated; enhanced elimination is not useful. [S1]

## Antidotes

- **Methaemoglobinaemia: methylene blue 1–2 mg/kg IV over 5 minutes.** [S1]
- **Lipid emulsion (intralipid 20%) for severe cardiovascular toxicity or cardiac arrest.** Caution with egg yolk, soya or peanut protein hypersensitivity. [S1]
- ⚠️ **20% lipid emulsion: bolus 1.5 mL/kg IV over 1 minute, then infuse 0.25 mL/kg/minute.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **After 5 minutes, if collapse persists or worsens: up to 2 repeat boluses (at least 5 minutes apart) and double the infusion to 0.5 mL/kg/minute.** Continue until circulation is stable or the **max cumulative 12 mL/kg** is reached. [S1]
- **Lipid emulsion adverse events:** allergy and anaphylaxis, laboratory interference, pancreatitis (rare), VTE (rare), pulmonary hypertension, acute lung injury, haematuria, hypertriglyceridaemia, ECMO circuit obstruction. [S1]

## Disposition

- ⚠️ **Lignocaine teething gel or other topical ingestion: hospital assessment if over 6 mg/kg or any symptoms.** [S1]
- **Ongoing care in a high-dependency or intensive care setting; consider tertiary transfer for any patient with local anaesthetic toxicity.** PIPER: 1300 137 650. [S1]
- **Consider admission for every intentional overdose.** [S1]
