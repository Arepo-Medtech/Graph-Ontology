## Key points

- ⚠️ **Eucalyptus oil is highly toxic: small ingestions of pure oil (5 mL or more) can cause severe symptoms.** [S1]
- **Onset is usually rapid (within 30 minutes) but can be delayed up to 4 hours.** **Watch for CNS depression and respiratory compromise** — the main features. [S1]
- **Found as** essential oil, medicinal and cleaning products, inhalational/vaporiser fluid and topical preparations. Symptoms usually resolve within 24 hours. [S1]

## Dose and assessment

- ⚠️ **2–3 mL can cause mild CNS depression** (drowsiness, dizziness, ataxia); **5 mL or more can cause significant CNS depression with coma.** [S1]
- **Assess:** all deliberate self-poisoning or significant accidental ingestion · any symptomatic child · **single ingestion over 5 mL** · any child whose developmental age does not fit accidental poisoning. [S1]
- **History:** route (inhalation, ingestion, intranasal, topical — consider frequent repeated applications), the exact product and % solution (**2–3 mL is significant in a child; over 5 mL may mean severe toxicity**), and co-ingestants such as paracetamol or other oils. [S1]
- **Examination:** miosis (more common) or mydriasis, myoclonus, CNS depression (ataxia, seizures, coma with 5 mL or more) · tachycardia, hypotension · ⚠️ respiratory depression, apnoea, bronchospasm, aspiration · early nausea, vomiting and diarrhoea. [S1]
- **Investigations:** usually none for an asymptomatic child with a small ingestion. Consider CXR and blood gas for aspiration signs, **UEC and LFT for significant illness or ingestions of 5 mL or more**, and a paracetamol level in all intentional overdoses. [S1]

## Management

- **Aspiration pneumonitis is managed supportively** (oxygen, bronchodilators, NIV or intubation if severe); **no corticosteroids or prophylactic antibiotics.** Fever is common, so **withhold antibiotics until there is objective evidence of bacterial infection.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Charcoal is contraindicated** (aspiration risk). [S1]
- **Enhanced elimination is ineffective; there is no antidote.** [S1]

## Disposition

- **Asymptomatic after significant exposure, with normal vital signs and GCS: observe 4 hours before discharge.** Symptomatic: admit for longer observation ± supportive care. [S1]
- **Consider mental health assessment and admission for all adolescents with an intentional overdose.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Transfer for significant CNS depression, seizures or respiratory compromise needing PICU.** [S1]
- **Discharge:** normal GCS · no respiratory symptoms · observation complete. Adolescents with deliberate ingestion need a self-harm risk assessment and drug and alcohol assessment first. [S1]
