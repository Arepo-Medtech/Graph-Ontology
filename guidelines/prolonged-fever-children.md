# Prolonged fever in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** fever of 38.0°C or more for over 7 days without a source: history, examination, tiered investigations, endemic Australian exposures, treatment and disposition. ## Definition and key points

- **Prolonged fever = 38.0°C or more for more than 7 days with no identifiable source.** [S1]
- **Infection is the commonest cause**, but consider malignancy, rheumatological disorders and other inflammatory conditions. Detailed history and serial examination are essential. [S1]
- **Use a tiered approach guided by the presentation** to avoid unnecessary tests. **Investigations may wait 10–14 days** in a previously healthy, immunised, well-looking child who is not a returned international traveller. [S1]
- **Out of scope:** sepsis, acute febrile illness, returned international travellers, opportunistic infection in the immunocompromised, neonatal and hospital-acquired infection. [S1]

## History

- **Ask about:** immune status · Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander status, country of birth, vaccination · fever onset, duration and pattern · resolved symptoms (rash, conjunctivitis, sore throat, skin infection) · weight loss, night sweats, bruising/bleeding, joint pain or swelling. [S1]
- **Exposure history:** urban vs rural · tank or town water, floods and swimming · soil ingestion (toddlers, pica) · unpasteurised milk or imported foods · pets and animals · arthropod bites · domestic or international travel. [S1]
- **Also ask:** recent antibiotics or steroids and possible drug fever · prior microbiology including multi-resistant organisms · family history of ARF/RHD, hereditary cancer, rheumatological, autoimmune or recurrent fever syndromes · sexual history. [S1]

## Examination

- **Examine head to toe:** neurology (personality change, lethargy, meningism, focal signs) · **new murmur may mean endocarditis** · subtle chest signs · abdominal tenderness or mass · lymphadenopathy or hepatosplenomegaly · inability to weight bear or bony tenderness · rash, purpura, **hidden tick or eschar**, mucosal changes · sinus, mastoid or dental infection. [S1]
- **Look for systemic inflammatory conditions:** Kawasaki disease (including incomplete), ARF, JIA and SLE. [S1]

## Tier 1 investigations

- **Tier 1 bloods:** FBE · UEC and LFTs · CRP, ESR · ferritin, LDH · **serial blood cultures, at least two sets initially** · spare serum and plasma stored for add-on tests. [S1]
- **Tier 1 other:** nasopharyngeal respiratory virus and *Mycoplasma* PCR · urinalysis and MCS · CXR · faecal PCR ± MCS and OCP if diarrhoea · CSF if CNS infection is a concern. [S1]

## Tier 2 and 3 investigations

- **Tier 2:** repeat FBE and film, LDH, urea · serology for EBV, CMV (± IgG avidity if IgM detected), GAS (ASOT, anti-DNAse), *Bartonella henselae*, *Toxoplasma* · throat swab MCS · enterovirus and adenovirus PCR · abdominal ultrasound · targeted imaging for focal findings. [S1]
- **Tier 3 bloods:** hepatitis A, B, C, E serology ± PCR if LFTs are deranged (**withhold hepatitis B serology in a vaccinated child**) · HIV · syphilis · IGRA ± TB PCR after TB contact or endemic travel · ANA, dsDNA, ENA, ANCA, RF · consider tumour markers, lymph node biopsy and/or bone marrow aspirate. **Tier 3 other:** mycobacterial culture (induced sputum, early-morning gastric aspirate, or stool) after TB contact or endemic travel · FOBT · **echocardiogram** · targeted imaging · CSF if not done. [S1]
- **Interpret results in clinical context** — a positive test (e.g. a respiratory virus) may not explain the severity or duration of the illness. [S1]

## Endemic exposures

- **Match exposure timing to incubation period.** PCR is most useful in the first 1–2 weeks (the seronegative window); serology early and again at 7–21 day intervals, **with serial samples tested in parallel** to show seroconversion. [S1]
- **Q fever** (cattle, sheep, goats, macropods; inhaled aerosols; QLD, NSW, VIC): incubation 2–3 weeks; acute flu-like illness, pneumonia, hepatitis, lymphopenia, thrombocytopenia; chronic endocarditis, osteomyelitis. **PCR days 1–14, serology from day 7.** [S1]
- **Brucellosis** (feral pigs — hunting, pig dogs, undercooked meat; QLD, NSW): incubation 5–60 days; night sweats, malaise, arthralgia, hepatosplenomegaly, lymphadenopathy. Blood culture, PCR, serology — **notify pathology before sending samples.** [S1]
- **Melioidosis** (contaminated water/soil; northern Australia): incubation 1–21 days; subclinical, localised or disseminated (sepsis, abscesses, septic arthritis, osteomyelitis, parotitis, CNS). Blood culture; serology is of limited value in endemic areas; MCS of relevant samples — **note the provisional diagnosis on the form for specific media.** [S1]
- **Leptospirosis** (fresh water/soil, water sports, animal urine; QLD, NSW, VIC): incubation 5–14 days; calf and lower-back myalgia, conjunctival suffusion, headache, jaundice, renal failure. Blood culture under 10 days; PCR under 7 days; IgM and MAT after 7 days. [S1]

## Treatment and disposition

- ⚠️ **Any febrile child who appears seriously unwell: manage as suspected sepsis.** [S1]
- **If stable, observe and investigate before antibiotics**; tailor specific treatment to the clinical picture and results. Give antipyretics for comfort and keep the child hydrated. [S1]
- **Discuss every child with prolonged fever with the local paediatric team.** Transfer if care or sub-specialty input is beyond the local hospital. [S1]
- **Discharge when** afebrile for more than 24–48 hours and improving, **or** the cause is diagnosed, treatment has started and outpatient care is feasible. [S1]
