## When to suspect it

- **Most young children with recurrent minor infections do not have a PID.** Healthy toddlers may have 5–10 minor infections a year, often more in childcare. [S1]
- **Consider PID in any child with** severe infection, infection with unusual or opportunistic organisms, or more infections than expected for age. [S1]
- **Recurrent infection at the same site may be anatomical** — e.g. recurrent meningitis with a blood–brain barrier defect. [S1]
- ⚠️ **In severe PIDs, early diagnosis can prevent infective complications and allows curative treatment.** [S1]

## Warning signs

- **History red flags:** poor growth (especially slow weight gain) · chronic diarrhoea · **four or more ear infections, or two or more serious sinus infections, in one year** · recurrent deep skin or organ abscesses · persistent thrush after 1 year of age · two or more months of antibiotics with little effect · repeated admissions for IV antibiotics · invasive pneumococcal or meningococcal disease · severe or prolonged common infections (e.g. adenovirus). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Family history:** immunodeficiency, a previous sibling death (unexplained or from infection), or parental consanguinity. [S1]
- **Examination:** persistent lymphadenopathy · oral candidiasis · gingivitis · severe or chronic otitis media or sinusitis · skin abscesses · clubbing · severe eczema or eczema-like rash · splenomegaly · poor muscle bulk, fat stores or nutrition. [S1]

## The six main types

- **Antibody deficiency** (e.g. XLA, CVID, specific antibody deficiency): poor growth, chronic diarrhoea or malabsorption, recurrent or severe sinusitis or otitis media, meningitis, sepsis. [S1]
- **Combined immunodeficiency** (e.g. SCID, 22q11 deletion/DiGeorge, X-linked hyper-IgM, Wiskott-Aldrich): poor growth, absent thymus, chronic diarrhoea, recurrent sinusitis or otitis, severe eczema or nappy rash, severe oral candidiasis, severe or chronic viral infection such as EBV. [S1]
- **Phagocytic cell deficiency** (e.g. CGD, LAD, Chediak-Higashi, cyclic neutropenia): recurrent skin or deep organ abscesses, gingivitis, osteomyelitis, severe or recurrent pneumonia. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Complement deficiency** (e.g. hereditary angioedema, specific complement deficiency): **meningitis or sepsis with encapsulated bacteria** (*N. meningitidis*, *S. pneumoniae*, Hib), recurrent angioedema. [S1]
- **Immune dysregulation** (e.g. ALPS, IPEX, APECED, familial HLH): endocrine disorders, chronic diarrhoea, lymphadenopathy. **Autoinflammatory disorders** (e.g. FMF, CRMO): recurrent fevers, inflammatory arthritis or peritonitis, rash, splenomegaly. [S1]

## Investigate

- **First line: FBE and differential** (compare with previous results) **and a blood film** (for Howell-Jolly bodies). [S1]
- **After specialist consultation:** total IgG/A/M (**not IgG subclasses routinely**) · lymphocyte subsets (CD3/CD4/CD8/CD19/CD16/56) · HIV antibody. [S1]
- ⚠️ **If SCID is suspected, assess naïve T cells (CD45RA/RO)** — CD4/CD8 counts may be normal. [S1]
- **Invasive meningococcal or pneumococcal disease: abdominal ultrasound for a spleen and complement studies** (e.g. CH50). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Investigate for immunodeficiency:** any child over 2 years with invasive pneumococcal disease (sepsis, meningitis, complicated pneumonia, septic arthritis) and no clear predisposition, and **every child with recurrent IPD.** [S1]
- **Normal first-line tests but high suspicion: refer to a paediatric immunologist** for second-line testing. [S1]

## Manage and refer

- ⚠️ **Do not give live vaccines to a child with suspected PID** — including varicella, MMR, rotavirus and BCG. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Discuss any suspected SCID immediately with a paediatric immunologist.** [S1]
- **Consult the paediatric team for** severe infection or poor response to therapy, **fever in a child with known or suspected PID**, or uncertainty about whether investigation and follow-up are needed. [S1]
- **Discharge when** the child is well, there is a clear management plan, and the family know when to return. [S1]
