# COVID-19 management in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** assessment, isolation, severity, respiratory support, additional treatments, post-vaccine myocarditis and PIMS-TS in children with suspected or confirmed COVID-19. Vaccination recommendations are in the counterpart `covid-19-vaccination` (Australian Immunisation Handbook), which covers vaccination only and supersedes this page on that topic.

## Key points

- **Children are less severely affected by COVID-19 than adults.** Provide appropriate respiratory support for any child with suspected or confirmed COVID-19. [S1]
- ⚠️ **High-flow oxygen or nebulised therapy: maintain airborne precautions** and manage in the highest level of isolation available. [S1]
- **COVID-19 may be incidental** — prioritise the child's presenting clinical syndrome. [S1]

## Assessment

- **Isolate in the highest level available**, ideally a single room with negative pressure if available. [S1]
- **Risk factors for severe disease:** immunosuppression, severe or complex chronic disease, obesity, pregnancy, and age — **under 3 months corrected age, and adolescents**. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Escalate for tachycardia or tachypnoea persistently breaching early-warning or pre-MET limits.** Disease may be severe without deranged vital signs. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Signs of severe illness:** severe respiratory distress, severe hypoxaemia or cyanosis, marked tachycardia, altered mental state. [S1]
- **Keep a low threshold to look for complications** (myocarditis/chest pain, respiratory, neurological) and progression. [S1]

## Testing

- **Swab every child who meets current testing criteria**; consider re-testing if the child deteriorates and suspicion stays high. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not swab a child with croup or suspected upper airway obstruction** until a senior clinician deems it safe. [S1]

## Treatment

- **Mild to moderate disease:** manage as per the clinical syndrome; base the decision to admit on clinical assessment (including risk factors), social and geographical factors and phase of illness. **Airborne precautions for all HCWs.** [S1]
- **Severe disease:** respiratory support as required. **High-flow nasal oxygen may be used with airborne precautions**; discuss with a senior clinician and consult ICU/Retrieval. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Reserve nebulised adrenaline for severe croup** because of aerosolisation risk. [S1]
- **Additional treatments are for very-high-risk children, moderate to severe disease, or PIMS-TS — always consult Infectious Diseases or a senior clinician.** [S1]
- **Corticosteroids for children needing ongoing supplemental oxygen** (including ventilated): **dexamethasone 0.15 mg/kg/day IV or oral, max 6 mg.** [S1]
- **VTE prophylaxis:** for hospitalised children, follow local thromboprophylaxis guidelines and consult haematology. **IVIG** is for PIMS-TS; other immunomodulators (anti-IL-1, anti-IL-6, anti-TNF) are third-line for PIMS-TS. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Not recommended:** vitamin D, ivermectin, hydroxychloroquine, colchicine or azithromycin — no supporting evidence. [S1]

## Post-vaccine myocarditis and pericarditis

- **Myocarditis and pericarditis are associated with mRNA COVID-19 vaccines** — rare, more common in **adolescent males and after the 2nd dose**, mostly **within 7 days** but reported up to 14 days. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, syncope or dizziness within 1 to 14 days of an mRNA COVID-19 vaccine** in an adolescent needs thorough evaluation. Report any adverse event after vaccination. [S1]

## Consultation, transfer and discharge

- **Consult the paediatric team** for any case needing respiratory or ongoing hydration support; **ICU/Retrieval** for escalating respiratory support; **Infectious Diseases** for severe disease, ongoing respiratory support or PIMS-TS. [S1]
- **Consider discharge** for cases that need no hydration or respiratory support. [S1]

## PIMS-TS

- ⚠️ **PIMS-TS is a very rare, serious complication 2–6 weeks after SARS-CoV-2 infection** (which may have been asymptomatic). **Consider it in an unwell child with fever (3 days or more), shock, rash and abdominal pain**; it overlaps with Kawasaki disease and toxic shock syndrome. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Discuss every suspected PIMS-TS case urgently with a senior clinician**; tertiary ICU transfer may be needed. With Infectious Diseases consider corticosteroids, IVIG and low-dose aspirin. [S1]
