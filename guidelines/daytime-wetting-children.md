# Daytime wetting (urinary incontinence) in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** assessment, investigation, behavioural management, oxybutynin and referral for daytime urinary incontinence in school-aged children. Night-time wetting is `nocturnal-enuresis`; the adult counterpart is `urinary-incontinence` (which excludes children). Related: `constipation`, `urinary-tract-infection-children`.

> **Behaviour modification is the most common treatment.** ⚠️ **Never dry by day, or continuous dribbling: strongly consider an anatomical abnormality** and refer to urology.

## Recognise it

- **Urinary incontinence = day wetting over 5 years of age, more than once per month, for 3 months or more.** Daytime continence is usually achieved by 4 years. [S1]
- **The aim of evaluation is to distinguish neurological and anatomical causes from functional bladder dysfunction.** A voiding history and a **bladder diary** are essential. [S1]
- **Estimate normal bladder capacity before adolescence as (age + 2) × 30 mL.** [S1]
- **Functional causes:** overactive bladder (urgency is the key feature) · voiding postponement · underactive bladder (infrequent voiding, overflow, large post-void residual) · non-neurogenic dysfunctional voiding (failure to relax the sphincter/pelvic floor, interrupted flow). [S1]

## Assess

- ⚠️ **Never been dry by day, or continuous (not intermittent) incontinence/dribbling: strongly consider an anatomical abnormality.** [S1]
- **Ask about** frequency, urgency, nocturia, polyuria, **holding manoeuvres** (tiptoes, leg-crossing, heel to perineum), straining, weak or interrupted stream, dysuria, and obtain a completed bladder diary. [S1]
- **Girls:** consider urethro-vaginal reflux (leakage from the vagina on standing after voiding). **Boys:** consider incomplete emptying or dysfunctional voiding. [S1]
- **Look for comorbidities:** constipation, UTI, night-time wetting. Tiredness or weight loss → chronic illness or renal impairment; polydipsia/polyuria → diabetes mellitus, diabetes insipidus, renal tubular disease or psychogenic. [S1]
- **Examine:** height, weight, BP; abdomen for bladder, renal or faecal mass; perineum and genitalia (**exclude epispadias**); **lower back/spine for occult dysraphism/tethered cord**; lower-limb neurology and gait. [S1]

## Investigate

- **Routine: urinalysis and culture**; repeat if previous UTIs or possible renal/metabolic disease. [S1]
- **Renal tract ultrasound with pre- and post-void residual** when initial behavioural management fails or there are red flags (e.g. continuous incontinence, recurrent UTIs). [S1]

## Initial management

- **Behavioural modification is standard initial management:** education about bladder function; timed voiding, no holding manoeuvres, good voiding posture; the bladder diary to track progress. [S1]
- **Fluids:** a drink with each meal and snack, spread over the day. [S1]
- **Actively manage for 3 months** (about 2 cycles of re-education); **if unsuccessful, refer to a local paediatrician.** [S1]
- **Treat constipation/soiling and UTI at the same time**; refer recurrent UTI to a paediatrician. **Combined day–night wetting: treat daytime symptoms first.** [S1]

## Overactive bladder: oxybutynin

- **Medication is second line**, started by the local paediatric team or continence service; **behavioural modification continues throughout.** [S1]
- **Oxybutynin is the oral first-line option. 5–12 years:** 2.5 mg orally twice daily initially, increasing gradually if needed to 5 mg 2 or 3 times daily. [S1]
- **Oxybutynin 12–18 years:** 5 mg orally 2 or 3 times daily initially, **max 5 mg 3 times daily.** [S1]
- **Transdermal patch** (off-label under 12) if the child cannot swallow or tolerate oral oxybutynin; **do not cut or divide patches.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Monitor for constipation, dry mouth and flushing**, and for incomplete emptying and retention; consider laxatives, since constipation can worsen incontinence. [S1]
- **Effect should appear within 2 months; continue for 3 to 6 months**, then wean gradually. [S1]

## Refer

- **Urology** if an anatomical abnormality is suspected (e.g. continuous wetting) or seen on imaging. **Nephrology/paediatrics** for significant haematuria and/or proteinuria, or polyuria/polydipsia once diabetes mellitus is excluded. [S1]
- **Refer to mental health services at the same time** if there are significant behavioural or psychological comorbidities. [S1]
