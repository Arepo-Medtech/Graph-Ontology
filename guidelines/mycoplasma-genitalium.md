# *Mycoplasma genitalium*

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-21 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults, primary care.

> **Australian primary source.** ASHM *Australian STI Management Guidelines for use in primary care* —
> *Mycoplasma genitalium* [S1], modified 30 June 2026.

## ⚠️ Do not screen asymptomatic people

> **Asymptomatic screening for *M. genitalium* is not currently recommended**, due to lack of knowledge
> regarding its natural history, rising antimicrobial resistance, and increasing complexities around access
> to effective treatments [S1].

This is the second explicit *recommendation against* in the set (after ABCD² in TIA), and it inverts the
usual reflex: finding the organism in an asymptomatic person creates a treatment problem without a
demonstrated benefit.

**Test only on clinical indication** [S1]: acute, persistent or recurrent non-gonococcal urethritis;
cervicitis; pelvic inflammatory disease; post-coital bleeding; and ongoing sexual contacts of known
infection.

**Throat swabs are not recommended** — pharyngeal infection is uncommon [S1].

## Why resistance drives everything here

| Resistance | Prevalence in Australia [S1] |
|---|---|
| **Macrolide (azithromycin)** | **exceeds 60%** in the majority of cases |
| Macrolide, in **men who have sex with men** | **exceeds 80%** |
| **Fluoroquinolone** (affects moxifloxacin) | rising, **approaching 20%** in many urban settings; mutations detected in **15–20%** in urban centres |

> **Use of macrolide-resistance assays and resistance-guided therapy has been shown to improve first-line
> cure from 60% to >90%** [S1].

That is the single most consequential number on the page: the same drugs, chosen by assay rather than
empirically, take cure from a coin-flip to near-certain.

**Where resistance testing is unavailable**, it is reasonable to **assume macrolide resistance** in
infections persisting after azithromycin failure, and **in men who have sex with men**, where resistance
exceeds 80% in most urban Australian settings [S1].

## Treatment — resistance-guided

Every regimen begins with doxycycline. The second agent is chosen by resistance status.

| Situation | Recommended [S1] |
|---|---|
| **Macrolide-susceptible** (known or suspected) | **Doxycycline 100 mg BD 7 days**, followed by **azithromycin 1 g immediately then 500 mg daily for 3 days (total 2.5 g)** |
| **Macrolide-resistant** (known or suspected) | **Doxycycline 100 mg BD 7 days**, followed by **moxifloxacin 400 mg daily for 7 days** |
| **PID due to *M. genitalium*** | **Moxifloxacin 400 mg daily for 14 days** |

**On PID timing** [S1]: results often arrive about a week after presumptive PID treatment has begun.
Empirical doxycycline and metronidazole has shown some efficacy against *M. genitalium*-associated PID — if
the patient is **clinically responding**, complete the 14-day empiric regimen and **do a test of cure**; if
they **remain symptomatic**, recall and switch to 14 days of moxifloxacin. The source notes this approach
was evaluated in an RCT of all-cause PID and **is not supported by randomised data** in *M. genitalium*-
associated PID specifically.

### Moxifloxacin cautions

**Private prescription, not recommended in pregnancy or breastfeeding, and expensive.** Associated with
diarrhoea and, uncommonly, tendinopathy, neurological and cardiac events. **Assess for contraindications and
review concurrent medications** before using a quinolone [S1].

**Not all fluoroquinolone resistance mutations are strongly associated with treatment failure.** If using a
resistance/susceptibility assay, **speak to your laboratory about how well it predicts moxifloxacin
outcomes** [S1] — an unusually specific instruction to interrogate your own lab's assay.

## When moxifloxacin fails or cannot be used

Seek specialist advice. Regimens with efficacy reported in published case series [S1]:

| Regimen | Cure | Access |
|---|---|---|
| **Pristinamycin 1 g TDS + doxycycline 100 mg BD, 10 days** | **75%** of macrolide-resistant infections | hospital pharmacies via **TGA Special Access Scheme** |
| **Minocycline 100 mg BD, 14 days** | **70%** of macrolide-resistant infections | private script |
| **Doxycycline 100 mg BD + sitafloxacin 100 mg BD, 7 days** | **>90%**, in cases failing all other therapies | hospital pharmacies via **TGA Special Access Scheme**; sitafloxacin more effective than moxifloxacin |

## Pregnancy

**Case by case, with the patient** [S1]. Published studies support an association with pre-term birth but
have tended **not to adjust for other STIs and confounders**, so more data is needed.

- **Macrolide-susceptible:** azithromycin is **category B1** and can be prescribed [S1].
- **Macrolide-resistant or at risk:** **discuss with a specialist — moxifloxacin is category B3 and
  contraindicated.** **Pristinamycin 1 g four times daily for 10 days** may be recommended; limited pregnancy
  data, but used in pregnancy in Europe and Australia [S1].
- If pristinamycin fails or is unavailable, options are very limited; moxifloxacin **may** be recommended
  after specialist consultation if benefits outweigh risks [S1].

## Follow-up

**Test of cure should not be done earlier than 14–21 days after treatment is completed** — earlier testing
can give **false positives** [S1]. Whether to do one at all is informed by ongoing symptoms and/or ongoing
risk of reinfection or sequelae.

**Advise no condomless sex until tested for cure** (14–21 days after completing treatment), and no sex with
untested previous partners [S1].

**Contact tracing: the time period is unknown.** Tracing is recommended for **ongoing sexual partners** [S1].
Contacts' treatment should be based on **their own** macrolide-resistance profile where available, otherwise
informed by the index case's infection status and treatment history [S1].

***M. genitalium* is not a notifiable condition** [S1].

## Knowing when to stop

> In ongoing persistent infection that has failed to respond, **the impact of repeated courses of
> antimicrobials and likelihood of adverse effects needs to be balanced against the likelihood of harm
> caused to the patient from *M. genitalium*** [S1].

The source treats "stop treating" as a legitimate outcome requiring a considered, shared decision — which
follows directly from not screening asymptomatic people in the first place.
