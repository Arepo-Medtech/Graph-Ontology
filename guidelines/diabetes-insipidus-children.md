# Diabetes insipidus in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognition, investigation, fluids, desmopressin safety and referral for central and nephrogenic diabetes insipidus in children. > ⚠️ **Never restrict water** in a child with suspected or known DI — thirst is what keeps their sodium safe.

> ⚠️ **Nephrogenic DI: no sodium chloride fluids beyond emergency resuscitation** without specialist (nephrology) consultation.

---

## Key safety points

- ⚠️ **Always allow free access to water; never restrict fluid intake.** Urine output may not reflect hydration status. [S1]
- **Inpatients need close electrolyte and fluid-balance monitoring** — most critical when the child is too young or too unwell to respond to thirst. [S1]
- ⚠️ **DI is dangerous when the child cannot drink freely** (very young, altered conscious state, nil by mouth, too unwell) **or has impaired thirst** (complex midline CNS defect, hypothalamic trauma or malignancy). [S1]

## Recognise it

- **Causes — central:** head trauma, pituitary-region tumour, post-neurosurgery, genetic, meningitis, idiopathic. **Nephrogenic:** genetic or acquired concentrating defects. [S1]
- **Polyuria = urine output >4 mL/kg/hr averaged over a 6 hour period** (may be higher in neonates — discuss with a specialist). [S1]
- **Polydipsia clues:** drinking overnight, persistent focus on drinking any fluid, drinking from unusual sources such as animal bowls. Also nocturia or new bedwetting, weight loss, constipation. [S1]
- **Babies present non-specifically:** irritability, poor feeding, slow growth — or excess weight gain when thirst is met with milk feeds. [S1]
- **Consider primary polydipsia (habitual drinking)** as an alternative diagnosis. [S1]

## Investigate

- **Urgent BGL to exclude diabetes mellitus**; UEC, CMP; urine dipstick for specific gravity and glucose; **paired early morning urine and serum osmolality** — in DI, serum osmolality is high with inappropriately dilute urine. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Water deprivation test only under specialist guidance**; may not be appropriate for infants <12 months for safety reasons. [S1]
- **Suspected central DI:** MRI brain and pituitary, and screen the other anterior pituitary hormones. Consider renal ultrasound if nephrogenic DI is suspected. [S1]

## Central DI: fluids

- **Strict fluid balance chart reviewed at least twice daily; daily weight.** ⚠️ **Excess IV fluid or excess desmopressin can rapidly cause fluid overload and life-threatening hyponatraemia.** [S1]
- **If IV fluids are needed: sodium chloride 0.9% with glucose 5%; avoid hypotonic fluids.** Correct electrolytes and osmolality slowly. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Sodium ≥170 mmol/L: specialist advice and consider ICU. Sodium 150-169 mmol/L: replace the free water deficit slowly over 48 hours** with specialist advice. [S1]
- ⚠️ **After neurosurgery, expect a possible triphasic response** (DI, then SIADH, then permanent DI); desmopressin is generally given as needed rather than regularly until the SIADH and sudden-hyponatraemia risk has passed. [S1]

## Central DI: desmopressin

- **Discuss every child with endocrinology before starting or changing desmopressin.** Effect is all or nothing; dose changes alter duration of action (8-24 hours), not degree of response. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Routes (oral, intranasal, subcut, IM, IV) are not equivalent.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Don't stop or withhold regular desmopressin without specialist discussion. Known central DI may coexist with adrenal insufficiency — consider stress-dose steroids when unwell.** [S1]
- **When unwell or changing treatment:** daily UEC and osmolality with paired urine osmolality until stable; more often if correcting hypernatraemia. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Before each dose:** serum sodium ≥135 mmol/L within 1-2 hours; check and document urine specific gravity (dilute <1.005); allow 1-2 hours of diuresis before further doses. [S1]
- **Urine output >4 mL/kg/hr for 2 consecutive hours:** may need repeat sodium and a higher or repeat dose. **No urine output for 12 hours:** may need to reduce or withhold the dose. [S1]

## Nephrogenic DI

- **Manage with a paediatric nephrologist and dietician.** Low-solute (low-sodium, low-protein) diet; consider thiazide diuretics; correct calcium, potassium and magnesium. [S1]
- **Desmopressin will not treat complete nephrogenic DI**; rarely considered in difficult non-genetic cases for a partial response. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Beyond a 10 mL/kg bolus of sodium chloride 0.9% for emergency resuscitation, discuss all fluids with a nephrologist.** [S1]
- **Genetic nephrogenic DI:** safest is enteral free water with careful sodium monitoring; glucose 5% if enteral fluids are contraindicated. ⚠️ **Sodium chloride fluids risk worsening hypernatraemic dehydration.** [S1]

## Refer, transfer, discharge

- **Endocrinology or nephrology:** all children being investigated for DI or starting/changing desmopressin; known DI with impaired thirst admitted, or with reduced access to water (fasting, cast, sedated, intubated); nephrogenic DI at risk of hypernatraemic dehydration. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Transfer: severe hypernatraemia (>170 mmol/L)** or complex fluid and electrolyte management. [S1]
- **Discharge when** the family can monitor thirst and urine output and knows when to seek advice. [S1]
