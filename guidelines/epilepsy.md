## ⚠️ Before treating: is it epilepsy?

> **EXCLUDE NON-EPILEPTIC CAUSES — breath-holding attack, FAINTING, ARRHYTHMIA, pseudoseizures** [E1].
> **CLASSIFY the type of epilepsy from the seizure description, neurological examination, EEG and
> neuroimaging** [E1].
> **IDENTIFY AND AVOID PRECIPITANTS — drugs, SLEEP DEPRIVATION, excess alcohol or alcohol withdrawal**
> [E1].

⚠️ **"Arrhythmia" on that exclusion list is not decorative** — this compendium's `arrhythmias` guideline
records that syncope in paroxysmal AF is often a sinus pause, and that long QT syndromes present as sudden
collapse.

## When to start [E1]

> **START WHEN THE IMPACT OF FURTHER SEIZURES OUTWEIGHS THE RISKS OF TREATMENT** [E1].

| | [E1] |
|---|---|
| **After ONE seizure** | ⚠️ **only 30–50% will have a recurrence** |
| **After TWO seizures** | ⚠️ **about 80% will have recurrent seizures** — treatment usually indicated when **2 or more have occurred within 6–12 months** |

**Factors in deciding after a first seizure:** **symptomatology** (previous seizures may have been
unrecognised) · **signs** — an abnormal EEG or neurological abnormality indicates increased recurrence risk
· **seizure type or syndrome** · **severity** · ⚠️ **AGE — elderly people are at higher risk of recurrence**
· **the person's wishes** [E1].

**LOWEST recurrence: a NORMAL EEG with NO identifiable cause, or a CLEAR AVOIDABLE PRECIPITANT** [E1].

**Exceptions to treating after two seizures: a clear avoidable precipitant, or some seizure types such as
benign childhood epilepsy with centrotemporal spikes** [E1].

## ⚠️ Drug choice — the table [E2]

> **SPECIFIC DIAGNOSIS OF SEIZURE TYPE AND, IF POSSIBLE, EPILEPSY SYNDROME ARE THE MOST IMPORTANT FACTORS
> FOR DRUG SELECTION** [E1]. **The classification is the prescription.**

| Seizure type | First line | Second line |
|---|---|---|
| **Focal (partial)** | **carbamazepine · lamotrigine · levetiracetam** | clobazam, gabapentin, lacosamide, oxcarbazepine, phenobarbital, phenytoin, pregabalin, tiagabine, topiramate, valproate, zonisamide |
| **Generalised tonic-clonic** | **lamotrigine · levetiracetam · valproate** | carbamazepine¹, clobazam, oxcarbazepine¹, phenobarbital, phenytoin¹, topiramate |
| **Absence** | **ethosuximide² · valproate** | clobazam, clonazepam, lamotrigine |
| **Myoclonic** | **valproate** | clobazam, clonazepam, lamotrigine³, levetiracetam³, phenobarbital |
| **Infantile spasms** | **prednisolone · tetracosactide** | vigabatrin⁴, clonazepam, valproate |

> ⚠️ **¹ DO NOT USE CARBAMAZEPINE, OXCARBAZEPINE OR PHENYTOIN IF JUVENILE MYOCLONIC EPILEPSY IS SUSPECTED**
> — which **often presents with a tonic-clonic seizure** — **as they may be INEFFECTIVE OR WORSEN
> SEIZURES** [E2]. **The commonest presentation of JME is the one that invites the wrong drug.**
>
> **² ETHOSUXIMIDE DOES NOT PREVENT GENERALISED TONIC-CLONIC SEIZURES**, which often coexist in juvenile
> absence epilepsy — **do not use it alone in mixed seizure types** [E2].
>
> **³ LAMOTRIGINE AND LEVETIRACETAM MAY BE FIRST LINE IN FEMALES OF CHILD-BEARING POTENTIAL** [E2] — see
> *Epilepsy in females* below, where the reason is quantified.
>
> **⁴ VIGABATRIN: USE ONLY IF NO SAFER ALTERNATIVE** [E2].

### Notes on individual agents [E1]

- ⚠️ **LAMOTRIGINE** appears **as effective as carbamazepine** as monotherapy — **but its use is LIMITED BY
  THE RISK OF SEVERE ADVERSE REACTIONS SUCH AS STEVENS-JOHNSON SYNDROME AND TOXIC EPIDERMAL NECROLYSIS.**
- **OXCARBAZEPINE**, structurally related to carbamazepine, **does NOT autoinduce its metabolism and has
  LESS interaction potential** — an alternative where carbamazepine is not tolerated.
- **Gabapentin, levetiracetam, topiramate**: mainly adjunctive, but may be monotherapy (particularly
  levetiracetam). **Lacosamide, pregabalin, zonisamide**: adjunctive in focal seizures; **brivaracetam**
  also marketed for it. ⚠️ **Tiagabine is rarely used.**
- **Perampanel**: adjunctive for refractory focal or generalised tonic-clonic seizures.
- **Cannabidiol, fenfluramine, rufinamide, stiripentol**: adjunctive in rarer epilepsies (Lennox-Gastaut,
  Dravet).
- ⚠️ **VIGABATRIN MAY CAUSE IRREVERSIBLE VISUAL FIELD DEFECTS IN 20–40% OF PATIENTS.** Use only when all
  other appropriate drugs have failed; **because visual fields are difficult to monitor in young children,
  consider it for infantile spasms only where there is no safer alternative.**
- **ACETAZOLAMIDE and SULTHIAME are approved but evidence is limited and they are NOT IN COMMON USE IN
  AUSTRALIA.**

## Treatment regimens [E1]

> **START WITH ONE FIRST-LINE DRUG ONLY. INCREASE THE DOSE GRADUALLY — ESPECIALLY FOR CARBAMAZEPINE AND
> LAMOTRIGINE.** **ABOUT HALF OF ALL PATIENTS ARE SYMPTOM FREE, with manageable adverse effects, ON THE
> FIRST DRUG TRIED** [E1].

### ⚠️ Before you change the drug, check three things [E1]

| | |
|---|---|
| **1. COMPLIANCE** | ⚠️ **noncompliance is a COMMON reason for treatment failure** — plasma concentration monitoring can help |
| **2. DOSE** | **ensure the first-line drug is at MAXIMAL dose with minimal adverse effects** |
| **3. DIAGNOSIS** | **re-evaluate and confirm it** |

**Then: ADD an alternative first-line or a second-line drug — and WHEN THE OPTIMAL DOSAGE OF THE SECOND IS
ACHIEVED, GRADUALLY DECREASE the first** [E1].

> **EXHAUST ALL REASONABLE OPTIONS FOR MONOTHERAPY before long-term treatment with more than one drug.**
> Evidence for the best combinations is lacking; **a reasonable approach is to combine drugs with DIFFERING
> MECHANISMS OF ACTION** [E1].
>
> ⚠️ **AND THE REVERSE IS ALSO TRUE:** **"In people with uncontrolled seizures taking more than one
> antiseizure drug, SIMPLIFYING THE REGIMEN MAY IMPROVE SEIZURE CONTROL. AN EFFECTIVE DOSE OF ONE DRUG IS
> BETTER THAN INEFFECTIVE DOSES OF TWO OR MORE"** [E1].

### Stopping [E1]

**May be attempted AFTER 2–3 YEARS WITHOUT SEIZURES.** **Good prognostic signs: few seizures, ABSENCE
seizures only, younger age when control was achieved, normal EEG, normal neurological examination, absence
of brain lesions** [E1].

> ⚠️ **DO NOT STOP ABRUPTLY — INCREASED SEIZURES AND STATUS EPILEPTICUS MAY OCCUR.**
> **Reduce OVER SEVERAL WEEKS TO MONTHS — UP TO 6 MONTHS OR LONGER FOR BENZODIAZEPINES OR BARBITURATES.**
> ⚠️ **WITHDRAW ONE DRUG AT A TIME** [E1].

**Other: SURGERY for intractable focal epilepsy; a KETOGENIC DIET under specialist guidance in refractory
epilepsy** [E1].

## ⚠️ Epilepsy in females — the section with the numbers

> **CONSIDER AND DISCUSS THE POSSIBILITY OF PREGNANCY BEFORE SELECTING A SPECIFIC ANTISEIZURE DRUG** — and
> discuss **the risks of unplanned treatment withdrawal** [E1].

### Malformation risk, quantified [E1]

| | [E1] |
|---|---|
| **General population** | **2–3%** |
| **On antiseizure drugs** | ⚠️ **4–6% — approximately 2–3 times higher** |
| ⚠️ **Valproate** | ⚠️ **approximately 10%** |
| **Lamotrigine, levetiracetam** | **approximately 3% — the lowest** |

> **The risk of a birth defect was thought to increase with the NUMBER of drugs taken, but MORE RECENT
> DATA SUGGEST THE RISK IS RELATED TO THE INDIVIDUAL DRUGS AND THEIR DOSES** [E1].
>
> ⚠️ **This is why lamotrigine and levetiracetam are flagged as possibly first line in females of
> child-bearing potential even for myoclonic seizures, where valproate is otherwise the sole first-line
> drug** [E2]. **This compendium's `bipolar-disorder` guideline carries valproate's other figure: delay in
> motor and cognitive development in up to 30–40% of children.**

### ⚠️ Contraception fails on enzyme inducers [E1]

> **CARBAMAZEPINE, PHENYTOIN, OXCARBAZEPINE, BARBITURATES and TOPIRAMATE INDUCE CYP3A4** and may reduce the
> efficacy of **combined oral contraceptives, the combined hormonal vaginal ring, oral progestogen-only
> contraceptives, the etonogestrel implant, and ULIPRISTAL** [E1].
>
> ✅ **USE LEVONORGESTREL IUD, MEDROXYPROGESTERONE DEPOT OR COPPER IUD IF POSSIBLE — unaffected by CYP3A4
> inducers.** **High-dose COCs may be used if these are unsuitable** [E1].
>
> ⚠️ **ULIPRISTAL is emergency contraception.** This compendium's `contraception` guideline covers both.

### Before and during pregnancy [E1]

- **CONSIDER WITHDRAWAL in women planning pregnancy who have been seizure-free FOR AT LEAST 2 YEARS.**
  ⚠️ **STOPPING AFTER THE DIAGNOSIS OF PREGNANCY MAY NOT LOWER THE RISK OF CONGENITAL MALFORMATIONS** — the
  decision has to be made beforehand.
- **FOLIC ACID 5 mg DAILY for at least 1 MONTH BEFORE and 3 MONTHS AFTER conception** — ⚠️ **although AMH
  notes the evidence for efficacy in this population is LIMITED.**
- ⚠️ **PLASMA CONCENTRATIONS MAY FALL DURING PREGNANCY.** **Monitor clinically and with concentrations if
  indicated — PARTICULARLY LAMOTRIGINE — and adjust dosage.**
- ⚠️ **VITAMIN K: hepatic enzyme inducers (carbamazepine, phenytoin, barbiturates, oxcarbazepine,
  topiramate) may cause fetal vitamin K deficiency and neonatal bleeding.** **ENSURE IM VITAMIN K 1 mg IS
  GIVEN TO THE BABY IMMEDIATELY AFTER BIRTH.** Antenatal oral vitamin K 10–20 mg daily from 36 weeks may be
  considered, **though evidence is inadequate for routine use.**

**The AUSTRALIAN PREGNANCY REGISTER collects information on pregnant women with epilepsy — neuroscience.org.au/apr,
1800 069 722** [E1].

## Acute situations [E1]

### Status epilepticus
⚠️ **Protocols vary for adults, children and neonates and differ between institutions — FOLLOW THE LOCAL
PROTOCOL IF AVAILABLE.** AMH's representative sequence [E1]:

1. **Basic life support** — oxygen, maintenance of BP and blood glucose
2. **FAST-ACTING BENZODIAZEPINE** — midazolam, diazepam, clonazepam or lorazepam; **choice largely depends
   on whether IV access is available**
3. **If seizures continue, or to prevent recurrence: LONG-ACTING agent** — IV phenytoin, valproate,
   phenobarbital or levetiracetam
4. ⚠️ **If status persists: ANAESTHETIC doses of thiopental, midazolam or propofol — assisted ventilation
   usually required because of severe respiratory depression risk**

**This compendium's `seizures-acute-management-children` guideline holds the RCH paediatric protocol.**

### Alcohol withdrawal seizures [E1]
**Monitor those at high risk by pulse rate, BP and mental state.** **BENZODIAZEPINES ARE PREFERRED — start
a loading dose regimen at the FIRST SIGNS of withdrawal.** ⚠️ **LARGE DOSES MAY BE REQUIRED because of
increased hepatic metabolism and CNS tolerance.**

### ⚠️ Acute brain insult — what NOT to do [E1]
> **PROPHYLACTIC ANTISEIZURE TREATMENT IS NOT INDICATED.**
> **Drugs used to treat provoked seizures SHOULD BE WITHDRAWN unless unprovoked seizures occur later.**
> ⚠️ **ANTISEIZURE DRUGS ARE NOT INDICATED FOR SEIZURES OCCURRING IMMEDIATELY AFTER A CONCUSSIVE CLOSED
> HEAD INJURY.**

### Simple febrile seizures [E1]
**Brief, generalised, once in 24 hours, fever from a source outside the CNS.** **About 3% of children aged
6 months – 5 years.** **30–50% risk of recurrent febrile seizures, LOW risk of chronic epilepsy, no
documented risk of other adverse outcomes** [E1].

- **Acute, if prolonged: DIAZEPAM (IV or rectal) or MIDAZOLAM (IV, IM, intranasal, buccal).**
- ⚠️ **PREVENTION IS RARELY NECESSARY.** Oral or rectal diazepam at fever onset may be used in children at
  high risk of severe or complicated seizures — **but its adverse effects MAY DELAY DIAGNOSIS OF AN
  UNDERLYING ILLNESS SUCH AS MENINGITIS.**
- ⚠️ **ANTIPYRETICS DO NOT PREVENT FEBRILE SEIZURES** (though they may make the child more comfortable).
- **Long-term prophylaxis with phenobarbital or valproate is effective but RARELY JUSTIFIED**; consider
  only with a history of prolonged seizures or post-ictal hemiparesis.

**See also this compendium's `febrile-seizure` guideline.**

## Concentration monitoring [E1]

> ⚠️ **ROUTINE PLASMA CONCENTRATION MONITORING IS NOT USUALLY USEFUL** [E1].

**It may be used to assess COMPLIANCE, or for some drugs in special situations such as pregnancy or renal
failure.** **For phenytoin, carbamazepine and phenobarbital it may help JUST AFTER A SEIZURE, if
dose-dependent adverse effects occur, AFTER A DOSAGE CHANGE, or if interacting drugs are started or
stopped** [E1].

> ⚠️ **INTERPRET ACCORDING TO CLINICAL CONDITION.** **"Some patients may become seizure free or experience
> dose-dependent adverse effects at SUBTHERAPEUTIC concentrations, while others will require and tolerate
> concentrations BEYOND THE THERAPEUTIC RANGE"** [E1]. **Treat the patient, not the number.**

## Two class-wide harms [E1]

> ⚠️ **SUICIDALITY.** **An FDA analysis of randomised placebo-controlled trials found an association
> between some antiseizure drugs and an INCREASED RISK OF SUICIDAL THOUGHTS AND BEHAVIOUR.** A warning now
> appears in the product information for **most** antiseizure drugs, **including drugs not in the
> analysis.** ⚠️ **AMH: "the association is CONTROVERSIAL and the benefits will usually outweigh the risks
> in epilepsy; the risk–benefit balance MAY BE LESS CERTAIN IN OTHER CONDITIONS"** [E1] — which matters
> because these drugs are widely prescribed for pain and mood.

> ⚠️ **FRACTURES.** **Reduced bone mineral density — PARTICULARLY WITH BARBITURATES, CARBAMAZEPINE,
> PHENYTOIN AND VALPROATE — plus CNS effects that increase falls.** **Consider fall prevention and BMD
> monitoring during long-term treatment, and ensure vitamin D status and calcium intake are adequate** [E1].
