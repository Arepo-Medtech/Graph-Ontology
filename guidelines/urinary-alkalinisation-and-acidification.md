## Alkalinisation

**May relieve the discomfort of UTIs, help treat certain types of renal stones, or be used in specialist
settings to treat some metabolic and renal disorders** [M1].

> ### ⚠️ HARD LIMIT
> **Alkalinisation is not possible if CrCl is below 30 mL/minute** [M1].

| Product | Dose [M1] | Electrolyte content [M1] |
|---|---|---|
| **Sodium bicarbonate + sodium citrate + citric acid + tartaric acid** (sachet) | **1–2 sachets in a glass of water up to 4 times daily**, for **symptomatic relief of UTI** | ⚠️ **approx. 28 mmol (644 mg) sodium** |
| **Sodium bicarbonate 840 mg capsule** | **1–6 capsules daily, dosed according to response** — used **alone as an alkaliniser, eg in renal tubular acidosis** | **10 mmol (230 mg) sodium** |
| **Potassium citrate 1.08 g tablet** | **3–6 tablets daily in 2 or 3 doses, with or after food** — to **prevent calcium or uric acid kidney stones** | **10 mmol (390 mg) potassium** |

> ### ⚠️ THE TWO PRODUCT FAMILIES ARE NOT INTERCHANGEABLE
> **The sodium-containing alkalinisers must be used with caution or avoided in people who require sodium
> restriction.** **The potassium-containing product must be avoided in people with, or at risk of,
> hyperkalaemia** [M1].
>
> At **up to 4 sachets daily**, the sodium load reaches roughly **112 mmol** — worth noting in heart
> failure, hypertension or renal impairment. *(The arithmetic is mine; AMH gives the per-sachet figure
> only. See* Unresolved *#2.)*

## Acidification

**May rarely be used** to increase the excretion of **lipid-soluble basic drugs**, to treat **certain types
of renal stones**, or **in combination with methenamine hippurate** [M1].

**Products used include ammonium chloride and ascorbic acid** [M1].

> ⚠️ **Evidence for efficacy is limited** [M1]. **No dose, no indication detail, and no monitoring
> guidance is given for either product.**
