# Ethanol poisoning in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** risk assessment, blood alcohol interpretation, investigations, supportive care and disposition for ethanol ingestion in children and adolescents. > ⚠️ **Young children get profound hypoglycaemia, coma and hypothermia from small amounts.** **Check the glucose.** A raised blood alcohol level does not rule out head injury or other drugs. ☎️ **Poisons Information Centre 13 11 26.**

## Key points

- ⚠️ **Confirm it is ethanol, not a toxic alcohol** — methanol and ethylene glycol cause severe toxicity in small doses. [S1]
- **Exploratory ethanol ingestion in children under 6 usually causes minor symptoms**, but ⚠️ **infants and young children are prone to profound hypoglycaemia, coma and hypothermia** even after small amounts. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Even with a raised blood alcohol, always consider more dangerous causes of a decreased conscious state**, including head injury and other drugs. [S1]

## Sources and blood alcohol

- **Household products can be highly concentrated:** perfumes and colognes over 60% · some hand sanitisers 60–70% · aftershaves 80% · some mouthwashes 25% · methylated spirit 95% (no methanol in Australian preparations). Spirits are 45%. [S1]
- **Units: a BAC of 0.05% = 0.05 g/100 mL = 11 mmol/L.** Peak BAC is at about 90 minutes; it usually falls by 0.01–0.02% per hour. [S1]
- **BAC effects in non-tolerant adults:** 0.02–0.05% reduced inhibition · 0.05–0.10% impaired judgement · 0.10–0.15% gait and balance difficulty · 0.15–0.25% lethargy · ⚠️ **0.30% coma · 0.40% respiratory depression.** [S1]

## History and examination

- **Ask about:** amount and type of alcohol · other drugs · **trauma, especially head injury** · progression of neurological depression suggesting intracranial pathology · cold exposure · drink spiking (eg GHB) · ⚠️ **possible sexual activity or assault** needing emergency contraception or forensic evaluation. [S1]
- **Hypotension and tachycardia** can come from vasodilation or volume loss (eg vomiting); **hypothermia** from vasodilation or cold; **hypoglycaemia** can lower the conscious state. [S1]

## Investigations

- **Use a lower threshold to investigate infants and young children** — their signs of intoxication are harder to read. **Mild symptoms only:** consider BAC and blood glucose. [S1]
- **More than mild, or not improving after two hours:** blood glucose, U&E, acid–base, ECG if polydrug overdose is possible, and **CT brain if the conscious state is decreased or deteriorating.** [S1]
- **BAC should ideally be done in everyone admitted.** After a motor vehicle accident, state law may require a police-sample BAC. [S1]

## Management

- **Care is supportive:** hypoglycaemia, hypovolaemia, hypothermia, decreased conscious state (⚠️ **it may not be the alcohol — consider trauma, polydrug ingestion, DKA**), respiratory depression and co-morbidities. [S1]
- **Enhanced elimination is ineffective** (ethanol is absorbed rapidly). **There is no antidote.** [S1]

## Disposition

- **Involve the paediatric team** for ongoing medical management and monitoring, or **child protection and safety concerns.** **Transfer** for significantly decreased or non-improving conscious state, or need for respiratory support. [S1]
- **Discharge when** blood glucose, temperature and investigations are normal and conscious level is normal. Adolescents with deliberate ingestion need a self-harm risk assessment and drug and alcohol assessment first. [S1]
