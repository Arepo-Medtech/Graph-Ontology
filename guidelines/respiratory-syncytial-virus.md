# Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV)

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** RSV prevention — maternal vaccination, infant monoclonal antibody, older-adult vaccination — and recognition.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 6 of 6 claims quoted verbatim from the **Australian Immunisation Handbook**; **8 fragments re-checked by machine. 4 quoted doses.**

> ⚠️ **This source disagrees with AMH on pregnancy timing.** AMH: *"between 28 and 36 weeks"*. The Handbook: **"from 28 weeks"** — no 36-week ceiling. See *Unresolved* #1.

> ✅ **The Handbook covers infant monoclonal antibody (nirsevimab); the AMH-derived [`immunisation`](immunisation.md) guideline does not mention it.**

---

## Pregnancy

- ⚠️ **A single dose of Abrysvo in each pregnancy from 28 weeks gestation** — the first opportunity to protect the infant. **Funded under the NIP for pregnant women.** [S1]

## Infants — passive immunisation

- ⚠️ **Infants under 8 months whose mothers were NOT vaccinated at least 2 weeks before birth, or who are at increased risk of severe disease, are recommended a long-acting RSV monoclonal antibody.** [S1]
- **Nirsevimab before the first RSV season** for infants whose mothers weren't vaccinated ≥2 weeks before birth, where maternal protection may be reduced, with medical risk factors, or who need extra protection. It **can be given year-round** — timing at birth versus pre-season is **weighed on individual risk and local seasonality**. [S1]
- **Monoclonal antibodies are funded by states and territories** for certain infants and young children. [S1]

## Older adults

- **Arexvy is NIP-funded for all adults 75 and over, and Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander adults 60 and over.** [S1]

## Recognition

- **Primary infection, often at 0–2 years, is generally more severe than later infections.** It starts as an upper respiratory infection — **congestion, cough, sore throat, fever, otitis media** — and may progress to lower respiratory tract infection. [S1]
