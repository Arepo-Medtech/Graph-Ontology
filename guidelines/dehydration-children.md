## Assess severity

- **Serial weights are the best measure of acute change in fluid status**; clinical signs are often imprecise. [S1]
- **Most accurate: pre-morbid weight (within 2 weeks) minus current weight** — a 10 kg child now 9.5 kg has a 500 mL deficit and is 5% dehydrated. Without a recent weight, estimate from history and examination. [S1]
- **Severity bands:** mild under 5% · moderate 5–9% (signs mildly to moderately abnormal) · severe 10% or more (signs markedly abnormal). [S1]
- **Mild dehydration has no clinical signs** — perhaps thirst or reduced urine output. More numerous or pronounced signs mean greater severity. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Shock** = one or more of reduced conscious state, tachycardia, tachypnoea, hypotension, weak peripheral pulses, mottled/cold peripheries, acidosis. **If in doubt, manage as severe dehydration.** [S1]
- **Higher risk of severe dehydration and electrolyte disturbance:** infants under 6 months, GI pathology (short gut, stomas, Hirschsprung), cystic fibrosis, renal impairment, diuretics, metabolic disorders. [S1]
- **Dehydration is especially dangerous** with complex/cyanotic congenital heart disease (especially shunts), slow weight gain, post organ transplant, or nephrotoxic medications. [S1]
- **Ask about hypertonic or hypotonic fluids** — diluted formula, soft drinks, water only, fortified feeds. [S1]

## Investigate

- **Mild–moderate dehydration with a clear explanation usually needs no investigations.** [S1]
- **Check electrolytes and BGL** in infants under 6 months with poor feeding or large losses, anyone needing IV fluids, severe dehydration, signs of electrolyte disturbance (hypertonia, hyperreflexia, convulsions, jitteriness, altered consciousness, irregular heart rate, doughy skin turgor), predisposing conditions, or hyper/hypotonic fluid intake. [S1]

## Treat

- ⚠️ **In shock, give prompt IV fluid boluses. Consider sepsis.** [S1]
- **Mild or moderate dehydration: enteral (oral or NG) rehydration is preferable.** [S1]
- **IV rehydration is required** for severe dehydration or when enteral rehydration is not tolerated. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Rehydrate rapidly in most gastroenteritis, but more slowly** in respiratory infection, DKA, meningitis and electrolyte disturbance including hypernatraemia. [S1]
- **Fluid management is set by the degree of dehydration, maintenance requirements and ongoing losses.** [S1]

## Disposition

- **Involve the paediatric team** for shock, electrolyte disturbance, or predisposing factors for severe or complicated dehydration. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Consider transfer if shock persists after a maximum of 40 mL/kg in boluses** — and look for another cause such as sepsis — or with severe electrolyte derangement. [S1]
- **Discharge** mild dehydration without a serious cause, with advice to continue rehydrating at home. **Consider review within 48 hours for young infants.** [S1]
