# Intranasal fentanyl in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** indications, contraindications, dosing, administration, monitoring, opioid-toxicity rescue and discharge for intranasal fentanyl in children aged 12 months and older. > ⚠️ **Not under 12 months.** The child must be awake or easily roused (UMSS 0–1) before each dose; have naloxone ready.

## When to use it

- **Intranasal fentanyl is a safe, non-invasive, effective analgesic for moderate to severe pain**, used alongside non-drug and other drug pain management. [S1]
- **Onset within 5 minutes; lasts about 30–60 minutes.** Prescribe in consultation with a senior clinician. [S1]
- **Indication: initial analgesia for any child aged 12 months and older in moderate to severe pain without IV access** — fractures, dislocations, amputations, burns, significant lacerations, painful procedures under nitrous oxide, sickle cell crisis, surgical pain. [S1]
- **Do not give it in isolation.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Contraindications:** age under 12 months · known fentanyl hypersensitivity · altered conscious state · both nasal passages occluded · active epistaxis. [S1]
- **With nitrous oxide:** give fentanyl **5 minutes before** starting nitrous oxide sedation; the combination **increases the risk of vomiting**. [S1]

## Before and during

- **Document a baseline pain score, sedation score and set of observations** before giving it. [S1]
- **UMSS:** 0 awake and alert · 1 minimally sedated · 2 moderately sedated, easily roused by light touch or simple command · 3 deeply sedated, rousable only by significant physical stimulation · 4 unrousable. [S1]
- ⚠️ **The child must be awake or easily roused to voice (UMSS 0–1) before each dose.** Monitor heart rate, respiratory rate, oxygen saturation, pain and sedation scores. [S1]
- **If sedated or vital signs are abnormal**, tell the treating clinician and continue observations until back to baseline. **After the last dose, do two further sets of observations 5 minutes apart.** [S1]

## Dose and technique

- **First dose: 1.5 microg/kg, max 100 microg**, using the 300 microg/mL nasal solution. **Use ideal body weight for overweight children.** [S1]
- **The usual maximum is 75 micrograms** with the 100 microg/2 mL concentration (volume limits); **100 micrograms** only with the hospital-only 300 microg/mL solution. [S1]
- **A second dose may be given 5–10 minutes after the first: 0.75–1.5 microg/kg, max 75–100 microg.** After it, review and consider IV access for further analgesia. [S1]
- **Draw up the dose plus 0.1 mL for the first dose** to prime the atomiser dead space; **not for the second dose** when re-using the device. [S1]
- **Sit the child at about 45 degrees** (or head to one side), **press the plunger quickly** so it atomises rather than drips, ask for only a gentle sniff, and **divide the dose between nostrils**. [S1]

## Side effects and toxicity

- **Side effects are uncommon:** sedation, respiratory depression, hypotension, nausea and vomiting (more with nitrous oxide), pruritus. Chest wall rigidity is reported only with large IV doses. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Opioid toxicity:** immediate senior review and move to resuscitation · stop further opioid · support airway and oxygenate · assist ventilation as required · give naloxone. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Naloxone:** **excessive sedation (UMSS 3): 1–5 microg/kg, max 100 microg, IM or IV**, repeated every 2–3 minutes if needed · **acute overdose (UMSS 4): 10 microg/kg, max 400 microg** · **no response to 10 microg/kg: 100 microg/kg, max 2 mg**. [S1]

## Discharge

- **Consider discharge when sedation score and observations are back to baseline** and the pain score is minimal and manageable with simple analgesics. [S1]
