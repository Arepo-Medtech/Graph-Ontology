# von Willebrand disease in children: acute bleeding

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** assessment and emergency treatment of bleeding in children with von Willebrand disease (VWD): tranexamic acid, desmopressin and VWF/FVIII concentrate (Biostate). For haemophilia see `haemophilia-children`; heavy menstrual bleeding in adolescents overlaps `heavy-menstrual-bleeding`.

## Key points and background

- ⚠️ **Discuss every child with VWD and bleeding with the paediatric haematology team**; plan treatment with a haematologist **before any procedure**. [S1]
- **VWD is the most common inherited bleeding disorder**, affecting males and females equally: easy bruising, mucosal bleeding (epistaxis, oral mucosa, menorrhagia) and post-operative bleeding. [S1]
- **Type 1 (common):** reduced VWF, mild bleeding. **Type 2 (uncommon):** abnormal VWF, variable bleeding. **Type 3 (rare):** near-absent VWF — may bleed like moderate–severe haemophilia, into joints and muscles. [S1]

## Assess and investigate

- ⚠️ **Major haemorrhage, head injury or any suspected internal bleeding: give clotting factor immediately**, before a full assessment is complete. [S1]
- **Establish** the site and extent of bleeding, the VWD type and severity, and any home treatment (e.g. tranexamic acid). [S1]
- **Routine coagulation studies are not required.** Consider FBE and ferritin after recurrent mucosal bleeding — anaemia and iron deficiency are common. [S1]

## Tranexamic acid

- **Tranexamic acid treats and prevents recurrence of mouth bleeds and epistaxis in all severities of VWD**, alone or with desmopressin or factor concentrate; often helpful for heavy menstrual bleeding. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Tranexamic acid is contraindicated for haematuria.** [S1]
- **Dose: tranexamic acid 25 mg/kg/dose (max 1.5 g/dose) orally three times daily for 5–10 days**, depending on severity. [S1]

## Desmopressin (DDAVP)

- **Desmopressin is for mild–moderate Type 1 VWD with a documented safe and satisfactory desmopressin challenge.** Occasionally effective in Type 2, **never in Type 3**, and **not adequate alone for major bleeding.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Generally not used under 3 years or with seizure disorders** — it can cause hyponatraemia and seizures. **Fluid-restrict to about 2/3 of maintenance for 24 hours afterwards.** [S1]
- **Dose: desmopressin 0.3 microgram/kg (max 20 micrograms) once, IV or subcut.** IV: dilute to 50 mL with 0.9% sodium chloride and infuse over at least 30 minutes. Subcut: press on the site for 1–2 minutes. [S1]

## Factor concentrate (Biostate)

- ⚠️ **Give clotting factor replacement immediately for severe or life-threatening bleeding.** Biostate (plasma-derived VWF/FVIII) may be needed in Type 1 with severe bleeding or DDAVP failure, and treats bleeding in Types 2 and 3. **Recombinant FVIII contains no VWF.** [S1]
- **Prescribe Biostate in Factor VIII units**, rounded to the closest vial size. [S1]
- **Biostate dose by bleed:** oral mucosa/epistaxis/menorrhagia 25 FVIII units/kg (60 VWF units/kg) · GI bleed 40 FVIII units/kg (96 VWF units/kg) · joint/muscle 40 FVIII units/kg (96 VWF units/kg). [S1]
- ⚠️ **CNS bleed, trauma or surgery: Biostate 60 FVIII units/kg (144 VWF units/kg).** [S1]

## Analgesia and disposition

- ⚠️ **No aspirin or NSAIDs** (e.g. ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac) — they may worsen bleeding. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Consider transfer for:** suspected intracranial haemorrhage · neck or throat bleeding · forearm or calf bleed risking compartment syndrome · hip or inguinal bleeding (iliopsoas risk) · undiagnosed abdominal pain · persistent haematuria · severe pain · **ongoing mucosal bleeding.** [S1]
- **Discharge when** there is no active bleeding, follow-up is arranged and the family has a management plan (e.g. Haemophilia Treatment Centre Card). [S1]
