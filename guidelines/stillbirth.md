# Stillbirth — prevention, investigation and care after loss

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-22 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** pre-conception, antenatal, intrapartum and bereavement care.

> **Australian primary source.** Australian Commission on Safety and Quality in Health Care (ACSQHC).
> ***Stillbirth Clinical Care Standard*** [S1]. **Ten quality statements** — the largest standard in this
> compendium.

**This opens obstetrics**, a domain that had appeared in this set only inside other guidelines — pregnancy in
the STI set, NRT in pregnancy, alcohol, iron.

## The arc

Ten statements spanning **before pregnancy → during pregnancy → the loss → the investigation → the
bereavement → the next pregnancy**. No other standard here covers a whole life event end to end, including
what happens when the care does not succeed.

## ⚠️ One question opens the whole pathway

> **Many women may not actively seek pre-conception care, even for a planned pregnancy. When providing care for
> women of reproductive age, consider opportunities to assess intentions about pregnancy. For example, the use
> of a simple question such as **'Would you like to become pregnant in the next year?'** can encourage
> pre-conception care discussions** *(Quality statement 1)* [S1].

**A single sentence, offered opportunistically, is the entry point to statement 1.** If she says yes:
**discuss the importance of pre-conception health, and provide information and support, to optimise her health
in preparation for pregnancy** [S1].

## ⚠️ Telling her stillbirth is possible

> **Early in pregnancy, a woman is informed about stillbirth as a potential outcome** *(Quality statement
> 3)* [S1].

> **Using careful and sensitive language, inform the woman about stillbirth as a possible pregnancy outcome.
> Explain that, although not every stillbirth can be prevented, there are strategies that may reduce the risk
> of stillbirth, including late-gestation losses (that is, after 28 weeks gestation)** [S1].

**A national standard requiring a clinician to raise stillbirth with a pregnant woman.** It is not left to
judgement or to the woman to ask — and the framing is prescribed: **not preventable in every case, but there
are things that help.**

### The three strategies, and all three are audited

**Smoking cessation, using a side going-to-sleep position from 28 weeks gestation, and being aware of fetal
movements** [S1].

| Indicator [S1] | |
|---|---|
| **3a** | **Proportion of pregnant women who reported smoking who were provided with advice on smoking cessation, offered a referral to a smoking cessation service (for example, Quitline), and/or nicotine replacement therapy if clinically indicated** |
| **3b** | **Proportion of women who gave birth who demonstrated their understanding of safe maternal going-to-sleep position at an antenatal visit from 28 weeks gestation** |
| **3c** | **Proportion of women who gave birth who reported from 28 weeks gestation that they knew how to monitor their fetal movements and what to do if they were concerned about a change** |

**Indicator 3a lands directly on the smoking cessation guideline in this compendium**, whose **recommendation
9** covers NRT for pregnant women unable to quit with behavioural support alone, and **recommendation 16**
telephone call-back counselling. This standard makes that recommendation an audited obligation in antenatal
care.

**Indicators 3b and 3c measure the woman's understanding, not the clinician's advice** — *demonstrated* and
*reported*, not *told*.

## ⚠️ Fetal movements — and the folk advice that is wrong

**Quality statement 5** [S1]: a woman who contacts her clinician or service with concerns about **a change in
the frequency, strength or pattern of her baby's movements** is offered **timely assessment and care** per the
**Decreased Fetal Movement Care Pathway** (Centre of Research Excellence in Stillbirth and PSANZ), or a
locally approved alternative.

> **Advice to attempt to stimulate the baby's movements (for example, by having something to eat or drink) is
> not correct** [S1].

**A named, common, well-intentioned piece of advice, explicitly corrected.** The cold drink and the sugary
snack delay presentation.

> **Note that a woman's concern about fetal movements is an important indicator, and should always be taken
> seriously** [S1].

- **Support the woman to become familiar with her fetal movements**, and tell her to **seek clinical advice
  immediately** if she notices any change — **particularly decreases** [S1].
- > **Emphasise that she should seek assistance without delay and that it is best to contact a clinician about
  > any concerns, even if subsequent assessment confirms the baby's health and wellbeing** [S1].
- **Provide clear advice on how and where to present for further evaluation, including contact phone
  numbers** [S1].
- **Consider options that will allow appropriate and timely assessment, especially for women in rural and
  remote areas who may have limited access to care. Discuss these options with the woman in advance** [S1].
- **When a woman presents with concerns, discuss her concerns and immediately assess the presence of fetal
  heart tones**; **provide or arrange urgent clinical review if fetal heart tones are** absent or
  abnormal [S1].
- **All women should be offered access to professional interpreting services, when required** [S1].

### The indicator that measures the patient's delay

> **Indicator 5b — Median time from when women report that they first noticed a change in fetal movements to
> when they contacted a clinician or healthcare service** [S1].

**This measures the woman's delay, not the service's.** In forty-nine guidelines it is the only indicator whose
subject is how long the patient waited before making contact — which makes it a measure of **health literacy,
confidence to present, and access**, and puts the responsibility for shortening it on the antenatal education
that statement 3 requires.

**Indicator 5a** covers the other half: **evidence of local arrangements to enable timely assessment and
appropriate care for women presenting with concerns** [S1].

## Risk assessment during pregnancy

**Quality statement 2** [S1]: risk factors **identified early, monitored and managed with evidence-based care
throughout her pregnancy**, with **the most appropriate available model of maternity care for her clinical,
personal and cultural needs**.

- **Assess the likelihood of risk factors for stillbirth as early as possible, and at each subsequent antenatal
  visit** [S1].
- > **Fetal growth restriction is a key contributor to stillbirth. Assess the risk of fetal growth restriction
  > as early as possible, and at each subsequent antenatal visit.** Provide care as described in the **Fetal
  > Growth Restriction (FGR) Care Pathway** [S1].

**Indicator 2a**: **proportion of women assessed for clinical risk factors for fetal growth restriction at
their first antenatal appointment at the hospital, with the outcomes documented** [S1].

## Ultrasound

**Quality statement 4** [S1]: **high-quality ultrasound to assess fetal growth and morphology, and identify
stillbirth risks**, with **performance, reporting and communication of outcomes in line with current
best-practice guidelines**.

> **Optimally timed, high-quality ultrasound can help identify factors that may affect a woman's stillbirth
> risk, including the presence of a multiple pregnancy, chorionicity, the likelihood of fetal or chromosomal
> anomalies, and fetal growth restriction** [S1].

**Discuss the recommended obstetric ultrasounds with the woman, including the reasons for them, what they may
reveal about her or her baby's health, and at what stage** they should occur [S1].

## ⚠️ Not before 39 weeks unless indicated

**Quality statement 6** [S1]: information enabling **informed decisions about timing of birth**, in line with
her individual risks and preferences. **Whenever a planned birth is being considered, including when there are
concerns about maternal or fetal health, the potential benefits and harms are discussed with the woman and
documented appropriately.**

> **While spontaneous onset of labour is generally preferred, a planned birth through induction or caesarean
> section may be considered if there are concerns about maternal or fetal health, including the risk of
> stillbirth. **Unless clinically indicated (that is, there is an identified increased risk to the health or
> wellbeing of the mother or fetus), birth should not be planned before 39 weeks gestation**** [S1].

**Three indicators sit on this one statement** [S1]:

| | |
|---|---|
| **6a** | **Proportion of women who had a planned birth who received written and verbal information on the potential benefits and harms of planned birth, including the timing of intervention** |
| **6b** | **Proportion of women who had a planned birth before 39 weeks gestation whose medical record documents the reason(s) for intervention** |
| **6c** | **Proportion of women with singleton pregnancies who had a planned birth before 39 weeks gestation** |

**6c measures the rate itself** — the same structural device the heavy menstrual bleeding standard uses for
hysterectomy: **count how often the intervention happens**, not only whether it was justified.

## ⚠️ The parents' account becomes part of the investigation

**Quality statement 7** [S1]:

> When a stillbirth is diagnosed, **the availability, timing and anticipated value of clinical investigations,
> including autopsy, are discussed with the parents. The parents are supported to share their views about
> factors they perceive may have contributed to the stillbirth, **including aspects of the woman's clinical
> care**. This information is documented and considered alongside the agreed clinical investigations, and as
> part of local perinatal mortality audit or incident investigation processes.**

**The parents' view of the care they received is entered into the formal mortality audit.** Nothing else in
this compendium gives a patient's account of the clinical care a standing place inside the investigation of an
adverse outcome.

**Recognise that this discussion is likely to occur at an acute time of loss and emotional distress for the
parents and their support people** [S1].

## Results, and the audit

**Quality statement 8** [S1]: results **reported in a timely manner, documented appropriately and discussed
with the parents, along with any information they have provided about perceived contributing factors**. The
stillbirth is **reviewed as part of a local perinatal mortality audit process, classified according to the
Perinatal Society of Australia and New Zealand classification system, and outcomes are used to inform local
improvements in care**.

- **Arrange a time with the parents to discuss clinical investigation results as soon as these are
  available** [S1].
- > **Inform the parents that the results of some investigations may become available before others, and that
  > more than one meeting may be required to discuss all results** [S1].
- **The discussion should be led by a senior clinician** [S1].

**Indicator 8a**: **proportion of stillbirths reviewed by the healthcare service for potential contributing
factors and classified according to the PSANZ classification system** [S1].

## Bereavement care

**Quality statement 9** [S1]:

> After a perinatal loss, **parents and their support people are provided with compassionate, respectful and
> culturally safe bereavement care that recognises their specific needs and preferences, and ensures that
> follow-up support is available after discharge.**

> **The death of a baby is a traumatic experience for parents, their families and support people, **and the
> clinicians involved in their care**. This is regardless of when the death occurs or the reasons for the
> loss** [S1].

**The clinicians are named among those affected** — in the statement's own reasoning, not a wellbeing
appendix.

Care is to be **in line with the recommendations in the *Clinical Practice Guideline for Care Around
Stillbirth and Neonatal Death* and *Sands Australian Principles*** [S1].

> **Indicator 7a — Proportion of clinicians who provide bereavement care to parents who have experienced a
> stillbirth who have completed an evidence-based bereavement care professional development program** [S1].

**An indicator measuring clinician training rather than patient care.** Bereavement care is treated as a
skill that must be taught, not a disposition that can be assumed.

**Indicator 9a**: **evidence of local arrangements to support the provision of bereavement** care [S1].

## The next pregnancy

**Quality statement 10** [S1]: antenatal care that **recognises factors that may have contributed to the
previous loss**, and ensures **access to appropriate clinical expertise and psychosocial support, as
required**.

- **Women who have experienced a perinatal loss may be at increased risk of adverse outcomes during a
  subsequent pregnancy** [S1].
- **For women planning a pregnancy after a perinatal loss, provide pre-conception care as described in Quality
  statement 1** [S1] — the standard loops back to its own first statement.
- **Provide sensitive and informed antenatal care, delivered by an experienced clinician or multidisciplinary
  team.** **The benefits of continuity of carer should be considered and discussed with the parents** [S1].

## Where this connects in the compendium

| Guideline here | Connection |
|---|---|
| **Smoking cessation** | **indicator 3a** requires advice, a Quitline-type referral **and/or NRT** for pregnant women who smoke — operationalising that guideline's recommendation 9 |
| **Alcohol problems** | **no safe level of alcohol in pregnancy**; alcohol-dependent pregnant women admitted to a maternity unit with an addiction specialist |
| **Iron deficiency** | iron deficiency **should be corrected before and during pregnancy** to protect the child's neurocognitive function |
| **Syphilis, hepatitis B, HIV, genital herpes** | vertical transmission and antenatal screening |
| **Heavy menstrual bleeding** | the same structural device — **an indicator counting how often the intervention occurs** |
| **Sepsis** | fetal vital signs by CTG are in its observation set |
