# Hyponatraemia in children

## Recognise it

- **Normal sodium is 135–145 mmol/L.** Hyponatraemia usually comes from excess water intake or impaired free water excretion; acute hyponatraemia can cause cerebral oedema. [S1]
- **Severity:** mild 125–135 (mostly asymptomatic) · moderate 120–125 (nausea, malaise) · **severe under 120 mmol/L (headache, obtundation, seizures).** [S1]
- **Chronic hyponatraemia (over 48 hours)** is subtler — restlessness, weakness, fatigue, irritability. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Speed of onset predicts neurological risk better than the sodium level.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Red flags:** nausea and vomiting, irritability, headache, decreased conscious state, seizures. [S1]

## Causes

- **Fluid status is key** to both the cause and the treatment. [S1]
- **SIADH causes:** pulmonary (pneumonia, bronchiolitis, ventilation), CNS (infection, injury, tumour), post-operative, trauma, pain, and endocrine (hypothyroidism, low cortisol). [S1]
- **Medication causes listed:** chemotherapy (cyclophosphamide, vincristine, platinum agents), antiepileptics (valproate, carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine), vasopressin. [S1]
- **Exclude pseudo-hyponatraemia** — e.g. high glucose, mannitol, sorbitol. **Corrected Na in hyperglycaemia = serum Na + (BSL − 5)/3.** [S1]

## Fluids

- **Isotonic fluids** (sodium 125–160 mmol/L): 0.9% sodium chloride, Plasma-Lyte 148, Hartmann's. **Hypotonic fluids** (sodium under 125 mmol/L): e.g. oral rehydration solution. [S1]

## Investigate

- **If Na under 130 mmol/L:** paired serum and urine osmolality, urinary sodium, BSL; blood gas if significantly unwell. [S1]
- **History:** fluid intake and losses, the underlying cause (infection, malignancy, trauma), SIADH-causing medications, neurological status. **Examine** fluid status, weight and signs of cerebral oedema. [S1]

## Treat

- ⚠️ **Hyponatraemic seizures are a medical emergency and may not respond to anticonvulsants — do not delay sodium correction.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Target correction 6–8 mmol/L in 24 hours; do not exceed 8 mmol/L in 24 hours in a child who is not seizing.** Rapid correction risks osmotic demyelination. [S1]
- **Management depends on seizures/altered conscious state and fluid status.** Strict fluid balance with weights for every child; treat the underlying cause. [S1]

## Escalation and discharge

- **Involve the paediatric team** if Na under 130 mmol/L or symptomatic, correction has exceeded 8 mmol/L in 24 hours, or fluid needs are complex (e.g. PN). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Consider transfer** if Na under 125 mmol/L or there are CNS symptoms, including seizures or altered conscious state. [S1]
- **Discharge** once the cause is identified and adequately treated. [S1]
