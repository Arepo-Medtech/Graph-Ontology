# Gastrointestinal decontamination in poisoning

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-22 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation

indicated, and who to ring. ⚠️ **Specific antidotes, toxidromes, emergency department protocols and
toxin-specific management are OUT OF SCOPE.** Only one antidote is named here, as an example, with no dose.

## ⚠️ What has been abandoned

> **Gastric lavage is INFREQUENTLY USED and INDUCTION OF EMESIS — for example with ipecacuanha — IS NO
> LONGER ADVISED** [G1].
>
> ⚠️ **IPECACUANHA is not as effective as, and is MORE TOXIC THAN, activated charcoal, and should not be
> used if activated charcoal is available** [G1].
>
> ⚠️ **HYPEROSMOLAR CATHARTICS SUCH AS SORBITOL ARE NO LONGER USED** [G1]. **Sorbitol provides no
> additional benefit to charcoal in most poisonings and may cause diarrhoea and volume depletion** [G2].
>
> ⚠️ **DO NOT USE CHARCOAL TABLETS OR CAPSULES FOR ACUTE POISONING — THEY ARE INEFFECTIVE** [G1][G2].
> **AMH says this on both pages.**
>
> ⚠️ **The use of charcoal for GI disturbances such as diarrhoea is NOT recommended** [G2].

**Gastric aspiration and lavage are RARELY INDICATED in a conscious patient and are NOT INDICATED IF MORE
THAN 4 HOURS have elapsed since poisoning** [G1].

---

## ⚠️ The time window

> **GI decontamination is RARELY INDICATED IF MORE THAN 2 HOURS HAVE ELAPSED since poisoning occurred** —
> ⚠️ **EXCEPT where a significant overdose of a CONTROLLED RELEASE medication has been taken, for example
> CALCIUM CHANNEL BLOCKERS or MODIFIED RELEASE PARACETAMOL** [G1].

⚠️ **The controlled-release exception is the clinically dangerous one**: this compendium's `angina` and
`hypertension` guidelines both prescribe controlled-release calcium channel blockers, and modified-release
paracetamol is over the counter.

**Consider decontamination if the ingestion is associated with significant risk of toxicity** [G1].

---

## ⚠️ Consent, and the duty of care

> **DECONTAMINATION MAY PROCEED IN INTOXICATED OR SUICIDAL PATIENTS WHEN MEDICALLY INDICATED — IMMINENT
> RISK OF DEATH OR SERIOUS HARM — WITHOUT THEIR PERMISSION, as there is a duty of care to those
> patients** [G1].

> **REASSURE THE PATIENT THAT THE PURPOSE OF TREATMENT IS THERAPEUTIC RATHER THAN PUNITIVE**, especially in
> deliberate self-poisoning. **Explain the concerns about potential toxicity and the need for
> decontamination** [G1].

⚠️ **AMH states the therapeutic-not-punitive framing before it states the consent override.** Both belong
in the same conversation.

## Activated charcoal

**Binds drugs and poisons, reducing absorption either by binding the substance before absorption in the GI
tract or by interrupting enterohepatic recirculation** [G2].

> **ACTIVATED CHARCOAL IS THE MOST EFFECTIVE DECONTAMINANT FOR THE MAJORITY OF POISONINGS**, and is
> **as effective as, or superior to, induced emesis or gastric lavage** [G1].

### ⚠️ What it does NOT bind [G1][G2]

> **ALCOHOLS** (ethanol, ethylene glycol, methanol) · **STRONG ACIDS OR ALKALIS** · **POTASSIUM CHLORIDE**
> · **METALS — GOLD, LITHIUM, IRON**

### Dose [G2]

| | Dose |
|---|---|
| **Adult** | **50 g SINGLE DOSE**, oral / nasogastric / orogastric |
| **Child** | **1 g/kg to a MAXIMUM of 50 g**, single dose |
| **Repeat doses** | **25 g every 2–4 hours for UP TO 3 DOSES** may be appropriate in some poisonings — **seek specialist advice or review** |

**Products: 50 g in 250 mL oral liquid — Carbosorb X in 46% sucrose, Carbosorb XS in 40% sorbitol** [G2].
**Dilute Carbosorb products with water before use via a nasal or orogastric tube** [G2].

### Getting it into the patient [G1][G2]

> **Give it ORALLY if the patient is conscious.** ⚠️ **It is tolerated by MORE THAN 90% OF PATIENTS and is
> less unpleasant than nasogastric or orogastric administration** [G1].

**To encourage the patient to drink it** [G2]:
- **reassure them that decontamination is indicated**
- **improve palatability by CHILLING it**
- **serve it in a COVERED CONTAINER WITH A LARGE STRAW**, or have them **drink it WITH THEIR EYES SHUT**
- **encourage them to drink the dose within a time limit, e.g. 20 MINUTES**

**The nasogastric or orogastric route may also be used in CONSCIOUS patients who refuse, or cannot take,
oral charcoal** [G2].

**Contraindicated in bowel obstruction.** **With laxatives such as sorbitol or mannitol it can cause volume
depletion and electrolyte abnormalities.** **Safe in pregnancy and breastfeeding** [G2].
**Common adverse effects: colicky abdominal pain (especially with laxatives), nausea and vomiting,
constipation. Rare: bowel obstruction and ASPIRATION** [G2].

### ⚠️ Repeat-dose charcoal: a much narrower claim

> **Repeat-dose activated charcoal can increase the elimination of many drugs** — by interrupting
> enterohepatic recirculation, or by direct dialysis of drug from the gut microvasculature into the
> intestinal lumen [G1].
>
> ⚠️ **BUT IT HAS BEEN SHOWN TO PRODUCE A CLINICALLY RELEVANT BENEFIT FOR ONLY A FEW DRUGS:**
> **CARBAMAZEPINE · ASPIRIN · PHENOBARBITAL · DIGOXIN · QUININE · THEOPHYLLINE · VERAPAMIL** [G1].

**It may also benefit drugs that form CONCRETIONS in the stomach — carbamazepine, aspirin, controlled
release products — or drugs that DELAY STOMACH EMPTYING, such as those with anticholinergic effects
including TCAs and phenothiazines** [G1].

> ⚠️ **TAKE CARE WITH REPEAT-DOSE CHARCOAL AFTER ANTICHOLINERGIC INGESTION: REDUCED PERISTALSIS OR ILEUS
> CAN OCCUR.** ⚠️ **CHECK BOWEL SOUNDS BEFORE GIVING A REPEAT DOSE** [G1].
>
> **Note the overlap: the anticholinergic drugs that make repeat dosing worth considering are the same
> ones that make it hazardous.**

## Whole bowel irrigation

**May be used for compounds NOT adsorbed onto activated charcoal — such as metals — and for poisonings
involving CONTROLLED RELEASE products** [G1]. **AMH's accepted indication names controlled release
products, IRON, LITHIUM and POTASSIUM** [G3] — ⚠️ **the same list charcoal does not bind.**

**An ISO-OSMOLAR solution — not dependent on fluid shifts across the intestine — containing electrolytes
and macrogol 3350, producing diarrhoea to decontaminate the gut** [G1][G3].

> ⚠️ **THE TREATMENT ENDPOINT IS PASSAGE OF CLEAR RECTAL EFFLUENT** [G1].

| Dose [G3] | |
|---|---|
| **Adult** | **15 mL/kg/hour oral or nasogastric; IF TOLERATED, INCREASE TO 25 mL/kg/hour**, continuing until the rectal effluent is clear |
| **Child** | **30 mL/kg/hour for 4–8 hours, or until the rectal effluent is clear** |
| **For scale** | **the typical dose for bowel preparation in adults is 3–4 LITRES** |

**Emesis may occur but may be controlled by REDUCING THE ADMINISTRATION RATE and using an antiemetic such
as metoclopramide** [G1][G3]. ⚠️ **AFTER 15 MINUTES, REASSESS PEOPLE WHO AGREE TO DRINK THE SOLUTION to
determine their compliance** [G3].

⚠️ **CONTRAINDICATED in suspected or proven bowel obstruction or perforation. Use with caution in children
under 2 years.** **Safe in pregnancy and breastfeeding** [G3].

## Special cases [G1]

### Unconscious patients
> ⚠️ **INTUBATE TO PROTECT THE AIRWAY if decontamination is indicated.** **Activated charcoal can then be
> given via orogastric or nasogastric tube** — and **an OROGASTRIC tube allows ASPIRATION OF STOMACH
> CONTENTS BEFORE administration** [G1].

### Children
> **THE MAJORITY OF ACCIDENTAL PAEDIATRIC OVERDOSES ARE LOW RISK** and can be managed with supportive care,
> generally requiring **NEITHER decontamination NOR a specific antidote.** ⚠️ **However, serious poisonings
> and occasional deaths continue to occur** [G1].

### ⚠️ What happens after the charcoal
> **The patient's long-term prognosis depends on the precipitant and what is done about it** [G1]:
> - **Deliberate self-poisoning** → **consider and assess for an underlying psychosocial disorder and
>   ARRANGE EARLY PSYCHIATRIC REVIEW**
> - **Accidental poisoning** → **consider poor work practices or an unsafe environment, which may require
>   intervention**

### A practical consequence often missed
> **ABSORPTION OF ORAL MEDICATIONS MAY BE REDUCED BY GI DECONTAMINATION.** **Where possible, give required
> medications BY ANOTHER ROUTE, e.g. parenterally** [G1].
> ⚠️ **The patient's own regular medicines stop working too.**
