# Sore throat in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** assessment, red flags, supportive care, antibiotics for suspected group A streptococcal pharyngitis and suppurative complications in children. The all-ages summary is `mouth-and-throat-conditions`; rheumatic fever prevention and treatment is `acute-rheumatic-fever-and-rhd`.

> ⚠️ **Impending airway obstruction: minimal handling and early referral to an experienced clinician for definitive airway management.**

> ⚠️ **Children at high risk of acute rheumatic fever get antibiotics for a sore throat, irrespective of other clinical features.**

---

## Recognise it

- **Viral pharyngitis is the most common cause.** GAS is the most frequent bacterial cause; it is usually self-limiting, hard to distinguish from viral infection, and **most patients do not need empiric antibiotics**. [S1]
- **GAS pharyngitis is uncommon under 4 years of age.** [S1]
- **High ARF risk:** Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander people; Māori and/or Pacific Islander people; personal history of ARF or RHD; communities with high rates of ARF. [S1]
- **Antibiotics for suspected GAS pharyngitis are not known to prevent invasive GAS**, but consider iGAS in children with more severe illness and treat accordingly. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Red flags:** unwell/toxic appearance, respiratory distress, stridor, trismus, drooling, 'hot potato' voice, torticollis, neck stiffness or fullness. In the acutely unwell child consider an alternative diagnosis or a complication. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Signs of upper airway obstruction: examine minimally so as not to upset the child.** [S1]

## Investigate

- **Throat swab is not routinely recommended. Streptococcal serology has no role** in diagnosing GAS pharyngitis. [S1]
- **Other tests only if indicated:** imaging for suspected suppurative complications; FBE, EBV serology ± monospot for hepatosplenomegaly. [S1]

## Treat — supportive

- **Supportive management is adequate for most sore throats.** [S1]
- **Severe pain unresponsive to simple analgesia:** consider dexamethasone 0.15 mg/kg (max 10 mg) oral/IV/IM single dose, or prednisolone 1 mg/kg (max 50 mg) oral single dose. [S1]

## Treat — antibiotics for suspected GAS

- ⚠️ **High ARF risk: treat any sore throat with antibiotics.** Otherwise, **empiric antibiotics for every child with a sore throat are not recommended.** [S1]
- **First line: phenoxymethylpenicillin 15 mg/kg (max 500 mg) oral twice daily for 10 days.** [S1]
- **Second line, for better adherence: amoxicillin 50 mg/kg (max 1 g) oral once daily for 10 days.** [S1]
- **Poor adherence or oral not tolerated: benzathine benzylpenicillin IM single dose** — <10 kg 450,000 units (0.9 mL); 10–20 kg 600,000 units (1.2 mL); ≥20 kg 1,200,000 units (2.3 mL). [S1]
- **Non-immediate penicillin hypersensitivity: cefalexin 25 mg/kg (1 g) oral twice daily for 10 days.** **Immediate beta-lactam hypersensitivity: azithromycin 12 mg/kg (max 500 mg) oral daily for 5 days.** [S1]

## Suppurative complications

- **Peritonsillar abscess (quinsy):** fever, odynophagia, dysphagia, 'hot potato' voice, trismus, peritonsillar swelling, uvula deviation. **Refer to ENT for drainage;** benzylpenicillin 50 mg/kg (max 1.2 g) IV 6-hourly, then phenoxymethylpenicillin 15 mg/kg (max 500 mg) oral twice daily to a total of 10 days. [S1]
- **Retropharyngeal/parapharyngeal abscess:** refer to ENT. **A normal lateral neck X-ray does not exclude it;** CT with IV contrast is the imaging of choice, only with advanced airway management available. [S1]
- **Retropharyngeal antibiotics:** amoxicillin with clavulanic acid 25 mg/kg (max 1 g) IV 8-hourly, then 22.5 mg/kg (max 875 mg) oral twice daily (doses by amoxicillin component). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Epiglottitis/bacterial tracheitis:** defer all unnecessary examination, procedures and imaging until advanced airway management is available; **early ICU/anaesthetic/ENT review.** Higher risk if unimmunised against HiB. [S1]
- **Epiglottitis antibiotics: ceftriaxone 50 mg/kg (max 2 g) IV/IM daily for 5 days;** consider dexamethasone 0.15 mg/kg (max 10 mg) oral/IV/IM stat, repeated in 24 hours if needed. [S1]

## Disposition and follow-up

- **Consult the paediatric team if** systemically unwell, suppurative complications, moderate/severe dehydration, or significant pain despite analgesia and steroids. [S1]
- **Transfer for** acute suppurative complications such as abscess, upper airway obstruction, or significant comorbidities such as immunosuppression. [S1]
- **Discharge when pain relief is adequate and oral intake is tolerated.** [S1]
- **Paediatric/ENT outpatient referral for recurrence:** 7 episodes in 1 year, 5 a year for 2 years, or 3 a year for 3 years. [S1]
