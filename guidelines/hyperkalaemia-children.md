# Hyperkalaemia in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** definition, severity, ECG changes, weight-based emergency drug doses and escalation for hyperkalaemia in children. The adult counterpart is `potassium-disturbances` (AMH, which gives no doses); related: `diabetic-ketoacidosis-children`.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 22 of 22 claims quoted verbatim from the **RCH Melbourne Clinical Practice Guideline *Hyperkalaemia*** (Last updated August 2024); **29 fragments re-checked by machine. 9 quoted doses.**

> ⚠️ **Moderate or severe hyperkalaemia: ECG, continuous cardiac monitoring and IV access. Calcium first if there are ECG changes** — it protects the heart but does not lower potassium.

---

## Recognise it

- **Hyperkalaemia = K over 5.5 mmol/L** (normal 3.5–5.0), **or over 6.0 mmol/L in neonates.** [S1]
- **Severity (mmol/L):** mild 5.5–6.0 · moderate 6.1–7.0 · **severe over 7.0.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Moderate or severe hyperkalaemia can cause life-threatening arrhythmias and arrest: do an ECG and start cardiac monitoring. Stop any potassium-raising medications or fluids.** [S1]
- **Confirm with a second sample** (collection artefact, e.g. a haemolysed sample, is a listed cause) — ⚠️ **but if the level is critically high and the scenario fits, treat before the repeat result.** [S1]
- **ECG changes:** peaked T waves (early), prolonged PR, flattened or lost P waves, widened QRS, bradyarrhythmia and blocks; **severe: sine wave, ventricular arrhythmia, PEA, asystole.** ⚠️ **A normal ECG does not exclude arrhythmia risk.** [S1]
- **Other tests:** UEC, glucose, venous gas, ± urine electrolytes; CK, cortisol/aldosterone (especially with hyponatraemia) or digoxin level as the cause suggests. [S1]

## Stabilise the heart: calcium

- ⚠️ **Calcium gluconate 10%: 0.15 mmol/kg (0.68 mL/kg), max 6.6 mmol (30 mL), slow IV/IO over 2–5 minutes if unstable, 15–20 minutes if stable.** Preferred with only a peripheral line. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Calcium chloride 10%: 0.14 mmol/kg (0.2 mL/kg), max 6.8 mmol (10 mL), slow IV/IO over 2–5 minutes if unstable, 15–20 minutes if stable.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Stop if the heart rate drops significantly; watch for extravasation. Never give with bicarbonate; do not give in digoxin toxicity.** [S1]
- **Onset under 3 minutes, lasts about 30 minutes.** The ECG should normalise; if not, repeat the dose (twice) or give an infusion titrated to response. [S1]

## Shift potassium into cells

- **Nebulised salbutamol:** 25 kg or less 2.5 mg; over 25 kg 5 mg (max 10–20 mg); **1–2-hourly.** Onset 30 minutes, lasts 2–3 hours. [S1]
- ⚠️ **IV salbutamol only if severe and after discussion with a senior clinician**; monitor for tachycardia. [S1]
- **Severe: glucose 10% 5 mL/kg IV bolus (if no hyponatraemia) plus short-acting insulin 0.1 unit/kg IV bolus (max 10 units)**, followed by an insulin/glucose infusion. [S1]
- **Moderate: glucose 10% with 0.9% sodium chloride at maintenance rate plus short-acting insulin infusion 0.1 unit/kg/hour IV.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **High risk of insulin error: dilute 10 units (0.1 mL) to 10 mL with 0.9% sodium chloride (1 unit/mL) and draw the dose from that. Check glucose every 30–60 minutes.** [S1]
- **Sodium bicarbonate — metabolic acidosis only.** Severe hyperkalaemia with acidosis: 8.4% 1–3 mmol/kg (1–3 mL/kg) IV over 5 minutes. Mild–moderate with acidosis: 1 mmol/kg (1 mL/kg) slow IV over 30 minutes. ⚠️ **Not with calcium.** [S1]

## Remove potassium

- **Polystyrene sulfonate resin 0.5–1 g/kg (max 30 g) 6-hourly PR or oral (with lactulose)** — mild effect, multiple doses needed. Separate from other oral medicines by at least 3 hours. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not use resins with ileus, recent abdominal surgery, perforation or hypernatraemia.** [S1]
- **Dialysis:** organise with the paediatric renal or intensive care team and transfer to a tertiary centre. [S1]

## Escalation and discharge

- **Involve the paediatric team for all moderate or severe hyperkalaemia**, or an underlying cause such as renal abnormality. **Seek specialist advice if there is renal disease or complex co-morbidity.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Consider transfer for all severe hyperkalaemia and anyone needing dialysis.** [S1]
- **Discharge** when potassium is normal and stable, the child is asymptomatic with a normal ECG, and the cause is treated. [S1]
