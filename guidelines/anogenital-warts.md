# Anogenital warts

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-21 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults, primary care.

> **Australian primary source.** ASHM *Australian STI Management Guidelines for use in primary care* —
> *Anogenital warts* [S1], modified 30 June 2026.

## Treatment is cosmetic, not curative

> **Treatment is cosmetic rather than curative** [S1].

Twenty-four guidelines in, this is the only one that states plainly that its own treatment does not cure the
infection. Everything downstream follows from it — no test of cure, no retesting, no contact tracing, and an
auditable outcome that measures **information** rather than treatment:

> **100% of patients diagnosed with genital warts are provided with information** [S1].

That is the only quality measure in the set that audits **what the patient was told** and nothing else. It
is consistent: if the drug removes a lesion rather than an infection, then the consultation's durable
product is the explanation.

## The sentence to have ready

> **The long latent period, just as with herpes, means that the presence of warts in only one partner does
> not necessarily imply recent infidelity** [S1].

The source cross-references HSV deliberately. Two topics in this set carry the same instruction, and in both
cases it is listed as clinical content, not as tact — because the alternative is a patient drawing a
conclusion about a relationship from a diagnosis that does not support it.

**Warty growths cause little discomfort (sometimes itchy) but often the psychological distress is
significant** [S1].

## Diagnosis is visual

**Diagnosis is usually based on visual appearance** [S1].

> **HPV PCR testing is not used to diagnose anogenital warts** [S1].

A rare explicit statement that an available molecular test has no role — worth holding, because every other
infection in this set is confirmed by NAAT.

**Consider other potential causes — e.g. syphilis presenting as condylomata lata** [S1].

**Most anogenital warts are caused by HPV types 6 and 11, and infection results in type-specific
protection** [S1]. **Most HPV infections are asymptomatic** [S1]. Transmission is **direct skin-to-skin
contact with apparent or subclinical lesions and contact with genital secretions**; **micro-abrasions in the
recipient's skin allow viral access to the basal cells of the epithelium** [S1].

## ⚠️ When to biopsy instead of treat

> **Atypical lesions, lesions with variable pigmentation, or raised plaque-like lesions should be biopsied
> to exclude pre-cancerous change — especially in patients who are immunosuppressed or have HIV** [S1].

> **Consider referral and biopsy of atypical lesions, or new lesions in elderly people, to test for
> malignancy** [S1].

**Malignancy (penile, anal, oropharynx; vulvar, vaginal, cervical, anal, oropharynx) is possible with
oncogenic HPV genotypes** [S1].

**New lesions in an older person** is the trigger most easily missed — the source treats age itself as a
reason to biopsy rather than treat empirically.

**Cervical lesions noted on vaginal examination should have cervical screening conducted as per national
guidelines** [S1].

## Treatment

| Situation | Recommended [S1] | Alternative [S1] |
|---|---|---|
| **Genital warts** | **Patient-applied podophyllotoxin paint topically, twice a day for 3 days, then 4 days off, repeated weekly for 4–6 cycles until resolution**<br>**OR**<br>**Patient-applied imiquimod 5% cream topically, 3 times per week at bedtime (wash after 6–10 hours) until resolution (up to 16 weeks)** | **Clinician-initiated cryotherapy weekly.** Rarely may need **excision under local anaesthetic or ablative therapy under general anaesthetic** — seek specialist advice |

Both recommended options are **patient-applied**. The clinician's contribution is the diagnosis, the
decision not to biopsy, and the instructions.

**By site** [S1]:

| Site | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Meatal warts** | **Cryotherapy** |
| **Intra-anal warts** | **Cryotherapy, or refer for surgical management** |
| **Cervical warts** | **Initial cervical cytology, and refer to a gynaecologist** for consideration of colposcopy, biopsy and treatment as indicated |

**In HIV infection, genital warts can have a poor response to treatment, may require longer cycles, and are
more likely to recur** [S1]. **Persistent intra-anal lesions in people living with HIV should be considered
for surgical excision and HPV DNA typing to inform follow-up** [S1] — the one situation where HPV typing
does have a role.

**Complicated or disseminated infection: consider referral for laser or diathermy** [S1].

## The advice that prevents spread

> **If warts are in the pubic region, avoid shaving or waxing, as this may facilitate local spread by
> autoinoculation of HPV into areas of microtrauma** [S1].

Practical, easily omitted, and the mechanism is the same one that establishes the infection in the first
place.

**Offer HPV vaccination if not already vaccinated** — but note: **HPV vaccination is not a therapeutic
vaccine. It may protect from future acquisition of other HPV types** [S1]. Vaccinating will not treat what
is already there, and the source says so rather than leaving the patient to assume otherwise.

**Genital warts is not a notifiable condition** [S1].

## Pregnancy

- **Warts can grow rapidly in pregnancy, and can be treated during pregnancy with cryotherapy or
  diathermy** [S1].
- **Cryotherapy can have a poor response** in pregnancy, and **lesions often resolve spontaneously
  postnatally when immune function returns to normal following delivery** [S1].
- **Pregnant people can undergo a normal vaginal delivery, as the risk of transmission to the baby is
  extremely low** [S1].

That last point matters because the visible lesion invites an intervention the evidence does not support.

## Contact tracing and follow-up

> **Not recommended. The majority of partners have probably acquired the infection subclinically** [S1].

The second topic in the set where tracing is recommended against, after HSV — and the reasoning is stated:
there is nothing to find, because it is already there.

**Follow-up is not required if symptoms resolve.** **Review if the patient is anxious, or if the warts are
difficult for the patient to visualise** [S1] — anxiety and anatomy, not pathology, are the stated reasons
to bring someone back.

**Test of cure: not applicable.** **Retesting: not required** — but consider testing for other STIs if not
done at first presentation, or retesting past the window period [S1].
