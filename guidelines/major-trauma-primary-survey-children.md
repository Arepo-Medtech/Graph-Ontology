# Major trauma in children: the primary survey

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** the ED primary survey of a child with major trauma: pre-arrival preparation, catastrophic haemorrhage, airway with c-spine control, breathing, circulation and fluids, disability and exposure. No adult trauma guideline exists in this compendium. Siblings: `cervical-spine-assessment-children` and `pelvic-injury-children`. > ⚠️ **Control catastrophic haemorrhage first, then ABCDE in parallel.** Every child with major trauma should go to a major trauma service.

## Before and on arrival

- **Before arrival:** activate the trauma team, brief the team on the anticipated injuries and priorities, **estimate the child's weight** and prepare age- and weight-appropriate doses and equipment. [S1]
- **Take a 'hands-off' ambulance handover** unless a life threat needs immediate action, then perform the primary survey. A dedicated staff member should support the family. [S1]
- **Priorities, assessed and managed in parallel:** catastrophic haemorrhage · airway with c-spine control · breathing · circulation · disability · exposure/environment. [S1]

## Catastrophic haemorrhage

- ⚠️ **Controlling exsanguinating external haemorrhage comes first.** Apply direct pressure with a gauze pad; **use a tourniquet for life-threatening limb haemorrhage.** [S1]
- **Leave bandages that control significant bleeding in place** until the child is stable and IV access is secured. Don't use layers of gauze that absorb and hide blood loss. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Apply a pelvic binder if a pelvic fracture is suspected and there is haemodynamic compromise**; activate the massive transfusion protocol if appropriate. [S1]

## Airway and cervical spine

- **Assess the airway, anterior neck and conscious level (AVPU)**; look for facial fractures and blood, vomit or loose teeth in the airway. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Burns — look for airway involvement:** singed facial or nasal hair, facial burns, hoarse voice, harsh cough, head or neck swelling, soot in the mouth, nose or saliva. [S1]
- **Use an age-appropriate neutral head position** — under 8 years, a thoracic elevation device or a towel under the shoulder blades. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Every spontaneously breathing patient gets high-flow oxygen** (typically 10–15 L via non-rebreather mask), whatever the need for airway support. [S1]
- **Airway support:** jaw thrust (no head-tilt or chin lift), gentle suction, an oropharyngeal airway if tolerated or a nasopharyngeal airway if base-of-skull fracture is unlikely, and endotracheal intubation. [S1]
- **Protect the cervical spine with manual in-line stabilisation until a soft foam collar is applied.** [S1]

## Breathing

- **Breathing life threats to find and treat:** tension pneumothorax, open pneumothorax, massive haemothorax, flail chest, tracheo-bronchial injury. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Tension pneumothorax: chest decompression with a finger or instrument, thoracostomy or intercostal catheter.** Needle decompression may be used in emergencies but is unlikely to work. [S1]
- **Open pneumothorax:** close it immediately and insert a chest drain. **Massive haemothorax:** chest drain and blood transfusion. **Flail chest:** analgesia; consider positive pressure ventilation. [S1]
- **Request a portable chest X-ray.** In intubated children, **insert an orogastric tube** so gastric dilatation doesn't impair ventilation. [S1]

## Circulation

- **Hunt for bleeding:** external (don't remove penetrating foreign bodies), intra-thoracic, intra-abdominal, intra-pelvic, long bones (particularly femoral), retroperitoneal, and scalp or intracranial in infants. [S1]
- **eFAST has a limited role in paediatric trauma.** Actively exclude obstructive shock (tension pneumothorax, cardiac tamponade) and neurogenic shock (spinal injury above T6). [S1]
- **Two IV cannulae, as large as practicable, ideally one in each cubital fossa**; send cross-match, FBE, VBG, LFTs, UEC, lipase and coagulation screen. **If IV access isn't rapid, go IO** into a non-injured leg or the humerus. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Inadequate circulation: 10 mL/kg bolus of sodium chloride 0.9%.** Ongoing visible or suspected occult bleeding: **packed red cells 10 mL/kg** if available. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Tranexamic acid 15 mg/kg** if there is haemodynamic compromise from suspected haemorrhage. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Life-threatening abdominal, pelvic or retroperitoneal bleeding may need surgery or interventional radiology — consult early.** Uncontrollable bleeding may need theatre before the secondary survey is finished; a chest drain in massive haemothorax may help ventilation but only theatre stops the bleeding. [S1]

## Disability and exposure

- **Any impairment on AVPU → formal GCS.** Check pupils, movement in all four limbs, and **blood glucose on arrival.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Prevent secondary brain injury:** keep oxygenation adequate, correct hypotension and hypoglycaemia, nurse head-up 30 degrees. Consider urgent CT brain and neurosurgical discussion. [S1]
- **Expose to find life-threatening injuries, but prevent hypothermia** — limit exposure and warm all ongoing fluids. [S1]

## Transfer

- ⚠️ **All children with major trauma should be transferred to a major trauma service** for definitive care, following local pre-hospital and inter-hospital transfer guidelines. [S1]
