# Risperidone poisoning in children

## Who needs urgent assessment

- ⚠️ **Drug-naive children: under 12 years, 1 mg is potentially toxic; 12 years or older, more than 5 mg.** These need urgent assessment. [S1]
- **Children on regular risperidone: more than 5 times their current single dose (not daily dose) is potentially toxic.** [S1]
- **Also assess:** any symptomatic patient · any ingestion of unknown quantity · every deliberate self-poisoning or significant accidental ingestion. [S1]
- **Symptoms usually start within 1–2 hours and peak by 4–6 hours**, but resolution can take several days (half-life 3–30 hours). **Alcohol and cannabis may deepen CNS depression.** [S1]

## Recognise toxicity

- **Mild to moderate:** tachycardia and hypotension are common, with depressed mental status, somnolence and extrapyramidal symptoms. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Severe:** QTc prolongation and ventricular arrhythmia; extrapyramidal symptoms likely; respiratory depression, seizures, coma or neuroleptic malignant syndrome can occur. [S1]
- **Screen with a 12-lead ECG, BSL and paracetamol level. ECG at presentation and at 4 hours** — tachycardia can be delayed up to 20 hours. [S1]

## Manage

- ⚠️ **Hypotension: IV fluids; if it persists, a vasopressor — norepinephrine preferred.** Avoid epinephrine and dopamine: beta stimulation may worsen hypotension with risperidone's alpha blockade. [S1]
- **Activated charcoal has a very limited role**; discuss with a toxicologist. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Seizures: IV benzodiazepines; add propofol or barbiturates if they recur or persist. Avoid phenytoin and fosphenytoin.** [S1]
- **Extrapyramidal symptoms — benzatropine IM or IV:** 3–12 years 0.02 mg/kg (max 1 mg); 12–18 years 1–2 mg. May be repeated in 15 minutes. EPS can appear up to several days after exposure. [S1]
- **Don't discharge a child who still needs benzatropine.** [S1]

## Admit, transfer, discharge

- **Consider admission for:** every intentional overdose · symptomatic patients · overdose with cardiovascular or neurological disease. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Transfer for significant CNS depression, seizures, or any cardiovascular instability.** [S1]
- **Discharge when GCS and ECG are normal after a period of observation. Warn that extrapyramidal movements can appear up to 3 days later.** [S1]
- **Before discharging an adolescent after a deliberate ingestion**, assess self-harm risk and other drug and alcohol use. [S1]
