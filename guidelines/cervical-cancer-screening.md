# Cervical screening — primary care essentials

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** primary care essentials of the National Cervical Screening Program: who is screened and when, self-collection, the first steps after an HPV test result, and when to refer for colposcopy. **Not** colposcopy practice, histology management or test of cure. ## Who, from when, how often

- **Cervical screening is for asymptomatic women and people with a cervix aged 25–74.** [S1]
- **Routine screening is not recommended under 25.** [S1]
- **If HPV is not detected, rescreen in 5 years.** [S1]
- **People aged 70–74 can leave the program after a screening test that does not detect oncogenic HPV.** [S1]
- **Certain immune-deficient groups screen three-yearly rather than five-yearly** (the groups are listed in a table not quoted here). [S1]

## Sample: self-collected or clinician-collected

- **Offer everyone eligible the choice of a clinician-collected cervical sample or a self-collected vaginal sample** for HPV testing. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Symptoms are not screening:** postcoital or persistent unexplained intermenstrual bleeding needs investigation, including a **clinician-collected** cervical sample for a co-test. [S1]

## HPV (16/18) detected

- **HPV (16/18) detected: refer directly for colposcopic assessment.** If the sample was self-collected, the LBC sample is taken at colposcopy. [S1]

## HPV (not 16/18) detected

- **HPV (not 16/18) detected, under 70: LBC is needed. After a self-collected sample, the person returns to their healthcare provider within 6 weeks** for the cervical sample. [S1]
- **If LBC is negative, pLSIL or LSIL: repeat the HPV test in 12 months.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **If LBC predicts invasive cancer: refer to a gynaecological oncologist or gynaecological cancer centre, ideally within 2 weeks.** [S1]
- **If LBC predicts pHSIL/HSIL or any glandular abnormality: refer for colposcopy, ideally within 8 weeks.** [S1]
