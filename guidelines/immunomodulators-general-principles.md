## What these drugs are [I1]

**Immunomodulators STIMULATE OR SUPPRESS the immune system via different mechanisms. Used in a wide range
of autoimmune and inflammatory diseases — rheumatoid arthritis, psoriasis, ulcerative colitis, Crohn's
disease, systemic lupus erythematosus, vasculitis — AS WELL AS IN ORGAN TRANSPLANTATION** [I1].

> ⚠️ **"THE RISK AND SEVERITY OF INFECTION ARE RELATED TO THE DEGREE OF IMMUNOSUPPRESSION, WHICH VARIES
> DEPENDING ON THE IMMUNOMODULATOR"** [I1]. **The checklist below is not uniform across drugs — its
> intensity should track the drug.**

## ⚠️ Before starting [I1]

| | [I1] |
|---|---|
| **Infection** | **IDENTIFY AND TREAT ANY ACTIVE OR LATENT INFECTIONS INCLUDING TB** |
| ⚠️ **Hepatitis B** | ⚠️ **SCREEN ALL PATIENTS — there is a RISK OF REACTIVATION.** **Seek specialist advice if positive: ANTIVIRAL PROPHYLAXIS MAY BE REQUIRED** |
| **Transplant candidates** | **additionally EBV, CMV, varicella-zoster virus, HIV, hepatitis C** |
| **Immunisation** | **immune response to INACTIVATED vaccines may be REDUCED while immunosuppressed;** ⚠️ **MOST LIVE VACCINES ARE CONTRAINDICATED IN SEVERE IMMUNOSUPPRESSION** — risk of vaccine-related disease |
| **Malignancy** | **consider any history of malignant disease** |
| **Dental** | ⚠️ **PERFORM A DENTAL EXAMINATION IF POSSIBLE, to manage potential sources of infection** |

⚠️ **The dental examination is the item most likely to be skipped and is on AMH's list.**

## ⚠️ Infection during treatment [I1]

> ⚠️ **THE PRESENTATION IS ALTERED, NOT JUST THE RISK.**
> **"Infections may be MORE DIFFICULT TO RECOGNISE (as the usual signs and symptoms are often reduced),
> often PROGRESS RAPIDLY and require EARLY TREATMENT. PATIENTS SHOULD KNOW HOW TO OBTAIN TREATMENT AT ANY
> TIME"** [I1].

**Immunosuppression may increase the risk of OPPORTUNISTIC infections, REACTIVATION of latent infections,
or SERIOUS infections — TB, hepatitis B, varicella-zoster virus, CMV, aspergillosis** [I1].

**Deciding whether to start or continue depends on: the type of infection (active or latent), its severity,
whether it can be treated or controlled, and the indication for the immunomodulator.** ⚠️ **Use caution
with a history of RECURRENT infections; seek specialist advice if unsure** [I1].

### Reducing the risk [I1]
- **ISONIAZID for latent TB infection**
- **ANTIVIRAL PROPHYLAXIS in inactive hepatitis B**
- **PNEUMOCOCCAL AND ANNUAL INFLUENZA VACCINATIONS**

### ⚠️ Chickenpox and measles [I1]

> **IMMUNOSUPPRESSED PATIENTS SHOULD AVOID EXPOSURE TO CHICKENPOX AND MEASLES — infections can be
> severe.**
> ⚠️ **Seek specialist advice if a patient is exposed to chickenpox or shingles during therapy.**
> **Consider NORMAL IMMUNOGLOBULIN for MEASLES and ZOSTER IMMUNOGLOBULIN for CHICKENPOX OR SHINGLES**
> [I1].

**This is a phone call to make on the day of exposure, not at the next review.**

## ⚠️ Malignancy [I1]

> **"Immunosuppression INCREASES THE RISK OF MALIGNANCIES, PARTICULARLY OF THE SKIN, and
> lymphoproliferative disorders, eg lymphomas. RISK INCREASES WITH THE DEGREE AND DURATION OF
> IMMUNOSUPPRESSION"** [I1].

**EXAMINE SKIN REGULARLY FOR SIGNS OF CANCER.** **Patients should avoid direct sun exposure, use sunscreen
with a HIGH SPF (30 or 50+), and wear a broad-brimmed hat and suitable clothing outdoors** [I1].

⚠️ **In Australia this is not a generic precaution:** this compendium's `local-anaesthesia` guideline
records that **keratinocyte cancers carry a lifetime risk of 69%** and that **most skin cancers, including
melanomas, are managed in primary care.** **A patient on long-term immunosuppression sits on top of that
baseline.**

## Organ transplantation [I1]

**Drugs used to prevent rejection** [I1]:

| Class | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Calcineurin inhibitors** | **ciclosporin, tacrolimus** |
| **Corticosteroids** | **prednisolone** |
| **Immunosuppressant antibodies** | **antithymocyte globulins, basiliximab** |
| **mTOR inhibitors** | **everolimus, sirolimus** |
| **Other immunosuppressants** | **azathioprine, mycophenolate** |

**Regimens usually COMBINE DRUGS FROM MORE THAN ONE CLASS**, using different modes of action **to improve
efficacy and reduce toxicity**; choice depends on the organ and is tailored to the patient [I1].

> **Intensity is INITIALLY HIGH, then reduced to a maintenance level.** ⚠️ **"The intensity required to
> preserve transplant function is also BALANCED AGAINST THE RISK OF POST-TRANSPLANT SKIN CANCER,
> LYMPHOMA, INFECTION AND END-ORGAN TOXICITY"** [I1].

### Infection prophylaxis after transplant [I1]
**Depending on organ and risk factors: TRIMETHOPRIM WITH SULFAMETHOXAZOLE for *P. jirovecii* and
*Toxoplasma gondii*; ANTIFUNGALS for *Candida* and *Aspergillus*.** **CMV prevention with VALGANCICLOVIR
or GANCICLOVIR — as prophylaxis or pre-emptive therapy — depending on donor and recipient CMV status,
organ type and intensity of immunosuppression** [I1].

### Rejection [I1]
- **ACUTE CELLULAR: HIGH-DOSE CORTICOSTEROIDS usually preferred.** **ANTITHYMOCYTE GLOBULIN if
  corticosteroid-resistant.**
- **ANTIBODY-MEDIATED (humoral): plasmapheresis, IV immunoglobulin, antithymocyte globulin, rituximab, or
  a combination.**
- **SEVERE: increase baseline immunosuppression or change agent.**
