# Orthostatic hypotension — and postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-22 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults. ⚠️ **Two different conditions are covered side by side on purpose** — see the box below.
**Syncope of other causes is out of scope.**

> ⚠️ **THESE ARE NOT THE SAME CONDITION, AND THEIR DOSES ARE NOT INTERCHANGEABLE.**
> **POTS is defined partly by the ABSENCE of orthostatic hypotension** [S1]. They are placed together
> because a patient who stands up and feels terrible is the presentation for both, and because **the same
> drugs appear in both lists at DIFFERENT DOSES**. See *The same drug, two indications, two doses*.

# Part 1 — Orthostatic hypotension [AMH]

> ⚠️ **ORTHOSTATIC HYPOTENSION IS A RISK FACTOR FOR FALLS, particularly in the elderly** [A1].

## ⚠️ Look at the drug chart first

**Drugs that exacerbate orthostatic hypotension** [A1]:

> **DIURETICS · ALPHA-BLOCKERS · TCAs · ANTIPSYCHOTICS · NITRATES · LEVODOPA · DOPAMINE AGONISTS**

⚠️ **Nitrates are on that list**, and this compendium's `angina` guideline prescribes them — **orthostatic
hypotension is a listed common adverse effect of every nitrate** there. **Alpha-blockers are on it**, and
the `benign-prostatic-hyperplasia-and-prostatitis` guideline prescribes them.

## Exacerbating factors to remove [A1]

**Getting up quickly · prolonged standing still · hot showers · dehydration · prolonged bed rest ·
⚠️ LOW SALT INTAKE · heavy meals · alcohol · many drugs** [A1].

## Non-drug treatment comes first [A1]

- **Sleep with the head of the bed tilted up**
- **Increase fluid intake; salt supplements**
- **Cross one leg in front of the other when standing, or contract calf muscles (toe-raising)**
- **Abdominal binder or waist-high compression stockings**
- **Exercise programs — swimming, rowing**

> ⚠️ **SPECIFIC DRUG TREATMENT IS ONLY OCCASIONALLY NECESSARY** [A1]. AMH's list, when it is:
> **low-dose fludrocortisone, midodrine, caffeine, NSAIDs, pyridostigmine, phenylephrine,
> pseudoephedrine and, rarely, octreotide** [A1].

## Midodrine [A2]

**An alpha1 agonist that increases BP by increasing peripheral resistance.** ⚠️ **Indicated for SEVERE
SYMPTOMATIC orthostatic hypotension due to autonomic dysfunction WHERE STANDARD TREATMENT IS INADEQUATE**
— not before [A2].

| | [A2] |
|---|---|
| **Dose** | **initially 2.5 mg THREE TIMES DAILY; increase at weekly intervals on supine and standing BP and tolerability, to a maximum of 10 mg three times daily** |
| **Slower option** | **2–3 monthly intervals may minimise adverse effects; slower titration is recommended in the elderly (limited data)** |
| ⚠️ **Timing** | **last dose AT LEAST 4 HOURS BEFORE BEDTIME**; **use only when the patient is awake and likely to be upright**; ⚠️ **AVOID EVENING DOSING** |

> ⚠️ **MIDODRINE CAN CAUSE OR WORSEN SUPINE HYPERTENSION.** **Monitor supine AND standing BP regularly.**
> ⚠️ **STOP TREATMENT if reducing the dose does not overcome supine hypertension** [A2].
> **Tell the doctor immediately about chest pain, palpitations, headache or blurred vision** [A2].

⚠️ **What the trials actually showed:** **in two placebo-controlled trials over 3–4 weeks, midodrine
increased standing systolic BP and improved some symptoms such as syncope — HOWEVER THE EFFECT ON CLINICAL
OUTCOMES SUCH AS FALLS AND QUALITY OF LIFE WAS NOT ASSESSED**, and **long-term safety and efficacy data are
limited** [A2]. **The indication is falls-adjacent; the evidence is not.**

**Contraindicated** in **phaeochromocytoma, hyperthyroidism or thyrotoxicosis, angle-closure glaucoma,
proliferative diabetic retinopathy, urinary retention or serious prostate disorder, uncontrolled
hypertension, severe heart disease, cerebro- or cardiovascular occlusive disease, blood vessel spasms,
acute kidney injury, and CrCl <30 mL/minute** [A2].

**Common adverse effects: piloerection, paraesthesia, scalp itch, dysuria, urinary retention or urgency,
bradycardia, headache, flushing, and DOSE-RELATED SUPINE HYPERTENSION** [A2].

> ⚠️ **AMH lists NO PBS listing for midodrine** — only 2.5 mg and 5 mg tablets [A2]. The patient pays.

**Avoid in women of child-bearing potential using inadequate contraception; not recommended in pregnancy;
avoid breastfeeding** [A2].

## Fludrocortisone [A3]

⚠️ **Orthostatic hypotension is an ACCEPTED indication, not an approved one** — the approved indications
are mineralocorticoid replacement in primary adrenal insufficiency and salt-losing congenital adrenal
hyperplasia, both with a glucocorticoid [A3].

| Indication | Dose [A3] |
|---|---|
| **Orthostatic hypotension** | **initially 100 micrograms daily; if necessary increase to 200 micrograms daily** |
| **Adrenal replacement** | **initially 50–100 micrograms once daily in the morning; range 50–300 micrograms daily** |

⚠️ **Different indications, different starting doses.** The only product is a **100 microgram scored
tablet**, so the 50 microgram adrenal starting dose is half a tablet [A3].

**Common adverse effects: sodium and water retention, oedema, HYPOKALAEMIA, HYPERTENSION.** **Heart failure
may be exacerbated by the electrolyte changes and fluid retention; potassium supplementation may be
needed** [A3].

**Titrate on symptoms, sitting and standing BP, plasma renin activity, and serum sodium and potassium.**
⚠️ **PATIENTS SHOULD NOT RESTRICT THEIR SALT INTAKE** [A3].

# Part 2 — POTS [S1]

**Postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome is a multi-system disorder of orthostatic intolerance,
particularly a rise in heart rate when standing, WITHOUT marked orthostatic hypotension or another cause
for tachycardia. Palpitations, weakness or lightheadedness improve on sitting** [S1].

> **Up to 80% of POTS patients are female, often from a White background. The most common age of symptom
> onset is 14 years (mean age 21 ± 12 years)** [S1].

**Co-existing conditions commonly include hypermobility spectrum disorder, fibromyalgia and migraines;
onset can be precipitated by a stressful event, commonly a viral infection** [S1].

## ⚠️ All four criteria must be met [S1]

> **As per the 2015 Heart Rhythm Society consensus statement:**
> 1. **sustained HR increase ≥30 bpm — or ≥40 bpm if aged 12–19 years — within 10 minutes of upright
>    posture**
> 2. ⚠️ **ABSENCE of an orthostatic BP drop ≥20/10 mmHg**
> 3. **symptoms of orthostatic intolerance worse upright, with rapid improvement on returning supine,
>    FOR MORE THAN 6 MONTHS**
> 4. **absence of other conditions that could explain sinus tachycardia** [S1]

⚠️ **Criterion 2 is what separates POTS from Part 1 of this guideline.** It also supplies the threshold —
**a drop of 20/10 mmHg** — from an **open, quotable** source.

## Three phenotypes, and they steer the drug [S1]

| Phenotype | Marker [S1] |
|---|---|
| **Autonomic neuropathy** | **small postganglionic sudomotor fibres, mainly in the feet — reported in up to 50% of POTS patients** |
| **Hypovolaemic** | **low plasma blood volume and 24-hour urine sodium <100 mmol/L**, from a dysfunctional renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system |
| **Hyperadrenergic** | **rise in systolic BP >10 mmHg during a 10-minute tilt table test, and plasma noradrenaline >600 pg/mL** |

## Work-up [S1]

**Orthostatic vital signs to confirm postural tachycardia; a 12-lead ECG and a 24-hour Holter monitor to
exclude arrhythmia; and pathology to screen for other causes — full blood count, iron studies, active
vitamin B12, electrolytes, renal function, thyroid function, plasma noradrenaline and morning cortisol**
[S1].

## Management [S1]

**Both non-pharmacological and pharmacological, aimed at expanding blood volume, enhancing venous return
and reducing tachycardia** [S1].

| Non-drug [S1] | Strength |
|---|---|
| **Exercise training and gradual reconditioning** | **Class IIa** |
| **Sodium intake >10 g/day and fluid intake of at least 2.5 litres/day** | **Class IIb** |
| **Class 2 compression garments, >30 mmHg of pressure** | **Class Ib for venous insufficiency; symptomatic improvement shown in POTS** |

### ⚠️ Doses — QUOTED, and re-checkable [S1]

**These are the only doses in this guideline that a machine can re-check. They come from S1's table and are
POTS doses, not orthostatic hypotension doses.**

| Goal | Drug and dose [S1] | Notes [S1] |
|---|---|---|
| **Reduce upright sinus tachycardia** | **propranolol 10–40 mg tid · bisoprolol 2.5–5 mg bid · metoprolol 25–100 mg daily · atenolol 12.5–50 mg daily** | **bradycardia, hypotension, fatigue, syncope**; ⚠️ **caution in asthma** |
| | **ivabradine 2.5–5 mg bd** | ⚠️ **NOT currently part of first- or second-line guidelines**, but **improves symptoms with very few side effects** and is **effective when beta-blockers are not well tolerated** |
| **Enhance vasoconstriction** | **midodrine 2.5–10 mg bd** | **supine hypertension, paraesthesias, urinary retention** |
| | **clonidine 0.2–0.6 mg bd** | for the **hyperadrenergic phenotype** or **hypertension on standing** |
| | **pyridostigmine 30–60 mg bd/tds** | for the **possible neuropathic phenotype** |
| **Augment blood volume** | **fludrocortisone 0.1–0.2 mg once daily** | **hypertension, hypokalaemia**; may suit the **hypovolaemic subtype** |
| | **desmopressin 0.1–0.4 mg bd** | ⚠️ **limited evidence**; **hyponatraemia** |

## ⚠️ The same drug, two indications, two doses

| Drug | Orthostatic hypotension [AMH] | POTS [S1] | |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Midodrine** | **2.5 mg → 10 mg THREE TIMES DAILY** [A2] | **2.5–10 mg TWICE DAILY** [S1] | ⚠️ **DIFFERENT FREQUENCY** |
| **Fludrocortisone** | **100 → 200 micrograms daily** [A3] | **0.1–0.2 mg once daily** [S1] | ✅ **the same dose, stated in two units** |
| **Ivabradine** | not listed | **2.5–5 mg bd** [S1] | ⚠️ this compendium's `angina` guideline gives **5 mg bd, range 2.5–7.5 mg bd**, from AMH — a **third** dose range for the same drug in a **third** indication |

> ⚠️ **DO NOT CARRY A DOSE ACROSS THESE ROWS.** Midodrine differs by frequency, which is a 50% difference in
> daily exposure of a drug whose headline harm — **supine hypertension** — is **dose-related** [A2].
> The fludrocortisone agreement is worth noting precisely because it is an agreement between an open source
> and a licensed one on a number.
