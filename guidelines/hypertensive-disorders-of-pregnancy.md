# Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy — including pre-eclampsia and eclampsia

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-22 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** **general practice** — preconception, early pregnancy, shared antenatal care and the postpartum
period, which is where [S1] says GPs are inevitably involved. ⚠️ **Inpatient and intrapartum management,
delivery decisions and neonatal care are OUT OF SCOPE** and are named here only as referral triggers.

> **Mostly OPEN, and mostly re-checkable — the best-sourced obstetric guideline in this compendium.**
> **S1** is the **open** RACGP *AJGP* article on hypertensive disorders in pregnancy (**December 2024
> supplement**), **quoted verbatim INCLUDING ITS DOSE TABLE** — 29 claims, **21 of them quoted doses that a
> machine can re-check**. **A1–A4** are four **AMH** pages, **paraphrased** — 21 claims, 11 queued doses.

> **The two sources do different jobs.** **S1** covers antenatal and postpartum management, which AMH does
> not. **AMH** doses magnesium sulfate, which **S1 does not dose at all.** Where they overlap — **the
> aspirin prophylaxis dose** — **both figures are stated.**

**AMH topic closed by this guideline:** *Pre-eclampsia and eclampsia* (the gap list carried it as
*Eclampsia and pre-eclampsia*).

## The spectrum [S1]

> **Up to 8–10% of pregnant women are affected** [S1]. **AMH puts pre-eclampsia alone at 2–8% of
> pregnancies, and eclampsia at 1 in 2000 deliveries in developed countries** [A1].

| | Definition [S1] |
|---|---|
| **Gestational hypertension** | **new onset, systolic >140 or diastolic >90 mmHg, AFTER 20 weeks' gestation**, **no proteinuria or manifestations of pre-eclampsia**, **resolves by 12 weeks postpartum** |
| **Chronic (pre-existing) hypertension** | **diagnosed any time BEFORE 20 weeks' gestation, OR does not resolve by 12 weeks postpartum** |
| **Pre-eclampsia / eclampsia** | **high BP after 20 weeks PLUS end-organ dysfunction** — **renal** (proteinuria, oliguria, raised creatinine) · **haematological** (thrombocytopenia, haemolysis, DIC) · **hepatic** (raised transaminases, epigastric/RUQ pain) · **neurological** (headaches, visual disturbances, hyperreflexia and clonus, generalised seizures of eclampsia, stroke) · **foetal** (intrauterine growth restriction) |
| **Pre-eclampsia superimposed on chronic hypertension** | — |
| ⚠️ **HELLP syndrome** | **haemolysis, elevated liver enzymes and low platelets — occurs in 20% of severe pre-eclampsia cases** |

⚠️ **The 20-week line is what separates the first two**, and **the 12-week postpartum line is what
retrospectively separates them again.**

## ⚠️ Prophylaxis is the one thing a GP can do before anything happens

> **Patients at higher risk should be commenced on prophylactic LOW-DOSE ASPIRIN (150 mg daily) AND CALCIUM
> (>1 g daily)**, **demonstrated to be significant in reducing the risk of pre-eclampsia IF COMMENCED
> BEFORE 16 WEEKS' GESTATION (and as early as 12 weeks)** [S1].

### Who qualifies [S1]

> **One or more HIGH risk factors, OR two or more MODERATE risk factors.**

| High risk | Moderate risk |
|---|---|
| **Chronic hypertension** | **Family history of pre-eclampsia** |
| **Previous hypertensive disorder in pregnancy** | **BMI >35 kg/m²** |
| **Chronic kidney disease** | **Age >40 years** |
| **Diabetes mellitus** | **Nulliparity** |
| **Autoimmune disease** (lupus, antiphospholipid syndrome) | **Interpregnancy gap of >10 years** |
| **Multiple gestation** | **Reproductive technology assisted pregnancy** |
| | **BP systolic >130 mmHg and/or diastolic >80 mmHg** |

⚠️ **Note the last moderate factor: 130/80 — a blood pressure that is not hypertension by any definition
in this compendium's `hypertension` guideline — counts as a risk factor here.**

### ⚠️ Two sources, two aspirin doses

| Source | Aspirin for pre-eclampsia prophylaxis |
|---|---|
| **[S1]**, open, quoted | **150 mg daily**, **before 16 weeks, as early as 12** |
| **[A3]** AMH, licensed | **75–150 mg once daily**, **starting at 12–16 weeks gestation and continuing UNTIL DELIVERY** |

> **Recorded, not resolved.** **S1 gives a single figure at the top of AMH's range.** **AMH is the only one
> of the two that says how long to continue — until delivery.** **Neither contradicts the other; the
> guideline that specifies the dose does not specify the duration, and vice versa.**

**AMH adds the qualifier on calcium:** **calcium supplements reduce the risk PARTICULARLY WHEN DIETARY
CALCIUM INTAKE IS LOW, and benefits appear greatest in women at higher risk** [A1]. ⚠️ **AMH gives no
calcium dose; S1's ">1 g daily" is the only number either source supplies.**

## Diagnosis [S1]

> **Systolic >140 mmHg and/or diastolic >90 mmHg, ON TWO OCCASIONS, AT LEAST 15 MINUTES APART** [S1].

**Symptoms:** **headaches, nausea/vomiting, visual disturbance, swelling, epigastric or right upper
quadrant pain.**
**Signs:** **elevated BP**, ⚠️ **CLONUS >3 BEATS**, **hyperreflexia**, **epigastric/RUQ tenderness**,
**oedema**, **visual disturbance (blurred vision, diplopia, scotoma)** [S1].

> ⚠️ **Once a persistently elevated blood pressure has been recorded in a consult, REFER URGENTLY to a
> tertiary healthcare centre or obstetric unit** for full assessment and initiation of investigations by a
> health professional trained in managing these disorders [S1].

### Work-up [S1]

| Proteinuria | Threshold |
|---|---|
| **Bedside urinalysis** | **positive if 1+ — then send urine for quantitative analysis** |
| **Protein–creatinine ratio** | **positive if >30 mg/mmol** |
| **Albumin–creatinine ratio** | **positive if >8 mg/mmol** |

**Pre-eclampsia bloods: FBE for thrombocytopenia · UEC for raised creatinine · LFTs for raised
transaminases · coagulation studies for coagulopathy** [S1].

| Foetal ultrasound [S1] | Schedule |
|---|---|
| **Chronic hypertension** | **early dating ultrasound, then growth scans at 28, 32 and 36 weeks** |
| **Gestational hypertension** | **growth scan at diagnosis, then 3–4 weekly** |
| **Pre-eclampsia** | **growth scan at diagnosis, then FORTNIGHTLY plus fortnightly AFI + Doppler** — or as directed by specialist care |

## ⚠️ Doses — quoted and re-checkable [S1]

**These come from S1's table and can be machine re-checked against the retrieved article.**

| Drug | Class | Dose | Adverse effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Labetalol** | **beta-blocker** | **100 mg bd – 400 mg tds–qid** | **bronchospasm, headache, bradycardia** |
| **Methyldopa** | **central action** | **250 mg bd – 750 mg tds–qid** | **depression, dry mouth, sedation** |
| **Nifedipine modified release** | **calcium channel blocker** | **20 mg od – 60 mg bd** | **headache (first dose effect), flushing, tachycardia, peripheral oedema** |

**Second line, deferred to specialist use: PRAZOSIN, CLONIDINE, HYDRALAZINE** [S1].

> ⚠️ **IN CHRONIC HYPERTENSION, PATIENTS ON ACE INHIBITORS, ARBs OR THIAZIDE DIURETICS MUST BE COUNSELLED
> ON THE INCREASED RISK OF CONGENITAL ABNORMALITIES AND SWITCHED TO AN ALTERNATIVE AGENT** [S1].
> **AMH says the same in one line: AVOID ACE INHIBITORS AND SARTANS DURING PREGNANCY** [A4].
> **This is a preconception conversation, not an antenatal one.**

**AMH's target and first-line list agree with S1's drugs:** **BP <135/85 mmHg for stable chronic,
gestational or non-severe pre-eclampsia; methyldopa, labetalol and nifedipine first-line, oral hydralazine
also considered** [A4]. ⚠️ **For severe hypertension — systolic >160 or diastolic >110 mmHg — AMH names
oral nifedipine as a CONVENTIONAL TABLET AVAILABLE THROUGH THE SAS, plus parenteral labetalol and
hydralazine** [A4].

### When to send her in [S1]

| | Threshold |
|---|---|
| **Referral to a tertiary unit for consideration of admission** | **BP >140/90 to <159/109 mmHg WITH clinical concerns for maternal or foetal wellbeing** |
| ⚠️ **Referral regardless** | **severe BP >160/110 mmHg** |

## Magnesium sulfate [AMH]

⚠️ **S1 does not dose magnesium sulfate at all. This section is entirely licensed and unquoted.**

> **MAGNESIUM SULFATE IS THE DRUG OF CHOICE** for **preventing seizures in pre-eclampsia**, **treating
> eclamptic seizures** and **preventing their recurrence** [A1].
> **It reduces the development of eclampsia in women with pre-eclampsia BY ABOUT HALF compared with
> placebo and may also reduce maternal mortality.** ⚠️ **OUTCOME FOR THE BABIES IS UNAFFECTED** [A1].

| Indication | Dose [A2] |
|---|---|
| **Pre-eclampsia** | **IV 4 g over 10–15 minutes**, then **1 g/hour**, continued **AT LEAST 24 HOURS AFTER DELIVERY** |
| **Eclampsia** | **IV 4 g over 5–10 minutes** — ⚠️ **a FASTER bolus** — then **1 g/hour**, continued **at least 24 hours AFTER THE LAST SEIZURE**; **a further 2–4 g over 5–10 minutes if there is another seizure** |

### ⚠️ Before you start, and while it runs [A2]

> **CHECK FIRST:** **a knee or other tendon reflex is PRESENT** · **respiratory rate >16/minute** · **urine
> output >100 mL in the previous 4 hours, or >25 mL/hour** [A2].
>
> **MONITOR:** **BP, heart rate, respiratory rate, oxygen saturation, urine output and reflexes.**
> ⚠️ **STOP THE INFUSION if signs of toxicity develop, and DO NOT RESUME until signs are normal** [A2].
>
> ⚠️ **HAVE CALCIUM GLUCONATE INJECTION — THE ANTIDOTE — AVAILABLE** [A2].

**Signs of toxicity: LOSS OF DEEP TENDON REFLEXES and RESPIRATORY DEPRESSION from neuromuscular blockade.**
Others: **thirst, muscle weakness, hypotension, bradycardia, CNS depression, coma, circulatory collapse,
cardiac arrest** [A2].

⚠️ **Contraindicated in heart block and hypermagnesaemia.** **In myasthenia gravis it may markedly increase
weakness, particularly of the respiratory muscles — monitor closely** [A2].

> ⚠️ **NIFEDIPINE INTERACTS.** **In obstetric patients being treated with nifedipine, nifedipine may
> increase the effects of magnesium sulfate and the risk of hypotension: use cautiously and monitor BP,
> deep tendon reflexes and respiratory function** [A2]. **Nifedipine is one of S1's three first-line
> drugs** — so this combination is the expected one, not an unusual one.

**Renal:** **magnesium is predominantly renally cleared.** **In reduced renal function or oliguria (urine
output <100 mL over 4 hours), monitor plasma magnesium regularly, e.g. every 6 hours, and reduce the dose
if necessary. Therapeutic range 1.7–3.5 mmol/L** [A2]. **It is UNNECESSARY to monitor plasma magnesium if
renal function is normal** [A2].

**Safe in pregnancy; unlikely to be of concern in breastfeeding** [A2].

## ⚠️ HELLP [S1]

> **Complications include placental abruption, placental failure, extreme prematurity and maternal cardiac
> and cerebrovascular complications — amounting to a 7–70% PERINATAL MORTALITY RATE and a 1–24% MATERNAL
> MORTALITY RATE** [S1].

> ⚠️ **ATYPICAL HELLP: haematological and biochemical derangement may PRECEDE hypertension and proteinuria,
> so HYPERTENSION AND PROTEINURIA ARE NOT ESSENTIAL FOR THE DIAGNOSIS** [S1].
> **A normal BP does not exclude it.**

**If HELLP is suspected, refer urgently to a tertiary obstetric unit** [S1].

## Timing of birth [S1] — referral context only

- **Without pre-eclampsia and BP below 160/110 mmHg: delivery should NOT be offered before 37 weeks'
  gestation unless there is another medical indication** [S1].
- **With pre-eclampsia: birth is recommended at 37 weeks' gestation** [S1].
- **If preterm birth is expected before 34 weeks, corticosteroids are recommended for foetal lung
  maturation** — a senior obstetrician's decision with the patient [S1].
- **AMH: pre-eclampsia resolves postpartum — ⚠️ ALTHOUGH NEW MATERNAL COMPLICATIONS MAY OCCUR UP TO A WEEK
  AFTER DELIVERY** [A1].

## ⚠️ Postpartum — where the GP work is

> ⚠️ **BLOOD PRESSURE PEAKS ON DAYS 3–6 POSTNATALLY** in both normotensive patients and those with a
> hypertensive disorder [S1]. **The peak is after most women have gone home.**

| Action [S1] | |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ **Change methyldopa to another agent** | **because of susceptibility to POSTNATAL DEPRESSION on the agent** |
| **Labetalol and nifedipine MR** | **continue to be safe postpartum** |
| **Good practice** | **change to ENALAPRIL 5 mg od – 10 mg bd** — safe and effective, and **preferred for less frequent dosing** than labetalol or nifedipine |
| **Breastfeeding** | **these medications are all safe** |

⚠️ **Note the reversal: ACE inhibitors are avoided in pregnancy [S1][A4] and are the preferred switch
immediately after it.**

> **At six weeks, a routine postpartum review should include BP and a review of symptoms and signs.**
> ⚠️ **HYPERTENSION INCLUDING PRE-ECLAMPSIA CAN BE DIAGNOSED IN THE POSTPARTUM PERIOD AND CERTAINLY UP TO
> SIX WEEKS POSTPARTUM** [S1].

**Where gestational hypertension and pre-eclampsia do NOT resolve by 12 weeks, this may herald essential
hypertension — but secondary causes need to be considered** [S1].

## Long-term [S1]

- **Check BP and perform a cardiovascular risk assessment ANNUALLY** in women who have had a hypertensive
  disorder of pregnancy and who do not have essential hypertension [S1].
- ⚠️ **In subsequent pregnancies the risk is approximately 20%** — so **discuss prophylactic low-dose
  aspirin and calcium from as early as 12 weeks** [S1].
