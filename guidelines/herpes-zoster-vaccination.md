# Zoster (herpes zoster)

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** shingles vaccination — who, which schedule, funding, prior Zostavax — plus recognition and complications. Antiviral treatment is covered separately.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 10 of 10 claims quoted verbatim from the **Australian Immunisation Handbook**; **13 fragments re-checked by machine. 5 quoted doses.**

> **Treatment of shingles** — famciclovir, valaciclovir or aciclovir within 72 hours — is in [`drug-choice-for-selected-infections`](drug-choice-for-selected-infections.md), **named there without doses**. This guideline is prevention.

## Who

- **All immunocompetent people aged 50 and over are recommended zoster vaccine.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **The lifetime risk of shingles for someone who lives to 80 is around 50%**, higher again if immunocompromised. [S1]

## Schedule

- **Immunocompetent: Shingrix, 2 doses, 2–6 months apart.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Immunocompromised: 2 doses of Shingrix 1–2 months apart** — a shorter interval. [S1]
- **No booster is currently recommended.** Current data show **good protection at 10 years after 2 doses**. [S1]

## Funding

- **Shingrix is funded under the NIP for all adults 65 and over, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people 50 and over, and selected groups 18 and over with moderate or severe immunocompromise.** [S1]

## Previously had Zostavax

- ⚠️ **People who had Zostavax can receive Shingrix** — Zostavax protection **wanes significantly from around 5 years**. Leave **at least 12 months** between them. [S1]

## Recognition and complications

- Shingles is **reactivation of varicella-zoster virus** in someone who has had chickenpox, usually a **painful, self-limiting vesicular rash in a dermatomal distribution**. [S1]
- ⚠️ **80% have a prodrome 48–72 hours before the rash** — headache, photophobia, malaise, and itching, tingling or severe pain in the dermatome. The rash usually lasts **10–15 days**. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Post-herpetic neuralgia is the most common complication** — about **1 in 5 cases over 80**, compared with about 1 in 10 at younger ages. Complications are commoner in older and immunocompromised people. [S1]
