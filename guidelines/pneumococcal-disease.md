# Pneumococcal disease

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** vaccination — 20vPCV for children, 21vPCV for adults — by age, Indigenous status and risk; plus recognition and notification.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 13 of 13 claims quoted verbatim from the **Australian Immunisation Handbook**; **15 fragments re-checked by machine. 8 quoted doses.**

> ⚠️ **This source is more current than AMH on adult vaccine.** The Handbook says **21vPCV replaced 13vPCV in 2026**. This compendium's AMH-derived [`immunisation`](immunisation.md) guideline still lists **13-, 15- or 20-valent** for adults. See *Unresolved* #1.

## Two vaccines, split by age

- **20vPCV for children (under 18); 21vPCV for adults.** [S1]

## Children

- **Non-Indigenous children without a risk condition: 20vPCV at 2, 4 and 12 months** — 3 doses. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children, and all children under 12 months with a risk condition: 20vPCV at 2, 4, 6 AND 12 months** — **4 doses, one more than the standard schedule.** [S1]
- **From 12 months to under 18 with a risk condition: a dose of 20vPCV at diagnosis.** [S1]
- The **first dose can be given as early as 6 weeks**; if so, the next dose stays **at 4 months**. [S1]

## Adults

- **Non-Indigenous adults without a risk condition: one dose of 21vPCV at 65 or older.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander adults without a risk condition: one dose of 21vPCV at 25 or older — 40 years earlier than for non-Indigenous adults.** [S1]
- **Adults 18–64 who get 21vPCV for a risk condition do not need another dose at 65.** Anyone previously vaccinated with 23vPPV, 13vPCV, 15vPCV or 20vPCV gets **21vPCV at least 12 months after their last dose**. [S1]
- ⚠️ **In 2026, 21vPCV replaced 13vPCV as the funded and recommended vaccine** under the NIP for non-Indigenous adults 65 and over and Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander adults 25 and over. [S1]

## Recognition

- *Streptococcus pneumoniae* causes **invasive disease — meningitis, pneumonia, bacteraemia —** and non-invasive disease including **otitis media**. [S1]
- ⚠️ **In children the commonest form is bacteraemia without a focus — about 70% of invasive disease.** **Meningitis is the least common but most severe.** [S1]
- Higher risk or severity with **household crowding, cigarette smoke, childcare attendance, excess alcohol** and some chronic conditions. ⚠️ **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people carry a disproportionately high burden.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Invasive pneumococcal disease is notifiable in every state and territory.** [S1]
