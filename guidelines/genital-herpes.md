# Genital herpes simplex virus (HSV)

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-21 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults, primary care.

> **Australian primary source.** ASHM *Australian STI Management Guidelines for use in primary care* —
> *Genital herpes simplex virus (HSV)* [S1], modified 30 June 2026.

## Treat first, test second

> **Initial episodes may be severe, and treatment should never be delayed while waiting for a test
> result** [S1].

This inverts the sequence used everywhere else in the set. Microbiological confirmation by NAAT **is
desirable but need not delay treatment** [S1]; the auditable outcome for the topic is that
**100% of patients presenting with an initial episode will be offered treatment** [S1].

## ⚠️ Do not screen asymptomatic people with HSV serology

> **Do not screen asymptomatic people with serological tests for HSV types 1 or 2.** Use of serology is
> limited to specific situations where results will provide meaningful clinical information (e.g. an
> asymptomatic pregnant partner of a newly diagnosed person) and **should be done in conjunction with an
> infectious diseases specialist** [S1].

The third explicit recommendation *against* in the set, after ABCD² in TIA and asymptomatic
*M. genitalium* screening.

**Diagnosis is a swab, not a blood test:** HSV NAAT on a swab from the **base of an ulcer or a deroofed
vesicle** [S1]. It is very sensitive and specific, but **false positives and false negatives are rare rather
than impossible**, and a negative result **may not** exclude HSV — consider the anogenital ulcer pathway [S1].

**Self-collection at the first onset of recurrent symptoms** may be useful in patients who have repeatedly
failed to get confirmation when attending services [S1] — the lesion is often gone by the time an
appointment arrives.

## Treatment

| Situation | Recommended [S1] | Alternative [S1] |
|---|---|---|
| **Initial episode** | **Valaciclovir 500 mg PO BD for 5–10 days** | Aciclovir 400 mg PO TDS for 5–10 days |
| **Recurrence — episodic**<br>*self-initiated at the first hint of symptoms* | **Valaciclovir 500 mg PO BD for 3 days** | Famciclovir 1 g orally twice a day for 1 day **or** aciclovir 800 mg TDS for 2 days |
| **Recurrence — suppressive** | **Valaciclovir 500 mg PO daily for 6 months** | Famciclovir 250 mg PO BD for 6 months **or** aciclovir 400 mg BD |
| **Suppression in pregnancy** | **Valaciclovir 500 mg PO BD from 36 weeks until birth** | Aciclovir 400 mg TDS from 36 weeks until birth |

**PBS streamline authority scripts are required** [S1].

Practical points [S1]:

- Episodic therapy is **given to the patient in advance to start themselves** — its value depends on being
  taken at the first hint of symptoms, not at the next appointment.
- **Initial episodes may need the full 10 days** if symptoms are slow to resolve.
- **Consider whether the patient can adhere to the dosing frequency** when choosing between agents — the
  reason the once-daily and one-day options are listed at all.
- **Review the need for suppressive therapy every 6 months**, as recurrences usually become less frequent
  and less severe with time.
- **Frequent recurrences (e.g. 2 or 3 times per month) or immunosuppression may need higher doses** —
  PBS **authority** required for the increased quantity.
- **Seek specialist advice** for people living with HIV or immunosuppression, and for any complicated
  presentation or allergy to the principal choice.
- **Ongoing symptoms despite antivirals should prompt consideration of another cause** — anogenital ulcer
  and genital dermatology pathways.

## Symptom relief, which the source treats as management not comfort

- Regular analgesia; **topical lignocaine** for pain from erosions, fissures and ulcers [S1].
- **Urinating in a bath or shower relieves superficial dysuria** [S1].
- **Neuropathic bladder requires urgent catheterisation and referral** [S1] — the one red flag on the page.
- Avoid intimate contact until symptoms have resolved; waterproof dressings as barriers [S1].

## The psychosocial load is part of the diagnosis

> **Genital HSV is highly stigmatised and poorly understood in the community. Patient education to address
> both clinical and psychosocial concerns is required at time of diagnosis** [S1].

> The psychosocial impact **can be profound but is often based on misinformation** [S1].

The facts the source says to give the patient, because they do the work [S1]:

| Fact | Figure |
|---|---|
| Community prevalence, HSV-1 | **70–80%** |
| Community prevalence, HSV-2 | **12–15%** |
| Primary genital infections caused by **HSV-1** in young people | **more than 50%** |

- **Transmission can occur from someone unaware they have the infection**, and first symptoms can appear
  **days or years** after acquisition [S1] — which is what defuses the assumption of a partner's infidelity.
- Most HSV is **asymptomatic or mild enough that diagnosis is never sought** [S1].
- Recurrences are more common in the first year with **HSV-2** [S1].
- **Refer to a psychologist** for patients unable to accept the diagnosis, significantly distressed, or
  **anxious about having the infection when tested negative** [S1]. Some Australian sexual health services
  have counselling services.

## Pregnancy

- **Neonatal infection is an extremely rare complication** [S1], but transmission may occur in pregnancy,
  during delivery, or **skin-to-skin post-natally via oro-labial HSV** [S1].
- **Greatest risk of sequelae for the baby is when HSV is acquired in the third trimester or close to
  delivery** [S1] — a new infection late, not an old one.
- **Routinely commence suppression from 36 weeks** in people who know they have the infection, **with or
  without lesions**; **start earlier** if there have been multiple recurrent lesions during pregnancy [S1].
- Talk to people with HSV about suppressive therapy to reduce transmission to a partner **before or
  during** pregnancy [S1]. Refer to a sexual health specialist for further information.

## Contact tracing and follow-up

> **Contact tracing is not recommended for HSV infections** [S1].

The only infection in this set where tracing is recommended against — but patients **may need support if
they wish to disclose** to current or future partners [S1].

**HSV is not a notifiable disease** [S1]. **Test of cure and retesting: not required** [S1].

**Review a week after first commencing treatment** to evaluate response, complete any sexual health testing
deferred because symptoms were too severe, and educate about condoms, contraception, HIV PrEP/PEP, safe
injecting, consent, cervical screening and HAV/HBV/HPV vaccination [S1].
