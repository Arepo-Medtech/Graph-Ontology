# Post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis (PSGN) in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognition, red flags, investigations, fluid, diuretic and blood-pressure management, disposition and follow-up of PSGN in children. Paediatric hypertension generally is `hypertension-children`; nephrotic syndrome is `nephrotic-syndrome-children`; the other post-streptococcal sequela is `acute-rheumatic-fever-and-rhd`. No adult or other-source PSGN guideline exists in this compendium.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 25 of 25 claims quoted verbatim from the **RCH Melbourne Clinical Practice Guideline *Post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis (PSGN)*** (Last updated June 2025); **32 fragments re-checked by machine. 4 quoted doses.**

> ⚠️ **Red flags — severe hypertension, critical electrolytes, pulmonary oedema, oliguria/anuria — need urgent discussion with retrieval, paediatric nephrology and/or ICU.** Otherwise most PSGN is managed locally with close follow-up.

---

## Key points

- **PSGN is usually mild and self-limiting**, but is a **strong risk factor for chronic kidney disease in Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people**, in whom incidence is much higher. [S1]
- **Priorities: blood pressure, oedema and early detection of complications.** Most cases can be managed locally, with close follow-up to confirm resolution by 8 weeks; microscopic haematuria may persist for up to 2 years. [S1]
- **Typically 2–4 weeks after group A streptococcal pharyngitis or impetigo.** Classical triad: **hypertension, haematuria (macro- or microscopic), oedema.** [S1]

## Red flags

- ⚠️ **Urgent management and discussion with retrieval, paediatric nephrology and/or ICU for:** severe hypertension (over the 95th centile + 30 mmHg for sex/age/height) · critical electrolytes (usually hyperkalaemia and acidosis) · pulmonary oedema · anuria or oliguria (under 0.5 mL/kg/hr) despite adequate intravascular volume. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Rising creatinine, oliguria/anuria, acidosis, refractory hyperkalaemia and hypertension can signal rapidly progressive GN (RPGN).** [S1]

## Assessment

- **Ask about** recent streptococcal infection, swelling, urine colour and volume, headache, altered mental state, blurred vision, vomiting, breathlessness, chest pain, palpitations. **Prior haematuria** suggests IgA nephropathy or Alport syndrome; **prior rash or joint swelling** suggests lupus or IgA vasculitis nephritis. [S1]
- **Weigh and compare with pre-illness weight** — it may be the only sign of fluid overload. **Confirm BP manually** against age and sex references; look for pulmonary oedema and end-organ effects (retinopathy, cardiac failure, facial nerve palsy, encephalopathy). [S1]

## Investigations

- **Investigations:** urine dipstick and MCS · spot urine protein/creatinine ratio · FBE · UEC, LFTs, albumin · **C3, C4** · ASOT ± anti-DNAse B. [S1]
- **Consider:** throat/skin swabs if active streptococcal infection · CXR if effusion or pulmonary oedema is a concern · renal ultrasound with Doppler if the diagnosis is unclear, flank pain, atypical history or poor diuretic response · blood culture if febrile or unwell. [S1]
- **Raised creatinine, atypical history or possible systemic disease:** consider autoimmune testing with nephrology and rheumatology. [S1]

## Fluid and oedema

- **Strict fluid balance, daily weights** (same time, same scales) and urine output in mL/kg/hr. [S1]
- **Fluid overload and oedema: furosemide 0.5–1 mg/kg (max 40 mg) IV or oral 12-hourly.** Consider IV if very oedematous (gut oedema may impair absorption); move to PRN as overload improves and stop once no longer oedematous and/or back to pre-illness weight. [S1]
- **Low or no-added-salt diet; fluid restrict to 750 mL/m²/day** (about half maintenance). [S1]

## Infection and kidney function

- **Active streptococcal infection: antibiotics per local guidance.** Consider family contact tracing, prophylaxis and eradication per local ID and public health guidance (PSGN is notifiable in some services). [S1]
- **AKI: calculate eGFR (revised Schwartz, over 2 years) = height (cm) × 36.5 ÷ creatinine (µmol/L).** Adjust medicines; if creatinine is raised at presentation, monitor bloods for progression to RPGN. [S1]

## Blood pressure

- **Target SBP below the 95th centile** for age, sex and height. Hypertension is mostly fluid-driven — **furosemide will treat it.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **SBP over the 95th centile on two readings 1 hr apart: PRN immediate-release nifedipine 0.25 mg/kg (max 20 mg) orally, up to 6-hourly.** [S1]
- **After day 2, if still needing more than 2 nifedipine doses a day: regular amlodipine 0.1 mg/kg/day, up to 0.6 mg/kg/day (max 10 mg)**, with PRN nifedipine to titrate. [S1]

## Escalation and discharge

- **Manage locally where possible** — many cases present to rural and remote services, and local care supports family-centred, culturally safe care. **Consult paediatric nephrology** for any red flag or hypertension needing multiple agents. [S1]
- **Transfer** for early significant renal impairment or systemic illness beyond what PSGN would explain, or care beyond the local hospital. [S1]
- **Discharge when** BP and renal function are stable or improving and weight is at or near baseline. **No furosemide at home**, though another agent may be needed for BP. [S1]

## Follow-up

- **Weekly BP and urinalysis; paediatric review at 8 weeks** with repeat C3/C4 and renal function. **Wean antihypertensives** as able — usually within 6 weeks. [S1]
- **Microscopic haematuria and intermittent proteinuria may persist up to 2 years** — but not macroscopic haematuria, proteinuria over 3+, or proteinuria that is increasing. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Refer to paediatric nephrology (consider biopsy) if:** macroscopic haematuria beyond 5 days or recurring · nephritic or nephrotic features · BP persistently over the 95th percentile · **cannot wean antihypertensives by 8 weeks** · **C3 still low** or renal function abnormal at 8 weeks · rising proteinuria. [S1]
- **Persistent proteinuria** (early-morning urine protein/creatinine ratio) — also a referral trigger: over 200 mg/mmol for 4 weeks · over 100 mg/mmol for 3 months · over 50 mg/mmol for 6 months. [S1]
