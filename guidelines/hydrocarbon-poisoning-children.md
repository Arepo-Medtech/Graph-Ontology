# Hydrocarbon poisoning in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** ingestion and inhalation of hydrocarbons (petrol, kerosene, solvents, essential oils and similar) in children: aspiration, CNS and cardiac toxicity, investigation, supportive care and disposition. Related RCH pages: `essential-oil-poisoning-children`, `eucalyptus-oil-poisoning-children`, `camphor-poisoning-children`. ## Key points

- **CNS, respiratory and cardiac effects are the main concern.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Inhalation injury may appear up to 6 hours after exposure.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Less than 5 mL of pure essential oil can cause significant CNS toxicity in children.** [S1]

## Background

- **Hydrocarbons can cause rapid-onset CNS depression and seizures**; volatile hydrocarbons can be aspirated and cause **chemical pneumonitis**. Dysrhythmias are less common. [S1]
- **Exposure is accidental (often younger children) or deliberate (often inhaled — 'sniffing' or 'chroming').** [S1]
- **Sources:** petrol, kerosene, lighter fluid, paraffin oil, 2-stroke fuel, diesel, solvents, white spirit, lubricating oil, furniture polishes, essential oils, mineral turpentine. [S1]

## Assessment

- **Assess:** all deliberate self-poisoning or significant accidental exposure · any symptomatic child · any child whose developmental age does not fit accidental poisoning. [S1]
- **History:** intent · type of compound · quantity ingested · duration of any inhalation · co-ingestants such as paracetamol. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Coughing, gagging or choking means aspiration.** Wheeze, tachypnoea, hypoxia, haemoptysis and pulmonary oedema signal evolving chemical pneumonitis. [S1]
- **Dysrhythmias occur early.** CNS depression, coma and seizures may follow large acute exposures, **usually within 2 hours.** [S1]

## Investigations

- **Asymptomatic child, small ingestion: usually no investigation.** Significant ingestion or symptoms: **12-lead ECG and cardiac monitoring for 4 hours**, FBE, UEC, LFTs, VBG, and CXR if respiratory symptoms. [S1]
- **Deliberate poisoning:** screen for co-ingestants — BSL and paracetamol level. [S1]

## Acute management

- **Supportive care; intubate early for progressive CNS depression.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Ventricular dysrhythmias:** advanced life support · intubate, hyperventilate and correct hypoxia · correct electrolytes · **withhold catecholamine inotropes if possible.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Seizures: benzodiazepines first line; avoid phenytoin.** [S1]
- **Chemical pneumonitis is managed supportively** (oxygen, bronchodilators, NIV or intubation if severe). **No corticosteroids or prophylactic antibiotics**; fever is common after aspiration, so **withhold antibiotics until there is objective evidence of bacterial infection.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Activated charcoal is specifically contraindicated:** it does not bind hydrocarbons and increases aspiration risk. [S1]

## Disposition

- **Asymptomatic with normal vital signs: observe 6 hours after exposure before discharge.** Milder respiratory or CNS symptoms: admit for longer observation ± supportive care. [S1]
- **Consider admission for all adolescents with an intentional overdose.** Poisons Information: 13 11 26. [S1]
- ⚠️ **CNS depression, seizures or dysrhythmia → paediatric intensive care.** PIPER: 1300 137 650. [S1]
- **Discharge criteria:** normal GCS · normal ECG · no cough, dyspnoea or wheeze · normal observations including oximetry · observation period complete · after deliberate ingestion, low assessed risk of further self-harm. [S1]
