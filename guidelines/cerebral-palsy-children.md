# Cerebral palsy in children

## What it is

- **CP is a non-progressive motor disorder with many different causes. Every child needs investigation** to find the underlying cause and exclude progressive conditions. [S1]
- **The diagnosis is clinical, typically before 12–24 months**, though it can be made as early as 3 months corrected gestational age. [S1]
- **The cause is non-progressive but clinical features evolve — review regularly for complications.** Infants with, or at high risk of, CP benefit from **early intervention**. [S1]

## When to refer

- **Refer (using corrected age) for:** persistent hand fisting or head lag beyond 4 months · unable to sit unsupported beyond 9 months · leg stiffness at 6–12 months · hand preference before 12 months · not walking by 18 months. [S1]
- **Consider referral for:** a persistent Moro reflex beyond 6 months · consistent asymmetric or toe-walking beyond 12 months · persisting primitive reflexes. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Think of another diagnosis (metabolic, neurodegenerative) if there is:** loss of skills or progressive symptoms · dysmorphic features · marked worsening with fasting or illness · muscle atrophy · sensory loss · a family history of another genetic condition. [S1]

## Investigate

- **MRI brain at term equivalent if impairment is severe or progressive**; consider repeating **after 2 years** if the first was normal, inconclusive or not done. About 10–15% of children with clinical CP have a normal MRI at 2 years. [S1]
- **Genetics: microarray first line**; if normal, consider whole exome sequencing (ideally trio) with or without genetics referral — 25% or more may have a genetic cause. [S1]
- **Other tests with the relevant specialist** where infection, metabolic or genetic causes are suggested — eg blood gas, CK, ammonia, urine metabolic screen; **thrombophilia screen for hemiplegia, infarct on imaging or a family history**. [S1]

## Treat tone

- **A multidisciplinary team is essential** — physiotherapy, occupational therapy and speech pathology, with dietitians, orthotists, social work, psychology, education and nursing. [S1]
- **Spasticity:** focal/segmental — **injected botulinum toxin**; generalised — **oral baclofen first line**. Intrathecal baclofen or selective dorsal rhizotomy are for rare cases, by rehab services. [S1]
- **Dystonia (weak evidence):** focal — botulinum toxin; generalised — **oral baclofen first line**, adding **oral/transdermal clonidine or oral gabapentin** if insufficient. [S1]

## Musculoskeletal surveillance

- ⚠️ **Every child with CP needs hip surveillance.** Start with a clinical assessment at **12 months**, with or without an AP hip X-ray (not needed in GMFCS I); concerns prompt orthopaedic referral. [S1]
- **Greater motor impairment means greater risk** of hip dislocation and scoliosis. [S1]
- **Knee surveillance:** monitor for knee flexion deformity in GMFCS I–III from 5 years to skeletal maturity — it can cost the child walking. [S1]

## Screen for associated problems

- **Saliva control:** anterior drooling causes skin breakdown and social difficulty; posterior drooling causes choking and aspiration. **Consider anticholinergics**; botulinum toxin to the salivary glands or surgery in some. [S1]
- **Growth and feeding:** monitor growth and micronutrients; **consider enteral tube feeding** for weight gain, deficiencies, a medication route and to reduce aspiration. [S1]
- **Respiratory:** increased risk of aspiration, restrictive lung disease, recurrent chest infection and sleep apnoea. **Immunise, including yearly influenza vaccine.** [S1]
- **How common:** hearing loss about 10% · vision loss 30–50% · intellectual disability up to 45% · epilepsy up to 50%. Arrange regular audiology and ophthalmology. [S1]
- **Formal cognitive assessment and educational support**, ideally in the year before school. [S1]
- **Low bone density:** increased pathological fracture risk. **Check vitamin D yearly**; optimise calcium, exercise and sunlight. [S1]
- **Urinary incontinence** may need medical management or clean intermittent catheterisation for neurogenic bladder; consider urology referral. [S1]

## Disposition

- **Involve the local paediatric team** for an infant with risk factors or features of CP, or a child needing admission. [S1]
