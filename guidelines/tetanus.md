## Schedule

- **Children: 5 doses at 2, 4, 6 and 18 months and 4 years**, given as **DTPa-containing** vaccine. [S1]
- **Adolescents at 11–13.** ⚠️ **All adults: a booster at 50, and at 65 if more than 10 years since the last dose.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Tetanus-toxoid vaccines are only available in Australia as combination vaccines** — with pertussis and diphtheria. [S1]

## ⚠️ The never-vaccinated adult

- **Adolescents and adults who have never had tetanus toxoid: 3 doses at least 4 weeks apart, then boosters at 10 and 20 years after the primary course.** [S1]
- Post-exposure management — **with or without tetanus immunoglobulin — depends on the nature of the wound and the vaccination history.** [S1]

## Recognition

- ⚠️ **Tetanus is acute and can be fatal.** The neurotoxin causes **muscle rigidity with painful spasms**. **Generalised tetanus is the most common form.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Early signs: trismus (lockjaw), dysphagia, and stiffness or pain in the neck, shoulder and back.** Some develop **paroxysmal, violent, painful generalised spasms**. [S1]
- **Rare in Australia because of high vaccination coverage** — and ⚠️ **notifiable in every state and territory.** [S1]
