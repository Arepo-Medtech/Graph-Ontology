# Peripheral intravenous catheters

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-22 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** deciding on, inserting, maintaining and removing a PIVC.

> **Australian primary source.** Australian Commission on Safety and Quality in Health Care (ACSQHC).
> ***Management of Peripheral Intravenous Catheters Clinical Care Standard*** [S1]. **Ten quality
> statements; eleven indicators.**

**The commonest invasive procedure in hospital medicine, and the first standard in this compendium about a
device rather than a condition.**

## ⚠️ The densest and most bedside-checkable indicator set in the compendium

| Indicator [S1] | |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ **1** | **Proportion of patients with a PIVC in situ that has not been used for a therapeutic purpose since it was inserted** |
| ⚠️ **2** | **Proportion of patients with a PIVC in situ that can identify the reason for the device** |
| **3** | Evidence of a locally approved policy on **competency** in insertion, monitoring and removal |
| **4a** | Evidence of local arrangements supporting **device selection** |
| ⚠️ **4b** | **Proportion of patients with a PIVC in situ over an area of flexion** *(24 hours or longer)* |
| **5a** | Evidence of a locally approved policy for **first-attempt insertion** |
| ⚠️ **5b** | **Proportion of patients who *report* their PIVC was inserted on the first attempt** |
| **7a** | Evidence of a locally approved policy on **documentation** |
| **7b** | **Proportion with the indication for insertion documented** |
| ⚠️ **8a** | **Proportion who have their insertion site inspected for complications at least every 8 hours** |
| ⚠️ **8b** | **Proportion with a clean, dry and secure PIVC dressing** |
| **9** | Proportion **assessed in the last 24 hours** to determine ongoing need |
| **10** | Proportion **not used for a therapeutic purpose** in a defined period |

**Most of these can be answered by walking a ward.** Is the dressing clean and dry? Is the cannula in the
elbow crease? Can the patient say why it's there? Has it been used?

**Two are unlike anything else in this set.** ⚠️ **Indicator 2 measures the patient's knowledge** — not
whether they were informed, but whether they can say. And ⚠️ **indicator 5b is patient-reported**: the number
of attempts as the patient experienced it, not as the inserter recorded it.

**Indicators 1 and 10 both count the idle cannula.** The standard measures the same failure twice, from
insertion and from dwell.

## ⚠️ Statement 1 — the IV route is the last one to consider

> **Consider whether another route of administration (for example, the oral, intramuscular, subcutaneous,
> rectal, intra-osseous or intra-peritoneal route) is appropriate, before using the IV route** [S1]
>
> **Ensure that organisational policies and processes support the consideration of all routes… and that the
> IV route is only used if other routes are not suitable** [S1]

**And the two reasons that are not reasons** [S1]:

> ⚠️ **Collection of pathology samples is not a valid reason to insert a PIVC; although this often happens in
> emergency departments, it can lead to unused PIVCs being left in.**
>
> ⚠️ **PIVCs should not be used to obtain blood cultures as this leads to an increased risk of contamination
> to the blood culture and PIVC.**

**The standard names the habit and the department.** Bloods are taken, a cannula goes in because the needle
is already there, and it stays — which is what indicators 1 and 10 count.

**Insertion of a PIVC pre-emptively may be appropriate for patients at risk of clinical deterioration** [S1],
so the anticipatory cannula is allowed; **the specimen cannula is not.**

## Statement 2 — and the patient's job

**Ask the patient about previous PIVC insertions to identify any concerns, such as difficulty with particular
access sites, allergies to tapes or antiseptics, or certain sites where a PIVC should not be inserted** [S1].
**Invite the patient to ask questions, and use methods such as teach-back to confirm they understand** [S1].

⚠️ > **Continue to ask the patient if they have any concerns while the PIVC is in place and for at least 48
> hours after it has been removed** [S1].

**The duty of enquiry outlives the device.** And the consumer text makes the patient an auditor:

⚠️ > **If your PIVC has not been used in the last 24 hours, ask your clinician if it is still needed** [S1].

**A national standard instructing patients to challenge their own treatment** — the same measure as indicator
9, handed to the person in the bed.

## Statements 4, 5 and 6 — site, first attempt, insertion

**Site** [S1]: **preferably veins in the non-dominant arm**; **ask the patient if they have a preference for
the location and follow it if therapy can be delivered safely**; ⚠️ **avoid areas of flexion unless clinically
necessary — re-site cubital fossa IVs as soon as appropriate**; ⚠️ **do not use veins in the lower extremities
unless necessary**. **Use a site likely to last the duration of the prescribed therapy to maximise dwell time
and patient comfort** [S1]. **Use devices with safety-engineered protective features to reduce the risk of
injury involving a sharp** [S1].

**First attempt** — a quality statement of its own [S1]:

> **Difficult or unsuccessful insertions can cause bruising, thrombosis, delays in treatment, reduced access
> to veins for future health needs, and undue pain and anxiety for the patient.**

**Strategies**: **escalating to a more experienced clinician** · **technology-assisted devices such as
ultrasound** · **adjunct supportive therapies such as local anaesthetics**; and ⚠️ **for paediatric patients,
consider using parental support or child play therapy** [S1].

⚠️ > **If more than one attempt to insert the PIVC is required, specify the reason (for example, lack of more
> experienced staff to escalate to in the after-hours setting, or patient dehydration) in the patient's
> medical record** [S1].

**A second attempt requires a written reason, and the standard's own worked example is a staffing
problem** — it anticipates that the honest answer is often *there was nobody more senior on at night.*

**Insertion** [S1]: **standard precautions including aseptic technique** and the **'5 Moments for Hand
Hygiene'**; ⚠️ **if there is hair at the insertion site, remove with clippers as shaving with a razor
increases the risk of infection**; **decontaminate the skin with 2% chlorhexidine gluconate in 70% alcohol
unless contraindicated, allowing for appropriate cleaning and drying time**; **secure with a sterile,
transparent, semipermeable dressing**; and **label in accordance with the *National Standard for
User-applied Labelling of Injectable Medicines, Fluids and Lines***.

## Statement 8 — every shift, and how to look

**Inspect at least once per shift (or per eight hours), when accessing the device, or if the patient raises
any concerns** [S1]. **More frequent inspection may be required… for example for paediatric patients** [S1].

**Check** [S1]: **pain, swelling or redness at the insertion site, by visual inspection through the
transparent dressing and gentle palpation through the dressing** · **the condition of the patient's veins,
and whether they have become hardened or thrombosed** · **signs of localised or systemic infection** ·
**leakage of fluid, signs of occlusion, infiltration or extravasation** · **whether the PIVC remains
appropriately dressed and secured**.

**Inspection happens through the dressing, not by lifting it** — which is why indicator 8b measures the
dressing's integrity rather than the site's appearance.

**Access**: ⚠️ **decontaminate needleless connectors before and after access with 70% alcohol… and allow to
fully air dry** [S1]. **Flush… at intervals according to local policy, to maintain line patency, reduce the
risk of blockage, and prevent mixing of incompatible medicines or fluids** [S1].

## ⚠️ Statement 9 — the IV-to-oral switch

> **Review and document the ongoing clinical need for a patient's PIVC at least once per day, or more often
> if clinically indicated.** ⚠️ **Review whether switching from IV to oral therapy is possible. Remove the
> PIVC immediately if it is no longer required** [S1].

**This is the antimicrobial stewardship IV-to-oral switch, arriving from the device side.** The stewardship
standard in this compendium asks the same question about the drug; **this one asks it about the line, daily,
and requires the answer in writing.**

## ⚠️ Statement 10 — two legitimate replacement policies

**Remove at the first sign of malfunction or local site complications** [S1]. **Signs**: **pain and tenderness
at the insertion site, with or without palpation** · **warmth, redness or swelling** · **leakage of fluid** ·
**resistance when flushing or absence of blood return** [S1].

**Replacement follows the *Australian Guidelines for the Prevention and Control of Infection in
Healthcare*** [S1]:

> ⚠️ **Do not routinely replace PIVCs in neonates and children.**
>
> **Option 1: Replace a PIVC every 72 hours** — **based on observational studies that show an increased risk
> of bloodstream infection with PIVCs left in place for more than 72 hours**
>
> **Option 2: Replace a PIVC based on clinical indication** — **informed by a systematic review, first
> published in 2011 and updated most recently in 2015, which concluded that rates of bloodstream infection
> and thrombophlebitis were not significantly different when PIVCs were changed based on clinical indication
> rather than routinely replaced.** **Can be cost saving and may reduce the discomfort for patients** [S1]

⚠️ **Option 2 is conditional on the service earning it.** It **"may be considered only when there is"**
surveillance of PIVC-related bloodstream infection, **comprehensive documentation with audit results
demonstrating sustained compliance with daily PIVC assessment**, and **compliance with competency
requirements** [S1].

**A standard offering two policies and gating the better one behind measurement.** The service that cannot
show it audits daily assessment must change every cannula at 72 hours.

**Also** [S1]: ⚠️ **consider re-siting PIVCs, within 24 hours, when adherence to aseptic technique is uncertain
or unknown, such as insertion during a medical emergency**; **observe the insertion site for 48 hours after
removal for signs of post-infusion pain, redness or swelling**; and **ensure that PIVCs are removed before
discharge** unless planned to continue.

**The surveillance target is named**: **PIVC-related *Staphylococcus aureus* bacteraemia (SAB)** [S1].

## Where this connects in the compendium

| Guideline here | Connection |
|---|---|
| **Antimicrobial stewardship** | ⚠️ **the daily IV-to-oral switch review, from the device side**; and **PIVC-related *S. aureus* bacteraemia** as the surveillance endpoint |
| **Sepsis** · **Sepsis in children** | ⚠️ **PIVCs should not be used to obtain blood cultures** — and the paediatric sepsis documents require blood cultures **at the time vascular access is obtained**. **The two are reconcilable only by distinguishing the insertion needle from the indwelling device**, and neither says so (see *Unresolved*) |
| **Anaphylaxis in children** · **Sepsis in children** | **intraosseous access** is listed here as a route to consider **before** IV, and is the fallback in both |
| **Venous thromboembolism prevention** | **thrombosis** as a named consequence of difficult insertion |
| **Colonoscopy** | the other ACSQHC standard built on **competency and credentialing** rather than a condition |
| **Delirium** · **Dementia** | the idle cannula is a tethering and agitation risk in exactly these patients |

## Unresolved

| Point | Kind | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| ⚠️ **Blood cultures from a PIVC** | **observation** | this standard says **PIVCs should not be used to obtain blood cultures**; the paediatric sepsis guidelines in this compendium direct that **blood cultures be taken at the time vascular access is obtained**. **Reconcilable if the first means an indwelling device and the second the insertion needle — but neither document makes that distinction explicit**, and it is not resolved here |
| **Flushing solution, volume and frequency** | **input_unavailable** | deferred to **local policy** and **current evidence-based or best-practice guidelines**; **no solution, volume or interval is stated** |
| **PIVC length and gauge selection** | **input_unavailable** | statement 4 requires the right **length and gauge**; **no sizes or selection criteria are given** |
| **Australian Guidelines for the Prevention and Control of Infection in Healthcare** | **input_unavailable** | the replacement recommendations in Box 6 are transcribed; **the guideline itself was not retrieved** |
| **IV-WISE creed** | **input_unavailable** | cited as the principles for patient partnership, **"on page 18"** of the Standard; **not retrieved** |
| **Indicators 9 and 10 wording** | **input_unavailable** | both were **truncated in the retrieved indicator table**; the periods they specify are not fully captured |
| ⚠️ **No indicator for statements 6 or 10** | **observation** | **insertion technique and removal/replacement are unmeasured**, although the dressing (8b) and flexion site (4b) indicators touch insertion indirectly |
| **72 hours versus clinical indication** | **evidence_unsettled** | the Standard presents **two legitimate options** with **opposing evidence bases** — observational studies favouring 72-hour replacement, a systematic review finding no difference — and gates the second behind local surveillance and audit rather than choosing |
