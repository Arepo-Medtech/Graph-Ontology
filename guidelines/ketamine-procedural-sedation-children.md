# Ketamine for procedural sedation in children

**Scope:** indications, contraindications, staffing, IV and IM dosing, monitoring, side effects, recovery and discharge for ketamine procedural sedation in the emergency department. ## What it does and when to use it

- **Ketamine is a safe, effective ED procedural sedation option** — a potent sedative, amnestic, analgesic and anaesthetic. [S1]
- **Dissociative state:** trance-like with eyes possibly open, muscle tone maintained, effective analgesia, usually total amnesia, **airway reflexes maintained**, BP and heart rate rise slightly; nystagmus and lacrimation are typical. [S1]
- **Use for short, painful procedures, especially needing immobilisation:** lacerations (especially facial), fracture reduction, abscess drainage, foreign-body removal where nitrous oxide is or would be inadequate. [S1]

## Contraindications

- ⚠️ **Absolute: infants under 3 months.** True allergy is rare; treat as usual allergy/anaphylaxis. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Relative — discuss with senior staff experienced in sedation:** infants under 12 months (absolute in some jurisdictions, including NSW) · significant current respiratory illness · known difficult airway or previous airway surgery or congenital anomaly · intraoral procedures or bleeding · posterior pharynx stimulation. [S1]
- **Also relative:** cardiovascular disease where a higher heart rate and workload are contraindicated (ischaemic heart disease, cardiac failure, hypertension, Wolff-Parkinson-White) · glaucoma or acute globe injury · porphyria · thyroid disease · bowel obstruction · psychosis. [S1]

## Staff and preparation

- ⚠️ **Staff:** senior staff aware and able to help immediately · **one registered nurse** competent in ketamine sedation with annual ALS assessment · **one senior doctor to sedate, credentialed in ketamine sedation and ALS certified** · one doctor to do the procedure. In some settings, including NSW Health, a third credentialled clinician is mandatory. [S1]
- **Resuscitation equipment must be readily available. Obtain informed consent**, with baseline BP, HR, RR and oxygen saturation. [S1]
- **Non-procedural conversation before and during administration may reduce unpleasant emergence.** Apply topical anaesthetic early — it takes about 45 minutes. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not pre-oxygenate** — it may mask hypoventilation. Fast as per local guidelines. [S1]

## Dose

- **IV is preferred where access is easy:** titratable, shorter recovery, less emesis. **Doses may need adjusting to ideal body weight.** [S1]
- **IV: 1–1.5 mg/kg over 1–2 minutes immediately before the procedure**, then **0.25–0.5 mg/kg every 10 minutes** as needed. **Maximum 4.5 mg/kg** (rarely needed). [S1]
- ⚠️ **IV doses over 2.5 mg/kg raise the risk of adverse events** — if more is needed, consider aborting or another sedation option. [S1]
- **IM: 4 mg/kg initially (max 6 mg/kg)**; a **repeat 2 mg/kg after 10 minutes** if sedation is inadequate. Ketamine can be used safely without IV access. [S1]
- **IM vs IV:** onset 3–4 minutes vs 1 minute · effective sedation 15–30 vs 10–20 minutes · average time to discharge 100–140 vs 90–120 minutes. [S1]
- **Intranasal ketamine is not currently recommended for procedural sedation** — limited evidence. [S1]

## Monitoring and side effects

- **Pulse oximetry, cardiac monitoring and a clinician in attendance until recovery is well established**, with close watch on airway and chest movement. [S1]
- **Common and insignificant:** purposeless movements, stiffness, twitching, rash, vocalisation. Tachycardia or hypertension is transient; hypersalivation rarely needs suction. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Transient laryngospasm (0.3%)** may need positive-pressure ventilation or RSI; **apnoea or respiratory depression (0.4%)** is usually transient. [S1]
- **Emesis is more common over 8 years; unpleasant emergence beyond mid-adolescence**, eased by a quiet, low-stimulation room; recovery agitation (1.4%) is uncommon and transient. [S1]

## Recovery and discharge

- ⚠️ **Do not discharge until back to premorbid neurological baseline** — able to walk and talk as before. Recover in a quiet, dim area and do not stimulate early. [S1]
- **If sedation fails**, discuss with senior staff or anaesthetics; the sedation and procedure may need abandoning. [S1]
- **At home: careful family supervision when mobilising for at least two hours.** [S1]
