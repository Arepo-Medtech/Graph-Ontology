# Sickle cell disease in children: acute crises

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** emergency assessment and management of acute complications of sickle cell disease in children: vaso-occlusive crisis, fever, acute chest syndrome, splenic sequestration, aplastic crisis, stroke and priapism. For the anaemia work-up see `anaemia-children`; antibiotic doses are in `empiric-antimicrobials-children`; opioid titration in `acute-pain-management-children`.

## Background and triggers

- **All patients become functionally asplenic** and are at risk of infection with encapsulated organisms (pneumococcus, meningococcus, *Haemophilus*). [S1]
- **Crises may be spontaneous or triggered by** infection, dehydration, hypoxia, or sedatives, local anaesthetics and surgery. SS disease, sickle β-thalassaemia and HbSC disease are all treated the same way. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Many presentations need urgent treatment, and discussion with the on-call Haematology Consultant is mandatory.** [S1]

## General crisis management

- **Investigations:** FBE with reticulocytes · group and crossmatch · blood and urine cultures if febrile · U&E and LFT if jaundiced or dehydrated · consider CXR if febrile with respiratory symptoms. **Compare the spleen with the patient's baseline**; do a neurological examination. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Start analgesia promptly and treat pain aggressively.** Mild: paracetamol or ibuprofen. **Moderate to severe: morphine 0.05 mg/kg/dose IV, repeated as necessary**; some benefit from PCA. [S1]
- **Fluids:** push oral fluids; IV if needed — **bolus 10–20 mL/kg 0.9% saline** if required, then maintenance (Plasma-Lyte 148 + 5% glucose, or 0.9% sodium chloride + 5% glucose). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Avoid excess fluids after initial resuscitation** (chest crisis risk). **Do not delay IV fluids or analgesia for topical anaesthetic cream.** Transfusion may be needed — discuss with the haematologist. [S1]
- **Treat all pain initially as vaso-occlusive** — but chest pain with respiratory symptoms may be acute chest syndrome. [S1]

## Acute chest syndrome

- ⚠️ **Life-threatening; patients may deteriorate quickly.** Lung infarction with lower respiratory symptoms, hypoxaemia and a new CXR infiltrate — **chest pain and hypoxaemia may be the only signs. Treat chest pain as acute chest syndrome.** [S1]
- **IV 3rd-generation cephalosporin plus atypical cover (roxithromycin or azithromycin)**; the CXR must not delay treatment. **Oxygen to keep saturation over 96%** or for comfort. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Refer early to PICU** for significant hypoxia or respiratory distress. [S1]

## Fever

- ⚠️ **Fever: IV 3rd-generation cephalosporin** (add atypical cover if there is a significant respiratory component) and **blood and urine cultures**. Treat co-existing pain as a vaso-occlusive crisis and cough or dyspnoea as acute chest syndrome concurrently. [S1]

## Acute splenic sequestration

- **Definition:** Hb drop of at least 20 g/L below the patient's baseline with an acutely enlarged spleen; often mild–moderate thrombocytopenia; reticulocytes at or above baseline. **Low reticulocytes → consider co-existent aplasia.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **While waiting for blood, give 0.9% sodium chloride 10–20 mL/kg for hypovolaemia.** Initial transfusion to aim for **Hb 5–6 g/dL** to treat shock (the page adds 'not >3 g/dL'). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not raise Hb too far or too fast** — the spleen shrinks and autotransfuses, overshooting Hb with a **risk of stroke from hyperviscosity.** IV 3rd-generation cephalosporin if febrile. [S1]

## Aplastic crisis

- **Aplastic crisis:** Hb below baseline with a markedly reduced reticulocyte count (usually under 1%), usually with acute infection including parvovirus; the spleen may also be enlarged. [S1]
- **IV plus oral fluids to a total of maintenance. Transfuse red cells if symptomatic or Hb under 5 g/dL — do not increase Hb by more than 3 g/dL.** IV 3rd-generation cephalosporin if febrile. [S1]

## Stroke

- **Acute neurological events occur in about 10% of patients with HbSS**, suddenly or as a complication of acute chest syndrome or aplastic crisis. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Image without delay: MRI is the modality of choice; if unavailable, CT with NO contrast** (hyperviscosity risk worsens the neurological state). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Transfusion is required:** options include initial simple transfusion to **Hb 10 g/dL**, then **red cell exchange** (in ICU acutely). [S1]

## Priapism

- **Two forms:** stuttering episodes of 2–4 hrs (often recurrent, may precede a severe episode) and **severe attacks over 4 hrs, which can cause impotence.** Most common in adolescence. [S1]
- **Empty the bladder (catheterise if needed); moderate exercise, bath or shower. Do NOT use ice.** Start hydration, oxygen, analgesia and urinary alkalinisation as soon as possible. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Priapism over 4 hours is a urological emergency** — consult General Surgery (Urology) and the on-call haematologist. [S1]
