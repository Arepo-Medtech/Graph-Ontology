# Influenza in children — clinical management

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation

## Recognise it

- **Most influenza in healthy children is self-limited**, but severe disease occurs both in previously well children and in those with risk factors. [S1]
- **Incubation 1–4 days (average 2).** Infectious from 24 hours before symptoms for 4–7 days of illness — longer in young or immunodeficient children. PCR can stay positive for up to 3 weeks. [S1]
- **Younger children** are less likely to have respiratory features and more likely to have febrile seizures, gastrointestinal features and severe disease. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Severe features — consider treatment:** pneumonia · secondary bacterial infection (especially *S. aureus*, *S. pneumoniae*) · respiratory failure · neurological complications (encephalopathy/encephalitis, aseptic meningitis, Guillain-Barré, cerebellar ataxia) · rhabdomyolysis · myocarditis/pericarditis · Reye syndrome (aspirin). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Risk factors — consider treatment:** severe chronic disease (neurological, respiratory, cardiac, haematological including sickle cell, hepatic, CKD, diabetes, obesity over 95th centile) · genetic syndromes such as trisomy 21 · neuromuscular disease · prematurity under 37/40 · immunosuppression · pregnancy · long-term aspirin. [S1]
- ⚠️ **In an unwell child, a positive influenza PCR does not exclude co-existing or secondary serious bacterial infection.** [S1]
- **Immunisation status does not change management** of suspected influenza. [S1]

## Test and support

- **Mild flu-like illness needs no test.** Test when the result will change management — e.g. cohorting inpatients, or directing therapy in high-risk children. [S1]
- **Supportive care** (hydration, analgesia, respiratory support); **isolate or cohort admitted children.** [S1]

## Antivirals

- ⚠️ **Hospitalised children with risk factors or severe features: start oseltamivir empirically.** [S1]
- **Oseltamivir is usually first line, oral or nasogastric.** [S1]
- **Benefit is modest** — about 1 day shorter illness on average, and it may prevent severe disease. **Most effective started within 48 hours**; some benefit up to 7 days. Neuraminidase inhibitors do nothing for other viruses. [S1]
- **Oseltamivir comes as 30, 45 or 75 mg capsules or a 6 mg/mL suspension**; capsule contents can be dissolved in water for immediate use. [S1]
- **Side effects:** headache; nausea and vomiting — give with food; vomiting is twice as likely (4–8% of unwell children). **Seek advice in renal impairment.** [S1]
- **Oseltamivir treatment, oral bd — preterm and infants:** under 38 weeks PMA 1 mg/kg/dose · 38–40 weeks PMA 1.5 mg/kg/dose · term birth to 12 months 3 mg/kg/dose; 5 days. [S1]
- **Oseltamivir treatment, 1–18 years, oral bd for 5 days:** under 15 kg 30 mg · 15–23 kg 45 mg · 23–40 kg 60 mg · over 40 kg 75 mg. [S1]
- **Peramivir (IV):** specialist advice, inpatients only, over 2 years, when the enteral route is not appropriate. [S1]

## Prophylaxis of contacts

- **After significant exposure** (within 1 metre for over 15 minutes without PPE), **oseltamivir prophylaxis is recommended** for children and family members with personal risk factors, household contacts with risk factors, household contacts aged 65 or over, or pregnant or up to 2 weeks post-partum. [S1]
- **Oseltamivir prophylaxis, oral once daily for 10 days:** term birth to 12 months 3 mg/kg/dose; 1–18 years under 15 kg 30 mg · 15–23 kg 45 mg · 23–40 kg 60 mg · over 40 kg 75 mg. [S1]

## Escalate, discharge, prevent

- **Involve the paediatric team** for suspected influenza with risk factors for severe disease; **consider transfer** for severe disease or care beyond the local hospital. [S1]
- **Discharge a well child** once carers have been taught to watch for complications; consider community follow-up (community nursing, hospital in the home, virtual ED or GP). [S1]
- **Vaccination is the best prevention:** annual, strongly recommended and funded for children 6 months to under 5 years, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children, children with risk factors, and pregnant women. Hand and respiratory hygiene also reduce spread. [S1]
