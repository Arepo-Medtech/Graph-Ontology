# Meningitis and encephalitis in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognition, lumbar puncture, empiric and directed antimicrobials, steroids, fluids and follow-up for bacterial meningitis and encephalitis in children. ## Recognise it

- ⚠️ **Bacterial meningitis is a medical emergency needing empiric antibiotics without delay.** Keep a high index of suspicion in any unwell child, especially with altered mental state or no clear focus. [S1]
- **Viral and bacterial meningitis cannot be reliably told apart clinically** — treat with empiric antimicrobials until the cause is confirmed. Presentation ranges from hours to days and may be altered by recent antibiotics. [S1]
- **Meningitis and encephalitis are hard to separate but have different pathogens** — if in doubt, cover both initially. [S1]
- **Infants with meningitis are often subtle:** irritability, lethargy or drowsiness, poor feeding, apnoea, vomiting and diarrhoea, temperature instability. On examination look for a full or bulging fontanelle, high-pitched cry, fever or hypothermia; **neck stiffness may be absent in infants**. [S1]
- **Encephalitis:** altered mental state can be subtle — unusual behaviour, confusion, personality change, emotional lability — and seizures are common. **Bradycardia and hypertension can signal raised intracranial pressure.** [S1]
- **Risk factors for meningitis:** maternal GBS colonisation (infants <3 months), unimmunised, immunocompromised, neurosurgery or penetrating head injury, VP shunt, cochlear implant, and age <5 years. [S1]

## Investigate

- **Urgent LP**, with urgent CSF microscopy and biochemistry, preferably with a simultaneous blood glucose. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Defer LP and start empiric treatment immediately if it is unsafe:** focal neurological signs, ongoing seizures, markedly reduced GCS, cardiovascular compromise or coagulopathy. Do the LP as soon as a senior clinician deems it safe. Tachycardia and tachypnoea without respiratory compromise are not contraindications. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not delay antimicrobials in an unwell child if the LP will take more than 30 minutes.** [S1]
- **Bloods:** blood culture, FBE (may be normal), glucose, gas for lactate, UEC for hyponatraemia; coagulation studies if shock or coagulopathy suspected. [S1]
- **Neuroimaging** for focal signs, raised ICP, encephalopathy or diagnostic uncertainty; not routine in meningitis. **A normal CT does not exclude raised ICP and should not drive the LP decision.** [S1]

## Treat — empiric

- **Cover meningitis first; add encephalitis treatment** if there is altered conscious state, focal signs, seizures, signs or risk of HSV, or the child is obtunded. **Give antibiotics within 30 minutes of the decision to treat.** [S1]
- **0–2 months (GBS, E. coli, Listeria):** benzylpenicillin 60 mg/kg IV — 12-hourly under 7 days old, 6–8-hourly from 7 days to <4 weeks, 4-hourly over 4 weeks — **and** cefotaxime 50 mg/kg IV — 12-hourly in week 1, 6–8-hourly from 7 days to <4 weeks, 6-hourly over 4 weeks — or ceftriaxone 100 mg/kg (max 2 g) IV daily. **Dexamethasone not advised.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Avoid ceftriaxone where possible in neonates <41 weeks gestation**, particularly if jaundiced or receiving calcium-containing solutions including TPN. [S1]
- **2 months and over (N. meningitidis, S. pneumoniae, HiB):** ceftriaxone 100 mg/kg (max 4 g) IV daily **or** cefotaxime 50 mg/kg (max 2 g) IV 6-hourly. **Add vancomycin if Gram-positive cocci on Gram stain.** [S1]
- **Dexamethasone (2 months and over):** 0.15 mg/kg (max 10 mg) IV 6-hourly for 4 days. [S1]
- **Penicillin/cephalosporin hypersensitivity:** moxifloxacin 10 mg/kg (max 400 mg) IV daily **and** vancomycin. [S1]
- **Encephalitis — add aciclovir to the antibiotics:** 20 mg/kg IV 12-hourly (<30 weeks gestation) or 8-hourly (>30 weeks gestation to <3 months corrected age); 20 mg/kg IV 8-hourly (3 months to 12 years); 10 mg/kg IV 8-hourly (>12 years). **Consider adding azithromycin.** [S1]
- **Unrecognised HSV encephalitis is devastating, but early aciclovir can lead to full recovery.** [S1]

## Steroids

- **Steroids may reduce the risk of hearing loss** in bacterial meningitis (evidence mixed). **Not recommended in neonates** because of possible neurodevelopmental effects. [S1]
- **Give the first IV dexamethasone just before or with the first antibiotic dose.** If given after, ideally within 4 hours and no more than 12 hours after starting antibiotics. [S1]

## Supportive care

- **Treat all seizures immediately.** Resuscitate shock with **10 mL/kg 0.9% sodium chloride IV boluses**. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Risk of excess ADH: give a maximum of 2/3 maintenance IV fluids**; further restriction may be needed for hyponatraemia or raised ICP (seek senior advice). **Always maintain normal blood pressure and adequate circulating volume.** [S1]
- **Reassess hydration, weight, sodium, acid–base and neurological state every 6–12 hours for the first 48 hours.** Droplet precautions for the first 24 hours of admission. [S1]

## Directed treatment

- **Directed durations (days):** *N. meningitidis* benzylpenicillin 7 · *S. pneumoniae* penicillin-sensitive benzylpenicillin 10 · penicillin-resistant cefotaxime/ceftriaxone (vancomycin if cephalosporin-resistant) 10 · HiB ceftriaxone/cefotaxime 10. [S1]
- **Gram-negative:** ceftriaxone/cefotaxime 21 days · **organism not isolated:** 7 days minimum · **GBS, Listeria:** benzylpenicillin 14–21 days · **HSV:** aciclovir 21 days minimum. [S1]
- **Consider ID consultation** for resistant organisms or immediate penicillin/cephalosporin hypersensitivity. [S1]
- **Notify presumed or confirmed N. meningitidis and HiB immediately**; confirmed S. pneumoniae within 5 days. [S1]
- **Fever persisting after 4–6 days of treatment:** consider nosocomial infection, subdural effusion or empyema, cerebral abscess or parameningeal focus, or inadequate treatment. [S1]

## Disposition and follow-up

- **Transfer if** haemodynamic or respiratory instability, altered conscious state, seizures or focal signs, care beyond local comfort, or complications/poor response. [S1]
- **IV treatment can be completed via HITH** once haemodynamically stable, afebrile and directed treatment decided. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Hearing loss is highest after S. pneumoniae meningitis (about 22%)** and can begin within 48 hours; do baseline audiology ideally as an inpatient. [S1]
- **Formal audiology 6–8 weeks after discharge for every child with encephalitis or bacterial meningitis**; monitor neurodevelopment; consider complement deficiency testing after more than one episode of meningococcal disease. [S1]
