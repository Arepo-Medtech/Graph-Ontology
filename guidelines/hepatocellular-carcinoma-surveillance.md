# Hepatocellular carcinoma surveillance — who to offer it to

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** who should be offered 6-monthly hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) surveillance in Australia: people with cirrhosis, HCV after sustained virologic response, F3 fibrosis, and chronic hepatitis B by population and age. **Not** HCC diagnosis or treatment. The counterparts are `hepatitis-b` (whose Unresolved item on *which populations need 6-monthly HCC screening* this page addresses) and `hepatitis-c`.

## Cirrhosis

- **In cirrhosis, offer 6-monthly HCC surveillance — ultrasound, with or without alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) — to people who are willing and suitable to receive HCC treatment.** [S1]
- **Do not routinely offer HCC surveillance to people with limited projected life expectancy.** [S1]
- **HCV-related cirrhosis with a sustained virologic response: still offer 6-monthly surveillance** (ultrasound, with or without AFP). [S1]

## Without cirrhosis

- **HCV with F3 (non-cirrhotic) fibrosis and a sustained virologic response: do not routinely offer HCC surveillance** — but monitor for progression to cirrhosis. [S1]
- **MAFLD/NAFLD without cirrhosis: monitor for progression to cirrhosis.** [S1]

## Chronic hepatitis B — by population

- **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people with chronic HBV: consider 6-monthly surveillance from age 50 or more** — or earlier with a family history of HCC, or from age 40 or more with a high-risk HBV genotype. [S1]
- **Asian or Pacific background with chronic HBV: consider 6-monthly surveillance for males aged 40 or more and females aged 50 or more.** [S1]
- **Sub-Saharan African background with chronic HBV: consider 6-monthly surveillance for males and females aged 20 or more.** [S1]
- **Chronic HBV outside these priority populations: offer 6-monthly surveillance if aged 40 or more AND there is a family history of HCC.** [S1]
- **With a family history of HCC, consider starting surveillance 10 years before the earliest case in the family.** [S1]

## Test

- **Where surveillance is recommended, consider adding 6-monthly AFP testing to ultrasound.** [S1]
