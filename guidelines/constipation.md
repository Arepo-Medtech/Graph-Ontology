# Constipation

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-22 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** children. Functional constipation, disimpaction, and maintenance laxative therapy.

> **Australian primary source.** The Royal Children's Hospital Melbourne. ***Constipation***, Clinical
> Practice Guideline, **last updated March 2020** [S1].

**AMH topic closed by this guideline:** *Constipation*.

**Constipation affects 1/3 of children and frequently occurs during the introduction of solid foods, toilet
training and school** [S1]. ⚠️ **Symptoms of constipation are under reported** [S1].

## ⚠️ Three pieces of standard advice the guideline contradicts

> **Increasing dietary fibre is not an adequate treatment for constipation** [S1]

> **There is no need to increase fluid intake beyond daily maintenance fluid requirements** [S1]

> **Rectal treatment with suppositories or enemas should be avoided** [S1]

And a fourth, aimed at the reason families stop treatment:

> **Osmotic and lubricant laxatives are usually required on a long term basis (months to years). Reassure
> parents that this is safe and doesn't produce a 'lazy bowel'** [S1].

**A common cause of recurrence is stopping laxatives too early** [S1]. **Treatment is usually required for
several months and if stopped early, may lead to recurrence** [S1].

## The target is a daily soft stool, titrated

> **Medications are often required and should be titrated to achieve one, soft, easy to pass bowel action per
> day** [S1].

## Diagnosis is clinical

> **The diagnosis is made clinically. Internal examination and X-ray are not required** [S1].

⚠️ **Internal examination should not be performed without first consulting a senior clinician** [S1].
**Investigations are not routinely required** [S1].

### Rome IV criteria for functional constipation

**Must include ≥2 criteria for at least 1 month in infants or 2 months in older children** [S1]:

- **≤2 stools/week**
- **History of retentive posturing or excessive volitional stool retention (ie withholding or incomplete evacuation)**
- **History of painful or hard bowel movements**
- **History of large-diameter stools**
- **Presence of a large faecal mass in the rectum**

**In toilet-trained children**, an additional criterion: **at least 1 episode per week of soiling/incontinence
after the acquisition of toileting skills** [S1].

### What is *not* constipation

⚠️ **Healthy infants (<6 months) can strain and cry before passing soft stools (dyschezia). Unless the stools
are also hard, this is not constipation and will self-resolve** [S1].

**Most children defaecate at least every 2–3 days however breastfed babies may defaecate as infrequently as
once per week** [S1].

### The withholding cycle

> **Young children may ignore the urge to defaecate, causing a build-up of large hard bowel actions. When
> this leads to painful defaecation it may cause apprehension, stool retention and passage of further hard
> stool — a cycle of withholding and constipation** [S1].

## ⚠️ Red flags

- **Infants presenting <6 weeks of age — should be discussed with a senior doctor**
- **Delayed passage of meconium — most infants pass meconium in the first 24 hours of life (consider Hirschsprung disease or anorectal malformation)**
- **Ribbon like stools — consider anorectal malformation**
- **Weight loss/poor growth**
- **Persistent vomiting**
- **Abdominal mass (not consistent with large faecal mass)** [S1]

**Organic causes** [S1] — medical: **coeliac disease**, **hypothyroidism**, **hypercalcaemia**, **slow-transit
constipation**, **neurological disorders**; surgical: **Hirschsprung disease**, **meconium ileus**, **anatomic
malformations of anus**, **spinal cord abnormalities**.

**Examination** should include the **lower back/spine — consider occult spinal dysraphism/tethered cord** and
**neurology — assessment of lower limbs, observation of gait** [S1].

⚠️ **Children with autism spectrum disorders and attention deficit / hyperactive disorder have an increased
risk of functional constipation** [S1].

## Behaviour modification

**Position** — **footstool to ensure knees are higher than hips. Lean forward and put elbows on knees** [S1].

**Toilet sits** — **up to 5 minutes, three times a day, preferably after meals**; **encourage child to bulge
out their abdomen**; **praise child for sitting on toilet**; **ensure toileting remains a positive
experience** [S1].

⚠️ **Delay toilet training attempts until child is painlessly passing soft stool** [S1].

**Review toilet access eg investigate barriers to using school toilets** [S1] — the guideline treats
school-toilet avoidance as a clinical variable.

**Excessive cow milk intake may result in inadequate dietary fibre; this may exacerbate constipation in some
children** [S1].

## First-line agents by age

- **Infants <1 month: Coloxyl drops**
- **Infants 1–12 months: Iso-osmotic laxative (Macrogol-3350 + electrolytes or Lactulose**
- **Children: Iso-osmotic laxative or lubricant (paraffin oil)** [S1]

**Children with stool withholding behaviours, pain while defecating or rectal bleeding or fissures may
benefit from inpatient disimpaction management** [S1].

**Anal fissures can be treated with topical Petroleum Jelly to provide pain relief** [S1].

## Maintenance dosing

> the verification note.

| Agent | Class | Dose [S1] |
|---|---|---|
| **Lactulose** (Actilax™) | osmotic | **1–12 mo 3-5 mL/day · 1–5 yo 5-10 mL/day · 5–14 yo 10-40 mL/day**; **split larger doses bd** |
| **Poloxamer** (Coloxyl™ drops) | stool softener | **<6 mo 0.3 mL tds · 6–18 mo 0.5 mL tds · 18 mo–3 yo 0.8 mL tds** |
| **Macrogol 3350 + electrolytes** (Macrovic™, Molaxole™) | iso-osmotic | **1–12 mo ½ sachet daily · 1–6 yo 1 sachet daily (max 2/day) · 6–12 yo 1–2 sachets daily (max 2/day) · >12 yo 1–4 sachets/day** · **PBS listed (authority not required)** |
| **Macrogol 3350** (OsmoLax™, ClearLax™) | iso-osmotic | **2–6 yo 1 small scoop/day · 6–12 yo 1 large scoop/day · >12 yo 1–2 large scoops/day** · **large (17 g) and small (8.5 g)** scoops · **PBS listed (authority not required)** |
| **Paraffin oil** (Parachoc™, Agarol™) | lubricant | **1–6 yo 10–15 mL/day · 6–12 yo 15–20 mL/day · >12 yo up to 40 mL/day** |
| **Sodium picosulfate / bisacodyl** (Dulcolax™) | stimulant | **6 mo–4 yo, 0.25 mg/kg (max 5 mg = 10 drops) nocte · 4-10 yo 5-10 drops nocte · >10 yo 10 drops nocte or 1–2 tablets nocte** |

⚠️ **Coloxyl+Senna: Senna is the stimulant component and should be avoided unless stools are soft, >2 yo** [S1].

⚠️ **Paraffin oil — avoid in children with swallowing problems due to aspiration risk, particularly those
<6 mo** [S1]. It **can cause orange oil seepage in underwear (reduce dosage)** [S1].

⚠️ **Stimulants — avoid if impacted**, **can cause abdominal cramps**, **do not use long term** [S1].

## Disimpaction

**Children with severe constipation benefit from a disimpaction regimen before maintenance treatment
begins** [S1]. **Oral medication as an outpatient is effective and preferred. Switch to maintenance therapy
immediately post disimpaction** [S1].

⚠️ **Only consider the use of glycerine suppository or Microlax™ enema as a one off treatment. Sedation should
be strongly considered** [S1].

**Review ongoing need for disimpaction on day 4 of treatment** [S1].

**Macrogol-3350 + electrolytes sachets (adult formulation), days 1–7** [S1]:
**1-6 yo — 1, 2, 2, 3, 3, 4, 4** · **6-12 yo — 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 6, 6** · **12+ yo — 8, 8, 8, then stop**.
⚠️ **1 sachet Macrogol-3350 with electrolytes adult formulation = 2 sachets of "Movicol Junior"
formulation** [S1].

**OsmoLax™ small scoops (8.5 g), days 1–7** [S1]: **2-6 yo — 2, 3, 3, 4, 5, 6, 6** · **6-12 yo — 3, 4, 6, 8,
9, 9, 9**.

### Inpatient

**Macrogol/electrolyte solutions (Colonlytely™, Glycoprep™) 1–3 L/day, via NGT at a rate of 25 mL/kg/hr
(maximum rate 400 mL/hr, or less depending on pump used)** [S1].

⚠️ **Normal maintenance oral fluids should be given in addition to maintain hydration. These solutions provide
no net fluid input and there is a risk of dehydration** [S1].

For **older children who refuse a nasogastric tube or prefer oral treatment**, **fixed dose sodium
picosulphate preparations can be used (Picolax™/Picoprep™)**: **children 4-9 yo: 1 sachet — first dose /
½ sachet - second dose (=15 mg sodium picosulphate/day)**; **children >9 yo: 1 sachet BD (= 20 mg sodium
picosulphate/day)** [S1].

⚠️ **Do not use if signs of obstruction or in renal impairment** [S1]. **Consider ceasing if child begins
passing clear fluid per rectum** [S1].

⚠️ **Oral medication taken during or within the hour before administration of a bowel washout may be flushed
from the gastrointestinal tract without absorption** [S1] — a drug-interaction hazard for every other
medicine the child takes.

## Follow-up

**Arrange follow up with GP or paediatrician within 4 weeks** [S1]. **Consider referral to a continence or
encopresis service for faecal/urinary incontinence, complex or difficult cases** [S1].

**Consult the local paediatric team** when **red flags present, or concerns of an underlying organic
pathology**; **non-resolution occurs despite optimising management over 6 months (outpatient)**; or
**outpatient disimpaction fails, requiring inpatient management** [S1].
