# Keratinocyte cancer (BCC and cSCC) — practice points

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** basal cell carcinoma and cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma: sun protection, who needs surveillance, biopsy and first-line treatment choices. **Not** melanoma (see `melanoma`). ## Prevention

- **Sun protection is needed whenever the UV index is 3 or above. Apply sunscreen of SPF 30 or greater liberally.** [S1]
- **Apply sunscreen 20 minutes before going outside and reapply it at least every 2 hours.** [S1]

## Who needs skin checks

- **There is insufficient evidence to recommend screening the general population** for keratinocyte cancer. [S1]
- **Monitor patients at very high risk, such as organ transplant recipients, in specialist clinics at least annually.** [S1]

## Diagnosis and biopsy

- **The best biopsy is complete excision of the lesion**, where appropriate. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Biopsy a presumed actinic keratosis if it persists after cryotherapy, enlarges or becomes tender.** [S1]
- **Manage keratoacanthomas by early excision.** [S1]

## First-line treatment

- **Remove uncomplicated small tumours by elliptical excision and direct closure.** [S1]
- **Skin biopsy is highly recommended before treating superficial BCC with imiquimod 5% cream.** [S1]
- **Imiquimod for primary superficial BCC: apply five times per week for 6 weeks.** [S1]
- **Bowen's disease: 5-fluorouracil 5% cream twice a day for 4–8 weeks.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not use cryotherapy for recurrent cSCC.** It is contraindicated. [S1]
- **If a cSCC cannot be surgically excised, consider referral for a radiotherapy opinion.** [S1]
