## ⚠️ Doses

### The conversion that causes errors [L2]

> ⚠️ **"Concentrations of local anaesthetics are expressed as a PERCENTAGE or in mg/mL. MULTIPLY BY 10 TO
> CONVERT FROM PERCENTAGE TO mg/mL"** — so **0.5% = 5 mg/mL**, **1% = 10 mg/mL**, **2% = 20 mg/mL** [L2].

### Maximum single doses [L2]

| Drug | Without adrenaline | With adrenaline | Onset (topical/infiltration) | Onset (nerve block) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lidocaine** | **3 mg/kg** | ⚠️ **7 mg/kg** | **5–10 min** | **5–15 min** | **medium** |
| **Bupivacaine** | **2 mg/kg** | ⚠️ **2 mg/kg — UNCHANGED** | **10–15 min** | **15–30 min** | **long** |
| **Ropivacaine** | **3 mg/kg** | — | **10–15 min** | **15–30 min** | **long** |
| **Prilocaine** | **6 mg/kg** | **8 mg/kg** | **5–10 min** | **5–15 min** | **medium** |
| **Cocaine** | **1.5 mg/kg** | — | **1–5 min** | — | **short** |
| **Tetracaine** | **1 mg/kg** | — | **30–60 min** | — | **long** |

> ⚠️ **ADRENALINE MORE THAN DOUBLES THE LIDOCAINE CEILING (3 → 7 mg/kg) AND DOES NOT CHANGE BUPIVACAINE'S
> AT ALL** [L2]. **The mental shortcut "adrenaline lets you give more" is true for one of these drugs and
> false for the other.**

> ⚠️ **"MAXIMUM DOSES… CONSIDER THEM ONLY AS A GUIDE"** — blood concentrations depend on the area
> anaesthetised, tissue vascularity, type of block, technique, and the patient's age and physical
> condition, as well as the intensity of block and duration required [L2].

### The adrenaline ceiling [L2]

> **MAXIMUM TOTAL DOSE OF ADRENALINE in combination with local anaesthetics is 500 MICROGRAMS.**
> ⚠️ **Concentrations above 5 micrograms/mL (1:200,000) PRODUCE LITTLE ADDITIONAL BENEFIT and are not
> usually advised, except in dentistry** [L2].

## ⚠️ Vasoconstrictor rules

| | [L2] |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ **CONTRAINDICATED** | **IV regional anaesthesia (Bier's block)** · **with ERGOMETRINE** · ⚠️ **PENILE BLOCK** |
| **NOT recommended** | **intrathecal anaesthesia** |
| ⚠️ **May be used WITH CAUTION near other terminal arteries** | **FINGERS · TOES · EARS · NOSE** — seek specialist advice |
| ⚠️ **AVOID** | **Raynaud's phenomenon or peripheral vascular disease — risk of TISSUE ISCHAEMIA** |
| **Caution** | **heart disease, hypertension, hyperthyroidism — increased risk of arrhythmias** |

**Most local anaesthetics except cocaine cause VASODILATION. Adding adrenaline slows vascular absorption
and prolongs the local effect** — ⚠️ **MOST effect with LIDOCAINE, LESS with bupivacaine, MINIMAL with
ropivacaine** [L2].

**A VASOCONSTRICTOR REACTION presents as anxiety, pallor, tachycardia, hypertension, sweating or
arrhythmias, and usually resolves on stopping administration** [L2].

## ⚠️ Systemic toxicity

> **AMH directs readers to the Association of Anaesthetists guideline, *Management of severe local
> anaesthetic toxicity*, 2023 edition** [L1]. ⚠️ **That guideline was NOT retrieved and this guideline does
> not reproduce a treatment protocol — see *Unresolved* #2.**

**High plasma concentrations may result from accidental intravascular injection, rapid absorption or rate
of administration, excessive dosage, or delayed elimination** [L1].

| System | Features [L1] |
|---|---|
| **CNS — usually first** | **EXCITATION: nervousness, CIRCUMORAL TINGLING, TINNITUS, tremor, dizziness, blurred vision, SEIZURES** → **DEPRESSION: drowsiness, loss of consciousness, respiratory depression, apnoea** |
| **Cardiovascular — usually later** | **hypotension, bradycardia, arrhythmias, cardiac arrest** — often from hypoxaemia secondary to respiratory depression, but may be direct myocardial depression and vasodilation |

> ⚠️ **TWO WAYS THE USUAL ORDER FAILS** [L1]:
> **"The excitatory effects may be VERY BRIEF OR EVEN ABSENT, and CNS DEPRESSION MAY PRESENT FIRST."**
> **"Occasionally, CARDIOVASCULAR COLLAPSE MAY PRECEDE CNS TOXICITY WITH LITTLE WARNING (particularly with
> BUPIVACAINE)."**

### Comparative toxicity [L2]

> ⚠️ **IN USUAL DOSES BUPIVACAINE IS MORE CARDIOTOXIC THAN ROPIVACAINE**, and **RESUSCITATION FROM CARDIAC
> ARREST CAUSED BY ROPIVACAINE HAS A HIGHER CHANCE OF SUCCESS** than from bupivacaine [L2].
>
> **A GIVEN DOSE OF LIDOCAINE IS LESS TOXIC THAN AN EQUIVALENT DOSE OF ROPIVACAINE OR BUPIVACAINE** [L2].

**Bupivacaine and ropivacaine give good sensory/motor differentiation at the lowest concentration; sensory
onset and duration are similar, but MOTOR block may be slower, shorter and less intense with ROPIVACAINE —
an advantage where motor block is not desired, e.g. obstetric epidural analgesia** [L2].

**PRILOCAINE suits IV regional anaesthesia (rapid metabolism, lower toxicity)** — ⚠️ **but
METHAEMOGLOBINAEMIA may occur, particularly with high doses** [L2].
**TETRACAINE has HIGH systemic toxicity, restricting it to TOPICAL use** — it is a particularly effective
analgesic for venous cannulation, with faster onset, longer duration and greater vasodilation than topical
lidocaine with prilocaine [L2].
**TACHYPHYLAXIS occurs with lidocaine, prilocaine and bupivacaine** [L2].

## ⚠️ Minimising the risk [L1]

| | |
|---|---|
| **Monitor** | **respiratory, cardiovascular and neurological function including consciousness for AT LEAST THE FIRST 45 MINUTES** after injection — longer where absorption is delayed. ⚠️ **MAINTAIN VERBAL CONTACT during administration and the procedure** |
| **Concentration** | ⚠️ **HIGH CONCENTRATIONS ARE RELATIVELY MORE TOXIC DOSE FOR DOSE THAN WEAKER ONES — use the LOWEST DOSE at the WEAKEST CONCENTRATION that works** |
| **Cumulative dose** | **ADD UP THE TOTAL DOSE and consider HALF-LIVES ELAPSED before supplemental doses** |
| ⚠️ **Injection** | **INJECT SLOWLY AND INCREMENTALLY — 3–5 mL AT A TIME — aspirating gently and frequently.** ⚠️ **"Since NEGATIVE ASPIRATION DOES NOT PRECLUDE this, ALLOW A SUFFICIENT PAUSE BETWEEN EACH BOLUS to identify systemic effects"** |
| **Absorption by site** | ⚠️ **INTERCOSTAL > EPIDURAL > BRACHIAL PLEXUS > SUBCUTANEOUS.** Absorption through **MUCOUS MEMBRANES may also be rapid** |
| **Topical** | ⚠️ **AVOID prolonged application or extensive areas — OCCLUSIVE DRESSINGS INCREASE ABSORPTION and systemic risk** |
| **Before a major block** | **optimise the patient; establish LARGE BORE IV ACCESS before epidural or intrathecal anaesthesia** |
| **Adjuvants** | **opioids or clonidine potentiate analgesia, allowing a LOWER concentration and less motor block** |

> ⚠️ **HEAD AND NECK: "accidental intravascular or subarachnoid injection following injection of SMALL
> DOSES of local anaesthetics into the head and neck area may produce adverse reactions SIMILAR TO THOSE
> SEEN WITH INADVERTENT INTRAVASCULAR INJECTION OF LARGER DOSES"** [L1]. **A small volume in the wrong
> place behaves like a large one.**

### Physiology worth knowing [L1]
**Smaller nerve fibres are more sensitive: PAIN AND TEMPERATURE go first, then proprioception and touch,
then MOTOR activity, which needs the highest concentrations. Increased skin temperature may occur early
when sympathetic nerves are blocked** [L1].

## Contraindications and cautions [L2]

⚠️ **CONTRAINDICATED:** **allergy to a local anaesthetic OF THE SAME GROUP (ester or amide)** ·
⚠️ **LOCAL INFLAMMATION OR INFECTION.**

> ⚠️ **ACIDOSIS, HYPOXIA, HYPERKALAEMIA AND HYPOCALCAEMIA INCREASE THE RISK OF TOXICITY** [L2].

- **Allergy is RARE, relatively more common with ESTERS, and ⚠️ THERE IS NO CROSS-REACTIVITY BETWEEN THE
  TWO GROUPS.** ⚠️ **PRESERVATIVES (methylparaben, sodium metabisulfite) and ANTISEPTICS (chlorhexidine)
  can also cause allergic reactions** — so a "local anaesthetic allergy" may not be the anaesthetic [L2].
- ⚠️ **ANTICOAGULATED patients: risk of haematoma, WHICH CAN CAUSE PARALYSIS, with epidural or intrathecal
  administration** — consult local protocols [L2].
- **Neuromuscular disease (e.g. myasthenia gravis) increases sensitivity and may depress respiration with
  central neural blockade; neurological disease may be worsened** [L2].
- ⚠️ **EARLY SIGNS OF TOXICITY MAY BE DIFFICULT TO DETECT IF A BLOCK IS GIVEN DURING GENERAL ANAESTHESIA**
  [L2].
- **Cardiac: local anaesthetics ENHANCE CONDUCTION DEFECTS — care in severe bradycardia, heart block or
  impaired conduction; risk of increased cardiovascular depression in cardiogenic shock or myocardial
  ischaemia; additive myocardial depression with ANTIARRHYTHMICS** [L2].
- ⚠️ **REDUCE THE DOSE in the ELDERLY and in CHILDREN UNDER 6 MONTHS.** **Renal or hepatic impairment:
  toxicity with prolonged or repeated administration; increased risk with bupivacaine and ropivacaine in
  severe renal dysfunction** [L2].
- **SAFE in pregnancy at the lowest effective concentration; unlikely to cause problems in breastfeeding**
  [L2].

### Complications of the block itself [L1]
**Most adverse effects relate to TECHNIQUE or to the EFFECTS OF THE BLOCK, rather than to the drugs** [L1].
**POST-DURAL PUNCTURE HEADACHE is the commonest neurological complication of central neural blockade.**
Others: **paraesthesia, persistent anaesthesia, weakness or paralysis, loss of sphincter control, anterior
spinal artery occlusion, cauda equina syndrome, arachnoiditis.** ⚠️ **Dense motor block MAY MASK
DEVELOPMENT OF EPIDURAL HAEMATOMA OR ABSCESS**, and ⚠️ **COMPARTMENT SYNDROME MAY BE MASKED IN ANAESTHETIC
LIMBS** [L1].
**Intra-articular infusions for 48–72 hours postoperatively — not a marketed indication — have been
associated with CHONDROLYSIS, mostly after shoulder surgery** [L1].

# The Australian general-practice setting [S1][S2]

> **MOST SKIN CANCERS DIAGNOSED IN AUSTRALIA, INCLUDING MELANOMAS, ARE IDENTIFIED AND MANAGED IN PRIMARY
> CARE**, and **procedures under local anaesthesia are a common and appropriate part of the general
> practice skill set** [S1]. **Keratinocyte cancers have an incidence of over 1% per year and a LIFETIME
> RISK OF 69%** [S1].

### Sampling [S1]

> ⚠️ **"FOR THE LESS EXPERIENCED CLINICIAN, AN EXCISIONAL BIOPSY OF THE ENTIRE LESION IS THE PREFERRED
> METHOD OF DIAGNOSTIC SAMPLE. Only with more experience should other methods be considered"** — because
> ⚠️ **"ONE OF THE WORST OUTCOMES FOR A PATIENT IS A MISSED DIAGNOSIS OF MELANOMA DUE TO POOR SAMPLING
> TECHNIQUE"** [S1].

**SHAVE suits a pink macule or scaly non-indurated patch; a NODULE OR INDURATED PLAQUE suits a PUNCH
biopsy.** **The question "WHAT AM I TRYING TO EXCLUDE?" should influence the type of biopsy** [S1].
**A punch defect of 8 mm or less contracts, so the final scar is 20–30% SMALLER than the initial defect;
too much suture tension can cause NECROSIS** [S1].

### ⚠️ The dose–complication signal, and what it does and does not support [S2]

> **An Australian pilot study found that BOTH THE DOSE AND VOLUME OF LIGNOCAINE were significantly
> associated with complication rates after excision of suspected skin cancers (P = 0.0084 and P = 0.0209)**
> [S2].
>
> **After an education intervention there was a 43% REDUCTION IN DOSE and a 25% REDUCTION IN VOLUME —
> WITHOUT EPISODES OF INADEQUATE ANALGESIA** [S2].
>
> **The association was with the overall complication rate and with DEHISCENCE.** ⚠️ **"Lignocaine dose or
> volume were NOT significant risk factors for INFECTION in either period"** [S2].

> ⚠️ **THE AUTHORS' OWN CAVEAT, QUOTED IN FULL:** **"This pilot study reported a relationship between
> lidocaine dose and volume and rates of complications. SHORTCOMINGS OF THIS STUDY LIMIT ATTRIBUTION OF
> FINDINGS TO CLINICAL PRACTICE. However, the results justify further rigorous research."** [S2]
>
> ⚠️ **AND A LIMIT VISIBLE IN ITS OWN DATA: ONE PRACTITIONER PERFORMED 54% OF ALL PROCEDURES, WHILE SIX
> PERFORMED FEWER THAN 1%** [S2].

**Context the study gives:** **the most common complications are wound dehiscence and/or infection**, and
**well-described risk factors include age over 80 and excisions on the limbs** — in this study, **lesions
on the LIMBS or MALIGNANT on histology** had a significant positive relationship with complications [S2].
**Previous research into complications after skin cancer excision has NOT considered local anaesthetic as a
contributor to wound healing, and wound healing research is INCONCLUSIVE about lignocaine** [S2].

> ⚠️ **HOW TO READ THIS.** **It is a signal, not a rule.** **AMH's mg/kg maxima remain the only limits in
> this guideline.** **What S2 supports is the weaker and still useful proposition that using less — the
> lowest dose at the weakest concentration, which is AMH's own instruction [L1] — was achievable in an
> Australian practice without losing analgesia.**
