# Salicylate poisoning in children

## Key points

- ⚠️ **Symptoms may be minimal at first, with severe toxicity not evident until 6–12 hours.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Serum concentration correlates poorly with toxicity; deterioration can occur as the serum level falls**, because CNS concentration is rising. [S1]
- **Classic acute features:** vomiting, tinnitus, hyperventilation, respiratory alkalosis and metabolic acidosis. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Hidden sources:** aspirin-containing compounds · **oil of wintergreen (1 teaspoon of 98% salicylate = 7000 mg)** · Dencorub · some teething gels · willow bark. [S1]

## Risk assessment

- **Under 150 mg/kg:** minimal symptoms. **150–300 mg/kg:** mild–moderate (tinnitus, vomiting, hyperventilation). ⚠️ **Over 300 mg/kg: severe (acidosis, seizures, hyperthermia).** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Hyperventilation is not always present in children — its absence does not exclude significant toxicity.** Assess dehydration carefully. [S1]
- **History:** immediate or sustained release, dose, time, co-ingestants, accidental or intentional. [S1]

## Investigations

- **Blood gases indicate severity:** phase 1 respiratory alkalosis with alkaluria → phase 2 paradoxical aciduria (pH under 6) with respiratory alkalosis → **phase 3 metabolic acidosis and hypokalaemia.** [S1]
- **Salicylate level at presentation, then 2–4-hourly if symptomatic or enteric-coated, until declining.** Also U&E, creatinine and glucose. [S1]

## Who needs treatment

- ⚠️ **Treat (and admit/consult):** acute ingestion of 150 mg/kg or more · all symptomatic patients · unknown quantity. [S1]

## Resuscitation and decontamination

- ⚠️ **Intubation apnoea can worsen acidosis and cause cardiac arrest.** Consider pre-loading with sodium bicarbonate and get senior help. **If ventilated, keep pH 7.45–7.5** — which may mean matching the pre-intubation respiratory rate. [S1]
- **Activated charcoal 1 g/kg** may be indicated in massive overdose, ideally within 1 hour — **discuss with toxicology (13 11 26) first.** [S1]
- **Rehydrate to euvolaemia; correct electrolytes and hypoglycaemia.** ⚠️ **Correcting metabolic acidosis is critical to limit CNS penetration.** [S1]

## Enhanced elimination

- **Urinary alkalinisation: slow IV bicarbonate bolus 2 mmol/kg, then infusion 1 mmol/kg/hr, keeping urine pH above 7.5.** Needs a catheter to monitor urine pH. For mild–moderate salicylism. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Alkalinisation is not possible with hypokalaemia and itself causes urinary potassium loss — replace potassium IV to keep a mid-range level.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Consider haemodialysis early in moderate–severe poisoning.** Indications include: alkalinisation not feasible (renal failure, pulmonary oedema) · **level over 7.2 mmol/L (100 mg/dL), or rising above 4.4 mmol/L (60 mg/dL) despite alkalinisation** · altered mental state, refractory acidaemia or electrolyte imbalance, hyperthermia over 39 °C despite cooling, or renal failure. [S1]

## Disposition

- **Discharge after 6 hours' observation** if still asymptomatic with normal acid–base status and a level in the therapeutic range. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Refer to an ICU capable of haemodialysis** if symptomatic after more than 300 mg/kg and/or meeting dialysis criteria. **PIPER: 1300 137 650.** [S1]
- **Intentional self-harm → local mental health services** (e.g. Orygen 1800 888 320); **recreational → YoDAA** (1800 458 685). [S1]
