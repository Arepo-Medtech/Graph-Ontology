## ⚠️ Psoriasis is a cardiovascular risk factor, not only a skin disease

> **Patients with psoriasis have an increased risk of cardiovascular disease, and possibly metabolic
> syndrome, compared with the general population. Manage cardiovascular risk factors and discourage
> smoking** [A1].

**Plaque psoriasis is the most common form of this chronic, inflammatory disease and classically affects
elbows, knees, buttocks and scalp** [A1].

**Monitor patients for the development of psoriatic arthritis; consider consulting a rheumatologist if
suspected** [A1].

## Triggers to remove first

**Minimise or eliminate potential triggers where possible** [A1]:

- **stress, trauma**
- **smoking, alcohol**
- ⚠️ **infections — streptococcal throat infection may precipitate guttate psoriasis**
- ⚠️ **drugs — lithium, hydroxychloroquine, ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers**

**That drug list matters**: four widely prescribed classes, at least two of which are first-line
cardiovascular therapy in a population already at raised cardiovascular risk.

## Before immunosuppressants or cytokine modulators

**Consider** [A1]:

- ⚠️ **presence of infection, including latent infection such as hepatitis B and TB**
- ⚠️ **immunisation requirements, especially for live vaccines**
- **history of malignant disease**

**Consider pneumococcal and annual influenza vaccinations for patients taking immunosuppressants or cytokine
modulators** [A1].

## Referral

**Refer to a dermatologist if appropriate topical therapy fails, phototherapy or systemic treatment is
needed, or if psoriasis is pustular, severe or extensive** [A1].

## Topical treatment

**Used for most patients with mild-to-moderate plaque psoriasis, and as an adjunct to phototherapy or
systemic treatment in more severe disease. Topical agents are commonly used in combination** [A1].

**Moisturisers** **hydrate the skin, soften scaling and reduce itching and irritation**. **Encourage regular
use, particularly after bathing or showering**; **advise emollient soap substitutes or fragrance-free bath
oils** [A1].

**Topical corticosteroids are a first-line option** and can be combined. **Choose potency and formulation on
disease severity and location, and on patient preference and age** — **mild corticosteroids may be useful on
face, flexures and genitals** [A1].

> ⚠️ **Psoriasis symptoms can worsen quickly after stopping topical corticosteroids, particularly when
> treatment is stopped abruptly** [A1].

**Calcipotriol** has **similar efficacy to potent or very potent corticosteroids**. ⚠️ **It is only available
in combination with betamethasone; this combination is more effective than either agent alone.** **It can
irritate, particularly the face or flexures** [A1].

**Salicylic acid** is a **keratolytic that reduces scaling and softens plaques**, often used with **coal tar,
dithranol and corticosteroids, as it allows them to penetrate lesions, increasing their efficacy** [A1].

**Coal tar** is **particularly used for scalp, hands and feet, and can be used for guttate psoriasis**.
⚠️ **Preparations can be messy, stain clothing and smell unpleasant. They are also photosensitising and may
be irritant** [A1].

⚠️ **Dithranol is rarely used. It is irritant and stains clothing, skin and hair. Avoid on the face, flexures
and genitals** [A1].

## Phototherapy

**UV light is used to reduce inflammation of the skin.** Types are **UVB therapy (narrow- or broad-band)** and
**photochemotherapy — topical or oral methoxsalen with UVA light (PUVA)** [A1].

**Treatment is generally given 2 or 3 times per week for a few months. Repeat treatment courses may be
needed** [A1].

## Systemic treatment

**For psoriasis that is severe or extensive, or for less severe psoriasis when topical therapy or
phototherapy are inadequate or inappropriate** [A1].

| Agent | Role and monitoring [A1] |
|---|---|
| **Methotrexate** | immunosuppressant for **severe psoriasis**; ⚠️ **requires careful monitoring, particularly for myelosuppression and hepatotoxicity** |
| **Ciclosporin** | immunosuppressant for **severe psoriasis**; ⚠️ **requires careful monitoring, particularly for nephrotoxicity and hypertension** |
| **Apremilast** | oral cytokine modulator that **may reduce pro-inflammatory TNF-alpha and interleukins-17 and -23, and increase anti-inflammatory interleukin-10**; used **when methotrexate is inadequate or inappropriate** |
| **Acitretin** | oral retinoid for **severe psoriasis — pustular or erythrodermic, or palm or sole psoriasis**. ⚠️ **Rarely used in women of child-bearing potential due to risk of teratogenicity** |

**Injectable cytokine modulators (biologicals)** are **usually used for severe psoriasis when phototherapy,
methotrexate, ciclosporin, acitretin or apremilast is inadequate or inappropriate; many also treat psoriatic
arthritis** [A1]. ⚠️ **They can cause immunosuppression and increase the risk of infection** [A1].

- **Interleukin inhibitors**: **guselkumab, ixekizumab, risankizumab, secukinumab, tildrakizumab, ustekinumab**
- **TNF-alpha antagonists**: **adalimumab, certolizumab, etanercept, infliximab** [A1]

## Dosing

> ⚠️ **Paraphrased from AMH monographs, no stored quote, flagged `dose: true`. Check before prescribing.**

| Agent | Dose [A1] |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ **Calcipotriol with betamethasone** | **maximum daily dose 15 g; maximum weekly dose 100 g. Do not apply to more than 30% of body surface.** **Cream** — scalp, trunk, limbs, adult, **once a day for up to 8 weeks**. **Foam** — **once a day for up to 4 weeks** |
| ⚠️ **Methotrexate** | **taken once a week, on the same day each week**. Adult **oral/SC/IM, initially 10–15 mg once a week**, adjusted to response; **usual maintenance 10–25 mg once a week (maximum oral 30 mg; SC/IM 25 mg)**. ⚠️ **When changing from oral to SC/IM, consider reducing the dose and/or monitoring carefully, especially for oral doses >15 mg, as bioavailability is higher parenterally** |
| **Ciclosporin** | **dose and duration differ depending on the disease — seek specialist advice** |
| **Apremilast** | **oral, initially 10 mg once on day 1, then 10 mg twice on day 2, then increase by 10 mg each day to 30 mg twice a day**. ⚠️ **Consider stopping if there is no response after 24 weeks.** **CrCl <30 mL/min: dose reduced** |
| **Acitretin** | adult **oral 25–30 mg once daily for 2–4 weeks**, then **maintenance generally 25–50 mg once a day** based on efficacy and tolerance. **Child: 0.5–1 mg/kg once daily, usual maximum 35 mg daily** |

> ⚠️ **Methotrexate is a once-weekly drug.** Daily administration is a recognised fatal error. The monograph
> states the same-day-each-week rule before it states any dose, and this guideline keeps that order.
