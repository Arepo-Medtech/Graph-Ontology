## Diagnosis

**Three key features per the 2014 AAO-HNS guideline** [S1]: **symptoms (otalgia, pruritus and aural
fullness) and signs (tenderness, oedema and erythema) of external auditory canal inflammation, with rapid
onset within 48 hours.**

> ⚠️ **Acute tragal tenderness is widely accepted as a pathognomonic sign** [S1].

**Otalgia can be elicited by tragal pumping and pinna retraction**, and **otoscopy… is often painful,
necessitating analgesia beforehand** [S1].

**Organisms:** **nearly always bacterial with *P. aeruginosa* or *S. aureus*** [A1][S1]. **Fungal causes
often manifest as co-pathogens, in immunocompromised patients, or after prolonged use of topical
antibacterial drops** [S1].

| On otoscopy | Suggests |
|---|---|
| **White 'blotting paper' debris** | ⚠️ **Candida** [A1] — **off-white sebaceous-like debris** [S1] |
| **Black spores** | ⚠️ **Aspergillus** [A1] — **white hyphae with black spores** [S1] |

⚠️ **The integrity of the tympanic membrane is imperative in choosing appropriate topical antibiotics, as
acute otitis externa can cause secondary perforation** [S1].

**Risk factors** [S1][A1]: **water exposure**, ⚠️ **direct trauma including cotton buds, which allow
pathogens to bypass the skin barrier**, **hearing aids which hinder ventilation and cerumen clearance**,
**humidity**, **skin conditions (psoriasis, dermatitis)**, **radiotherapy**, and ⚠️ **diabetes — associated
with microangiopathy along the canal, reduced immune response and impaired wound healing** [S1].

## ⚠️ Swab or not

> **There are many effective agents with little difference in clinical cure rates between them — routine
> ear swabs are not required** [A1].
>
> **Topical antibacterial ear drops… are empirically commenced without the need for swab confirmation
> because a significant proportion of cases are bacterial in origin.** ⚠️ **Generally, 65–90% of patients
> experience clinical improvement within 7–10 days, REGARDLESS OF THE SPECIFIC TOPICAL FORMULATION
> USED** [S1].

⚠️ **The sources agree. Swab only if empirical treatment fails** [S1], or **to guide systemic therapy in
severe infection** [A1].

## Treatment

**Give all patients adequate pain relief, eg an NSAID. If the pain is severe, consider early referral to an
ENT specialist** [A1].

⚠️ **Keep the external ear canal dry by meticulous gentle cleansing and drying with tissue spears (rolled
tissue paper), 2–4 times daily if possible, but at least once daily** [A1].

### Drops — with dose and price [S1]

| Drug | Perforation? | Dose | ~$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ciprofloxacin 0.3%** | ⚠️ **safe ±** | **5 drops twice a day until a few days after symptoms have cleared** | **31** |
| **Ciprofloxacin 0.2% + hydrocortisone 1%** | ⚠️ **safe ±** | **3 drops twice a day** | **47** |
| **Framycetin 0.5%** | ⚠️ **NOT suitable** | **3 drops three times a day** | **12** |
| **Framycetin + gramicidin + dexamethasone** | ⚠️ **NOT suitable** | **3 drops 3–4 times a day** | **11** |
| **Neomycin + gramicidin + triamcinolone** | ⚠️ **NOT suitable** | **3 drops 2 or 3 times a day** (also otomycosis) | **11** |
| **Clioquinol + flumetasone** | ⚠️ **NOT suitable** | **3 drops twice a day** (otomycosis) | **23** |
| **Clotrimazole 1%** | ⚠️ **NOT suitable** | **3 drops twice a day** (otomycosis) | **11** |
| **Acetic acid + isopropyl alcohol** | ⚠️ **NOT suitable** | **4–6 drops into each ear after water exposure** (prevention) | **11** |

⚠️ **Treatment should not exceed 2 weeks** [S1]. ⚠️ **Stinging on instillation is common** with the acetic
acid/alcohol preparation [S1].

**A1's frequencies agree** and add ⚠️ **a risk of fungal infection with prolonged use, eg >7 days** [A1].

### ⚠️ The perforation rule

> **Aminoglycosides are associated with ototoxicity: AVOID if eardrum perforation cannot be excluded and use
> ciprofloxacin instead. If this is not possible, limit use to the shortest possible duration and consider
> ENT referral if prolonged (>10 days) or repeated courses are required** [A1].

⚠️ **Framycetin and neomycin are the aminoglycosides in the table above — and they are the cheap ones.**
**Avoid neomycin if eardrum perforation cannot be excluded** [A1]. ⚠️ **Only the ciprofloxacin preparations
are marked safe with or without perforation, and they cost two to four times as much.**

### ⚠️ What not to use

> ⚠️ **Anaesthetic ear drops are not appropriate; they may interfere with the penetration of topical
> antibacterials AND MASK DISEASE PROGRESSION** [A1].

**Antiseptics and drying agents (acetic acid, ethanol, isopropyl alcohol) may be effective for mild otitis
externa but can be painful and irritating to broken or inflamed skin** — ⚠️ **they are more often used long
term to PREVENT recurrences** [A1].

### Administration [A1]

**The patient should receive help if possible. The ear should be clean and dry (rolled tissue paper spears)
and the patient should lie with the affected ear UPPERMOST.** ⚠️ **Warm the container in a cup of warm water
if necessary to reduce viscosity. Instil directly into the ear canal then remain in position for 3–5
minutes; gentle massage or pressure on the tragus may aid penetration.**

⚠️ **If the ear canal is too swollen to allow drug entry, drops may be instilled via a clinician-inserted
wick or ribbon gauze**, reviewed every few days until swelling subsides [A1]. **Ointments are used less
often as they may accumulate and cause obstruction** [A1].

### When it does not work

⚠️ **Reassess at 72 hours if there is no clinical improvement** — **ensure compliance and that the canal is
adequately receiving treatment; consider repeat microsuction or wick insertion; swab if a non-pseudomonal
cause is suspected** [S1].

**Fungal infection is rare but may occur after extended use of antibacterial/corticosteroid drops. If there
is no improvement in 48 hours, or if there are signs of fungal infection, use a topical product with
antifungal activity** [A1]. ⚠️ **There are no single-agent antifungal ear products available, but most
antifungals for the skin are suitable** [A1].

### Systemic antibacterials

⚠️ **Generally NOT required except for severe infections, eg with significant cellulitis, fever or cervical
lymphadenopathy; P. aeruginosa is usually involved. Both systemic and topical treatments are then needed,
chosen on ear swab culture** [A1].

## ⚠️ Necrotising (malignant) otitis externa

> ⚠️ **Patients who are immunocompromised or have diabetes are at high risk of developing malignant otitis
> externa — osteomyelitis of the temporal bone. Consider early ENT referral for patients presenting with
> otitis externa and SEVERE PAIN** [A1].
>
> ⚠️ **Patients with persistent or worsening otologic symptoms, or those developing cellulitic or
> neurological signs, should be investigated for the possibility of necrotising otitis externa** [S1].

**Involvement of the soft tissues surrounding the ear, perichondritis of the pinna or suspicion of malignant
otitis externa generally requires HOSPITAL ADMISSION AND IV ANTIBACTERIALS** [A1].

## Prevention

**Keep ears dry during treatment and for 2 weeks after; use cotton wool balls smeared with white soft
paraffin while showering or bathing, and avoid swimming.** **Afterwards use ear plugs, and consider an
antiseptic ear drop or drying agent after exposure to water** [A1].

⚠️ **Avoid local trauma to the ear canal; advise patients not to use cotton ear buds** [A1].

# Ear wax

**Wax build-up may cause deafness and pain and is common in older people, young children and those with
cognitive impairment; it is associated with hearing aids and cotton bud use** [A1].

> ⚠️ **Avoid using cotton buds and ear candles** [A1].

## ⚠️ Often it needs nothing

> ⚠️ **Over 30% of ears impacted with wax will clear WITHOUT TREATMENT within 5 days** [A1].

## And the evidence for the rest is thin

⚠️ **Direct comparative trials between interventions are lacking**, and ⚠️ **there is no good evidence for
the appropriate duration of treatment with ear drops alone** [A1].

**A reasonable guide is 2–5 drops once or twice daily into the affected ear for 3–5 days** [A1].
**Cerumenolytics include water and saline** [A1].

**S1's two cerumen products** [S1]: **docusate sodium 0.5% — fill the ear canal on 2 consecutive nights**;
**carbamide peroxide — 5–10 drops twice daily for up to 4 days**. ⚠️ **Neither is suitable for tympanic
membrane perforation.**

**Syringing:** **gentle syringing with warm (body temperature) water or sodium chloride 0.9% may help loosen
and remove wax; if wax appears hard and impacted, consider cerumenolytic drops 15 minutes before
syringing** [A1].

> ⚠️ **AVOID syringing if there is a history of otic surgery, perforated eardrum or otitis (externa or
> media). IN PARTICULAR, PEOPLE WHO ARE DEAF IN ONE EAR SHOULD NOT HAVE THE GOOD EAR SYRINGED** [A1].
