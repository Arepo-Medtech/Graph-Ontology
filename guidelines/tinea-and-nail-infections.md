## ⚠️ Two ways to get this wrong

### 1 · Treating tinea as eczema

> ⚠️ **Exclude discoid eczema when ringworm-like lesions are present; THIS IS A COMMON MISDIAGNOSIS** [A1].

### 2 · Treating tinea with a steroid

> ⚠️ **Corticosteroid-modified tinea, 'tinea incognito', MASKS the classical clinical presentation and can
> delay diagnosis. Topical steroids have an immunosuppressive effect that can COMPROMISE THE EFFICACY of
> antifungal treatment** [S1].

⚠️ **The two errors compound: a ringworm lesion mistaken for eczema gets a steroid, which makes it look
less like ringworm.** S1 lists **atypical presentation due to partial treatment and/or corticosteroid use**
as a named diagnostic nuance [S1].

**Confirmation of fungal infection is warranted as the clinical picture may be very similar to non-fungal
conditions** [A1].

### ⚠️ And one red flag

> ⚠️ **Isolated tinea unguium requires careful exclusion of other more sinister conditions — squamous cell
> carcinoma or nail bed melanoma** [S1].

## What points to fungus, and what does not [S1]

| Suggestive | **Not** suggestive |
|---|---|
| **skin and/or hair and nail involvement** | ⚠️ **systemic symptoms** |
| **pruritus** | ⚠️ **constitutional symptoms** |
| **risk factors: public changerooms, pets, overseas travel, immunosuppression** | ⚠️ **pain** |
| | **triggers: photosensitivity, new medication, new topical exposures** |

**Also consider:** **diabetes, immunosuppression, obesity** [A1].

**Organisms:** ⚠️ ***Trichophyton rubrum* is the leading cause worldwide.** ***Microsporum canis***,
***T. mentagrophytes*** and ***T. verrucosum*** **may be associated with zoonotic exposure through pets
(cats, dogs) or other animals (cows, horses)** [S1].

**Transmission** is **through direct skin contact with an infected person or animal, or indirectly through
exposure to fungal spores** [S1]. ⚠️ **The dermatophyte infects the stratum corneum only, with an
incubation period of 1–3 weeks** [S1].

## ⚠️ Sampling — and the sources differ on the test

| Source | Test |
|---|---|
| **A1 (AMH 2026)** | **skin scrapings from the edge of the lesion for MICROSCOPY AND CULTURE** |
| ⚠️ **S1 (AJGP March 2026)** | **microscopy, culture AND PCR.** ⚠️ **PCR is available in some Australian laboratories and PREFERRED because of its faster turnaround and superior sensitivity and specificity compared with MCS.** **If PCR is not accessible, fungal MCS is inexpensive and acceptable — though CULTURE CAN TAKE UP TO 6 WEEKS** |

⚠️ **Both are stated. S1 is the more specific and the more recent.**

**How to take it** [S1]:
- ⚠️ **Collect samples PRIOR to treatment initiation, and of sufficient quantity to reduce false negatives**
- ⚠️ **Skin scrapings should ideally come from a lesion NOT recently treated with a topical antifungal**
- **Skin:** **a scalpel at a blunt angle**
- **Nail:** **clippings and scrapings of subungual debris using a scalpel or curette**
- **Hair:** **removed using tweezers to include the ROOT, with surrounding skin scrapings**
- **Yellow-top specimen containers for all sample sites**, labelled with specimen type and location

⚠️ **Do not wait for the culture to treat:** **an obvious clinical picture or positive microscopy result is
enough; it is not necessary to wait for culture results** [A1].

## Treatment

### Topical — for mild, localised disease

**Mild localised skin infections usually respond to topical treatments including azoles, terbinafine and
tolnaftate** [A1]. ⚠️ **Topical treatment is NOT usually successful for infections involving the hair, and
hyperkeratotic lesions also respond poorly** [A1].

| Agent | [A1] |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ **Terbinafine** | **generally preferred** — **more rapid response than azoles**, **may be slightly more effective for tinea pedis**, ⚠️ **FUNGICIDAL**; **gel, cream, spray, single-application liquid** |
| **Azoles (clotrimazole, miconazole)** | **an alternative**; ⚠️ **FUNGISTATIC**; **cream, liquid, spray, powder**; **well tolerated** |
| **Tolnaftate** | ⚠️ **may irritate skin; less effective** than azoles and terbinafine; **powder spray** |
| **Miscellaneous** (benzoic with salicylic acid, undecanoic acid salts, selenium sulfide, methylrosanilinium chloride) | ⚠️ **less effective than azoles, terbinafine and tolnaftate** |

> ⚠️ **Continue topical treatment for 2 weeks AFTER clinical signs resolve — EXCEPT terbinafine** [A1].

**Formulation:** **creams are generally preferred; liquids or sprays for large and/or hairy areas; powders
on feet, groin and other intertriginous areas and inside socks and shoes** [A1].

### Oral — when and how much

**Indicated for tinea capitis, widespread infection, immunocompromised status, failed response to topical
treatment, or involvement of the palms, soles or nails** [S1]; **and for tinea of the scalp and hair, palms
or soles, widespread or disseminated infection, and immunocompromised patients** [A1].

| | Dose [S1] |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ **Terbinafine** | ⚠️ **250 mg daily for 2 weeks** — **the preferred regimen**, with **longer courses when there is nail or scalp involvement** |
| **Itraconazole** | **100 mg daily for 2–4 weeks** — **an oral alternative** |

**A1's agent choice is organism- and site-specific** [A1]:

| Site / organism | First choice | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| **Scalp and hair — unconfirmed or *Trichophyton*** | **terbinafine** | **itraconazole or fluconazole (last line)** |
| ⚠️ **Scalp and hair — *Microsporum*** | ⚠️ **griseofulvin** | — |
| **Other areas (skin, groin, feet)** | **terbinafine** | **fluconazole, itraconazole or griseofulvin (last line)** |

⚠️ **The one place the organism changes the drug is *Microsporum* scalp infection, where A1 puts griseofulvin
first and terbinafine nowhere.**

**Adjunct for tinea capitis:** **ketoconazole or selenium sulphide shampoo** [S1].

### ⚠️ Terbinafine safety

**Generally well tolerated with a small risk of gastrointestinal side effects and transient liver enzyme
elevation.** ⚠️ **Rare complications include hepatitis, photosensitivity, pancytopenia, cutaneous lupus and
Stevens–Johnson syndrome / toxic epidermal necrolysis** [S1].

> ⚠️ **Dose adjustment is indicated in renal impairment.** ⚠️ **Monitoring of liver function and full blood
> count is recommended with courses longer than 6 weeks** [S1].

**That threshold matters** — nail infections routinely run longer than six weeks.

### ⚠️ When it does not work

> **If there is good compliance and appropriate route of therapy, yet inadequate treatment response,
> alternative diagnoses AS WELL AS TERBINAFINE-RESISTANT SPECIES should be considered.** ⚠️ **High rates of
> terbinafine-resistant *T. interdigitale* have been reported in India, Europe and, MORE RECENTLY,
> AUSTRALIA** [S1].

### Other measures

**Drying agents and/or antibacterial agents (eg Condy's crystals or Burow's solution) may be needed for
severe interdigital infection**, and **saline compresses, drying agents and sometimes topical
corticosteroids may be necessary for acute vesicular tinea pedis** [A1].

⚠️ **Note the exception: A1 permits a topical corticosteroid in acute vesicular tinea pedis, while
steroid-modified tinea is the trap described above. The indication is narrow.**

## Nail infections (onychomycosis)

**Most commonly caused by dermatophytes, rarely yeasts (*Candida* spp.) or moulds. Tinea unguium refers
specifically to dermatophyte infection of the nail** [A1].

**Why treat:** **reduce pain; improve appearance and function; prevent nail loss or destruction; reduce
spread of infection** [A1]. **Confirm diagnosis by microscopy and culture; consider diabetes and
immunosuppression** [A1].

> ⚠️ **Oral treatments are more effective than topical treatment in most cases** [A1]. **Oral antifungals
> are usually needed for proximal nail disease or severe nail-bed involvement** [A1].

| Oral agent | [A1] |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ **Terbinafine** | **treatment of choice for dermatophyte nail infections**; **high affinity for keratinous tissues**; **once a day**; **generally well tolerated** |
| **Itraconazole** | **active against most *Candida* spp. and effective in dermatophyte nail infections**; **once a day OR as pulse treatment** |
| **Fluconazole** | **active against most *Candida* spp. and dermatophytes**; ⚠️ **seems less effective (few comparative trials)**; ⚠️ **once a WEEK until the infected nail has completely grown out** |
| **Griseofulvin** | ⚠️ **other agents are preferred** |

| Topical agent | Frequency [A1] |
|---|---|
| **Amorolfine** | **once or twice a week** |
| **Ciclopirox** | **once a day** |
| **Miconazole** | **twice a day** |

⚠️ **Topicals are usually adequate ONLY for superficial nail infections or infection involving distal ends
of nails — and comparative trials are lacking** [A1].

**Avulsion:** **chemical or surgical nail removal may be combined with oral and/or topical antifungals for
resistant infections, generally restricted to one or a few nails.** ⚠️ **Chemical avulsion (eg topical urea
40%) causes minimal to no pain, whereas surgical avulsion is painful and may be disfiguring** [A1].

## Prevention and recurrence

⚠️ **Cases of recurrent tinea should be evaluated for any potential reservoir of dermatophytes, COMMONLY
TOENAILS** [S1] — **treat the nail or the skin keeps coming back.**

**Fungal spores shed from the skin persist in warm, moist environments** [S1]. ⚠️ **Discard old shoes that
may have a high density of fungal spores** [A1].

**Hygiene** [A1][S1]: **drying between toes and toe web spaces; a separate towel for the infected area;
thongs in public showers and change rooms; changing socks (preferably cotton) daily; avoid sharing combs,
hats, towels, bedding or clothing.**

⚠️ **Family members should be evaluated for asymptomatic carriage, particularly if infection is persistent
or recurrent** [A1].

## What happened to S1's one patient

⚠️ **This is a case report, not evidence of effect.** **Both skin scraping and nail clipping returned
positive for *T. rubrum* on PCR, but only the nail clipping showed fungal filaments on microscopy,
demonstrating the rapid and superior sensitivity of PCR.** **Given his good clinical response but presence
of nail involvement, he was continued on oral terbinafine to complete a total 3-month course** [S1].
