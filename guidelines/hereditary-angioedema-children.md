# Hereditary angioedema in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognition of hereditary angioedema (HAE, C1-esterase inhibitor deficiency), acute attack treatment with C1-INH concentrate or icatibant, airway, peripheral and abdominal attacks, disposition, short- and long-term prophylaxis and diagnostic testing in children. ## Key points

- **HAE angioedema is non-pitting swelling without urticaria, itching or redness.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Life-threatening laryngeal oedema can occur at any age.** [S1]
- **Every child diagnosed with HAE should have a personalised ASCIA HAE management plan** saying when and how to treat attacks. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Antihistamines, corticosteroids and adrenaline have no role in HAE angioedema.** Early specific treatment improves outcomes. [S1]

## Background

- HAE is autosomal dominant: **Type 1** is an absolute C1-INH deficiency (85% of cases), **Type 2** a functional one. [S1]
- **Presentations:** cutaneous swelling of face, limbs (especially hands and feet) and genitals in over 90% · upper airway swelling (tongue, soft palate, larynx), which can be life-threatening · abdominal pain with nausea, vomiting and diarrhoea, which can cause dehydration and collapse · a non-pruritic erythema marginatum rash in about one third. [S1]
- **Attacks typically peak at 24 hours and resolve over 48–72 hours.** [S1]
- **Triggers:** injury (including dental or other surgery), infection, psychological stress, medications (especially oestrogen-containing and ACE inhibitors), menstruation, vigorous exercise, certain foods — or none. [S1]
- Symptoms typically start between 5 and 11 years (40% by age 5) and may worsen in adolescence. **A family history is present in 75%.** [S1]

## Assessment

- ⚠️ **Examine the airway:** tongue, soft palate, uvula; laryngeal oedema signs — stridor, hoarseness, drooling, altered voice. **Angioedema behind the teeth is more likely to progress to airway obstruction than angioedema in front of them.** [S1]
- **Redness or itching suggests a diagnosis other than HAE.** Differentials: allergic reactions and anaphylaxis, idiopathic, drug-induced and (exceedingly rare in childhood) acquired angioedema. [S1]
- **Suspected acute attack with no prior HAE diagnosis: check C4.** [S1]

## Acute attack treatment

- Treating a non-life-threatening attack is at the discretion of the child and parent. **Treatments: icatibant (subcutaneous) or C1 esterase inhibitor concentrate (intravenous).** [S1]
- ⚠️ **First line — C1-INH concentrate (Berinert®), any age: 20 international units/kg, rounded to the nearest 500 units, by slow IV injection (4 mL/min).** Symptoms usually stabilise in 30 minutes; a second dose is rarely needed but may be given 30 min to 2 hours after the first (<1% relapse). Do not shake the solution. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Icatibant, slow SC injection:** under 12 kg or under 2 years — seek Immunology advice; **12–25 kg 10 mg (1 mL) · 26–40 kg 15 mg (1.5 mL) · 41–50 kg 20 mg (2 mL) · over 50 kg 30 mg (3 mL)**. A second dose can be given 6 hours after the first (<10% relapse); **max 3 doses/24 hours**. Injection-site reactions are usually mild. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Airway attack unresponsive to icatibant: give C1-INH concentrate as rescue.** [S1]
- **No response after 2 hours: reconsider the diagnosis** and look for other causes. [S1]
- **Second line, only if the above are unavailable: solvent/detergent-treated FFP** for laryngeal and/or severe abdominal attacks — it may paradoxically worsen angioedema. Consult immunology/haematology, but don't delay in an emergency with no alternative. Adverse effects: commonly nausea, pruritus, rash; rarely allergic reactions, bronchospasm, cardiorespiratory collapse. [S1]

## Special clinical features

- ⚠️ **Airway/laryngeal attack is a medical emergency: immediate icatibant or C1-INH concentrate.** Signs of impending obstruction → senior, ICU and anaesthetic involvement, ideally with fibre-optic-guided intubation and surgical airway skills. [S1]
- **Peripheral swelling:** treat significant pain or disability, or angioedema of the face or genitals; mild swelling that doesn't affect function may not need treatment. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Abdominal attack with moderate–severe pain, vomiting and/or distension: give C1-INH concentrate**; consider fluid resuscitation if severe. Mild pain: simple analgesia including NSAIDs. [S1]

## Consultation, transfer and discharge

- **Consult the paediatric team:** first episode of swelling or no previous diagnosis · airway/laryngeal attack · no response to medication within 2 hours · relapse after first-line treatment. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Transfer:** airway/laryngeal attack (usually needs PICU/HDU observation) · immediately life-threatening situation · care beyond the hospital's comfort level · inadequate response to acute treatment. [S1]
- **Discharge:** after successful emergency treatment, children improving from peripheral or abdominal attacks may go home even if peripheral swelling has not fully resolved. **Every child diagnosed with HAE needs an allergy/immunology consultation.** [S1]

## Prophylaxis and diagnosis

- **Short-term prophylaxis, first line: C1-INH concentrate (Berinert® IV), any age, 20 units/kg rounded to the nearest 500 units, 1–6 hours before any medical, surgical or dental procedure.** [S1]
- **Second line, only if first-line prophylaxis is unavailable: icatibant slow SC**, 2 years and over (under 2: seek Immunology advice), under 30 minutes before the procedure; keep an additional dose for emergency and **observe for over 12 hours** afterwards. Doses: under 12 kg seek Immunology advice; 12–25 kg 10 mg (1 mL); 26–40 kg 15 mg (1.5 mL); 41–50 kg 20 mg (2 mL); over 50 kg 30 mg (3 mL). [S1]
- **Long-term prophylaxis** is prescribed by the child's immunologist: oral tranexamic acid, subcutaneous C1-INH concentrate (Berinert® SC), lanadelumab or garadacimab. [S1]
- **Non-acute screening, with an immunologist:** serum C4, C1-INH level and C1 functional assay; **two sets at least 3 months apart**, with at least 1 set after the age of 1 (results are hard to interpret under 1 year). Genetic testing is not usually needed. **Screen the family if confirmed.** [S1]
- **Type 1:** C1-INH function, C1-INH protein and C4 all low. **Type 2:** C1-INH function low, C1-INH protein normal or raised, C4 low. [S1]
