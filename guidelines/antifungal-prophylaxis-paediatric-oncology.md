# Antifungal prophylaxis in children with cancer or HSCT

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** invasive fungal infection (IFI) risk stratification, prophylactic antifungal doses, therapeutic drug monitoring and azole drug interactions in children with cancer or undergoing HSCT. Related: `febrile-neutropenia-children` (empiric antifungal treatment for suspected IFI, which this page defers to). Adult/general counterparts: `candidiasis`, `anti-infectives-general-principles` — neither covers oncology prophylaxis.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 25 of 25 claims quoted verbatim from the **RCH Melbourne Clinical Practice Guideline *Antifungal prophylaxis for children with cancer or undergoing haematopoietic stem cell transplant*** (Last updated December 2025); **39 fragments re-checked by machine. 11 quoted doses.**

> ⚠️ **Prophylaxis, not treatment.** Suspected IFI (prolonged or recurrent fever) is worked up and treated empirically as in `febrile-neutropenia-children`.

> ⚠️ **Azoles interact heavily** — itraconazole is contraindicated with vinca alkaloids, and azoles are stopped around bortezomib and before busulfan/thiotepa conditioning.

## Who needs prophylaxis

- **Primary antifungal prophylaxis is recommended when the underlying IFI incidence exceeds 10%**, adjusted for institutional epidemiology. **High- and intermediate-risk patients should receive it.** [S1]
- **High risk (≥10%), mould more than yeast:** high-risk/very-high-risk ALL in intensive phases · AML · relapsed acute leukaemia · allogeneic HSCT with acute grade II–IV or chronic extensive GvHD · severe aplastic anaemia. **Yeast more than mould:** allogeneic HSCT pre-engraftment. [S1]
- **Intermediate risk (yeast more than mould):** autologous HSCT or medulloblastoma, neutropenic phase. **Low risk (≤5%):** non-relapse ALL outside HR/VHR intensive phases, lymphoma and solid tumours (excluding autologous HSCT). [S1]
- **An azole is preferred where possible**, with TDM for posaconazole and voriconazole. [S1]
- **Secondary prophylaxis is required** for further chemotherapy after proven or probable IFI; the agent that treated it may be used if it was tolerated and effective. Discuss with infectious diseases. [S1]

## Doses

- **Fluconazole 6–12 mg/kg (max 400 mg) oral or IV daily**; round oral doses to the nearest 50 mg capsule. Adjust in renal impairment. [S1]
- **Posaconazole tablets (preferred): 300 mg orally daily** at ≥13 years, or ≥10 years and ≥30 kg. **IV if oral is not tolerated or absorbed: 6 mg/kg (max 300 mg) daily, 2–18 years.** Trough target over 0.7 µg/mL, taken 7 days after starting or any dose or formulation change. [S1]
- **Posaconazole IV via central line only, over 90 minutes.** Under-10 tablet dosing and the oral suspension (poorly absorbed) only with CCC pharmacy and ID. [S1]
- **Voriconazole oral: 9 mg/kg (max 350 mg) BD** for 2 to under 12 years, or 12–14 years under 50 kg; **200 mg BD** for ≥15 years, or 12–14 years ≥50 kg. **IV: 8 mg/kg BD (9 mg/kg on day 1)** or **4 mg/kg BD (6 mg/kg on day 1)** in the same bands. Trough target 1–2 µg/mL. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Voriconazole: take the trough 2–5 days after starting or changing;** give oral doses 1 hour before or after food; counsel sun avoidance (skin cancer reported with use over 1 year). A trough over 5–6 mg/L raises the probability of neurotoxicity (visual disturbance, hallucinations, encephalopathy). [S1]
- **Itraconazole oral solution 2.5 mg/kg BD (max 200 mg daily)**, on an empty stomach at least 1 hour before food. Solution and capsules are not interchangeable. Trough target over 0.5 µg/mL, 10–15 days after starting or changing dose. [S1]
- **Liposomal amphotericin 2–3 mg/kg IV three times a week (max 100 mg daily)**, rounded to 50 mg vials but at least 2 mg/kg. Monitor renal function, potassium, magnesium and liver; consider 10 mL/kg normal saline pre/concurrent hydration. [S1]
- **Caspofungin: 25 mg/m² (max 50 mg) IV daily at 1–3 months; 50 mg/m² (max 50 mg) IV daily from 3 months.** **Micafungin 1 mg/kg (max 50 mg) IV daily.** Both can cause histamine-type reactions. [S1]

## Therapeutic drug monitoring

- ⚠️ **Every patient on itraconazole, voriconazole or posaconazole must have TDM**, plus one repeat level to confirm stability; after that, not routinely. [S1]
- **Repeat TDM for:** dose or formulation change (including IV/oral switch) · a new interacting drug · suspected toxicity (**take the level before withholding or adjusting**) · a proven, probable or possible IFI. [S1]
- **Discuss dose adjustments with oncology pharmacy or infectious diseases**, and repeat the level first. [S1]

## Drug interactions

- ⚠️ **Vinca alkaloids (vincristine, vinblastine): itraconazole is strictly contraindicated**; avoid posaconazole where possible. **Weekly IV vinca — use a non-azole.** **Monthly — withhold voriconazole the day before, of and after**; if posaconazole is used, withhold it 5 days before, the day of and the day after. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Bortezomib: stop all azoles 72 hours before and restart 72 hours after the last dose (24 hours for fluconazole)**, substituting a non-azole in between. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Busulfan and thiotepa: avoid voriconazole, posaconazole and itraconazole for 7 days before HSCT conditioning** (fluconazole is exempt). [S1]
- **Tyrosine kinase inhibitors:** itraconazole, voriconazole and posaconazole are not recommended in most protocols; fluconazole is usually usable, **except with sorafenib, which mandates a non-azole.** [S1]
- **Venetoclax: avoid the combination**; if unavoidable, involve cancer pharmacy and the oncology consultant (venetoclax dose reduction may be needed). [S1]
- **Sirolimus, tacrolimus, ciclosporin, everolimus, temsirolimus:** levels rise significantly — monitor levels; dose reduction is usually required. Use itraconazole with extreme caution with sirolimus. [S1]
- **Rifampicin and rifabutin induce azole metabolism** — avoid where possible; a higher azole dose may be needed. **PPIs raise voriconazole levels** (monitor when starting or stopping) and reduce posaconazole liquid absorption. [S1]
- ⚠️ **QT-prolonging drugs** (gemtuzumab, macrolides, quinolones, conventional antipsychotics): ECG before starting and weekly. **Do not use azoles with additional cardiac risk factors** such as reduced ventricular function or electrolyte disturbance. [S1]

## Escalate

- **Discuss every start, stop or tolerability problem with the primary treating team.** Discuss all suspected or confirmed fungal infections with them and consider transfer to a tertiary paediatric cancer centre. [S1]
