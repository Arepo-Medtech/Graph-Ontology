# Acute coronary syndromes

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-21 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** acute hospital settings, public and private — **from onset of symptoms to completion of treatment
in hospital**, including patients who develop a suspected ACS while in hospital for another condition [S1].

> **Australian primary source.** Australian Commission on Safety and Quality in Health Care (ACSQHC).
> ***Acute Coronary Syndromes Clinical Care Standard*** (2019) [S1]. **Six quality statements.** Page last
> updated 30 April 2026. **Endorsed by** the Australasian College for Emergency Medicine, the Australian
> College of Rural and Remote Medicine, CRANAplus, Heart Foundation Australia and the Royal Australasian
> College of Surgeons.

## ⚠️ The Commission states its own currency gap, precisely

> **Key evidence sources that underpin the Standard are clinical guidelines from the Heart Foundation of
> Australia and Cardiac Society of Australia and New Zealand: *Australian Clinical Guidelines for the
> Management of Acute Coronary Syndromes 2016*.**
>
> **Note that these clinical guidelines were updated in 2025. The Acute Coronary Syndromes Clinical Care
> Standard has not been updated against the 2025 guidelines** [S1].

> **The Commission has commenced a review of the Acute Coronary Syndromes Clinical Care Standard (2019) to
> ensure that the Standard aligns with the latest evidence-based guidance and clinical practice...
> It is anticipated that the updated Standard will be finalised in late 2027** [S1].

**This is the most precisely stated currency gap in forty-three guidelines.** Not a vague "under review" —
a standard naming **the superseding guideline, the year it superseded, the fact that it has not been
reconciled, and the year the replacement is due.** The Standard was first published in 2014 and last reviewed
against the guidelines in 2019.

**Consequence for use:** the **quality statements** below are the 2019 Standard. The **clinical content they
point to** — the NHFA/CSANZ ACS guidelines — **is now the 2025 edition**, which this Standard has not been
checked against. **Use the current NHFA/CSANZ guideline for clinical decisions and this Standard for the care
process.** Flagged `time_sensitive`.

## Why it matters

> **Coronary heart disease (the main cause of acute coronary syndromes) kills more people in Australia than
> any other disease** [S1].

> **While there are well-developed guidelines for managing acute coronary syndromes, not all people receive
> appropriate treatment and there is variation in the type of care received across Australia** [S1].

**Acute coronary syndromes include ST elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI), non-ST elevation myocardial
infarction (NSTEMI), and unstable angina** — chest pain usually due to restricted blood flow to the heart
muscle, which can lead to a heart attack [S1]. **An ACS results from a sudden blockage of a blood vessel in
the heart, typically by a thrombosis that reduces blood supply to a portion of heart muscle; if the blockage
is severe enough to lead to injury or death of the heart muscle, the event is an acute myocardial
infarction** [S1]. **The most common cause is atherosclerosis** [S1].

## The time targets

| Statement | Target [S1] |
|---|---|
| **QS2 — Early assessment** | **A 12-lead ECG, with the results analysed by a clinician experienced in interpreting an ECG, within 10 minutes of the first emergency clinical contact** |
| **QS3 — Timely reperfusion** | **In general, primary PCI is recommended if the time from first medical contact to balloon inflation is anticipated to be less than 90 minutes; otherwise, the patient is offered fibrinolysis** |
| **Indicator 3b** | **Fibrinolysis before or within 30 minutes of hospital presentation** |
| **Indicator 3c** | **PCI within 90 minutes of first clinical contact** |
| **QS6 — Care plan** | **Provided to the patient and their GP or ongoing clinical provider within 48 hours of discharge** |

**Note what the 10-minute clock is measured from: *first emergency clinical contact*, not arrival at
hospital** — and the standard anticipates that meeting it **"may involve facilitating referral to a clinician
experienced in performing or interpreting an ECG"** [S1]. The requirement is interpretation, not acquisition.

**Offer primary PCI or fibrinolysis to all eligible patients diagnosed with an acute STEMI**, within the
timeframes of the **current NHFA/CSANZ guidelines**, and **ensure the patient understands the risks and
benefits of their proposed treatment, and provides their consent** [S1].

## Immediate management

**Quality statement 1** [S1]: care **guided by a documented chest pain assessment pathway** for **all**
patients presenting with symptoms of an ACS. **Ensure that your patients understand what is happening and
why.** A chest pain assessment pathway taken from the national guideline is **available from the Heart
Foundation**.

**The first indicator audits whether a documented pathway was used at all** — before any drug, procedure or
timing.

## Risk stratification and angiography

**Quality statement 4** [S1]: a patient with a **non-ST-segment-elevation ACS (NSTEACS)** is managed **based
on a documented, evidence-based assessment of their risk of an adverse event**.

> **Use an evidence-based risk assessment tool to stratify the patient's risk of future cardiac events.
> Discuss the identified level of risk with the patient, and use a shared decision making process to plan
> their treatment based on their risk of an adverse event and their treatment preferences** [S1].

**Tools named** [S1]: **GRACE ACS Risk Calculator**; **TIMI Risk Score for UA/NSTEMI**; and **NHFA
*Decision-making and timing considerations in reperfusion for STEMI***.

**Quality statement 5** [S1]: **the role of coronary angiography, with a view to timely and appropriate
coronary revascularisation, is discussed with a patient with NSTEACS who is assessed to be at intermediate or
high risk of an adverse cardiac event** — **discuss with them the risks and benefits**.

**Risk stratification is not a triage device here — it is the input to a conversation.** Both statements make
the discussion the deliverable.

**Indicator 4b audits something structural rather than clinical:** the **proportion of NSTEACS patients
transferred to a hospital with angiography facilities** [S1].

## Before the patient leaves

**Quality statement 6** [S1]: an **individualised care plan** developed **with** the patient before they leave
hospital, which:

- **identifies the lifestyle modifications and medicines needed to manage their risk factors**
- **addresses their psychosocial needs**
- **includes a referral to an appropriate cardiac rehabilitation or another secondary prevention program**
- **is provided to the patient and their GP or ongoing clinical provider within 48 hours of discharge**

**Seven indicators hang off this one statement** [S1], and together they are the most specific medication
audit in any standard in this compendium:

| Indicator | Measure |
|---|---|
| **6a** | **Provided with an individualised care plan** |
| **6b** | **On aspirin or dual antiplatelet therapy on separation from hospital** |
| **6c** | **Prescribed lipid-lowering therapy on separation from hospital** |
| **6d** | **Myocardial infarction patients who had their left ventricular ejection fraction measured prior to separation** |
| **6e** | **Patients with reduced LVEF prescribed a beta blocker *and* an ACE inhibitor on separation** |
| **6f** | **Referred to cardiac rehabilitation or other secondary prevention program** |
| **6g** | **Discharge summary provided to the GP or ongoing clinical provider within 48 hours of separation** |

**Indicator 6d is the quiet one:** measuring ejection fraction before discharge is what makes 6e possible —
and **6e is the entry point to the heart failure guideline in this compendium.** An MI without an LVEF is an
undiagnosed heart failure patient.

**No benchmarks are set for these indicators by the Commission**, and definitions are specified at
**METEOR** [S1].

## Rural and remote implementation

> **In rural and remote settings, different strategies may be needed to implement the standard** [S1] — for
> example **hub-and-spoke models integrating larger and smaller health services and ACCHOs**, **telehealth
> consultations**, and **multidisciplinary teams including allied health involvement where clinically
> appropriate**.

The 90-minute PCI window and the 30-minute fibrinolysis window are geography-dependent, and the Standard says
so rather than assuming a metropolitan default.

## Cultural safety and equity for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples

This page carries the Commission's cultural safety section in full — it applies across the Clinical Care
Standards, several of which are already in this compendium, and is reproduced here because it is the first
time it was retrievable.

> **Health outcomes for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples can be improved by addressing systemic
> racism and other root causes that reduce access to care. Historical and current contributing factors include
> a lack of culturally safe care, culturally appropriate health education and sociocultural determinants such
> as differences in employment opportunities** [S1].

> **Cultural safety and equity recommendations in this document have been developed in consultation with
> Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander individuals, clinicians and representative health service
> organisations. However, it is recognised that cultural safety is determined by the Aboriginal and Torres
> Strait Islander individuals, families and communities experiencing the care** [S1].

**Cultural safety is defined by the recipient, not the provider** — stated plainly, in a national standard.

**Building culturally safe systems** [S1]:

- **Ensure systems and processes support people to self-report their Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander
  status and to record self-identification.**
- **Ensure all staff engage regularly in cultural safety training.**
- > **Disaggregate data by Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander status when using the indicators included in
  > this Standard. This will support identification of access and outcome issues so that improvements can be
  > made.**
- **Implement the six actions for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Health from the NSQHS Standards.**

**Flexible and connected service delivery** [S1]:

- **Provide flexible service delivery to optimise attendance and help develop trust** with individuals and
  communities.
- **Establish robust communication channels and referral pathways with primary healthcare providers, including
  Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Community Controlled Health Organisations (ACCHOs).**
- **Where possible, provide outreach services close to home, on Country, or in collaboration with ACCHOs or
  other community healthcare providers.**

**Communication and person-centred care** [S1]:

- **Take a collaborative approach to ensure interventions are suitably tailored to the individual's personal
  needs and preferences for care.**
- **Encourage the inclusion of support people, family and kin, and the person's trusted healthcare provider
  (such as their ACCHO) in all aspects of care, including decision making and plans for treatment and
  management.**
- **Engage culturally appropriate interpreter services and cultural translators when this will assist the
  patient.**
- **Involve Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Health Workers or Health Practitioners as part of a
  patient's multidisciplinary team, and involve Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Liaison Officers in
  hospital settings.**
- **Use culturally and linguistically appropriate materials to aid in communication and discussion, accounting
  for varying levels of health literacy.**

**The Better Cardiac Care for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander People project has also informed the
development of the Standard**, providing national recommendations for better cardiac care [S1].

The instruction to **disaggregate the indicators by Indigenous status** is the operational one: it converts
every measure above into an equity measure.

## Where this connects in the compendium

| Guideline here | Connection |
|---|---|
| **Cardiovascular disease risk** | primary prevention; this standard begins where that one ends |
| **Heart failure** | **indicator 6d/6e** — LVEF measured before discharge, and beta blocker plus ACE inhibitor if reduced |
| **Atrial fibrillation** | the AF guideline's **anticoagulant-plus-antiplatelet** section governs patients who arrive here with AF and leave on DAPT |
| **Stroke and TIA** | shared risk factors and shared antiplatelet decisions |
