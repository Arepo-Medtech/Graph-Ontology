# Fever and suspected or confirmed neutropenia in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognition, initial antibiotics, fluid resuscitation, referral and ongoing antibiotic/antifungal decisions for fever with suspected or confirmed neutropenia in children receiving cancer treatment. Related: `sepsis-children` (carries an oncology row), `empiric-antimicrobials-children` (which left FN out of scope). Adult/general counterparts `anti-infectives-general-principles` and `anticancer-drugs-general-principles` name febrile neutropenia but give no regimen.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 27 of 27 claims quoted verbatim from the **RCH Melbourne Clinical Practice Guideline *Fever and suspected or confirmed neutropenia*** (undated page); **46 fragments re-checked by machine. 11 quoted doses.**

> ⚠️ **FN is a medical emergency: first antibiotic within 60 minutes of arrival — 30 minutes if there is sepsis or systemic compromise.** Do not wait for the neutrophil count.

> ⚠️ **Vancomycin maximum here is 500 mg**, not the 750 mg used elsewhere in the RCH tables — see *Unresolved*.

## Recognise it

- ⚠️ **Fever with suspected or confirmed neutropenia is a medical emergency.** Signs of sepsis = Triage Category 2; at risk of imminent cardiovascular collapse = Category 1; everyone else starts treatment within 30 minutes of arrival (Category 3). [S1]
- **Fever** = a single temperature ≥38.5°C, or ≥38.0°C sustained over 1 hour. [S1]
- **Neutropenia** = absolute neutrophil count <500/mm³, or <1000/mm³ with a predicted fall to <500/mm³ over the next 48 hours. **Suspect it** in any oncology patient given oral or IV chemotherapy in the last 14 days, and in other children with recurrent neutropenia. [S1]
- **High-risk cancer treatment protocols:** AML treatment · ALL induction, ALL delayed intensification, infant ALL · lymphoma induction · allogeneic transplant (day-14 to day+356, as printed) · autologous transplant (day-7 to day +30) · re-induction for any relapse. [S1]
- **Systemic compromise** = needing a fluid bolus or inotrope, any respiratory support, and/or altered conscious state. [S1]

## First hour

- **Ask for the patient's Febrile Neutropenia Treatment Card or Letter** — it carries the diagnosis, risk status and recommended antibiotics. [S1]
- **CVAD or IV access within 30 minutes of arrival.** If accessing the CVAD is delayed, insert a peripheral line. ⚠️ **With signs of sepsis, do not wait for anaesthetic cream to work.** [S1]
- **Bloods:** FBE and differential, electrolytes and creatinine, 2 sets of blood cultures, group and hold, venous gas and lactate; urine M/C/S. ⚠️ **Do not delay antibiotics waiting for the neutrophil count or the urine.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Give the first antibiotic within 60 minutes (30 minutes if systemic compromise/sepsis), and make it the broadest — piperacillin-tazobactam.** With a double-lumen CVAD, divide doses down both lumens, or alternate if that is not possible. [S1]

## Antibiotics

- **Clinically stable:** piperacillin-tazobactam 100 mg/kg (max 4 g) IV 6-hourly (dose is the piperacillin component). [S1]
- **Non-life-threatening penicillin allergy (rash):** cefepime 50 mg/kg (max 2 g) IV 8-hourly (preferred), **or** ceftazidime 50 mg/kg (max 2 g) IV 8-hourly. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Ceftazidime is weaker against viridans streptococci** — avoid it with extensive mucositis, recent high-dose methotrexate or cytarabine, or ciprofloxacin prophylaxis. [S1]
- **Life-threatening (immediate) penicillin or beta-lactam allergy:** ciprofloxacin 10 mg/kg (max 400 mg) IV 12-hourly **and** vancomycin 15 mg/kg (max 500 mg) IV 6-hourly, target trough 10–15 mg/L before the 5th dose. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Add amikacin for systemic compromise/sepsis, high-risk cancer treatment, or FN starting as an inpatient:** 22.5 mg/kg (18 mg/kg if over 10 years) (max 1.5 g) IV 24-hourly, target trough <2 mg/L before the 3rd dose. Regional centres may use another aminoglycoside such as gentamicin. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Never give an aminoglycoside through the same line at the same time as piperacillin-tazobactam** (or another IV penicillin); flush well with sodium chloride 0.9% before and after each. [S1]
- **Suspected resistant Gram-positive infection** (cellulitis, obviously infected line, proven Gram-positive bacteraemia, MRSA colonisation with extensive skin breaks): **add vancomycin 15 mg/kg (max 500 mg) IV 6-hourly**, trough 10–15 mg/L before the 5th dose. **Prolonged fever in a stable child is not an indication for vancomycin.** [S1]
- **Abdominal or perineal infection:** add metronidazole 7.5 mg/kg (max 500 mg) IV/oral 8-hourly **only if on cefepime, ceftazidime or ciprofloxacin** — piperacillin-tazobactam already covers anaerobes, except for suspected *C. difficile* colitis. [S1]

## Fluids

- ⚠️ **Signs of sepsis: 20 mL/kg normal saline IV over no more than 10 minutes (not through a pump).** If improvement is only transient, further boluses up to 40 mL/kg; discuss more than 40 mL/kg with a senior clinician; if no improvement, consider inotropes. [S1]

## Referral, transfer and further tests

- ⚠️ **Discuss every patient with the on-call oncology consultant/fellow (or consultant paediatrician in regional centres) — after the 1st IV antibiotic dose**, so the call does not delay it. [S1]
- **Consider transfer to the Primary Treatment Centre** for severe sepsis, deterioration despite broad-spectrum antibiotics, or FN lasting more than 72 hours despite them. [S1]
- **New CNS symptoms or signs:** CT brain and LP may be indicated — discuss with the on-call oncologist first. ⚠️ **Correct thrombocytopenia and/or coagulopathy before LP.** [S1]

## After 24 hours

- **No routine daily blood cultures in a well, afebrile child.** Repeat one set on Day 2 (± Day 3) only if still febrile. [S1]
- **Stop amikacin and/or vancomycin after 24–48 hours** unless the child is clinically unstable or a resistant organism needs them (discuss with Infectious Diseases). **Do not broaden the regimen for persistent fever alone in a stable child.** [S1]
- **Stop empiric antibiotics with marrow recovery** once blood cultures are negative at 48 hours and the child has been afebrile for at least 24 hours. [S1]
- **Low-risk FN:** consider stopping at 72 hours if cultures are negative and afebrile for at least 24 hours, **irrespective of marrow recovery**, provided careful follow-up is ensured. Discuss every change, including stopping, with the primary oncology team. [S1]
- **Fever beyond 72 hours or recurrent despite broad-spectrum antibiotics: evaluate for invasive fungal infection.** Empiric antifungal: **amphotericin** (caspofungin or voriconazole if contraindicated). IFI high risk — start it; IFI low risk — consider it. [S1]
- **High risk for IFI:** relapsed acute leukaemia · AML · GvHD · allogeneic HSCT · severe aplastic anaemia · prolonged corticosteroids · prolonged ICU stay. [S1]
