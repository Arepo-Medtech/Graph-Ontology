# Acute meningococcal disease in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognition, empiric and directed antibiotics, resuscitation, isolation, notification and disposition for invasive meningococcal disease in children. Prevention by vaccination is `meningococcal-disease`; general sepsis care is `sepsis-children`; meningitis more broadly is `meningitis-and-encephalitis-children`.

> ⚠️ **Give ceftriaxone or cefotaxime within 15 minutes of suspecting meningococcal disease** (benzylpenicillin if unavailable). Take blood cultures first if possible, but never delay the antibiotic.

> ⚠️ **A blanching rash does not exclude meningococcal disease** — the rash can start macular or maculopapular.

---

## Recognise it

- ⚠️ **Meningococcal disease can be rapidly fatal, even with treatment.** Mortality is up to 10% despite prompt treatment; 10–20% have long-term morbidity including deafness, neurological deficits and amputations. [S1]
- **It presents as sepsis with DIC and a rapidly progressive non-blanching petechial/purpuric rash** (higher mortality), **or as meningitis with or without a rash**; more rarely septic arthritis, pneumonia, pharyngitis, conjunctivitis or occult bacteraemia. [S1]
- **History:** rapid onset (<12 hours) of headache, loss of appetite, nausea, vomiting, sore throat and coryza; fever; leg pain or myalgia. **Infants may show only poor feeding, irritability, lethargy, mottling or a high-pitched moaning cry.** [S1]
- **Examination:** pallor or mottling and/or cool peripheries, altered conscious state, neck stiffness, headache, photophobia, bulging fontanelle, **non-blanching petechiae/purpura**. [S1]
- ⚠️ **A blanching rash does not exclude it**; the rash can initially be macular or maculopapular and blanch. [S1]
- **Risk factors:** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander; immunodeficiency (asplenia, immunoglobulin or complement deficiency, sickle cell disease, HSCT, HIV); cigarette smoke; crowded housing; prolonged contact with a carrier. **Ask about vaccination status, including MenB.** [S1]

## Investigate

- ⚠️ **Investigations must not delay antibiotics.** Blood (IV or intraosseous) for culture before antibiotics if possible, plus PCR in a separate EDTA tube. [S1]
- **CSF only once stabilised and with no contraindication to LP:** Gram stain (Gram-negative diplococci), cell count, biochemistry, culture ± meningococcal PCR. [S1]

## Treat — initial

- **Until meningococcal infection is confirmed, treat and resuscitate as for sepsis.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Give ceftriaxone/cefotaxime as soon as meningococcal disease is suspected, within 15 minutes**; if unavailable, give benzylpenicillin. Empiric treatment is a third-generation cephalosporin. [S1]
- **Ceftriaxone 100 mg/kg (max 4 g) IV daily** (over 4 weeks of life). [S1]
- **Or cefotaxime 50 mg/kg (max 2 g) IV** — 12-hourly in week 1 of life, 6–8-hourly in weeks 2–4, 6-hourly after week 4. [S1]
- **No IV or intraosseous access: give it IM** — may need two injections because of volume or muscle size — and repeat the dose once IV access is available. [S1]
- **If ceftriaxone/cefotaxime is unavailable: benzylpenicillin 60 mg/kg (max 2.4 g) IV** — 12-hourly in week 1, 6-hourly in weeks 2–4, 4-hourly after week 4. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Children treated with benzylpenicillin also need contact-prophylaxis antibiotics** to eradicate carriage. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Anticipate rapid deterioration.** Carefully titrated fluid resuscitation with repeated assessment, and **early consideration of inotropes**. [S1]

## Ongoing management

- **Confirmed disease: 5 days of IV treatment.** Continue the third-generation cephalosporin until sensitivities are confirmed. [S1]
- **If benzylpenicillin susceptibility is confirmed, de-escalate to benzylpenicillin** 60 mg/kg (max 2.4 g) IV — 12-hourly in week 1, 6-hourly in weeks 2–4, 4-hourly after week 4. [S1]
- **Dexamethasone given for empiric meningitis can be stopped once N. meningitidis is confirmed** — no clearly demonstrated benefit. [S1]
- **Isolate with droplet precautions** per local infection-control guidelines. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Notify every presumed or confirmed case immediately** to the local state authority. [S1]
- **Give contact prophylaxis to at-risk contacts as soon as possible.** [S1]

## Vaccination after a case

- **Cases:** vaccination not recommended unless the case has underlying risk factors. **Contacts:** secondary-prevention vaccination may be offered to non-immunised contacts for serogroups A, C, W or Y; **a single first dose of MenB is unlikely to protect a contact during the risk period.** [S1]

## Disposition

- ⚠️ **Manage every child with acute meningococcal disease in a facility that can provide intensive care**; if unavailable, stabilise and transfer. Consult the local paediatric team for all suspected cases. [S1]
- **Discharge:** IV treatment can be completed through HITH once haemodynamically stable and afebrile, and after contact prophylaxis has been considered. [S1]
