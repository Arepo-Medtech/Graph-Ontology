# Melanoma — non-table practice points

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** cutaneous melanoma: early detection, surveillance of very-high-risk people, biopsy of a suspicious lesion, excision margins and follow-up. **Not** keratinocyte cancer (see `keratinocyte-cancer`). Related corpus guideline: `local-anaesthesia` (skin biopsy and excision in general practice).

## Early detection

- ⚠️ **Suspect melanoma in any lesion that is Elevated, Firm and Growing for more than one month.** [S1]
- **A history of how long a lesion has been present, and of any change in it, is a minimum requirement.** [S1]

## People at very high risk

- **Six-monthly full skin examination, supported by dermoscopy with reference to total body photography, benefited people at very high risk of melanoma.** [S1]

## Biopsy of a suspicious lesion

- **The ideal method for a lesion suspected of being melanoma is complete excision with a 2 mm margin.** [S1]
- **Avoid skin flaps or grafts after excisional biopsy**, because they may compromise the definitive re-excision. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Avoid punch biopsy if melanoma is at all possible.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **If suspicion remains despite a negative report from a partial biopsy, re-biopsy or excise.** [S1]

## Definitive excision margins (background text)

- **Today a maximum margin of 2cm is recommended for thick tumours and 1cm for thin tumours.** [S1]
- **The guideline continues to recommend 1-2 cm margins for melanomas of Breslow thickness 2-4 mm.** [S1]
- **Most international guidelines suggest 5 mm margins for melanoma in situ.** [S1]

## Follow-up

- **Guidelines agree by consensus that patients should be taught skin self-examination.** [S1]
- **Routine standard blood tests are not recommended for patients presenting with stage I or II melanoma**, according to evidence from previous guidelines. [S1]
