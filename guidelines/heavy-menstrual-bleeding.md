# Heavy menstrual bleeding

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-22 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** primary care and specialist care.

> **Australian primary source.** Australian Commission on Safety and Quality in Health Care (ACSQHC).
> ***Heavy Menstrual Bleeding Clinical Care Standard*** [S1]. **Eight quality statements**, plus an
> **overall indicator**.

**This opens a domain essentially absent from the compendium.** Women's health has appeared only inside other
guidelines — pregnancy in the STI set, osteoporosis, contraception mentioned in passing. This is the first
guideline here whose subject is a condition of the menstrual cycle.

## ⚠️ The standard measures how often the uterus comes out

> **Overall indicator 9a — Hospital rate of hysterectomy per 100 episodes.** **A tool is available to support
> the calculation of local age-standardised rates and comparison against national reference rates** [S1].

Forty-six guidelines in, this is the clearest instance of a standard whose **overall measure is how often the
most invasive option is used** — with national reference data supplied so a service can see where it sits.

And it is not the only structural defence. **An entire quality statement exists to put something between
medical management and hysterectomy:**

> **Quality statement 7 — Uterine-preserving alternatives to hysterectomy.** A woman who has heavy menstrual
> bleeding of benign causes and who is considering non-medical management **is offered uterine-preserving
> procedures that may be suitable** (such as **endometrial ablation, uterine artery embolisation or surgical
> removal of local pathology**). **She is supported to make an informed decision and is referred
> appropriately** [S1].

**Indicator 7a measures it**: *proportion of patients with benign causes who received uterine-preserving
procedural alternatives to hysterectomy* [S1].

## The escalation ladder

| Step | Statement [S1] |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Assessment and diagnosis** — history, quality-of-life impact, examination where clinically appropriate, **exclusion of pregnancy, iron deficiency and anaemia** |
| **2** | **Informed choice and shared decision making** |
| **3** | **Initiating medical management** — **oral treatment offered at first presentation when clinically appropriate** |
| **4** | **Quality ultrasound** — where required |
| **5** | **Intrauterine hormonal devices** — the **52 mg LNG-IUD**, the most effective medical option |
| **6** | **Specialist referral** — suspicion of malignancy or other significant pathology, or non-response |
| **7** | **Uterine-preserving alternatives to hysterectomy** |
| **8** | **Hysterectomy** — when other options are ineffective or unsuitable, **or at the woman's request** |

## ⚠️ Don't leave her bleeding while she waits

> **Oral treatment is offered at first presentation when clinically appropriate, including when a woman is
> undergoing further investigation or waiting for other treatment** *(Quality statement 3)* [S1].

> **When clinically appropriate, offer initial oral treatment such as tranexamic acid and/or NSAIDs at first
> presentation to relieve symptoms and limit complications (for example, from iron deficiency) if the
> patient's preferred treatment option is not immediately apparent or available, when further investigations
> are recommended, or the patient needs to wait for further appointments (for example, for insertion of the
> LNG-IUD)** [S1].

**Indicator 3a measures exactly this**: *proportion offered medical management at their first presentation*
[S1]. The waiting period is treated as a period of **ongoing harm** — blood loss and iron depletion — rather
than a neutral gap before the real treatment.

**Consider medical management before more invasive treatment options.** **In the absence of significant
pathology, many patients may be effectively managed with medical therapy alone** [S1].

### The medical options [S1]

- **Non-hormonal**: **tranexamic acid** (an antifibrinolytic) and **NSAIDs such as ibuprofen, mefenamic acid or
  naproxen**, which **reduce blood flow and/or provide analgesia during menstruation**.
- **Hormonal**: **combined oral contraceptives, oral progestogens, or depot medroxyprogesterone**. **Oral
  hormones may be taken intermittently (during a menstrual cycle) or continuously**, after discussion of
  issues, **implications for contraception** and side effects.
- **The 52 mg levonorgestrel-releasing intrauterine device (LNG-IUD)** — **currently the most effective medical
  therapy for heavy menstrual bleeding in women without significant pathology, in addition to its contraceptive
  action**.

**Choice is influenced by the woman's preferences and priorities, including her need for contraception, the
cause(s) of bleeding, coexisting conditions, and associated symptoms requiring further investigation,
including pressure and pain** [S1].

## The device, named and dosed

**Quality statement 5** [S1]:

> When medical management options are being considered, a woman is offered a **52 mg levonorgestrel-releasing
> intrauterine device if clinically appropriate, as it is currently the most effective medical option for
> managing heavy menstrual bleeding.**

> **Evidence supports the use of the LNG-IUD to treat heavy menstrual bleeding in patients without malignancy
> or other significant pathology because it is more effective and provides greater improvements in quality of
> life compared with other medical treatments** [S1].

> **Explain what to expect, including that it may take three to six months or more to experience the full
> benefit of the treatment** [S1].

**That expectation-setting is the difference between a working treatment and an abandoned one.** A device
judged at six weeks will look like a failure.

**If necessary, refer the patient to a trained inserter** [S1] — and the Standard audits this as a **service
capability**, not only a clinical act:

- **Indicator 5a**: *proportion deemed clinically suitable for a 52 mg LNG-IUD **who had one inserted or were
  referred** to another clinician for insertion* [S1].
- **Indicator 5b**: *evidence of local arrangements to refer or recommend patients to a clinician trained to
  insert LNG-IUDs* — **only applicable to services without a clinician trained in intrauterine device
  insertion** [S1].

**A service without the skill is expected to have a referral pathway instead.** That is the workforce gap
written into the measurement.

## Assessment

**Quality statement 1** [S1]: **a thorough history, assessment of impact on quality of life, a physical
examination (where clinically appropriate), and exclusion of pregnancy, iron deficiency and anaemia.** Further
investigations are based on the initial assessment.

**Consider the diverse possible causes as per the PALM-COEIN classification system**, and **other possible
diagnoses such as endometriosis or miscarriage** [S1].

**History should include** [S1]:

- **the nature of bleeding — duration, frequency, heaviness and pattern (regular or irregular)**
- > **post-coital, intermenstrual and postmenopausal bleeding require specific investigation**
- **the impact on quality of life, including impact on mental health and limitations on work, exercise and
  social functioning**
- **related symptoms such as pelvic pain or pressure**

### Iron deficiency *and* anaemia — two things, not one

**Indicator 1a**: *proportion of patients with heavy menstrual bleeding who were **tested for iron deficiency
and anaemia*** [S1].

The Standard separates them at both the statement and indicator level. **Iron deficiency without anaemia is
still iron deficiency**, and it is the reversible harm accruing while a woman waits for definitive treatment —
which is why statement 3 names it as the complication oral treatment is meant to limit.

## Ultrasound

**Quality statement 4** [S1]: a **pelvic (preferably transvaginal) ultrasound, which assesses all pelvic
structures, including the uterus and endometrium, and is ideally performed in days 5–10 of her menstrual
cycle.**

**Three specifications in one sentence** — route, completeness, and timing in the cycle. A scan on the wrong
day, or one that reports only the uterus, does not satisfy it.

**Ultrasound may be required** in patients [S1]:

- **with an increased risk of malignancy based on history** — **older age, personal or family history of
  endometrial or colorectal cancer, use of unopposed oestrogen or tamoxifen, obesity, young age at menarche or
  older age at menopause, nulliparity, diabetes or endometrial hyperplasia**
- **with features suggestive of pathology on examination** — for example **an enlarged or irregular uterus,
  suggesting fibroids**
- **with symptoms such as deep dyspareunia, severe dysmenorrhoea or secondary heavy menstrual** bleeding

**Two indicators for ultrasound are about the system, not the patient**: evidence of local arrangements **to
support appropriate referral** (4a) and **for conducting** the scans (4b) [S1].

## Referral

**Quality statement 6** [S1]: **early specialist review when there is a suspicion of malignancy or other
significant pathology based on clinical assessment or ultrasound**; referral is **also offered to a woman who
has not responded to medical management**.

**From primary care, offer early referral** for [S1]:

- **suspicious clinical findings on assessment or ultrasound**, considering endometrial cancer risk factors —
  > **age, with increased suspicion warranted in a woman aged over 45**; personal or family history of
  > endometrial or colorectal cancer; unopposed oestrogen or tamoxifen; obesity; young age at menarche or
  > older age at menopause; nulliparity; diabetes; endometrial hyperplasia
- **significant pelvic pathology on ultrasound such as large fibroids or endometrial polyps**
- **severe symptoms at initial presentation**

**Indicator 6a** audits **evidence of protocols or pathways to ensure timely and appropriate referral** [S1].

## Uterine-preserving procedures

**Endometrial ablation** [S1] — **a minimally invasive surgical procedure for women without substantive
structural uterine pathology and who have no desire for future pregnancy.** It involves **the removal or
destruction of the endometrium including the basal layer**, using **resection and/or ablation, laser, bipolar
radiofrequency or thermal balloon ablation**.

> **Women must be informed about the need for contraception following** the procedure [S1].

Also named: **uterine artery embolisation** and **surgical removal of local pathology** [S1].

## Hysterectomy

**Quality statement 8** [S1]:

> Hysterectomy **is considered when other treatment options are ineffective or are unsuitable, or at the
> woman's request.** A woman considering a hysterectomy **is fully informed about the potential benefits and
> risks of the procedure before making a decision.**

**"Or at the woman's request"** sits in the statement itself — the ladder is a default, not a gate.

**Support the patient to consider less invasive alternatives prior to considering hysterectomy, as appropriate
to their clinical situation and personal circumstances** [S1]. **Ensure they understand the benefits, risks and
probable outcomes of the surgery**, and **discuss minimally invasive approaches to hysterectomy (vaginal or
laparoscopic) where clinically appropriate** [S1].

## Shared decision making

**Quality statement 2** [S1]: **informed about her treatment options and their potential benefits and risks**,
and **supported to participate in shared decision making based on her preferences, priorities and clinical
situation**.

> **Shared decision making enables individuals' preferences and priorities to be considered alongside the best
> available evidence. It can lead to improved satisfaction and better-quality decisions** [S1].

To support it [S1]: **discuss the condition, the treatment options and the benefits, risks and probable
outcomes — including side effects and complications, and the possibility of treatment failure**; **discuss the
patient's treatment goals and their preferences and priorities, including desire for pregnancy and/or future
fertility**; and **provide high-quality patient information in the most appropriate format based on their
needs and preferences**.

**"The possibility of treatment failure"** is named as something to discuss up front — which is what makes the
three-to-six-month LNG-IUD expectation honest rather than optimistic.

## Where this connects in the compendium

| Guideline here | Connection |
|---|---|
| **Low back pain** | **NSAIDs are treatment here** (ibuprofen, mefenamic acid, naproxen, for blood loss and analgesia) and are only **conditionally** for in osteoarthritis — the same class, different standing by condition |
| **Osteoporosis** | shared population; the Standard does not address bone effects of the hormonal options and none is asserted here |
| **Antimicrobial stewardship, opioid stewardship** | the same structural idea — **a national measure of how often the most aggressive option is chosen** |
| **Iron deficiency** | **not yet written in this compendium**; indicator 1a makes testing for it auditable, and statement 3 names it as the harm of waiting |
