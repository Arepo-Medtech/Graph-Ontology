## Recognise it

- **A common, self-limiting viral skin infection of childhood.** [S1]
- **Diagnosis is clinical, from the characteristic lesions; no investigations are needed.** [S1]
- **Firm, pearly, dome-shaped papules with a central pit**, usually 1–3 mm but up to 1–2 cm. [S1]
- **Anywhere except the palms and soles**; commonest on the trunk, axillae, antecubital and popliteal fossae and groin. **Autoinoculation explains most anogenital lesions in children.** [S1]
- **Spread is by skin-to-skin contact, fomites (eg towels) or autoinoculation** by scratching or touching a lesion. [S1]

## Eczema

- **Eczema raises the risk of molluscum, and molluscum worsens the surrounding eczema** — children with eczema may have more extensive, longer-lasting lesions. **Optimise eczema management.** [S1]
- **Eczema can hide the primary lesions**; a careful history of how the first lesions looked is usually diagnostic. [S1]

## Advise

- **Most cases clear in 6–9 months, but lesions can persist over a year**; full resolution needs an immune response, which may take 3 months to 3 years. [S1]
- **No exclusion from day care or school**, and no isolation or activity restriction. **Avoid sharing towels and bathing together** to protect siblings. [S1]

## Treat

- **Most children need no treatment.** Consider it if quality of life is affected or the child is immunocompromised. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Cryotherapy and curettage are not routinely recommended in children** — they may scar. **Refer to dermatology for cantharidin**, a topical blistering agent that should not scar when used appropriately. [S1]
- **Inflamed lesions often need no antibiotics**; consider them for purulent exudate, abscess or extending erythema. [S1]
- **Consult the local paediatric team** if the diagnosis is uncertain or eczema is severe. [S1]
