# The limping or non-weight-bearing child

## Key points

- **Most limping children need no investigation**; watching the gait helps localise the problem. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Acute inability to walk or weight bear is a red flag.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Septic arthritis is an orthopaedic emergency** — suspect it in any child with limp, swelling and reduced range of motion, especially with fever. [S1]
- **Transient synovitis, acute myositis and minor trauma are common**, but always consider serious pathology. **Pain can be referred** — examine the joints above and below (including the spine) and the abdomen. [S1]

## Causes at any age

- **At all ages consider:** infection (osteomyelitis, septic arthritis, sepsis, bursitis, discitis, epidural collection) · trauma including inflicted injury · malignancy (haematological, bone, soft tissue). [S1]
- **Also:** rheumatological causes including reactive arthritis, HSP and acute rheumatic fever · **appendicitis and ovarian or testicular torsion** · sickle-cell crisis or haemophilia · **vitamin C deficiency** with a restricted diet · functional limp. [S1]

## History and examination

- **History red flags to ask about:** symptoms over 7 days or repeated presentations · severe localised joint pain · pain waking from sleep · inability to walk or weight bear · fever, night sweats, rigors · weight loss, lethargy, anorexia (consider malignancy). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Absence of fever does not exclude infection.** Pallor, petechiae, purpura or ecchymosis should raise HSP, malignancy or inflicted injury. [S1]
- **Hip pain may localise to the knee.** Examine with Look, Feel, Move, including the joints above and below. [S1]
- ⚠️ **A marked reduction in range of motion suggests significant pathology such as septic arthritis** — compare active and passive movement on both sides. [S1]
- **Also examine** neurology and neurovascular status of the limb, the heart where ARF is possible, and the abdomen, scrotum, back and hips. [S1]

## Investigations

- **No investigations are needed if all apply:** no red flags · walking with mild or no discomfort on simple analgesia · a working diagnosis and/or a plan for review within 7 days of onset if the limp persists. [S1]
- **X-ray the area of suspicion.** A pelvis AP or frog-leg view helps identify SUFE, DDH (over 6 months), Perthes disease and pelvic avulsions. ⚠️ **A normal X-ray does not exclude septic arthritis or early osteomyelitis**, and signs of inflicted injury may be subtle. [S1]
- **Hip ultrasound** looks for an effusion when septic arthritis is suspected, but **an effusion often cannot distinguish septic arthritis from transient synovitis.** [S1]
- **Bone scan or localised MRI** if the limp is ongoing at 7 days or there are features of osteomyelitis. [S1]
- **Raised inflammatory markers** (platelets, WCC, CRP, ESR) suggest infection or inflammation, but **may be normal in contained or chronic infection.** [S1]
- **Blood cultures before antibiotics if possible** when osteomyelitis, septic arthritis or septic bursitis is suspected. FBE and film if malignancy is a concern; vitamin C level with a restricted diet; streptococcal serology and ECG if ARF is suspected. [S1]

## Treatment

- **Simple analgesia (paracetamol and an NSAID) and child-directed limitation of activity.** ⚠️ **Needing stronger analgesia should prompt reassessment.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Septic arthritis: consult the local orthopaedic specialist ASAP**; refer to the right sub-specialty and follow local antibiotic guidelines. [S1]

## Escalate and discharge

- **Involve the paediatric team** if symptoms last more than 7 days or the child cannot bear weight or permit movement after analgesia · systemically unwell · features of malignancy · concern for abuse · suspected inflammatory arthritis. **Suspected septic arthritis: consult orthopaedics.** [S1]
- **Discharge when** the cause has been found and managed, **or** the child walks comfortably (with or without analgesia) **and** high-risk diagnoses are unlikely **and** follow-up is arranged. [S1]
