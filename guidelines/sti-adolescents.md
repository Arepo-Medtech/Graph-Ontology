# Sexually transmitted infections in adolescents

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** confidential assessment, testing, weight-based treatment, contact tracing and follow-up of STIs, PID and bacterial vaginosis in adolescents. The adult ASHM-derived counterparts are `chlamydia-and-gonorrhoea`, `sti-syndromes-and-screening` and `sti-care-by-population`, with the organism pages `genital-herpes`, `syphilis`, `trichomoniasis`, `bacterial-vaginosis` and `anogenital-warts`. Regimen differences against them are listed under *Unresolved*.

> ⚠️ **Adolescents have a legal right to confidential care.** If sexual abuse is a concern, **refer urgently** — time-critical samples may be needed.

> ⚠️ **Page last updated November 2022.** Several regimens differ from the current ASHM-derived adult guidelines in this compendium — see *Unresolved* before prescribing.

## Approach

- **Offer STI screening and prevention counselling opportunistically to every sexually active adolescent who presents for healthcare.** Many STIs are asymptomatic but cause serious complications. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Confidentiality holds unless the adolescent cannot be considered a mature minor and/or there is significant risk** — harm to self or others, physical or sexual abuse. If abuse is suspected, **urgent referral** to local services. [S1]
- **Also assess pregnancy risk (test where appropriate), the need for emergency contraception, and ongoing contraception.** [S1]

## Chlamydia

- **Test:** first-pass urine, self-collected vaginal swab or clinician-collected endocervical swab for NAAT; anorectal and pharyngeal swabs for MSM. **50% of men and 75% of women have no symptoms.** [S1]
- **Uncomplicated genital or asymptomatic rectal:** doxycycline 2 mg/kg (max 100 mg) PO bd for 7 days (generally not for children 8 years or under) **or** azithromycin 20 mg/kg (max 1 g) PO single dose. [S1]
- **Symptomatic anorectal:** doxycycline for 21 days **or** azithromycin 1 g stat, repeated in 12–24 hours. [S1]
- **If suspicion is high, treat without waiting for results.** No sex for 7 days after treatment, and none with partners from the last 6 months until they are tested and treated. [S1]
- **Follow up after 1 week.** Test of cure is not routinely recommended; **retest for re-infection at 3 months.** [S1]

## Gonorrhoea

- ⚠️ **Always collect gonococcal culture before treatment, but do not delay treatment for the result.** Symptomatic: NAAT and MCS. **Co-infection with chlamydia is common.** [S1]
- **Uncomplicated genital and anorectal:** ceftriaxone 50 mg/kg (max 500 mg) IM single dose **and** azithromycin 20 mg/kg (max 1 g) PO single dose — **2 g azithromycin for pharyngeal infection.** [S1]
- **No sex for 7 days after treatment**, and none with partners from the last 2 months until tested and treated. **Test of cure by NAAT at each infected site 2 weeks after treatment**; retest at 3 months. [S1]

## Pelvic inflammatory disease

- ⚠️ **Refer to gynaecology and begin treatment without waiting for results.** [S1]
- **Outpatient:** ceftriaxone 50 mg/kg (max 500 mg) IM single dose **plus** doxycycline 2 mg/kg (max 100 mg) PO bd for 14 days **plus** metronidazole 10 mg/kg (max 400 mg) PO bd for 14 days. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Admit for:** surgical emergency not excluded (e.g. appendicitis) · pregnancy · failure or intolerance of oral treatment · severe illness, vomiting or high fever · tubo-ovarian abscess · immunodeficiency. [S1]
- **IV regimen (for admitted patients):** ceftriaxone 100 mg/kg (max 2 g) IV daily **or** cefotaxime 50 mg/kg (max 2 g) IV 8-hourly, **plus** azithromycin 10 mg/kg (max 500 mg) IV daily, **plus** metronidazole 12.5 mg/kg (max 500 mg) IV 12-hourly. [S1]

## Genital herpes

- **Do not delay treatment for results, particularly if severe.** Swab the ulcer base or de-roofed vesicle for HSV NAAT; **serological screening of asymptomatic people is not recommended.** [S1]
- **First episode:** valaciclovir 20 mg/kg (max 500 mg) PO bd for 5–10 days **or** aciclovir 10 mg/kg (max 400 mg) PO tds for 5–10 days. [S1]
- **Episodic recurrence:** valaciclovir 20 mg/kg (max 500 mg) PO bd for 3 days **or** famciclovir 1 g PO bd for 1 day (over 12 years). **Suppression:** valaciclovir 20 mg/kg (max 500 mg) PO daily for 6 months **or** famciclovir 250 mg PO bd for 6 months (over 12 years). [S1]
- **Contact tracing is not recommended.** GP follow-up in 1 week. [S1]

## Bacterial vaginosis and trichomoniasis

- **Bacterial vaginosis:** metronidazole 10 mg/kg (max 400 mg) PO bd for 7 days **or** metronidazole 0.75% gel intravaginally nocte for 5 nights (not on PBS) **or** clindamycin 2% vaginal cream nocte for 7 days (not on PBS). [S1]
- **Trichomoniasis:** metronidazole 2 g PO single dose (over 12 years) **or** metronidazole 10 mg/kg (max 400 mg) PO bd for 7 days. **Topical treatment is not advised**; no sex for 7 days; **treat the current partner.** [S1]

## Syphilis and warts

- **Primary, secondary and early latent syphilis:** benzathine penicillin G 50 000 units/kg IM (max 2.4 million units) single dose, given as 2 injections (max 1.2 million units each). [S1]
- **Late latent (over 2 years) or unknown duration:** the same dose, weekly for 3 doses. **Refer to infectious diseases; repeat RPR at 3, 6 and possibly 12 months.** [S1]
- **Genital warts:** treatment is cosmetic, not curative; avoid shaving or waxing the area; **refer to gynaecology** for imiquimod, cryotherapy or excision. **Prevent with HPV vaccination.** [S1]

## Escalation and discharge

- ⚠️ **Consult gynaecology when inpatient PID management is needed.** If sexual assault is suspected, follow local guidelines — **early specialist consultation is vital.** [S1]
- **Discharge with a clear plan for following up results and any further treatment.** [S1]

| 1 | ⚠️ **Chlamydia first line — differs from ASHM (`chlamydia-and-gonorrhoea`).** RCH gives **doxycycline OR azithromycin 20 mg/kg (max 1 g) single dose as equal options**, including for **asymptomatic rectal** infection. ASHM makes **doxycycline 100 mg BD 7 days the recommended regimen**, with azithromycin 1 g stat only as an alternative where adherence is poor and anorectal infection is less likely; for **anorectal** infection ASHM's azithromycin alternative is **1 g stat repeated in 12–24 hours**, which RCH reserves for symptomatic anorectal infection. | `observation` |
| 2 | ⚠️ **Chlamydia test of cure — differs from ASHM.** RCH: not routinely recommended. ASHM: not routine **except in pregnancy and anorectal infection treated with azithromycin**, no earlier than 4 weeks. RCH has **no pregnancy regimen** for chlamydia (ASHM: azithromycin 1 g stat). | `observation` |
| 3 | **Chlamydia presumptive treatment — tension with ASHM.** RCH treats the index patient on high suspicion without waiting for results; ASHM's stewardship point is that **immediate treatment is not recommended for all sexual contacts** of chlamydia. The populations differ (index patient vs contact), so this is a caution, not a direct contradiction. | `observation` |
| 4 | **Gonorrhoea — matches ASHM at the adult cap** (ceftriaxone 500 mg IM + azithromycin 1 g; 2 g if pharyngeal; test of cure at 2 weeks; 2-month contact window), but RCH **omits the 1% lignocaine diluent**, **gives no regimen for gonococcal conjunctivitis** (ASHM adult: ceftriaxone 1 g) although it lists conjunctivitis as a symptom, and does not address presumptive treatment of contacts (ASHM: consider if contact within 2 weeks). | `observation` |

RCH says treat the current partner; ASHM says consider presumptive treatment if contact within 2 weeks and retest at 4 weeks if still symptomatic. | `observation` |
