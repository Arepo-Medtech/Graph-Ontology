## ⚠️ The sentence that governs everything else

> ⚠️ **"Effects of probiotics are STRAIN- AND DISEASE-SPECIFIC, so that benefits reported with one
> preparation MAY NOT APPLY TO OTHERS"** [P1].

**S1 demonstrates exactly that, twice:**

> **Two trials of L. rhamnosus GG found NO symptomatic improvement compared with placebo** — in the same
> condition where **L. reuteri DSM 17938 has the strongest evidence.** ⚠️ **"The positive results of one
> strain do not necessarily imply the same effects using other strains"** [S1].
>
> **One study combined L. rhamnosus GG with another strain and the MIXTURE HAD NO EFFECT.** ⚠️ **"The
> effects of mixing different strains of probiotics could not be predicted from the results of using a
> single strain"** [S1].

⚠️ **This means a product recommendation is a STRAIN recommendation, not a "probiotic" recommendation —
and that the strain on the label, not the genus, is what the evidence attaches to.**

---

## ⚠️ Safety — the one absolute bar

> ⚠️ **PROBIOTICS SHOULD BE AVOIDED IN PATIENTS WHO ARE SEVERELY ILL OR IMMUNOCOMPROMISED, AS THEY CAN
> CAUSE INFECTIONS IN THESE PATIENTS** [P1].

**Against that, in healthy infants:** **all the probiotic strains studied for infantile colic were SAFE AND
WELL TOLERATED**, and **there have been NO REPORTS OF ADVERSE EFFECTS in all the trials performed thus far**
[S1]. ⚠️ **Two populations, two different risk statements. The bar in [P1] is about who is taking it, not
about the organism.**

⚠️ **S2 adds the gap on the other side: ABSENCE OF LONG-TERM SAFETY DATA** [S2].

## Infantile colic — the one worked example [S1]

**Infantile colic is benign and self-limiting, affects approximately 20% of all infants worldwide, peaks
around the sixth week of age and ceases naturally after 4–6 months** [S1].

### Diagnose it properly first [S1]

> **Rome IV requires ALL of:**
> - **infant under five months of age when symptoms start and stop**
> - **recurrent and/or prolonged crying, agitation and irritability reported by caregivers, occurring
>   without obvious cause and not preventable or resolvable by them**
> - ⚠️ **ABSENCE of infantile failure to thrive, fever or illness** [S1]

> ⚠️ **WARNING SIGNS of a pathological cause: FEVER · PROSTRATION · VOMITING · DIARRHOEA · SUBOPTIMAL
> GROWTH VELOCITY** [S1].

**Children with colic have HIGHER proteobacteria (E. coli) and LOWER Bifidobacterium and Lactobacillus than
healthy infants** [S1] — the observation the whole line of research rests on.

### ⚠️ The dose, and the population it applies to [S1]

> **L. reuteri DSM 17938 at a dose of 1 × 10⁸ colony-forming units (CFU)/day significantly reduces crying
> time in infants who are PREDOMINANTLY BREASTFED**, and is **superior to placebo and to other therapies —
> diet, manipulative interventions, reassurance, massage, herbal treatment, acupuncture and simethicone**
> [S1].
>
> ⚠️ **This is a QUOTED dose from an open source and is machine re-checkable here.**

**L. reuteri DSM 17938 is the most studied strain and has the strongest evidence for reducing crying time
and fussing** [S1].

### ⚠️ And the evidence against it, stated with equal weight

> ⚠️ **THE AUSTRALIAN STUDY, WHICH HAD THE LARGEST SAMPLE SIZE, DID NOT FIND A SIGNIFICANT DIFFERENCE
> BETWEEN INTERVENTION AND PLACEBO** [S1].

**A meta-analysis of four high-quality double-blind trials, 345 infants (174 probiotic / 171 placebo),
found a reduction in daily crying of 25 MINUTES from baseline by the 21st day** [S1].

⚠️ **The meta-analysis authors considered there was INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE to draw conclusions in
FORMULA-FED infants** [S1].

### The other strains [S1]

| Strain | Result [S1] |
|---|---|
| **L. reuteri DSM 17938** | **strongest evidence; exclusively/predominantly breastfed infants** |
| ⚠️ **L. rhamnosus GG** | **NO symptomatic improvement versus placebo in two trials** |
| **B. breve CECT7263** | **12% of children cried >3 h/day**, versus **21% on L. fermentum CECT5716** and **29% control**; **greater efficacy in BOTH breastmilk-fed and formula-fed infants** |
| **L. fermentum CECT5716** | **21% cried >3 h/day** |
| ⚠️ **L. rhamnosus GG + another strain (mixture)** | **NO effect** |

### What guidelines say [S1]

> **The UK guideline and the Royal Children's Hospital Melbourne clinical practice guideline note that
> there is LIMITED EVIDENCE TO SUPPORT GENERALISED PROBIOTIC USE, with ONLY L. reuteri DSM17938 having
> evidence of efficacy in EXCLUSIVELY BREASTFED infants** [S1].
>
> **S1's own conclusion: the evidence is LIMITED AND STILL EMERGING** [S1].

## ⚠️ How to read this guideline

**Nothing here says "probiotics work" or "probiotics don't work", because no retrieved source says either.**
What the sources support is narrower and more useful:

1. ⚠️ **The strain is the intervention.** A benefit shown for one strain does not transfer to another, to a
   mixture, or to a genus [P1][S1].
2. ⚠️ **The population is part of the dose.** The colic evidence is for **predominantly breastfed** infants;
   in formula-fed infants the same review says the evidence is insufficient [S1].
3. ⚠️ **Avoid in the severely ill or immunocompromised** [P1] — the only hard safety rule in any source here.
4. **In IBD, C. difficile-associated diarrhoea and atopic eczema, the retrieved Australian position is that
   probiotics are not useful, not better than placebo, or unsupported** [P1][P2][P3][P5].
