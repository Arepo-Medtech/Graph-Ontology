## ⚠️ Confirm the diagnosis before treating

> **A moving louse must be found to confirm infestation (combing with conditioner is better than visual
> inspection for doing this). Itching or the presence of eggs (nits) does not necessarily indicate active
> infestation** [A1].

**Pediculosis or infestation by the head louse, *Pediculus humanus capitis*, can occur at any age but is most
common in school-age children** [A1].

**Examine family members and close contacts. Family members with live head lice should be treated at the same
time to prevent reinfestation** [A1].

## Two mechanisms, and only one faces resistance

### Physical insecticides — resistance unlikely

**Kill lice via physical action, eg by disrupting water balance. Effective against resistant lice; resistance
is unlikely** [A1].

| Agent | Age | Application | Notes [A1] |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Benzyl alcohol 5%** (NeutraLice Advance) | **>6 months** | **10 minutes** | **little or no odour, well tolerated**; **may kill some eggs**; ⚠️ **repeat after 7 *and* 14 days** |
| **Dimeticone 4%** (Hedrin 15) | **all ages** | **15 minutes**, ⚠️ **other brands may differ** | **odourless, well tolerated, safe in children, pregnancy and breastfeeding**; **may kill some eggs**; **repeat after 7 days** |
| **Isopropyl myristate** (Full Marks Solution) | **>2 years** | **10 minutes** | **odourless, well tolerated**; ⚠️ **does not kill eggs**; **repeat after 7 days** |

### Neurotoxic insecticides — resistance is common

> ⚠️ **Resistance is common; check lice are killed (no movement) after the recommended application time and
> repeat successful treatment after 7 days. If lice are not killed, use a different treatment as soon as
> possible** [A1].

**Although they may kill some eggs, they do not remove them** [A1].

**Pyrethrins with piperonyl butoxide** (Banlice, Pyrenel) — **all ages**, **foam or mousse, 10 minutes**;
**probably as effective as other insecticide treatments, and safe in pregnancy and breastfeeding** [A1].

⚠️ **Preferred treatments in pregnancy: dimeticone, and pyrethrins with piperonyl butoxide** [A1].

## How to apply insecticide

⚠️ **Apply to hair that has no conditioner or hair products in it** [A1].

⚠️ **Avoid using a hair dryer after insecticide treatment, as heat may destroy the active ingredient** [A1].

**Use 2 applications 7 days apart — 3 applications of benzyl alcohol are recommended. This ensures that lice
that have hatched since the first application are killed. Occasionally a third application may be
needed** [A1].

**Between treatments, apply conditioner to dry or wet hair, detangle using an ordinary comb, then comb using a
fine-tooth comb to remove lice and eggs (nits), or pull the eggs off with your fingernails** [A1].

> ⚠️ **Do not overuse insecticide treatments or use them to prevent head lice infestation. Overuse may cause
> irritation and, in the case of neurotoxic insecticides, result in resistant lice** [A1].

## Non-drug treatment

**Conditioner and combing** **assists the physical removal of lice and eggs (it does not kill them), so
requires motivation to be effective**. ⚠️ **Repeat often, eg every 1 or 2 days, until no head lice are seen
for at least 10 days** [A1].

**Electric combs** must be **used on dry hair** and **appear to offer little advantage over fine-tooth
combs** [A1].

**Essential oils and herbal products**: **there is some evidence to suggest that MOOV Head Lice Solution®
(eucalyptus oil 11%) or NeutraLice Lotion® (tea tree oil 10% and lavender oil 1%) are effective, however they
may cause adverse effects, eg stinging or burning** [A1].

**Oral ivermectin**: ⚠️ **specialists may use ivermectin for head lice refractory to all other
treatments** [A1].

## Treatment failure

**May result from inadequate or incorrect application, pediculicide resistance or reinfestation** [A1] — in
that order, and the first is addressable without changing drug.

## Counselling

> ⚠️ **Children can be sent back to school after the first treatment** [A1].

**Tell children not to share hats and hairbrushes** [A1]. **Soak combs and hairbrushes in hot water (>60°C)
for 30 seconds and wash pillowcases in hot water or put in a clothes dryer for 15 minutes** [A1].
