# Substance use in children and adolescents: acute presentations

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** assessment, calming care, stabilisation and disposition of children and adolescents presenting intoxicated or in withdrawal. Drug-specific toxicity is in `recreational-drug-overdose-children`; the adult and young-person counterpart is `alcohol-problems`.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 17 of 17 claims quoted verbatim from the **RCH Melbourne Clinical Practice Guideline *Substance use (abuse)*** (undated page); **20 fragments re-checked by machine. 0 quoted doses.**

> ⚠️ **Intoxicated or withdrawing children can deteriorate fast** — observe and monitor closely, and **reassess for withdrawal once they appear stable.** No drug or dose is given on this page.

---

## Why it is risky

- ⚠️ **These children may deteriorate rapidly or become extremely agitated, aggressive and violent**, and may conceal or deny use because of how parents may respond. [S1]
- **Response to a drug depends on** the individual (age, size, gender, health, mood), the drug's pharmacology and pharmacokinetics, the dose, the setting, combinations with other substances, and previous experience. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Even small or infrequent use can cause a dramatic response** in a young person with underlying psychological or psychiatric issues. [S1]

## Assess

- **Intoxication signs:** slurred speech, poor concentration, altered perception, agitation, confusion, disorientation, unstable mood or gait, vomiting, diarrhoea, incontinence. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Exclude medical causes first:** head injury, acute infection, electrolyte imbalance, CVA, hypoglycaemia, psychosis, severe liver disease. [S1]
- **Take an alcohol and other drug (AOD) history** — what, when and how much today; if the child cannot or will not say, **use collateral sources** (companions, parents, family, guardians). [S1]
- **Examine for signs of use:** puncture marks, cellulitis, phlebitis, abscesses, nasal erosion, irritation or rash around nostrils or mouth, rectal damage, dehydration, rapid weight loss. [S1]
- **Review general functioning and psychosocial circumstances; consult mental health** if background mental health issues are suspected. [S1]

## Calm, safe care

- **A simple, quiet, uncluttered room; as few staff as possible; a calm, confident, respectful approach**; speak clearly and slowly and use the child's name. [S1]
- **Brief, frequent reassurance** helps avoid agitation. ⚠️ **Protect from accidental harm** — never leave them on a bed without safety guards; lower the bed as close to the floor as possible. [S1]
- **For the confused child:** regular reality orientation, a familiar object in view, correct perceptual errors, accompany them to the toilet. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Aggressive child:** keep space and an easy exit, don't crowd them, keep furniture between you, **work in pairs or call security** if at risk; try to identify and remove the cause of anger. [S1]
- ⚠️ **A child refusing treatment and wanting to leave when unsafe:** you may need to exercise your duty of care — follow Code response procedures and consult senior staff. [S1]

## Stabilise

- ⚠️ **Observe and monitor intoxicated or withdrawing children closely** — they are at risk of medical complications. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Alcohol withdrawal can start before the blood alcohol reading reaches zero.** Seizures may signal alcohol or benzodiazepine withdrawal, or stimulant intoxication. [S1]

## Disposition

- ⚠️ **Once an intoxicated child seems stable, reassess for withdrawal** — early recognition and treatment can prevent life-threatening complications. [S1]
- **Refer to the child's primary health practitioner**, and consider a specialist AOD or mental health service. **If AOD raises concern for future care or safety, consider notifying Child Protective Services.** [S1]
