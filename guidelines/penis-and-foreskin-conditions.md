# Penis and foreskin conditions in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** normal foreskin development, balanitis and penile cellulitis, phimosis, paraphimosis, zipper injury, hair tourniquet, post-circumcision problems and priapism in boys. Adult candidal balanitis is in `candidiasis`; the acute scrotum is in `acute-scrotal-pain-children`; sexually active adolescents are covered by `sti-syndromes-and-screening`.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 23 of 23 claims quoted verbatim from the **RCH Melbourne Clinical Practice Guideline *The penis and foreskin*** (Last updated September 2025); **40 fragments re-checked by machine. 3 quoted doses.**

> ⚠️ **Red flags: urinary retention, a swollen red penis with fever, and blue or black discolouration of the distal penis.** A non-retractable foreskin alone is normal.

## Normal foreskin

- **A non-retractable foreskin is a normal variant and needs no intervention** — it is not true phimosis. Full retraction is possible in 10% by 1 year, 50% by 10 years and 99% by 17 years. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Never forcibly retract the foreskin for cleaning.** Once it retracts naturally, retract it in the bath and **replace it immediately** to prevent paraphimosis. [S1]
- **Smegma lumps and residual foreskin attachments are normal** and need no intervention; smegma discharge is sometimes mistaken for pus. [S1]
- **Problematic ballooning:** betamethasone 0.05% sparingly to the preputial ring three times daily for 6–12 weeks (success over 90%, recurrence up to 17%). **Circumcision is not indicated** unless phimosis is pathological. [S1]

## Balanitis and infection

- **Minor redness or soreness at the foreskin tip:** reassurance and avoiding chemical and physical triggers. [S1]
- **Balanitis:** warm weak salt-water soaks; barrier cream or 1% hydrocortisone; clotrimazole or miconazole if candida is suspected; oral analgesia as needed. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Topical antibiotics are not effective or recommended**, and **do not retract the foreskin during acute inflammation** — it can cause paraphimosis. [S1]
- **Secondary bacterial infection or cellulitis:** cefalexin 20 mg/kg (max 750 mg) orally three times daily for 7 days. **Send urine culture if febrile** to exclude UTI. [S1]
- **Group A streptococcus can cause a severe weeping, raw genital rash.** Swabs are often contaminated and not routinely recommended — treat on clinical merit. [S1]

## Phimosis

- **Pathological phimosis = scarring of the preputial ring**: a visible scar ring, non-retractable at the end of puberty, a previously retractable foreskin that no longer retracts, or persistent ballooning with a pinhole opening unresponsive to steroid. [S1]
- **Trial betamethasone 0.05% cream 2–3 times daily for 2–4 weeks**; if it works, continue to a total of 6–12 weeks. **No or poor response → paediatric surgery.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Urgent surgical referral if the child cannot pass urine.** [S1]

## Paraphimosis

- ⚠️ **Paraphimosis is a urological emergency** with risk of preputial necrosis, but can usually be corrected without surgery. [S1]
- **Oral analgesia, then a firm compression bandage (ideally 1 inch) from the penile tip over the oedema for 10–15 minutes**, then attempt reduction; if that fails, re-bandage for a further 15 minutes and retry. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Failed manual reduction → urgent surgical consult. Dusky or dark tissue → urgent paediatric surgeon review.** [S1]
- **After reduction, no circumcision or follow-up is needed.** Advise no retraction for a few days, retract only for cleaning, and always replace fully. [S1]

## Zipper injury and hair tourniquet

- **Zipper entrapment:** oral analgesia ± sedation; cut the median bar of the slider, then across the zipper, with wire cutters, and separate the teeth. **Glans injury → paediatric surgery.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Hair tourniquet in infants:** red, swollen distal penis with a demarcation line — **divide the fibre or hair ring** and check skin integrity; ask a senior if unsure. [S1]

## Circumcision and priapism

- **Medical indications for circumcision: pathological phimosis or recurrent UTIs.** An inflamed, crusted glans afterwards is common; petroleum jelly to the nappy or a liner for a few days may help. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Significant post-circumcision bleeding:** apply compression, get urgent surgical advice and **consider coagulopathy.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Priapism (erection over 4 hours) is a surgical emergency**; commonest causes are sickle cell disease (65%), leukaemia (10%) and trauma (10%). [S1]
- **Older children:** running up stairs, urination, cold bath, cold packs — ⚠️ **no cold packs if the child could have sickle cell disease.** Seek care early if not resolved in under 2 hours. [S1]
- **Consult the paediatric or surgical team for:** inability to pass urine · paraphimosis with ischaemia · priapism · zipper injury involving the glans. [S1]
