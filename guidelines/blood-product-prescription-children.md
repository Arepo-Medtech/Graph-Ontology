# Blood product prescription in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** indications, volumes, rates and product modifications for red cells, platelets, FFP and cryoprecipitate in infants and children (not neonates). ## Before any transfusion

- ⚠️ **Record consent in the medical record before transfusion**, and follow hospital transfusion procedures. [S1]
- **Give a transfusion only when the expected benefit is likely to outweigh the potential hazards.** [S1]

## Red cells

- **Hb under 70 g/L: red cell transfusion is often indicated.** Lower thresholds may be acceptable if there are no clinical features (tachycardia, flow murmur, lethargy, dizziness, breathlessness, cardiac failure) and specific therapy such as iron is available. [S1]
- **Hb 70–90 g/L: may be indicated depending on the setting** — bleeding or haemolysis, symptoms and signs of anaemia, or uncorrected congenital heart disease. [S1]
- **Hb over 90 g/L: often unnecessary and may be inappropriate**, except in specific situations such as haemoglobinopathies (thalassaemia, sickle cell disease) or congenital anaemia on a chronic transfusion program. [S1]
- **Volume, under 20 kg:** mL = weight (kg) × 0.5 × desired Hb rise (g/L), prescribed in mL; **maximum Hb increment 20 g/L.** Example: 10 kg child from 60 to 80 g/L = 100 mL. **Over 20 kg: 1 unit, then reassess.** [S1]
- **Rate: 5 mL/kg/hr**, usual maximum about 150 mL/hr; consider starting at half the prescribed rate for the first 15 minutes. [S1]

## Platelets

- **Prophylactic platelets are not indicated for immune-mediated thrombocytopenia** (ITP, HIT, TTP/HUS); congenital thrombocytopenia is an individualised decision. [S1]
- **No pre-procedure platelets are needed for bone marrow aspirate or trephine, or for peripheral IV insertion.** [S1]
- **Volume: under 15 kg, 10 mL/kg (prescribed in mL); over 15 kg, 1 unit.** Rate 10–20 mL/kg/hr — faster transfusion (e.g. over 30 minutes) may cause a reaction. [S1]

## Grading bleeding (BASIC)

- ⚠️ **Severe bleeding = any of:** organ dysfunction within 24 hours · haemodynamic instability (HR up or BP down by more than 20% from baseline) · Hb drop over 20% in 24 hours · measurable loss ≥5 mL/kg/hr for ≥1 hour · intraspinal bleeding with neurological loss · intra-articular bleeding limiting movement · intraocular bleeding impairing vision. [S1]
- **Moderate bleeding = all of:** more than minimal but not severe · no organ dysfunction · no haemodynamic instability · Hb drop ≤20% · measurable loss ≥1 but <5 mL/kg/hr. [S1]

## Fresh frozen plasma

- **FFP is appropriate for:** acute bleeding with significant coagulopathy · warfarin reversal with significant or life-threatening bleeding (with vitamin K and prothrombinex) · liver disease with significant bleeding and coagulopathy · acute DIC with bleeding · massive haemorrhage · bleeding during or after cardiac bypass · plasma exchange for TTP · factor deficiencies with no concentrate available. [S1]
- ⚠️ **FFP is not indicated** to correct minor INR/APTT prolongation in a non-bleeding child, for minor abnormalities in liver disease without bleeding, or to reverse an INR under 2.0 for minor procedures. [S1]
- **FFP dose: 10–20 mL/kg for all children, at 10–20 mL/kg/hr.** [S1]

## Cryoprecipitate

- **Cryoprecipitate when fibrinogen is under 1.5 g/L** with active bleeding, in massive haemorrhage (or hyperfibrinolysis), or bleeding during or after cardiac bypass. [S1]
- **Cryoprecipitate when fibrinogen is under 1.0 g/L** with acquired deficiency or acute DIC and significant bleeding, or before an invasive procedure with significant bleeding risk or at a critical site (neurosurgery, eye surgery). [S1]
- **Not indicated** for non-bleeding children with mildly low fibrinogen, or liver disease with minor abnormalities and no active bleeding. [S1]
- **Cryoprecipitate dose: 5–10 mL/kg for all children, at 10–20 mL/kg/hr.** [S1]

## Rate, timing and modified products

- ⚠️ **In a critical bleeding event, transfuse as fast as clinically indicated.** **Every transfusion must be completed within four hours** of the product leaving blood bank. [S1]
- **Maximum prescribed volume should not exceed the equivalent adult transfusion.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Irradiated products** for all immunocompromised children (including all immunology and oncology patients), preterm and low-birth-weight neonates, neonates with cardiac disease, HLA-matched products and directed donations — to prevent transfusion-associated graft-versus-host disease. [S1]
- **CMV-negative products only** for neonatal exchange transfusion, neonates up to 28 days post-term, pregnant women, and CMV-negative children with SCID. At RCH, leucocyte-depleted products are an acceptable alternative. [S1]
- **Phenotype-matched red cells** for chronically transfused children or red cell alloantibodies; **IgA-deficient products** for IgA deficiency with anti-IgA antibody; **HLA-matched platelets** for HLA-antibody refractoriness. [S1]
