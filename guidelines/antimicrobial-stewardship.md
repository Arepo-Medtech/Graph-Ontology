# Antimicrobial stewardship

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-21 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** all settings — hospital, primary care and community.

> **Australian primary source.** Australian Commission on Safety and Quality in Health Care (ACSQHC).
> ***Antimicrobial Stewardship Clinical Care Standard*** [S1]. **Eight quality statements** and a set of
> indicators.

**This is the standard that sits underneath the dozen infection guidelines already in this compendium** —
UTI, chlamydia and gonorrhoea, syphilis, *M. genitalium*, trichomoniasis, LGV, donovanosis, mpox, the
hepatitides, and acute rheumatic fever. Each names an organism; this one governs how the drug is chosen,
documented, reviewed and stopped.

## ⚠️ Stewardship does not mean fewer antibiotics

The first quality statement is not a restriction. It is an instruction to **treat immediately**.

> **A patient with a life-threatening condition due to a suspected infection receives an appropriate
> antimicrobial immediately, without waiting for the results of investigations** *(Quality statement 1)* [S1].

> **Obtain clinical specimens as appropriate, but do not delay administration of antimicrobials and do not
> wait for results of investigations. When results are received, immediately reassess the treatment** [S1].

The standard states its own goal in the same terms [S1]:

> **Using the right antimicrobial to treat the condition, at the right dose, at the right frequency, by the
> right route of administration, at the right time and for the right duration, based on accurate assessment
> and timely review** — and **"including assurance when an antimicrobial is not needed."**

**Right, not fewer.** Under-treating sepsis is a stewardship failure in exactly the same way over-treating a
viral sore throat is.

### When the drug is not there

> **If there is no immediate access to the appropriate antimicrobials, arrange for immediate transfer of the
> patient to an acute care facility; for example, ambulance transfer to a hospital. This is relevant in
> community settings or for some rural hospitals** [S1].

Access written in as a clinical step: **if you cannot give it, move the patient.**

### End of life

> **When considering the administration of antimicrobials for patients with life-threatening or serious
> infections, the patient's advance care plan should be considered** [S1].

Even the "give it immediately" statement has a clause that stops it being automatic.

## Prescribing to the guidelines

**Quality statement 2** [S1]: prescribing **in accordance with the current *Therapeutic Guidelines* or
evidence-based, locally endorsed guidelines and the antimicrobial formulary** — including the **appropriate
active ingredient, dose, formulation, route and frequency of administration, and duration** for the patient's
clinical condition.

**Prescribe, dispense and administer in line with local antimicrobial formularies and restrictions, where
available, including those applied to broad-spectrum antimicrobials** [S1].

**Consider the individual patient's characteristics — age, weight, renal function, allergies, other medicines
prescribed and other health conditions** [S1].

## ⚠️ The allergy label

**Quality statement 3** [S1]: when an adverse reaction (including an allergy) is reported or recorded, **the
active ingredient(s), date, nature and severity of the reaction are assessed and documented** —
**"This enables the most appropriate antimicrobial to be used when required."**

**Before prescribing, dispensing or administering**, check the healthcare record **and ask the patient** about
previous adverse reactions — **the active ingredient(s), the date, the nature and severity of the reaction
including how it was managed, and where they recorded it, such as on an allergy bracelet or a medicines
list** [S1].

**The six essential elements for assessing an adverse reaction** [S1]:

| Element | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Patient's description of the event** | what happened to the patient |
| **Nature of the reaction** | diagnosis of the reaction |
| **Active ingredient(s)** | thought to have caused the adverse reaction |
| **Assessment of likelihood** | certainty it was caused by the active ingredient |
| **Severity of event** | consequence to the patient, for example hospitalisation |
| **Date and location of the care** | **because the original record may have more detail** |

> **If any of the essential elements for assessing an adverse reaction are unknown, this should be explicitly
> documented in the patient's healthcare record** [S1].

**Documenting the absence of information is itself the requirement.** An unexplained "penicillin allergy"
carried forward for decades is what closes off first-line therapy — and this statement is the mechanism for
unpicking it.

- **Assess the likelihood of the reaction being caused by the active ingredient.** Relevant factors include
  **the potential for the reaction to recur and the likely consequences to the patient** [S1].
- **Review the accuracy of the documentation and update the patient's healthcare record** [S1].
- > **Communicate any changes to a patient's adverse reaction or allergy status to the patient and their
  > regular clinician** — for example, in My Health Record or a discharge summary for the general
  > practitioner. **It may also be necessary to update the patient's medicines list** [S1].
- **If a patient experiences a new adverse reaction, document the essential elements** [S1].

## Microbiological testing

**Quality statement 4** [S1]: appropriate samples **as clinically indicated, preferably before starting
antimicrobial therapy** — because:

> **This ensures that treatment can be specific for the infecting organism, and that the most appropriate
> narrow-spectrum antimicrobial is used** [S1].

**For life-threatening or serious infection such as sepsis, obtain clinical specimens as appropriate but start
administering antimicrobials as soon as possible. Reassess the treatment as soon as the test results are
available** [S1].

**This is the *M. genitalium* guideline's resistance-guided therapy stated as a general principle** — and the
LGV guideline's reflex-typing instruction is the same idea applied to one organism.

## The consultation where nothing is prescribed

**Quality statement 5** [S1]: information about condition and treatment options **in a way that they can
understand**, and if antimicrobials are prescribed, **how to use them, when to stop, potential side effects
and a review plan**.

> **Discuss the expected progression of the infection and the potential benefits and harms of the treatment
> options, which may or may not include antimicrobials** [S1].

> **If antimicrobials are not needed, reassure the patient and inform them of other treatments that can help
> with symptoms** [S1].

**Not prescribing is a quality statement with content** — reassurance plus an alternative — rather than an
absence of action.

**If antimicrobials are needed, discuss the importance of using them as prescribed, how long to take them, any
potential adverse effects, any potential drug interactions with existing medicines, and when the treatment
will be reviewed or ceased** [S1]. **Provide verbal and/or written information** [S1].

## Documentation

**Quality statement 6** [S1]: document **the indication, active ingredient, dose, frequency and route of
administration, and the intended duration or review plan** in the healthcare record — **and any plans for
therapeutic drug monitoring** if required.

**For services:** where electronic records are used, **incorporate flags and reminders to support
documentation in all relevant fields, or consider making them mandatory fields** [S1].

**The indication is the item most often missing**, and it has its own indicator.

## Review

**Quality statement 7** [S1]: **regular clinical review, with the frequency dependent on patient acuity and
risk factors**. The **need for ongoing use, appropriate microbial spectrum of activity, dose, frequency and
route are assessed and adjusted**. **Investigation results are reviewed promptly when they are reported.**

- > **If microbiological tests are ordered, review the results within 24 hours of them being available, and use
  > this information to consider whether changing or stopping antimicrobials is appropriate** [S1].
- > **If the patient is on intravenous agents, consider oral options to reduce hospital-acquired
  > infections** [S1].
- **Ensure the agent and dose are appropriate for the site of the infection and patient parameters, such as
  renal function** [S1].
- **When prescribing a broad-spectrum antimicrobial, review clinical status and microbiology results to
  determine whether treatment can be switched** [S1].

The IV-to-oral switch is justified here **not by convenience but by infection risk** — the line itself is a
hazard.

## ⚠️ Surgical and procedural prophylaxis

**Quality statement 8** [S1]: prophylaxis **in accordance with the current *Therapeutic Guidelines* or
evidence-based, locally endorsed guidelines**, covering **the need for prophylaxis, choice of antimicrobial,
dose, route and timing of administration, and duration**.

Three explicit avoidances [S1]:

> **Avoid prescribing antimicrobials post-procedurally, as prolonged antimicrobial use is not usually
> required.**

> **Topical antimicrobials are not recommended for surgical prophylaxis.**

> **Avoid using off-label routes of administration for antimicrobials such as irrigations, pastes, washes or
> topical applications for surgical prophylaxis.**

**Consider the patient's clinical condition — for example, colonisation with a multidrug-resistant organism,
or patient allergies** [S1].

This is the statement behind the **hip fracture** standard's instruction to *"prescribe surgical antibiotic
prophylaxis... according to current guidelines"*, and behind the **ARF/RHD** guideline's endocarditis
prophylaxis rules.

## Indicators

| Indicator [S1] | |
|---|---|
| **2a** | **Proportion of antimicrobial prescriptions in accordance with current *Therapeutic Guidelines* or evidence-based, locally endorsed guidelines** |
| **2b** | **Proportion of prescriptions for restricted antimicrobials in accordance with the locally endorsed approval policy** |
| **3a** | **Proportion of patients with an adverse reaction with comprehensive documentation of the reaction in their healthcare record** |
| **6a** | **Proportion of prescriptions for which the indication is documented** |
| **6b** | **Proportion of prescriptions for which the duration, stop date or review date is documented** |
| **7a** | **Proportion of prescriptions for which an antimicrobial review and updated treatment decision is documented within 48 hours from the first prescription** |
| **8a** | **Perioperative prophylactic antimicrobial prescribed in accordance with guidelines** |
| **8b** | **Perioperative prophylactic antimicrobial dose prescribed in accordance with guidelines** |

**No benchmarks are set for any indicator by the Commission** [S1]. Definitions are specified at
**METEOR**, and an **Indicator Monitoring Tool** is published in the Commission's Resource hub [S1].

**Note what is audited:** not *whether* an antibiotic was given, but **whether the indication was written
down, whether an end date exists, and whether someone reviewed it within 48 hours.**

## Where this connects in the compendium

| Guideline here | What this standard supplies |
|---|---|
| ***M. genitalium*** | resistance-guided therapy is QS4 as a general rule — sample first, narrow later |
| **LGV** | reflex typing is QS4 applied to one organism; the request-form instruction is how it happens |
| **Chlamydia and gonorrhoea, syphilis** | guideline-concordant prescribing (QS2) and review (QS7) |
| **Acute rheumatic fever / RHD** | endocarditis prophylaxis is QS8; the standard's "right duration" is the 10-year secondary prophylaxis question |
| **Hip fracture care** | surgical prophylaxis "according to current guidelines" is QS8 |
| **UTI** | QS5 — the consultation where reassurance and symptom relief replace a prescription |
| **Mpox** | QS4 — the swab type warning, and the laboratory-availability problem |
