# Hypernatraemia in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** severity, causes, assessment, safe correction rate and IV fluid rates for mild and moderate hypernatraemia in children; **severe hypernatraemia (170 mmol/L or more) is referred to a tertiary centre and ICU and is not managed here.** No adult hypernatraemia guideline is in this corpus. Related RCH pages: `hyponatraemia-children`, `intravenous-fluids-children`, `dehydration-children`.

## Key points and severity

- **Start treatment early with IV sodium chloride 0.9% + glucose 5%.** ⚠️ **Do not exceed 0.5 mmol/L/hr (10–12 mmol/L per day)** — risk of cerebral oedema, seizures and permanent neurological injury. [S1]
- **Paired serum and urine osmolality for all moderate or severe hypernatraemia — but do not delay treatment for it.** [S1]
- **Severity:** mild 146–149 · moderate 150–169 · **severe 170 mmol/L or more.** Moderate to severe can cause brain shrinkage with vascular rupture, haemorrhage, demyelination and permanent injury. [S1]
- **Infants and small children are more vulnerable** (greater insensible losses; cannot ask for or reach fluids). **Chronic hypernatraemia (over 48 hours) is often well tolerated.** [S1]
- **Common water-deficit causes:** GI loss (diarrhoea, stoma), skin loss (sweating, burns), renal loss, and **inability to obtain water — including breastfed babies with inadequate milk supply.** [S1]
- **Sodium excess:** wrongly concentrated formula, high-osmolality rehydration solutions, salt poisoning, or iatrogenic hypertonic saline or sodium bicarbonate. [S1]

## Assess

- **History:** detailed feeding history — **check the feed concentration**; losses (GI, polyuria, skin); midline brain defect; renal disease; diuretics, desmopressin, hypertonic fluids. [S1]
- **Weigh the bare child** against a recent (within 2 weeks) weight; serial weights up to every 6 hours are most helpful. **Oedema suggests sodium excess.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Hydration assessment may underestimate hypovolaemia** in chronic or severe hypernatraemia. [S1]
- **Signs:** irritability, restlessness and weakness first, then vomiting, twitching, fever, doughy skin, high-pitched cry in infants. ⚠️ **Severe signs with an acute rise above 160 mmol/L:** altered mental state, lethargy, seizures, hyperreflexia, coma. [S1]
- **If Na is 150 mmol/L or more:** UEC, calcium, magnesium, phosphate and glucose. **Use a blood gas sodium if albumin is under 30 g/L.** [S1]
- **Paired serum and urine sodium, creatinine and osmolality are ideal;** if delayed, a **urine dipstick specific gravity** will do — **do not delay treatment.** [S1]

## Treat

- ⚠️ **Treat shock first.** Once circulating volume is restored, **correct slowly — no more than 0.5 mmol/L/hour.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Severe (170 mmol/L or more) is a medical emergency not covered by this guideline: refer to a tertiary centre and contact ICU.** [S1]
- **Mild (146–149): manage the cause and repeat UEC in 4–6 hours.** [S1]
- **Moderate (150–169):** restrict and record oral intake, **stop feed fortifications**, weigh at least daily (up to 6-hourly), **repeat UEC 1–2 hours after starting then 4–6-hourly** if falling appropriately. [S1]
- ⚠️ **If sodium falls faster than 0.5 mmol/L/hr, stop or slow the fluids and seek expert advice early.** If it rises or does not change, seek advice about hypotonic solutions. Monitor neurological status closely. [S1]
- **Water deficit:** total fluid = maintenance + deficit + ongoing losses. **Replace the deficit over 48 hours with IV sodium chloride 0.9% + glucose 5%**; replace ongoing losses (excluding urine) mL for mL with sodium chloride 0.9%; **add potassium once urine output is established.** [S1]
- **Starting rates, sodium chloride 0.9% + glucose 5%, Na 150–169 (mL/hr):** 4 kg 22 · 10 kg 55 · 20 kg 90 · 30 kg 114 · 40 kg 138 · 50 kg 160 · 70 kg 200. They assume 7% dehydration, include maintenance plus the 48-hour deficit, **exclude ongoing losses**, and must be adjusted to the sodium trend. [S1]
- ⚠️ **If seizures occur:** consider venous sinus thrombosis or cerebral infarction, consider contrast CT, and **contact ICU — hypertonic saline may be needed to slow a rapid fall in sodium.** [S1]
- **Sodium excess:** reduce sodium intake and **get expert advice** — hypotonic fluid (e.g. sodium chloride 0.45%) or dialysis if overloaded may be needed. [S1]

## Escalation and discharge

- **Involve the paediatric team for any moderate hypernatraemia (150 mmol/L or more).** ⚠️ **Consider transfer** if the cause is unclear, Na is 170 or more, there are neurological symptoms, or the response is not as expected. [S1]
- **Discharge once the cause is identified and adequately treated.** [S1]
