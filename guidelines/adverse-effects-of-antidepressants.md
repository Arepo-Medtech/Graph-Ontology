# Adverse effects of antidepressants

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-22 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults, with paediatric and perinatal content where the sources address it. ⚠️ **CHOOSING an
antidepressant and treating depression are OUT OF SCOPE** — this compendium's `major-depressive-disorder`

# Part 1 — Serotonin toxicity [A1]

**Occurs when synaptic serotonin rises in the CNS, hyperstimulating 5HT2A receptors. Drugs that increase
serotonergic effects are the most likely to be causative** [A1].

> ⚠️ **THREE WAYS IT HAPPENS** [A1]:
> **a HIGH DOSE of a single drug** · **MORE THAN ONE serotonergic agent used together** · **CHANGING
> ANTIDEPRESSANTS WITH AN INADEQUATE WASHOUT PERIOD**

### Recognising it [A1]

| | |
|---|---|
| **Neurological** | **hyperreflexia, clonus, tremor, incoordination** |
| **Mental state** | **confusion, hypomania, agitation** |
| **Autonomic** | **shivering, sweating, fever** |
| **GI** | **diarrhoea** |

> ⚠️ **GENERALISED HYPERREFLEXIA IS THE MOST IMPORTANT NEUROLOGICAL SIGN.** **The LOWER LIMBS usually
> display more hyperreflexia and clonus than the upper limbs** [A1] — **so examine the legs.**

> **It warrants STOPPING THE IMPLICATED AGENTS PROMPTLY, as it may be serious and DEATHS HAVE BEEN
> REPORTED.** **Cyproheptadine is sometimes used with other measures, but EVIDENCE FROM CONTROLLED TRIALS
> IS LACKING** [A1].

### ⚠️ What does NOT cause it

> **THERE IS NO SIGNIFICANT RISK with serotonin antagonists or drugs affecting other 5HT receptor
> subtypes — antipsychotics, anxiolytics, TRIPTANS and 5HT3 antagonists** [A1].
>
> **Triptans being on this list matters**: the migraine–SSRI combination is common and is not the hazard
> it is often treated as, on AMH's reading.

### The drugs that do [A1]

| Class | Drugs |
|---|---|
| **Antidepressants** | **citalopram, clomipramine, desvenlafaxine, duloxetine, escitalopram, fluoxetine, fluvoxamine, imipramine, moclobemide, paroxetine, phenelzine, sertraline, ⚠️ ST JOHN'S WORT, tranylcypromine, venlafaxine, vortioxetine** |
| ⚠️ **Opioids** | **DEXTROMETHORPHAN · FENTANYL · PETHIDINE · TRAMADOL** |
| **Others** | **dapoxetine, fenfluramine, illicit drugs (MDMA, hallucinogenic amphetamines, LSD), LINEZOLID, ⚠️ LITHIUM, METHYLENE BLUE, phentermine, tryptophan** |

⚠️ **Three of these are easy to miss**: **ST JOHN'S WORT is bought over the counter**; **TRAMADOL and
DEXTROMETHORPHAN are prescribed and sold for unrelated reasons**; and **LITHIUM is on the list**, which
this compendium's `bipolar-disorder` guideline also records from AMH's lithium page.

### ⚠️ Washout intervals [A2]

| Change | Interval |
|---|---|
| **Starting an SSRI on, or soon after, moclobemide or a MAOI** | ⚠️ **CONTRAINDICATED within 14 DAYS of stopping a MAOI, or within 2 DAYS of stopping moclobemide** |
| **Stopping an SSRI → starting moclobemide or a MAOI** | **2–4 DAYS** |
| ⚠️ **Except FLUOXETINE → moclobemide or a MAOI** | ⚠️ **AT LEAST 14 DAYS** |

**The fluoxetine exception is a pharmacokinetic one:** ⚠️ **its active metabolite norfluoxetine has a
half-life of UP TO 16 DAYS, while other SSRIs are around 24 hours** [A2]. **Adverse effects and
interactions with fluoxetine MAY PERSIST AFTER TREATMENT IS STOPPED** [A2].

# Part 2 — Withdrawal

## What AMH says [A1]

> **SOME PEOPLE EXPERIENCE WITHDRAWAL EFFECTS AFTER MISSING 1 OR 2 DOSES**, especially with short
> half-life drugs — **venlafaxine, paroxetine**. **Other risk factors: high doses and a long treatment
> course** [A1].

> **At the end of a treatment course, TAPER OVER SEVERAL WEEKS OR MONTHS and monitor for withdrawal
> symptoms. This can minimise the likelihood of RELAPSE as well as withdrawal** [A1].
>
> **An example is PROPORTIONATE TAPERING, where dose reductions become SMALLER when lower doses are
> reached** [A1].

| Drug class | Withdrawal effects [A1] | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| **MAOIs** | **nausea, vomiting, nightmares, panic, restlessness, hallucinations** | **DELIRIUM may also occur with tranylcypromine, particularly where there was dependence** |
| **SSRIs** | **dizziness, nausea, paraesthesia, anxiety, agitation, tremor, sweating, confusion, ELECTRIC SHOCK-LIKE SENSATIONS** | ⚠️ **more common with PAROXETINE, least likely with FLUOXETINE** |
| **TCAs** | **CHOLINERGIC REBOUND — hypersalivation, runny nose, abdominal cramping, diarrhoea, sleep disturbance** | **more common with amitriptyline, doxepin** |
| **SNRIs** | **similar to SSRIs** | ⚠️ **more common with VENLAFAXINE** |
| **Others** | **mirtazapine: anxiety, headache, dizziness, nausea** | **more likely with mirtazapine; rare or not observed with agomelatine, mianserin, vortioxetine** |

**Paroxetine has the shortest half-life of the SSRIs and no active metabolite, so its withdrawal potential
is probably greater than the others'** [A2].

> ⚠️ **WHEN CHANGING ANTIDEPRESSANTS: educate patients about possible withdrawal effects and adverse
> effects of the new drug — RATHER THAN EXPLAINING SYMPTOMS IN TERMS OF RELAPSE OR WORSENING OF
> DEPRESSION — and monitor closely** [A1].

## What the open source AMH points to says [S1]

⚠️ **AMH names this article on its own page** [A1]. **It is a Viewpoint from December 2021. Its
epidemiology is cited data; its final paragraphs are an argued position. Both are quoted; they are
labelled separately here.**

### The epidemiology [S1]

| | [S1] |
|---|---|
| **Australia's rank** | ⚠️ **SECOND HIGHEST USER OF ANTIDEPRESSANTS PER CAPITA among OECD countries excluding the USA — around ONE IN 10 ADULTS** |
| **PBS** | **in the year to June 2020, for the first time an antidepressant (sertraline) was in the top 10 PBS/RPBS medications by defined daily dose/1000 population/day** |
| **What is driving it** | ⚠️ **LONG-TERM USE (>12 months), RATHER THAN NEW DIAGNOSES** |
| **Who prescribes** | **GPs prescribe 86.3% of antidepressants in Australia** |
| **General practice** | **12.4% of encounters are mental health-related; 61.6% of those are managed with medication** |
| **Guideline duration** | **6–12 MONTHS for a single episode of moderate-to-severe depression** |
| ⚠️ **Actual duration** | ⚠️ **AVERAGE APPROXIMATELY FOUR YEARS — and half of users are long-term users** |
| **Variation** | **higher in lower socioeconomic and inner regional areas; NEARLY DOUBLE at age ≥65 vs younger people** |
| **Aged care** | **nearly ONE IN THREE older Australians admitted to residential care in 2008–15 was taking antidepressants on admission — AND THIS ONLY INCREASED AFTER ADMISSION** |

> ⚠️ **The gap between 6–12 months of guideline-recommended therapy and an average of four years is the
> single most actionable number in this guideline.**

### The withdrawal claims [S1]

> **A PHYSIOLOGICAL WITHDRAWAL SYNDROME often makes it difficult for people to stop long-term
> antidepressants.** **Symptoms include anxiety, irritability, dizziness, confusion, low mood, headache and
> electric shock sensations described as "BRAIN ZAPS"** [S1].
>
> ⚠️ **"These symptoms are readily misconstrued as impending relapse, prompting fear and perpetuating
> prescribing"** [S1].
>
> **A range of evidence suggests APPROXIMATELY HALF of people may experience withdrawal symptoms, which
> CAN LAST FOR WEEKS OR MONTHS, with severity and duration likely proportional to duration of use** [S1].
>
> **The incidence, severity and duration have ONLY RECENTLY BECOME WIDELY KNOWN, prompting an update of
> the Royal College of Psychiatrists clinical guidelines to encourage SLOW TAPERING** [S1].

⚠️ **AMH agrees on the direction — taper over weeks to months, proportionately [A1] — and does not put a
figure on how many people are affected. S1's "approximately half" is the additional claim, and it is
sourced by S1 to "a range of evidence" rather than to a single study.**

### ⚠️ The argued position, labelled as such [S1]

**These are the authors' stated views in a Viewpoint, not findings, and they are contested in the
literature. They are recorded because AMH names the article, and because they bear directly on how
adverse effects are weighed:**

> **"every GP knows that antidepressants cannot treat common social issues such as grief, loneliness,
> unemployment or poverty"** [S1]
>
> **"There is general agreement that antidepressants are no more effective than medication placebo in less
> severe depressive disorders"** [S1]
>
> **Adverse effects named: lethargy; SEXUAL DYSFUNCTION, WHICH MAY BE PERSISTENT, including failure to
> orgasm in both sexes; and EMOTIONAL NUMBING — "feeling emotionally detached", "reduced sympathy and
> empathy".** **Antidepressants also cause weight gain and INCREASE THE RISK OF FALLS AND FRACTURES.**
> **Long-term use can lead people to "feeling addicted" or dependent on medication** [S1].

⚠️ **AMH lists sexual dysfunction as a COMMON SSRI adverse effect [A2] but does NOT describe it as
persistent, and does NOT list emotional numbing or falls and fractures at all.** **That is a difference
between the two sources, recorded rather than merged.**

### One practical proposal, from the correspondence [S2]

> **"The obvious critical moment at which GPs can and should consider reduction or cessation is when they
> are about to RE-PRESCRIBE the medicine after the initial duration of treatment (12 months for
> antidepressants...)"** [S2].

# Part 3 — SSRI class effects [A2]

## ⚠️ The four that change management

> ⚠️ **1. MANIA.** **ALL antidepressants may provoke a manic episode in people with bipolar disorder**,
> and **some patients WITHOUT a bipolar history develop an antidepressant-induced manic episode — WHICH
> DOES NOT NECESSARILY IMPLY A DIAGNOSIS OF BIPOLAR AFFECTIVE DISORDER** [A2].
>
> ⚠️ **2. BLEEDING.** **In people at high risk — age >80, previous upper GI bleeding, regular aspirin or
> NSAIDs, or undergoing surgery — serious bleeding may be more likely. Consider avoiding an SSRI or
> monitor carefully** [A2]. Infrequent effects include **abnormal platelet aggregation with bruising, nose
> bleeds, and GI, vaginal or INTRACEREBRAL bleeding** [A2].
>
> ⚠️ **3. HYPONATRAEMIA.** **Usually occurs EARLY in treatment, may be ASYMPTOMATIC, and is part of
> SIADH.** **Drugs that may cause hyponatraemia increase the risk** [A2].
>
> ⚠️ **4. ACUTE ANGLE-CLOSURE CRISIS**, rarely, in those with risk factors — **especially with
> paroxetine** [A2].

**Common: nausea, diarrhoea, agitation, insomnia, drowsiness, tremor, dry mouth, dizziness, headache,
sweating, weakness, anxiety, SEXUAL DYSFUNCTION, rhinitis, myalgia, rash** [A2].
**Rare: elevated liver enzymes, hepatitis, hepatic failure, hyperprolactinaemia including galactorrhoea,
blood dyscrasias, akathisia, paraesthesia, taste disturbance** [A2].

## Choosing between them on adverse effects [A2]

| | [A2] |
|---|---|
| **Fewest CYP-mediated interactions** | **citalopram, escitalopram, sertraline** |
| ⚠️ **QT interval** | **citalopram, fluoxetine and escitalopram may increase it** |
| **Longest half-life / least withdrawal** | **fluoxetine** |
| **Shortest half-life / most withdrawal** | **paroxetine** |
| **Overdose** | **SSRIs are relatively safe in overdose** |
| **Timing** | **usually taken in the MORNING to minimise insomnia; in the evening if somnolence occurs** |
| **Hepatic impairment** | **dose is usually DECREASED** |

## ⚠️ Children and adolescents [A2]

> **A child psychiatrist should supervise treatment.** **Evidence for efficacy of SSRIs in depression in
> children is generally WEAK — if one is used, FLUOXETINE IS FIRST CHOICE and PAROXETINE SHOULD BE
> AVOIDED** [A2].
>
> ⚠️ **A TGA REVIEW of antidepressants, focused on SSRIs in depressed children and adolescents, CONCLUDED
> THAT RISKS HAD BEEN UNDERSTATED — PARTICULARLY THE EARLY APPEARANCE OF SUICIDAL THOUGHTS AND
> BEHAVIOUR** [A2].
>
> **Activating adverse effects such as agitation are MORE LIKELY IN CHILDREN than adolescents, and LEAST
> likely in adults** [A2].

## Pregnancy and breastfeeding [A2]

**Most data indicate SSRIs are not associated with increased malformations, although some studies suggest
an increased risk PARTICULARLY WITH PAROXETINE (fetal heart defects). Some epidemiological data suggest an
association with PREMATURE DELIVERY** [A2].

> ⚠️ **NEONATAL TOXIC SYMPTOMS INCLUDING PERSISTENT PULMONARY HYPERTENSION have been reported — especially
> with paroxetine — after maternal use in LATE pregnancy**, along with self-limited withdrawal effects such
> as irritability and altered muscle tone [A2].

**In late pregnancy, some women may need dose INCREASES; decreasing the dose in the weeks before delivery
may reduce neonatal withdrawal or toxic effects — ⚠️ ALTHOUGH SUPPORTING EVIDENCE IS LACKING** [A2].

**Breastfeeding: considered safe. Some regard SERTRALINE as one of the preferred antidepressants because
its levels in breast milk are low. Drowsiness and irritability occasionally occur in breastfed babies** [A2].
