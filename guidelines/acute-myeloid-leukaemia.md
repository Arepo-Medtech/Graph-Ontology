# Acute myeloid leukaemia in adults (including acute promyelocytic leukaemia)

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** induction, consolidation and maintenance for newly diagnosed APML and AML (fit and unfit for intensive chemotherapy), relapsed/refractory AML, and antimicrobial prophylaxis, in adults. Allogeneic transplant indications are outside the source's scope. Drug doses are given where the source states them; administration detail is in the eviQ protocols. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

> ⚠️ **Australian practice differs from ELN here.** Double induction is less common in Australia, so this guideline treats a less-than-partial response to the *first* induction as refractory (ELN 2022 requires failure of two). Several recommended drugs are not PBS-funded (ivosidenib; venetoclax in relapsed AML), and GO and CPX-351 are PBS-listed but not funded for inpatient use.

## Principles and response definitions

- **Refer every patient who might be a transplant candidate to a transplant centre early;** this guideline does not cover transplant selection or timing. Offer trials at every stage, and remember palliative care can be the right pathway for some. [S1]
- **Partial response** means marrow blasts down by at least 50% from baseline, usually to 5–15%, after the first induction; these patients get a second induction. Those without that response after induction 1 go to relapsed/refractory treatment, because ELN 2022's two-cycle definition of refractory disease does not fit Australian single-induction practice. [S1]

## Acute promyelocytic leukaemia (APML)

- **Low or intermediate risk (WCC ≤10 ×10⁹/L): ATRA plus arsenic trioxide (ATRA-ATO) for induction, then 4 cycles of ATRA-ATO consolidation and no maintenance**. APL0406 found ATRA-ATO at least as good as ATRA-chemotherapy, with the gap widening over time; the trial enrolled only those ≤70 years with performance status 0–2. [S1]
- ⚠️ **High risk (WCC >10 ×10⁹/L): add idarubicin to ATRA-ATO for induction,** age-adjusted at 12 mg/m² (≤60 years), 9 mg/m² (61–69) or 6 mg/m² (≥70); if the count is easily controlled and differentiation syndrome is not a problem, the first two doses usually suffice. Then 4 cycles of ATRA-ATO consolidation without maintenance (APOLLO, patients under 65, unpublished at the time). [S1]
- **Where 4 cycles of intravenous ATO cannot be delivered locally,** give 2 cycles of ATRA-ATO, then eight 3-month maintenance cycles: ATRA 45 mg/m²/day orally on days 1–14, methotrexate from 5 mg/m² weekly on days 15–90 and 6-MP from 50 mg/m²/day on days 15–90, starting 3–4 weeks after consolidation cycle 2. [S1]

## Newly diagnosed AML: fit for intensive chemotherapy

- ⚠️ **FLT3-ITD or FLT3-TKD: 7+3 plus midostaurin.** Cytarabine 200 mg/m² daily by continuous infusion on days 1–7 with idarubicin 12 mg/m² (or daunorubicin 60 mg/m², as in RATIFY) on days 1–3, then midostaurin 50 mg twice daily on days 8–21. No minimum allelic ratio is required. Idarubicin is suggested because daunorubicin is costly and sometimes short. With idarubicin, cytarabine 100 mg/m² a day is acceptable at ≥60 years. [S1]
- **FLT3 AML after the first induction:** a partial response gets a second induction with intermediate-dose cytarabine plus midostaurin (repeating 7+3 with midostaurin often fails). An inadequate response means switching to gilteritinib, which beat further chemotherapy in ADMIRAL and COMMODORE and got more patients to transplant. [S1]
- **FLT3 consolidation and maintenance:** cytarabine 1.5 g/m² twice daily on days 1–3 (1 g/m² if ≥60 years), then midostaurin 50 mg twice daily on days 8–21, for 3–4 cycles. Then midostaurin 50 mg twice daily on days 1–28, for a maximum of 12 cycles or until allograft. High-dose cytarabine gives no survival gain over intermediate dose except in CBF AML. [S1]
- **Core-binding factor (CBF) AML: 7+3 plus GO** at 3 mg/m² (capped at one 5 mg vial per dose), on day 1 or on days 1, 4 and 7; in AML15 a single day-1 dose raised 5-year survival (79% v 51%). Do not give GO in consolidation; use high-dose cytarabine 3 g/m² twice daily on days 1–3 (1 g/m² if 60 or older) for 3–4 cycles, and monitor MRD. [S1]
- **AML after MDS or CMML, with MDS-related cytogenetics, or after prior chemo/radiotherapy: CPX-351** 100 units/m² (cytarabine 100 mg/m² with daunorubicin 44 mg/m²) on days 1, 3 and 5; a second induction for partial response is on days 1 and 3. Consolidation: in older patients, 2 cycles of CPX-351 65 units/m² given on days 1 and 3, or cytarabine 1.5 g/m² twice daily on days 1–3 for 3–4 cycles in younger ones. [S1]
- **CPX-351 funding:** the PBS covers therapy-related AML and AML with prior MDS or MDS-related changes, including adults under 60. Antecedent CMML is not PBS-listed but was in the trial, so still consider it. There are not enough data after an antecedent MPN. The biggest survival gain was in patients who went on to allograft. ⚠️ CPX-351 and GO are mostly given to inpatients, yet the PBS does not fund inpatient use, so each hospital must approve other funding and access varies. [S1]
- **All other fit patients: 7+3** (cytarabine 200 mg/m² daily by infusion on days 1–7 with idarubicin 12 mg/m² or daunorubicin 60 mg/m² on days 1–3); more intense induction adds toxicity without proven survival benefit. For partial response, a second induction of cytarabine 1 g/m² twice daily on days 1–6 with amsacrine 120 mg/m² over 1 hour on days 4–6. Consolidate with cytarabine 1.5 g/m² twice daily (1 g/m² if ≥60) on days 1–3 for 3–4 cycles. [S1]
- **Oral azacitidine maintenance** (300 mg daily for 14 days each cycle) for non-CBF AML in CR/CRi after intensive therapy when no transplant is planned. It is not curative and is usually continued until progression. Evidence is lacking under age 55, and it is not for CBF AML (excluded from QUAZAR and not PBS-funded there). [S1]

## Newly diagnosed AML: not fit for intensive chemotherapy

- ⚠️ **Standard: azacitidine 75 mg/m² subcutaneously or intravenously on days 1–7 with venetoclax (AZA-VEN),** which improved response and survival over azacitidine alone in VIALE-A. CBF AML and AML after MPN were excluded, so no firm advice for them. Safe use needs close monitoring by staff experienced with the regimen. [S1]
- **'Unfit' in VIALE-A** meant age 75 or older, or 18–74 with a comorbidity: ECOG 2 or 3; treated heart failure, chronic stable angina or an ejection fraction ≤50%; FEV1 or DLCO ≤65%; creatinine clearance 30 to under 45 mL/min; moderate liver impairment (bilirubin >1.5 to ≤3.0 × ULN); or another comorbidity the physician judges incompatible with intensive chemotherapy. [S1]
- **IDH1-mutated: azacitidine with ivosidenib is preferred when obtainable** (better tolerated and simpler; AGILE median survival 29.3 v 7.0 months), but ivosidenib is not PBS-funded, so AZA-VEN is the effective alternative. [S1]
- **Too frail for AZA-VEN:** oral decitabine-cedazuridine, azacitidine alone or best supportive care. The PBS limits azacitidine and decitabine-cedazuridine to higher-risk MDS or AML with 20-30% marrow blasts. Very poor-risk disease (such as TP53-mutated) in patients not headed for transplant may also be spared myelosuppressive therapy. Refer early to palliative care. [S1]

## Relapsed or refractory AML

- ⚠️ **Re-test FLT3 in every relapsed or refractory patient, including after allograft; if FLT3-ITD or -TKD is present, use gilteritinib** (better survival and fewer adverse events than salvage chemotherapy in ADMIRAL and COMMODORE). In FLT3-mutated disease failing to reach partial response after induction, move to gilteritinib then, not after more chemotherapy. [S1]
- **Fit, without FLT3: FLAG-Ida, or FLAG-Ida with venetoclax** (no evidence the addition is better; venetoclax is neither TGA-approved nor PBS-funded here). Both are highly myelosuppressive: use them only as a bridge to allograft and do not repeat them in remission without a planned transplant. [S1]
- **Unfit for intensive salvage:** AZA-VEN (expert opinion; unfunded for relapse; poor responses with adverse cytogenetics). For IDH1-mutated relapse, ivosidenib 500 mg daily (CR/CRh 30%), TGA-approved but not PBS-funded. [S1]
- **After non-intensive salvage fails:** low-dose cytarabine with or without thioguanine, or best supportive care. Outcomes after AZA-VEN failure are dismal, so discuss goals of care early, involve palliative care, and state the toxicity risks plainly before any further chemotherapy. [S1]

## Infection prevention

- **At diagnosis, screen for hepatitis B (HBsAg, anti-HBc, anti-HBs), hepatitis C and HIV;** tailor screening for fungal infection, TB and strongyloides to exposure and geography. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Antifungal prophylaxis:** give routine mould-active prophylaxis during intensive induction or re-induction, and in intensive consolidation when fungal risk is high. With venetoclax-based non-intensive therapy, use it only when risk is high, and cut the venetoclax dose alongside an azole (posaconazole, itraconazole and voriconazole inhibit CYP3A4 and raise venetoclax exposure). [S1]
- **No routine antibacterial prophylaxis while neutropenic after intensive chemotherapy,** and fluoroquinolones are not advised for everyone (C. difficile and resistance risk). Neutropenic outpatients should be in a comprehensive care programme with access to emergency antibiotics. [S1]
- **PJP and herpes prophylaxis:** trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole with fludarabine-based intensive regimens (and other T-cell-impairing therapy); valaciclovir against HSV/VZV for all patients on active treatment. [S1]
