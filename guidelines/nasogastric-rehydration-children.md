# Nasogastric rehydration and maintenance fluids in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** short-term nasogastric (NG) rehydration and maintenance fluids in children beyond the newborn period: fluid choice, rapid vs slow rehydration rates, ongoing losses and monitoring. This is the page `gastroenteritis-children` and `dehydration-children` defer to for NG rates; the IV counterpart is `intravenous-fluids-children`. Neonates: `neonatal-intravenous-fluids`. ## Scope and principles

- **For children beyond the newborn period.** Use specialised guidance for neonates, long-term NG feeding, and diabetes, cardiac, metabolic or renal conditions. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Use the enteral route (oral or NG) whenever possible; shocked children need IV resuscitation.** [S1]
- **Serial weights are the best measure of acute fluid change**; clinical signs are often imprecise. **Rehydration does not treat the cause** — review regularly. [S1]
- **NG beats IV on safety**, electrolyte risk and recovery speed from gastroenteritis, and can deliver EBM or formula. [S1]

## Who should (and should not) have NG fluids

- **Indications:** rehydration after GI losses or sustained poor intake; replacing usual intake in acute illness (e.g. bronchiolitis, gingivostomatitis); replacing ongoing losses. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Contraindications:** nil by mouth/fasting · shock or critically unwell · possible bowel obstruction (bilious vomiting, severe distension, ileus) · altered conscious state or aspiration risk · facial trauma or suspected base-of-skull fracture. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Discuss with a senior clinician:** age under 6 months · severe dehydration, electrolyte abnormality or hypoglycaemia · high ADH risk (acute CNS or significant pulmonary conditions) · short gut or chronic GI disorder · craniofacial abnormality. [S1]
- **Usually no investigations.** Consider glucose and electrolytes in young infants, significant ongoing losses, or NG support beyond the initial 4–6 hour rehydration phase — **ORS is hypotonic and hyponatraemia can occur.** [S1]

## Fluid choice and maintenance volume

- **Rehydrate with ORS in all children over 1 month**; available ORS brands do not differ in a clinically significant way. [S1]
- **After rehydration, milk-fed infants resume their usual EBM or formula** (orally or NG). **Stop fortification in GI illness** — the osmotic load can worsen diarrhoea. [S1]
- ⚠️ **2/3 maintenance suits most unwell children needing hydration support**, especially respiratory and CNS infections. **Full maintenance** for well children not drinking enough (e.g. fasting for surgery) and the maintenance phase of gastroenteritis. [S1]
- **Full maintenance (4:2:1):** 3–10 kg 100 mL/kg/day (4 mL/kg/hr) · 10–20 kg 1000 + 50 × (weight − 10) mL/day [40 + 2 × (weight − 10) mL/hr] · 20–60 kg 1500 + 20 × (weight − 20) mL/day [60 + 1 × (weight − 20) mL/hr] · over 60 kg normal maximum 2400 mL/day (100 mL/hr). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not prescribe maintenance from infant milk volumes (150–200 mL/kg/day)** — those meet growth needs in well babies, not fluid needs in unwell ones. [S1]

## Rapid NG rehydration

- **For moderate dehydration in common illnesses such as gastroenteritis**, to speed discharge home. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Not suitable for:** dehydration from lower respiratory infection, meningitis or significant electrolyte abnormality · **age under 6 months** · significant comorbidity · abdominal pain. **Severe dehydration needs IV fluid resuscitation.** [S1]
- **ORS 10–25 mL/kg/hr for 4 hours.** Evidence supports 25 mL/kg/hr; some centres use 10–12.5 mL/kg/hr. [S1]
- **Many NG pumps max out at 300 mL/hr** — divide the target volume by 300 mL (e.g. 1800 mL for an 18 kg child runs as 300 mL/hr for 6 hours). [S1]

## Slow NG rehydration

- **Preferred for moderate dehydration where rapid volumes are inappropriate.** First 24 hours: **replace a 5% deficit over the first 6 hours**, reassess, then give the daily maintenance volume over the next 18 hours; next 24 hours: remaining deficit if still needed, plus maintenance. [S1]
- **Slow NG rates (0–6 h deficit / 7–24 h maintenance), mL/hr:** 3 kg 25/15 · 4 kg 35/20 · 5 kg 40/25 · 6 kg 50/30 · 7 kg 60/40 · 8 kg 70/45 · 9 kg 75/50 · 10 kg 85/55 · 12 kg 100/60 · 15 kg 125/70 · 20 kg 165/80 · 30 kg 250/95. [S1]

## After rehydration, losses and monitoring

- **Return quickly to normal fluids and food; keep breastfeeding.** No benefit from gradual milk reintroduction or dilute feeds. If diarrhoea worsens with lactose or fructose, consider 1–2 weeks of lactose-free milk and no fruit/juice. [S1]
- **Replace ongoing losses mL for mL with ORS**, on top of maintenance (e.g. 200 mL of diarrhoea over 4 hours → ORS 50 mL/hr for the next 4 hours). [S1]
- **Weigh at the start and at least daily**; document inputs and losses (including urine) with at least 12-hourly subtotals. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Vomiting: slow the fluids temporarily and consider ondansetron if ≥6 months.** Significant abdominal pain or ongoing vomiting: senior review. [S1]
- **Involve the paediatric team** for severe dehydration or electrolyte/glucose abnormalities. [S1]
