# Quetiapine poisoning in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** risk assessment, hypotension management, observation periods and discharge after quetiapine ingestion in children and adolescents. General poisoning steps are in `poisoning-initial-management-children`; the other atypical antipsychotic page is `risperidone-poisoning-children`.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 15 of 15 claims quoted verbatim from the **RCH Melbourne Clinical Practice Guideline *Quetiapine Poisoning*** (Last Updated April 2019); **21 fragments re-checked by machine. 5 quoted doses.**

> ⚠️ **Hypotension: saline boluses, then noradrenaline — not adrenaline**, which paradoxically worsens hypotension.

## Who needs assessment

- **Assess:** every deliberate self-poisoning or significant accidental ingestion · any symptomatic child · **any child whose developmental age doesn't fit accidental poisoning — consider non-accidental poisoning.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Significant dose:** 10 mg/kg in an adult; **more than 3 g — or more than 100 mg in a child under 12 — may cause severe toxicity.** [S1]
- **Adolescents: under 3 g usually means mild CNS depression and tachycardia; over 3 g can cause delirium, coma, hypotension and, rarely, seizures (under 5%).** [S1]
- **Tablets are 25–400 mg, immediate or modified release:** identify the exact product and tablet size, and calculate the maximum possible dose per kg. [S1]
- **Immediate release peaks at 1–2 hours; modified-release overdose brings delayed onset and prolonged symptoms**, for example 24–72 hours. [S1]
- **Recreational drugs are common co-ingestants.** ⚠️ **Every intentional adolescent self-poisoning needs a paracetamol screen and admission.** [S1]

## Examine and investigate

- **Look for CNS depression (any decrease is significant), tachycardia and hypotension.** [S1]
- **ECG at presentation and repeat at 4 hours until normal.** A long QTc is usually insignificant: no torsades de pointes has been reported and it does not correlate with toxicity. **Paracetamol level in all intentional overdoses.** [S1]

## Manage

- ⚠️ **Hypotension: 20 mL/kg normal saline bolus, repeated if still hypotensive; then a noradrenaline infusion. Adrenaline paradoxically worsens hypotension.** [S1]
- **Charcoal only for a massive ingestion within 1 hour, or once intubated.** Enhanced elimination is ineffective and **there is no antidote.** [S1]
- **Watch for urinary retention** — consider it as a cause of agitation — and place an IDC if needed. [S1]
- **Monitor 4 hours** for ingestions under 10 mg/kg and under 100 mg; **monitor 12 hours** for over 100 mg, over 10 mg/kg, or any sustained-release product. [S1]

## Transfer and discharge

- ⚠️ **Transfer any child who needs noradrenaline or intubation.** [S1]
- **Discharge when GCS and ECG are normal and the observation period is complete.** [S1]
- **Before discharging an adolescent after a deliberate ingestion**, assess self-harm risk and other drug and alcohol use. [S1]
