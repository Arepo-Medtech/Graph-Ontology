## Key points

- **Most cyanotic episodes in children with CHD are self-limiting.** [S1]
- **Let the parent comfort the child**; clinicians use a **minimal handling** approach with a targeted examination. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Prolonged episodes may be life-threatening** and need active treatment with **oxygen and opiates**; involve senior staff and specialists early. [S1]

## Background

- **A cyanotic ('tet') spell** is acute cyanosis, or cyanosis worse than the child's baseline — most often in **Tetralogy of Fallot and pulmonary atresia**. [S1]
- **Mechanism:** a pulmonary–systemic vascular resistance imbalance reduces pulmonary flow and increases right-to-left shunting. [S1]
- **Peak age 2–6 months**; usually triggered by stress — infection, being upset, or on waking. [S1]

## Assessment

- **History:** known structural heart disease, **severe cyanosis (more than usual), hyperpnoea**, precipitants (distress, prolonged crying, dehydration, intercurrent illness), lethargy or altered conscious state; older children may have a history of **squatting**. [S1]
- **Examine gently and briefly** so as not to distress the child: compare cyanosis with baseline on **oxygen saturation monitoring**, assess hydration and perfusion, auscultate for a murmur if able. [S1]

## Initial management

- ⚠️ **Calm the child — distress can worsen or prolong the spell.** Keep handling minimal and the child with the parent as much as possible. [S1]
- **Place the child in the knee-to-chest position** (lying supine or held upright) to increase systemic vascular resistance. [S1]

## After the episode

- **Investigate after the spell has resolved**, guided by likely precipitants; an **ECG helps exclude an arrhythmia**. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Monitor until the spells have resolved and the child is not sedated from opiates.** Discuss ongoing observation and management with Cardiology. [S1]
- **Consult the local paediatric team** for any spell needing treatment; **consider transfer** if ICU admission or Cardiology review is needed. [S1]
- **Consider discharge when** the child is back to baseline, not sedated, and Cardiology has been consulted. [S1]
