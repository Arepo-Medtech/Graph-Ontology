# Serotonin toxicity in children

## Key points

- ⚠️ **Severe serotonin toxicity is a medical emergency**; untreated it can progress to multi-organ failure and death. **Severe cases belong in a paediatric ICU.** [S1]
- **A triad of CNS, autonomic and neuromuscular dysfunction**, on a spectrum from mild to life-threatening. Seek senior advice and discuss with a toxicologist if suspected. [S1]
- **Settings:** interaction of two serotonergic drugs (**most common**) · starting or increasing one · switching without adequate washout · interaction with an illicit drug or herbal · deliberate self-poisoning · accidental ingestion. [S1]

## Who needs assessment

- **Assess:** any symptomatic patient · all deliberate self-poisoning or significant accidental ingestion of serotonergic agents. ⚠️ **Co-ingesting several serotonergic agents carries significantly greater risk.** [S1]
- **Antidepressants implicated:** MAOIs, TCAs, SSRIs, SNRIs, lithium, bupropion. [S1]
- ⚠️ **High-risk analgesics/antitussives: tramadol, pethidine, dextromethorphan**; also methadone, tapentadol, fentanyl. [S1]
- **Also implicated:** MDMA, LSD, amphetamines, cocaine · ondansetron, granisetron, metoclopramide · sumatriptan · St John's wort, ginseng, nutmeg. [S1]

## Diagnosis

- **Diagnosis rests on ingestion of one or more serotonergic agents plus characteristic signs.** Clarify recent prescriptions, illicit drugs and herbals, amounts and timing. [S1]
- **Exclude mimics:** neuroleptic malignant syndrome · withdrawal syndromes · malignant hyperthermia · non-convulsive seizures · CNS infection. [S1]

## Investigations

- **Deliberate self-poisoning: 12-lead ECG, blood glucose and paracetamol level.** Depending on severity: urea, creatinine, electrolytes, **creatine kinase**, troponin. [S1]

## Treatment

- **Stop any causative agent.** Supportive care, monitoring **temperature, muscle tone and mental status.** [S1]
- **First line: titrated IV benzodiazepines**, with a toxicologist. [S1]
- **Refractory symptoms:** consider a serotonin antagonist — **cyproheptadine or chlorpromazine** — with a toxicologist. [S1]
- **Cyproheptadine, oral: under 7 years 2 mg; 7 years and over 4 mg.** For prolonged symptoms repeat the same dose 3 times a day until resolved — usually within 24 hours. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Chlorpromazine 0.5 mg/kg (max 25 mg) IV in sodium chloride 0.9% 10 mL/kg (max 500 mL) over 30–60 minutes**; repeat every 6–8 hours until resolved, **provided the patient is not hypotensive.** [S1]

## Disposition

- **Symptomatic child: admit for monitoring and treatment.** ⚠️ **At-risk children (deliberate self-poisoning or significant accidental ingestion): observe for at least 12 hours.** [S1]
- **Consider admission for any child or adolescent with intentional overdose.** **Severe toxicity → PICU.** [S1]
- **Discharge:** normal GCS · normal ECG · observation period complete. **Expect full recovery in 24–48 hours.** [S1]
- **Before discharge, adolescents with deliberate ingestion need a self-harm risk assessment and drug and alcohol assessment**, with linkage to services where needs are identified. [S1]
