# Chronic myeloid leukaemia in adults

## Diagnosis and risk

- **At diagnosis:** marrow morphology and chromosome banding analysis, plus RT-PCR on blood or marrow to stage the disease, find additional chromosomal abnormalities and identify the transcript type for later monitoring. Do not test for BCR::ABL1 kinase mutations at diagnosis in chronic phase; use targeted NGS for patients presenting in or progressing to blast phase. [S1]
- **Use the ELTS score** (it replaces Sokal, Euro and EUTOS) and treat high-risk additional chromosomal abnormalities as a warning sign in chronic phase. WHO 2022 dropped accelerated phase and defines blast phase as >20% blasts; the ELN panel could not fully endorse either the WHO or the older classification. [S1]

## Monitoring and milestones

- **Monitoring schedule:** blood counts every 2 weeks until complete haematologic response; BCR::ABL1 at least every 3 months until MMR is reached and confirmed, then every 4–6 months while in stable MMR or deeper, and more often if levels fluctuate or rise or around TKI discontinuation. [S1]
- **Report on the International Scale** (BCR::ABL1IS). Deep molecular response means MR4 or deeper; tests should be able to detect MR4.5 routinely. Atypical fusions (2% of patients) need personalised RT-qPCR or RT-ddPCR. [S1]
- **Milestones at 3, 6 and 12 months judge TKI efficacy** (not side effects) and are now labelled favourable, warning and unfavourable. Never switch on a single result: read the trend. A rise in RT-qPCR with no obvious explanation (such as missed doses) should trigger chromosome banding, kinase-domain mutation analysis and a TKI switch. [S1]
- **BCR::ABL1IS >10% at 3 months (confirmed at least 4 weeks later) stays 'unfavourable',** but check response kinetics, comorbidities, dose changes and adherence before switching. [S1]
- **Unfavourable milestones in older or comorbid patients** may justify a more conservative approach, but for fit patients (including many over 60) and those pursuing treatment-free remission, mutation analysis and a TKI switch remain the recommendation. [S1]

## Resistance and mutation testing

- **About 15–20% respond unfavourably to first-line TKI** (up to 50% in later lines). Always consider poor adherence first. [S1]
- **Test for BCR::ABL1 mutations** on resistance or a 'warning' response, on progression to or presentation in blast phase, and on relapse after allograft if a mutation was present before; **not** after loss of MMR following a TFR attempt. cDNA-based NGS is preferred; Sanger sequencing is acceptable where NGS is unavailable. [S1]

## First-line treatment

- **Approved first-line TKIs:** imatinib; the second-generation dasatinib, nilotinib and bosutinib; asciminib (some countries); radotinib (South Korea only). Second-generation TKIs give faster, deeper responses and less transformation but no trial has shown an overall survival benefit over imatinib. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Asciminib 80 mg daily first line** (ASC4FIRST, 405 patients) gave higher MMR at week 48 than investigator-chosen TKI (67.7% v 49%) with fewer discontinuations; survival data are pending. It is a useful first-line choice where available and affordable. [S1]
- **Labelled starting doses:** imatinib 400 mg daily, dasatinib 100 mg daily, bosutinib 400 mg daily, nilotinib 300 mg twice daily. Dasatinib 50 mg daily looks promising but is not formally recommended. [S1]
- **Bosutinib step-up** to limit early diarrhoea and dropout: 100–200 mg daily for 1–2 weeks, 300 mg daily for 1–2 months, then 400 mg daily as tolerance and response allow. [S1]
- **Choosing the TKI:** a second-generation TKI is reasonable in younger patients seeking TFR and in high ELTS risk. Avoid dasatinib with lung disease; consider avoiding bosutinib with gastrointestinal, renal or liver problems; nilotinib is inadvisable with diabetes, high cardiovascular risk (including past vaso-occlusive events) or pancreatitis. Transcript type should not drive the choice, and interferon should not be added routinely. [S1]

## Second line and beyond

- **Side effects in responding patients (at least MMR): reduce the dose, then monitor closely.** Imatinib 400 mg to 100–300 mg daily; dasatinib 100 mg to 20–50 mg daily; nilotinib 300 mg twice daily to 150–200 mg twice or once daily. Switch TKI instead for recurrent pleural effusion, pulmonary hypertension, venous or arterial occlusive events, enterocolitis, serious neurological disease or immune-mediated myocarditis, hepatitis or nephritis. [S1]
- **After imatinib,** any suitable second-generation TKI, guided by mutations and comorbidity. **After resistance to a first-line second-generation TKI,** another one rarely works: move early to ponatinib or asciminib. Asciminib beat bosutinib after at least 2 prior TKIs (ASCEMBL MMR at 96 weeks 37.6 v 15.8%). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Ponatinib dosing (OPTIC):** start at 45 mg and reduce to 15 mg once BCR::ABL1IS falls below 1%; 45 mg is the preferred start with a BCR::ABL1 mutation (especially T315I) or BCR::ABL1IS >10%. With T315I, asciminib has been used at 150–200 mg twice daily. [S1]

## Advanced phase and transplant

- ⚠️ **Blast phase:** intensive combination chemotherapy with a TKI (ideally dasatinib or ponatinib), then allograft; check for CNS involvement in lymphoid blast phase. Aim to regain chronic phase before transplant; transplanting resistant blast phase outside a study is not recommended. [S1]
- **Plan transplant in chronic phase:** start a donor search at resistance to a second-generation TKI and refer at resistance to ponatinib and/or asciminib. Use myeloablative conditioning where possible; after allograft, monitor BCR::ABL1 at least 3-monthly early on and lifelong. [S1]

## Treatment-free remission (TFR)

- **TFR is safe only with high-quality molecular monitoring and careful selection.** Trials mostly required at least 3 years of TKI and sustained deep molecular response for at least 1 year; about 40–50% stay off treatment. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Restart the TKI on loss of MMR,** without waiting for confirmation; over 80% of losses happen in the first 6–8 months, so monitor often then. About 90–95% regain their prior deep response. Late loss (up to 14% after 2 years) means monitoring must continue long term. A withdrawal syndrome (muscle and joint pain) affects 20–30%. [S1]

## Parenting

- **Men should continue their TKI while trying to conceive.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Women: in general, no TKI during pregnancy or breastfeeding;** interferon (including pegylated) is safe for count control. Women in chronic phase with regular cycles may continue the TKI until the first positive pregnancy test (about 4 weeks' gestation). Avoid dasatinib throughout pregnancy. Obstetric collaboration is essential. [S1]
