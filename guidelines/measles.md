> ⚠️ **"There is no specific therapy for an acute measles infection"** [S1]. Prevention and post-exposure
> prophylaxis are the whole of management.

---

## ⚠️ Notify immediately

**Measles is notifiable in every state and territory and is an urgent public health priority** [S1].

## Schedule

| who | what |
|---|---|
| **all children ≥12 months** | **2 doses — MMR at 12 months, MMRV at 18 months** [S1] |
| ⚠️ **adolescents and adults born during or since 1966** | **documented 2 doses ≥4 weeks apart, both given at ≥12 months — or serological immunity** [S1] |

**Especially:** healthcare workers · childhood educators and carers · long-term care and correctional
facility staff · travellers [S1].

⚠️ **Not before 12 months, routinely** — maternal antibodies persist in many infants through the first year
and can interfere with active immunisation [S1].

## ⚠️ Post-exposure prophylaxis

> **Non-immune people exposed to measles should receive MMR — ideally within 72 hours** [S1].
> The Handbook's outer window for post-exposure prophylaxis is **within 6 days (144 hours) of first
> exposure** [S1].

- A child over 12 months who has had 1 dose **can have the 2nd dose early** if exposed or at risk,
  **provided at least 4 weeks have passed since the 1st** [S1].
- ⚠️ **MMRV is not routinely recommended as the 1st dose in children under 4** [S1].

## Recognition

- **Incubation usually 10–14 days**; spread by respiratory secretions **including aerosols** [S1].
- ⚠️ **Infectious from the start of the prodrome — typically 2–4 days before the rash — until up to 4 days
  after it appears** [S1]. **People are contagious before anyone suspects measles.**
- **Prodrome 2–4 days:** fever, malaise, **cough, coryza, conjunctivitis**. Then a **maculopapular rash
  starting on the face and upper neck**, becoming generalised [S1].

## Complications

**About 10% of cases** — otitis media ~9%, diarrhoea ~8%, pneumonia ~6% [S1].

> ⚠️ **Acute encephalitis in 1 per 1000 cases, with 10–15% mortality — and a high proportion of survivors
> have permanent brain damage.** **Subacute sclerosing panencephalitis** follows **on average 7 years**
> later [S1].

More common and more severe in **the chronically ill, children under 5, and adults**. **In pregnancy,
measles can cause miscarriage and premature delivery**, but is **not associated with congenital
malformation** [S1].

## Safety

- ⚠️ **Contraindicated in pregnancy.** **Avoid pregnancy for 28 days after vaccination** [S1].
- ✅ **No risk to pregnant women from contact with recently vaccinated people — the vaccine virus is not
  transmitted.** Breastfeeding women **can** receive MMR [S1].
- ⚠️ **Contraindicated in the severely immunocompromised** — MMR contains **live attenuated viruses** [S1].
- **Anaphylaxis** to a previous dose or any component [S1].
- ⚠️ **No immunoglobulin-containing products for 3 weeks after vaccination** — except **anti-D, which can
  be given at the same time at a different site, or any time before or after** [S1].
