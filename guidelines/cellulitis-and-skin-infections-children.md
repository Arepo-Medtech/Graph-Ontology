# Cellulitis and other bacterial skin infections in children

## Recognise it

- **Cellulitis spreads through skin into subcutaneous tissue; many conditions mimic it** — always consider the differential. [S1]
- **Itch without tenderness makes cellulitis unlikely**; allergic reactions and contact dermatitis are often misdiagnosed as cellulitis. [S1]
- **Large local insect-bite reactions are a common mimic**: a punctum, prominent itch, redness and induration but rarely pain. [S1]
- **Usual organisms: group A streptococcus and *S. aureus*.** [S1]
- **Grade severity.** Mild: no systemic features or significant comorbidity. Moderate: moderate swelling and tenderness with systemic features (e.g. fever, tachycardia). Severe: severe swelling or tenderness, a large area (bigger than the patient's handprint), or marked systemic features — hypotension is a late sign. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Necrotising fasciitis:** pain out of keeping with apparent severity, rapid progression, marked systemic features (high fever with rigors, tachycardia, tachypnoea, hypotension, confusion, vomiting). Mortality is about 25%; recent varicella is a risk factor. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Red flags:** abscess or suppuration · animal or human bite · deep structure involvement · foreign body · immunosuppression · lymphangitis · MRSA · multiple comorbidities · periorbital, facial or hand involvement · varicella-associated infection. [S1]
- **Impetigo** is a highly contagious epidermal infection (GAS, *S. aureus*), common in young children and may accompany scabies. **SSSS** is a toxin-mediated blistering disorder of neonates and young children. [S1]

## Investigate

- **Swab for Gram stain and culture only if there is discharge. Blood culture is not useful in mild or moderate cellulitis.** Consider ultrasound for suspected abscess, deep infection or foreign body. [S1]

## Treat

- **Treat sepsis if present, and control the source** — remove foreign bodies, drain abscesses. [S1]
- **Cellulitis often looks worse after 24 hours of treatment; consider waiting 48 hours before changing therapy.** Follow local susceptibility guidance. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Young unvaccinated children are at risk of Hib.** [S1]
- **Impetigo:** topical mupirocin 2% to crusted areas three times daily, **or** cefalexin 20 mg/kg (max 750 mg) orally three times daily if widespread or large lesions — **5 days.** [S1]
- **Mild cellulitis: cefalexin 20 mg/kg (max 750 mg) orally three times daily for 5 days.** [S1]
- **Moderate cellulitis:** a trial of oral cefalexin 20 mg/kg (max 750 mg) three times daily with close review, or ceftriaxone 50 mg/kg (max 2 g) IV daily via ambulatory care/HITH — **5–10 days.** If oral is not tolerated or there is no improvement after 48 hours, manage as severe. [S1]
- **Severe cellulitis or SSSS: cefazolin 50 mg/kg (max 2 g) IV 8-hourly**, adding clindamycin 10 mg/kg (max 600 mg) IV 6-hourly if rapidly progressive — **5–10 days.** Consider early HITH once stable; switch to oral as for mild cellulitis when improving. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Necrotising fasciitis: vancomycin and meropenem 20 mg/kg IV (max 1 g) 8-hourly, AND clindamycin 10 mg/kg (max 600 mg) IV 6-hourly.** **Urgent surgical referral for debridement**; seek specialist antibiotic advice; consider IVIg. [S1]
- **Uninfected mammalian bites often need no prophylaxis.** When indicated: amoxicillin/clavulanate (7:1 liquid, 80 mg/mL amoxicillin) 22.5 mg/kg (max 875 mg) orally twice daily for **5 days.** [S1]
- **Bite prophylaxis is indicated for:** presentation delayed >8 hours · puncture wound that cannot be adequately debrided · bites on hands, feet or face · deep tissue involvement (bone, joint, tendon) · open fracture · immunocompromise · cat bites. [S1]
- **Infected animal or human bite:** amoxicillin/clavulanate 22.5 mg/kg (max 875 mg) orally twice daily, or 25 mg/kg (max 1 g) IV 6–8-hourly if oral is not tolerated — **5 days**, longer if severe, penetrating or deep. Seek specialist advice. [S1]
- **Waterborne (sea or fresh water) infection:** cefalexin 20 mg/kg (max 750 mg) three times daily **plus** ciprofloxacin 10 mg/kg (max 500 mg) twice daily, **or** trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole 8/40 mg/kg (max 320/1600 mg) twice daily, all oral — **5–10 days.** Clean and debride as needed; prophylaxis is not recommended. [S1]

## Suspected MRSA

- **MRSA risk factors:** living in a high-prevalence area (e.g. Northern Territory, remote northern Queensland) · previous MRSA colonisation or infection · Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander or Pacific Islander child. [S1]
- **Mild cellulitis, MRSA suspected:** trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole 4/20 mg/kg (max 160/800 mg) twice daily **or** clindamycin 10 mg/kg (max 450 mg) four times daily, oral — **5 days.** [S1]
- **Moderate:** oral trial with close review, or vancomycin IV. **Severe or SSSS:** vancomycin IV **or** clindamycin 10 mg/kg (max 600 mg) IV 6-hourly. Switch to oral as for mild when improving. [S1]

## Disposition and follow-up

- **Discuss with the paediatric team** if there is no improvement or deterioration after 24–48 hours; **get a surgical opinion** if deep abscess or necrotising fasciitis is suspected. [S1]
- **Discharge when oral antibiotics are tolerated.** [S1]
