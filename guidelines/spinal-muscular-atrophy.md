# Spinal muscular atrophy — newborn screening, diagnosis and treatment initiation

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** 5q (SMN1-related) spinal muscular atrophy found by newborn screening: the screening analyte, SMN2 copy number, diagnostic confirmation and timelines, uncertain or discordant results, disclosure to families, the first clinical assessment, and the decision to start SMN-augmenting treatment (nusinersen, risdiplam, onasemnogene abeparvovec) or to follow up. Long-term management after the initial post-diagnostic period, later-onset (type IIIb/IIIc) SMA and non-5q SMA are outside the source's scope. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 23 claims quoted verbatim from **University of New South Wales (Kariyawasam D and the Guideline Development Group); recommendations approved by the NHMRC CEO on 22 April 2025 — *Recommendations for Newborn Screening in Spinal Muscular Atrophy in Australia and Aotearoa New Zealand*** (April 2025; origin: AU); **30 fragments re-checked by machine; 0 doses.** Licence: *CC BY 4.0*.

> ℹ️ **NHMRC-approved Australian guideline, focused on the screening pathway.** It does not recommend medicines or services that are unavailable or restricted in Australia or Aotearoa New Zealand, so treatment advice is limited to starting SMN-augmenting therapy.

## Screening

- **Screen on the routine newborn dried blood spot, targeting absence of SMN1 exon 7.** (Evidence-based; strong) [S1]
- **The screening method should have a sensitivity of at least 95% for 0 SMN1 copies.** (Consensus) [S1]
- **Report a screen-positive SMN1 result to clinical services as soon as it is available, without waiting for SMN2 copy number.** (Consensus) [S1]
- **Measure SMN2 copy number quickly, ideally within the screening process, but never let it delay notification of absent SMN1 exon 7.** (Consensus) [S1]
- **SMN2 copy number is the main predictor of severity:** more copies generally mean a milder, later-onset phenotype. It is a guide, not absolute, because genotype and phenotype can be discordant. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Infants with 2 SMN2 copies are at highest risk of early onset:** up to 47% of screen-positive newborns with 2 copies show signs of SMA within the first month. [S1]
- **Screening programs should notify a screen-positive result to a paediatric neurologist and a local practitioner at the same time.** (Consensus) [S1]

## Confirming the diagnosis

- **Diagnosis requires confirmed absence of SMN1 exon 7 (zero SMN1 copies).** (Evidence-based; strong) [S1]
- **Diagnostic testing (whole blood, or a repeat dried blood spot from the recalled infant) should include SMN2 copy number** to predict severity and guide treatment decisions. (Consensus) [S1]
- ⚠️ **Timelines:** diagnostic SMN1 results within 7 days at most of the lab receiving the sample; the full diagnosis (SMN1 and SMN2) within 30 days of birth, so treatment is not delayed. (Consensus) [S1]
- **At the diagnostic visit, do immediately:** a neurological examination, venous blood for SMN1 exon 7, and venous blood (or a repeat dried blood spot) for SMN2 copy number. (Consensus) [S1]
- **False positive, false negative or uncertain results:** hold a joint case review across screening, diagnostic and clinical services and explain the findings to the family; if screening and diagnostic copy numbers differ, consider retesting by another method or laboratory. (Consensus) [S1]
- **If the diagnosis stays uncertain, a paediatric neurologist should follow the child until it is certain.** (Consensus) [S1]

## Telling the family

- ⚠️ **Disclose a screen-positive result to the family within 2 working days of notification, and offer paediatric neurology or neuromuscular review within 2 working days of disclosure.** (Consensus) [S1]
- **A paediatric neurologist should disclose the diagnosis once SMN1 confirmation arrives, even if the SMN2 result is not yet back.** (Consensus) [S1]
- **Provide culturally safe care to Aboriginal, Torres Strait Islander, Pacific Islander, Māori and CALD families,** using a professional interpreter where the practitioner is not bilingual and Indigenous Health Liaison support where appropriate; direct families to reliable, nationally consistent resources. (Consensus) [S1]

## Starting treatment

- **Available SMN-augmenting treatments:** nusinersen and risdiplam (SMN2 splice modifiers) and onasemnogene abeparvovec (SMN1 gene transfer by viral vector). They support surviving motor units but cannot restore ones already irreversibly lost. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Symptomatic screen-positive newborns:** a paediatric neurologist should discuss immediate SMN-augmenting treatment with the family. (Consensus) [S1]
- **Presymptomatic newborns with 1, 2 or 3 SMN2 copies:** a paediatric neurologist should discuss immediate SMN-augmenting treatment with the family. (Consensus) [S1]
- **Start with a single agent (monotherapy), in a paediatric neurology treatment centre,** since there are no comparative data. (Consensus) [S1]
- **4 or more SMN2 copies is the grey zone:** international advice ranges from immediate treatment to watching for symptoms, and access to treatment varies between countries. [S1]

## Follow-up without treatment, and genetics

- **Untreated infants** (no access to approved and reimbursed treatment, or the family chooses to wait) need review at least every 3 months for the first two years after diagnosis, then at least every 6 months. (Consensus) [S1]
- **Offer the family genetic counselling and cascade testing,** which may include referral to clinical genetics. (Consensus) [S1]
