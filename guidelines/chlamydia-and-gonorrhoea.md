# Chlamydia and gonorrhoea

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-21 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** uncomplicated genital, anorectal and pharyngeal infection in adults. Treated together because
they are co-tested, frequently co-infecting, and their regimens interlock.

> **Australian primary source throughout.** ASHM *Australian STI Management Guidelines for use in primary
> care* [S1][S2], last modified **30 June 2026**. Supported by RACGP, RACP, ASHHNA, SRHA and others.
>
> **This is the first guideline in this set that states doses.** Every previous one listed dosing as
> unresolved because the source gave agents and durations only. This source gives complete Australian
> regimens, so they are reproduced here.

## Why they are managed together

Concurrent gonorrhoea testing should accompany chlamydia testing [S1]. Rectal co-infection has a combined
regimen [S1][S2]. **But their contact-tracing windows differ — 6 months for chlamydia, 2 months for
gonorrhoea** [S1][S2], which is easy to conflate when managing a co-infected patient.

## Chlamydia

**The most reported communicable disease in Australia**; those under 30 at greatest risk; **85–90% have no
symptoms**; previous infection confers no immunity [S1].

**Testing.** NAATs are **the only recommended test** [S1]. Best test if examined: clinician-collected
endocervical swab. Best if not examined: **self-collected vaginal swab** — more sensitive than first-pass
urine. Anorectal and pharyngeal swabs for all men who have sex with men; anorectal for anyone with
anorectal symptoms. Asymptomatic patients can collect most samples themselves [S1].

### Treatment

| Infection | Recommended | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Uncomplicated genital or pharyngeal | **Doxycycline 100 mg PO BD, 7 days** | Azithromycin 1 g PO stat — consider where adherence to daily treatment likely to be poor, especially where anorectal infection is less likely |
| **Anorectal** | **Doxycycline 100 mg PO BD — 7 days if asymptomatic, 21 days if symptomatic** | Azithromycin 1 g PO stat **and repeat in 12–24 hours** |
| **Pregnancy** | **Azithromycin 1 g PO stat** | — |
| Rectal co-infection with gonorrhoea | **Ceftriaxone 500 mg IMI stat in 2 mL 1% lignocaine PLUS doxycycline** as above | — |

**Antibiotic stewardship point:** *immediate treatment is **not** recommended for all sexual contacts of
chlamydia* — offer testing of exposed anatomical sites and await results [S1]. That is a deliberate
departure from presumptive treatment.

## Gonorrhoea

Most commonly diagnosed in men who have sex with men, young heterosexual Aboriginal and Torres Strait
Islander people in remote and very remote areas, and travellers returning from high-prevalence areas;
**increasing prevalence in the general population, especially women of reproductive age** [S2].

**Symptom pattern differs by site:** anal and pharyngeal infections asymptomatic in most people; vaginal
often asymptomatic (**80%**); **penile urethral almost always symptomatic (85–90%)** [S2].

### Culture before treatment — always

> **ALWAYS collect samples for gonococcal culture before treating**, to determine antimicrobial sensitivity
> and contribute to resistance surveillance. **However, do not delay treatment to wait for results** [S2].

**Clinicians must specifically request "gonococcal culture" rather than general "culture"**, as gonococci
require specific conditions; samples should reach the laboratory **within 24 hours** [S2].

### Treatment

| Situation | Recommended |
|---|---|
| Uncomplicated genital and anorectal | **Ceftriaxone 500 mg IMI stat in 2 mL 1% lignocaine PLUS azithromycin 1 g PO stat** |
| **Pharyngeal** | **Ceftriaxone 500 mg IMI stat in 2 mL 1% lignocaine PLUS azithromycin 2 g PO stat** — note the **doubled** azithromycin |
| Adult gonococcal conjunctivitis | **Ceftriaxone 1 g IMI stat in 2 mL 1% lignocaine PLUS azithromycin 1 g PO stat** |
| Regional/remote where penicillin-susceptible (confirm with local public health unit) | Amoxicillin 3 g PO stat PLUS probenecid 1 g PO stat PLUS azithromycin 1 g PO stat (when chlamydia not excluded) |
| Pregnancy | same as principal option |
| Allergy | seek specialist advice |

**Alternative treatments are not recommended because of high levels of resistance**, except in some remote
Australian locations and for severe allergic reactions [S2].

### Resistance — the reasoning behind dual therapy

- **Reduced susceptibility to first-line ceftriaxone and azithromycin is emerging in urban Australia** and
  is being monitored [S2].
- **The pharynx is the most likely site of treatment failure** — sharing of resistance genetic material
  between bacteria, and reduced drug penetration to pharyngeal mucosa [S2]. Hence the doubled azithromycin.
- **Dual antibiotic treatment is recommended to create a pharmacological barrier to the development of more
  widespread resistance** [S2]. The second agent is a resistance strategy, not extra coverage.
- **Ceftriaxone monotherapy should not be used outside specialist centres** monitoring culture-based
  susceptibility [S2].
- **Ciprofloxacin 500 mg PO stat can be used only if susceptibility has been confirmed** on NAAT or
  culture — and this should not delay treatment [S2].

## Side by side — the numbers that differ

| | Chlamydia | Gonorrhoea |
|---|---|---|
| Contact tracing back | **6 months** [S1] | **2 months** [S2] |
| No sex with partners from | last **6 months** until tested/treated [S1] | last **2 months** until tested/treated [S2] |
| No sexual contact after treatment | **7 days** (or until course complete and symptoms resolved, whichever later) [S1] | **7 days** (same) [S2] |
| Test of cure | **not routine** — only pregnancy, and anorectal treated with azithromycin; **no earlier than 4 weeks** [S1] | **yes, each site**, by NAAT **2 weeks** after treatment [S2] |
| Retest for re-infection | **3 months** [S1] | **3 months** [S2] |
| Presumptive contact treatment | **not recommended** routinely [S1] | consider if contact within past 2 weeks [S2] |

Both are **notifiable to the state or territory health department** [S1][S2].

**Consider HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis for anyone diagnosed with gonorrhoea** [S2].

## Auditable outcomes

The source states these as measurable standards [S1][S2]:

- 100% of patients diagnosed with **chlamydia** treated with an appropriate antibiotic regimen
- 100% of patients diagnosed with **gonorrhoea** treated with an appropriate antibiotic regimen
- 100% advised to avoid sexual contact for 7 days after treatment
- 100% recalled for repeat testing after 3 months
