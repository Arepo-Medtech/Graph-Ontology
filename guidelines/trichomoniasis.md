# Trichomoniasis

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-21 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults, primary care.

> **Australian primary source.** ASHM *Australian STI Management Guidelines for use in primary care* —
> *Trichomoniasis* [S1], modified 30 June 2026.

## Geography decides whether you test

This is the only infection in the set where **where the patient lives** changes the recommendation.

> In Australia, trichomonas is **more common in older people, people from regional and remote areas,
> especially Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people, and street-based sex workers** [S1].
> It is an **uncommon cause of vaginal discharge or penile urethritis in urban settings** [S1].

Three consequences the source draws from that distribution [S1]:

- **Testing is not routinely recommended for people with urethritis.**
- **Screening is not recommended except in high prevalence populations** — refer to local guidelines.
- **Treat contacts presumptively.**

The fourth recommendation *against* in the set, and the only one qualified by population rather than by
evidence. In an urban clinic, testing an asymptomatic person is unlikely to be useful; in a remote
community it may be routine, and the source defers to local guidelines rather than setting one rule.

## The natural history is asymmetric

> **Long natural history (years) if not treated.** Without treatment, **the infection is cleared more
> quickly in a penis than in a vagina** [S1].

That single sentence drives three separate pieces of advice on the page:

1. **Up to 50% of vaginal infections are asymptomatic**; urethral infections are **usually** asymptomatic [S1].
2. The auditable outcome names only vaginal infections: **100% of vaginal infections diagnosed are treated
   with the recommended principal treatment option** [S1].
3. **Partners with a penis may test negative**, because it is more likely to resolve spontaneously in these
   people [S1] — so a negative partner test does not settle the question, and is a reason to treat
   presumptively rather than await a result.

## Diagnosis

| Site/specimen | Test | Note [S1] |
|---|---|---|
| **High vaginal swab** | NAAT | **Preferred option in people with a vagina.** Ideally clinician-collected if symptomatic, but can be self-collected if the client declines examination |
| **First pass urine** | NAAT | Available in major laboratories in each state |

## Treatment

| Situation | Recommended [S1] | Alternative [S1] |
|---|---|---|
| **Uncomplicated infection** | **Metronidazole 400 mg PO with food, BD for 7 days** | Metronidazole 2 g PO with food, stat |

**Avoid alcohol during metronidazole treatment and for 24 hours afterwards** [S1].

**Advise no sexual contact for 7 days after treatment is commenced, or until the course is completed and
symptoms have resolved — whichever is later** [S1].

**Recommend that the current sexual partner is treated** [S1].

**Trichomoniasis is only a notifiable condition in the Northern Territory** [S1] — the same geographic
pattern, this time in the law.

**In persistent or recurrent infection, rule out re-infection and poor adherence** before anything else;
**seek specialist advice if metronidazole resistance is suspected or metronidazole is contraindicated** [S1].

## Special situations

| Situation | Recommended [S1] |
|---|---|
| **Pregnancy** | Metronidazole is **category B2** in pregnancy |
| **Breastfeeding** | Metronidazole **may affect the taste of breast milk**; **avoid high doses** while breastfeeding |
| **Allergy to the principal treatment** | **There is no effective alternative to 5-nitroimidazole compounds.** Metronidazole desensitisation has been described |
| **People living with HIV** | Reports indicate **single-dose metronidazole is less effective than extended metronidazole** |

The allergy row is worth pausing on: unlike every other infection in the set, there is **no second drug
class**. An allergy here means desensitisation, not substitution.

The breastfeeding advice is the only entry in eighteen guidelines that turns on **taste**. It matters
because a refused feed is the actual clinical consequence.

The HIV row is why the 7-day course is the recommended option and the 2 g stat dose the alternative,
despite the stat dose being easier to give.

## Complications

Associated with **prostatitis**; with **premature rupture of membranes, pre-term delivery and low birth
weight**; and with **post-partum sepsis** [S1]. **May enhance HIV transmission** [S1].

## Contact tracing and follow-up

**There is currently insufficient data to provide a definitive period for contact tracing** — focus on
**current and recent partners**, and test from genital sites [S1].

**Consider presumptive treatment** if there has been sexual contact within the past **2 weeks**, or when the
person's individual circumstances mean **later treatment may not occur** [S1]. That second clause is a
recognition that for some patients there is no second appointment.

**Review in 1 week** to confirm adherence and symptom resolution, confirm or support contact tracing, and
educate about condoms, contraception, HIV PrEP/PEP, safe injecting, consent, cervical screening and
HAV/HBV/HPV vaccination [S1].

**Test of cure: not recommended unless symptoms persist** [S1].

**Retesting at 4 weeks** for patients who remain symptomatic or where **partner treatment remains
uncertain** — which also allows retesting for other STIs past the window period [S1].
