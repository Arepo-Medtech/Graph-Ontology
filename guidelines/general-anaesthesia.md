# General anaesthesia — the parts a non-anaesthetist acts on

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-22 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** ⚠️ **GENERAL ANAESTHESIA IS NOT A GENERAL-PRACTICE PROCEDURE AND THIS GUIDELINE DOES NOT PRETEND
OTHERWISE.** It is scoped to the parts of AMH's page a **referring or shared-care doctor** acts on:
**which regular medicines to continue, the fasting intervals, and aspiration prophylaxis.**
⚠️ **NO INDUCTION, MAINTENANCE, NEUROMUSCULAR BLOCKER OR REVERSAL AGENT IS NAMED OR DOSED.**

> ⚠️ **ENTIRELY LICENSED AND UNQUOTED.** All 15 claims come from **one AMH page**, paraphrased.

## The five stages [G1]

| Stage | [G1] |
|---|---|
| **Preoperative assessment** ± premedication | **to make the process as safe and smooth as possible** |
| **Induction** | **render the patient unconscious and unreactive to surgical stimuli — IV or inhaled drugs** |
| **Maintenance** | **IV and/or inhaled drugs, with muscle relaxants and analgesics as necessary** |
| **Reversal** | **drugs for reversing neuromuscular blockade are usually required IF NON-DEPOLARISING blockers were used** |
| **Recovery** | **begins when the anaesthetic stops; lasts until the patient is fully conscious, cardiovascularly stable, maintaining their own airway and comfortable** |

## ⚠️ Regular medications: the default is CONTINUE

> ⚠️ **"MOST MEDICATIONS CAN AND SHOULD BE CONTINUED THROUGHOUT THE PERIOPERATIVE PERIOD, PARTICULARLY
> ANTIANGINAL, ANTIARRHYTHMIC, ANTIHYPERTENSIVE, ACID SUPPRESSION, ANTISEIZURE, BRONCHODILATOR AND
> CORTICOSTEROID TREATMENT"** [G1].

**This is the same instruction several other guidelines in this compendium give from the drug's own side:**
`angina` (never stop a beta-blocker abruptly), `hypertension` (continue through the perioperative period),
`epilepsy` (do not stop antiseizure treatment abruptly), `inflammatory-bowel-disease` (continue
5-aminosalicylates perioperatively). ⚠️ **AMH says it once more here, as a general rule.**

| | [G1] |
|---|---|
| **Timing** | **oral products may be swallowed with a SMALL AMOUNT OF WATER UP TO 2 HOURS BEFORE SURGERY** |
| **If oral is not possible** | **topical, inhaled or parenteral products are generally available** |
| ⚠️ **Deciding to stop or alter** | **must be INDIVIDUALISED** — patient's condition, type of surgery, possible interaction with anaesthetic or analgesic agents. **CONSULTATION BETWEEN THE RELEVANT SPECIALISTS IS OFTEN NECESSARY** |
| **After any change** | **monitor the patient's medical condition closely** |

⚠️ **Two guidelines in this compendium carry perioperative exceptions worth reading alongside this rule:**
`bipolar-disorder` (consider interrupting lithium briefly, as fasting and fluid changes alter the level)
and `local-anaesthesia` (anticoagulated patients and neuraxial blocks).

## ⚠️ Fasting and aspiration [G1]

**Risk of aspiration increases in: OBESITY · significant GORD · HIATUS HERNIA · OBSTETRIC PATIENTS ·
EMERGENCY SURGERY WITHOUT AN ADEQUATE PERIOD OF FASTING** [G1].

| Fast from | Hours [G1] |
|---|---|
| **Food, infant milk formula, animal milk** | **6 hours** |
| **Breast milk** | **4 hours** |
| **Clear fluids** | **2–3 hours** |

> ⚠️ **THE CLOCK IS NOT THE WHOLE ANSWER.** **"Gastric emptying can be delayed by PAIN, TRAUMA, OPIOIDS,
> DISORDERS OF GASTRIC MOTILITY AND OBSTRUCTION"** [G1]. **A patient who has fasted the correct interval
> may still have a full stomach.**

### Drug prophylaxis [G1]

| | |
|---|---|
| **Already on acid suppression** | **CONTINUE it throughout the perioperative period** |
| **High risk of aspiration, if time allows** | **an H2 ANTAGONIST THE NIGHT BEFORE AND THE MORNING OF SURGERY** |
| **Other measures** | **METOCLOPRAMIDE** (antiemetic, increases gastric motility) · **30 mL of SODIUM CITRATE 0.3 MOLAR SOLUTION**, a NON-PARTICULATE antacid, just before induction |
| ⚠️ **Avoid** | ⚠️ **PARTICULATE ANTACIDS — THEY CAN CAUSE PNEUMONITIS IF ASPIRATED** |

## Premedication [G1]

**May be given before induction to:**

| Aim | Drugs [G1] |
|---|---|
| **Relieve anxiety** | **benzodiazepines** |
| **Sedation and amnesia** | **benzodiazepines, clonidine, ketamine** |
| **Relieve pain** | **opioids, paracetamol** |
| **Reduce secretions** | **glycopyrronium** |
| **Help empty the stomach** | **metoclopramide** |
| **Reduce volume and acidity of gastric contents** | **H2 antagonists** |
| **Reduce pain during venipuncture** | **a TOPICAL LOCAL ANAESTHETIC — commonly applied preoperatively, PARTICULARLY IN CHILDREN** |

> **Some of these objectives can be achieved by drugs given during induction or maintenance — and
> ⚠️ "EXPLANATION AND REASSURANCE IS OFTEN SUFFICIENT TO RELIEVE ANXIETY"** [G1].

⚠️ **The topical local anaesthetic row is the one a GP may set up.** This compendium's `local-anaesthesia`
guideline carries AMH's warning that **occlusive dressings increase absorption and systemic risk**, and
that **tetracaine has high systemic toxicity restricting it to topical use**.

## Pain relief during anaesthesia [G1]

**Can be given at any stage; type, timing and dose depend on the patient, the procedure and coadministered
drugs** [G1].

> **OPIOIDS ARE USED MOST COMMONLY.** ⚠️ **SHORT-ACTING agents AVOID DELAYED RECOVERY, while LONGER-ACTING
> agents PROVIDE POSTOPERATIVE PAIN RELIEF** — the trade-off is explicit [G1].
> **Other analgesics: NSAIDs, paracetamol, ketamine, clonidine, local anaesthetics** [G1].
