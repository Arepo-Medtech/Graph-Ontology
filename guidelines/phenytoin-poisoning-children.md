# Phenytoin poisoning in children

## Key points

- **Phenytoin intoxication is usually benign, with dose-related symptoms.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Rapid IV administration can cause cardiac arrhythmias** — from the propylene glycol in the formulation, not the phenytoin. [S1]
- **Serum levels correlate well with clinical features but cannot predict how long symptoms will last.** [S1]
- **Onset: within 1 to 2 hours orally, minutes IV.** ⚠️ **Maximal effect can be delayed up to five days after oral ingestion** (zero-order kinetics, prolonged absorption). [S1]

## Who needs assessment

- **Assess:** any deliberate self-poisoning · accidental ingestion over 20 mg/kg (or over 10 mg/kg above the usual daily dose if on phenytoin) · any symptomatic child · unknown quantity. **Consider non-accidental poisoning** if developmental age doesn't fit. [S1]
- **Examine for:** nystagmus, dysarthria, ataxia, tremor, drowsiness, involuntary movements, seizures, coma · nausea and vomiting · hypernatraemia and hyperglycaemia (massive ingestion) · ⚠️ **hypotension, bradycardia, arrhythmias (rapid IV).** [S1]

## Investigations

- **ECG (especially after IV) at presentation and repeat at 6 hours until normal.** Also BSL, and paracetamol in all intentional overdoses. [S1]
- **Levels:** 40–80 micromol/L (10–20 mg/L) therapeutic · 80–120 (20–30 mg/L) horizontal nystagmus · 120–160 (30–40 mg/L) vertical nystagmus, diplopia, ataxia, slurred speech, tremor, hyperreflexia, drowsiness, vomiting. [S1]
- ⚠️ **160–200 micromol/L (40–50 mg/L): confusion, lethargy, mania, respiratory depression. Over 200 micromol/L (50 mg/L): extreme lethargy, coma, paradoxical seizures.** [S1]

## Treatment

- **Treat:** all symptomatic children · unknown quantity · over 20 mg/kg, or over 10 mg/kg above the usual daily dose if on maintenance phenytoin. [S1]
- **Standard supportive care. Decontamination is not usually required.** [S1]

## Observation and disposition

- **Mild symptoms: observe 6 hours after exposure;** discharge once symptom-free. [S1]
- **Moderate–severe or persisting after 6 hours: admit.** Repeat the level at 6 hours if still symptomatic; if rising or above range, admit for serial levels with a toxicologist. [S1]
- **Consider admission for every intentional overdose** and any child still symptomatic after 6 hours. [S1]
- **Transfer to a tertiary centre** for severe symptoms that may need intensive care. **PIPER: 1300 137 650.** [S1]
- **Discharge when:** normal GCS, normal ECG, observation period complete, and — after deliberate ingestion — a risk assessment shows low risk of further self-harm. [S1]
- **Refer** intentional self-harm to local mental health services (e.g. Orygen Youth Health 1800 888 320) and recreational use to YoDAA (1800 458 685). [S1]
