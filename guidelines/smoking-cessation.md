# Smoking cessation

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-21 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults, primary care.

> **Australian primary source.** RACGP. *Supporting smoking cessation: A guide for health professionals*,
> 2nd edition [S1]. **Last revised 29 September 2021.** **16 recommendations**, GRADE-style: strength
> (strong / conditional) paired with certainty (high / moderate / low).

> ⚠️ **Currency — the regulatory statement is dated.** This guideline states the nicotine vaping position
> **as at 1 October 2021** (see below). **Australia's vaping regulations changed again from 1 October
> 2024** [S2], and the RACGP has since issued guidance covering smoking **and vaping** cessation. **The
> regulatory content here is transcribed as the 2021 source has it and must not be relied on as current** —
> recorded under Unresolved. The clinical recommendations are unaffected.

**Six of the sixteen are Strong on high certainty**, and **four of those six are about the system rather than
the drug** — offer advice to everyone, identify and document everyone, advise in routine consultations, and
follow everyone up. The strongest evidence in smoking cessation is that **someone raises it, and raises it
again**.

## Choosing a pharmacotherapy

**Three forms of medicine — NRT, varenicline and bupropion — are licensed and available in Australia to
assist smoking cessation** [S1].

> **Varenicline is the most effective single-form pharmacotherapy for smoking cessation.**
> **Combination NRT is as effective as varenicline and more effective than single types of NRT** [S1].

**At the standard dose, varenicline can more than double the chances of successful long-term smoking
cessation compared with pharmacologically unassisted quit attempts** [S1]. In a Cochrane review
meta-analysis, **varenicline was more effective than bupropion, more effective than NRT monotherapy, and
similar in effect to combination NRT** [S1]. **Varenicline improves smoking cessation rates two-fold over
bupropion and is well tolerated** [S1].

So the practical ranking is: **varenicline or combination NRT** at the top, equivalent to each other;
**single-agent NRT** below; **bupropion** below that; **nortriptyline second-line on adverse effects**.

### NRT

> **Smoking cessation using NRT is always less harmful than continuing to smoke** [S1].

**NRT provides lower doses of nicotine at a slower rate than tobacco smoking; none of the available forms
(transdermal patch, gum, inhalator, lozenge, mouth spray) offer the same rapid nicotine delivery of a
cigarette** [S1]. **The advantage of acute-dosing NRT is that both the amount and timing of doses can be
titrated by the person who smokes** [S1].

> **Standard dosing references and product information guides for NRT tend to recommend more conservative
> doses** [S1].

A guideline noting that the product information **under-doses** relative to its own guidance is unusual, and
it matters: under-dosed NRT is a common reason a quit attempt fails.

> **Regular use of NRT beyond 12 months is not generally recommended as there is no evidence of efficacy
> beyond 24 weeks** [S1].

**In pregnancy:** **NRT may be considered in women who are pregnant if they were unsuccessful in stopping
smoking without pharmacotherapy. If NRT is used, the benefits and risks should be explained carefully by a
suitably qualified health professional. The clinician supervising the pregnancy should also be
consulted** [S1].

### Varenicline

**A nicotinic receptor partial agonist that relieves symptoms of craving and withdrawal** [S1].

**Two equally effective quitting options, chosen by preference** [S1]:

- **Fixed option** — the person sets a date to stop smoking; **varenicline should start one to two weeks
  before this date**.
- **Flexible approach** — the person begins varenicline dosing, **then quits smoking between days 8 and 35 of
  treatment**.

> **Varenicline can be used in those who smoke with mental health problems, but must be monitored during quit
> attempts. These patients should be advised to report unusual mood changes, depression, behaviour disturbance
> and suicidal thoughts, and stop using the medicine if these occur** [S1].

**Varenicline is not recommended for pregnant and breastfeeding women, nor for adolescents** [S1].

**On a second course** [S1]: the studies that extended treatment did so for an additional 12 weeks and an
additional 40 weeks, and **the benefit appears to be maintained only for the period of use of varenicline**.

**On combining with NRT** [S1]: the Expert Advisory Group rated certainty as moderate and concluded there is
**a small but not trivial improvement**. **There is no clinical study of varenicline combined with oral NRT**;
the rationale offered is mechanistic — **varenicline helps relieve background cravings and reduce the
stimulatory effects of smoking, and oral NRT products alleviate cue-induced triggers**.

## ⚠️ Reduction without nicotine is not recommended

> **Reducing cigarette intake without a nicotine supplement is not recommended and has little proven health
> benefit. Research has found that when reducing cigarette intake, those who smoke adjust their smoking
> topography (ie number of puffs, depth of inhalation) to maintain the desired level of nicotine** [S1].

**"Just cut down" fails for a physiological reason**, and the guideline names it.

**What does work as a bridge** [S1]:

> **Those who are not willing to quit can be advised to partially substitute their cigarette intake with NRT.
> Gradually, cigarette intake can be reduced and NRT increased. The use of NRT in this way can double the odds
> of progressing to complete smoking cessation.**

**Those who use NRT for smoking reduction are approximately twice as likely to progress to quitting as those
who do not** [S1]. But: **long-term partial replacement with nicotine is not recommended as no clear health
benefit has been demonstrated** [S1].

**On reduction itself** [S1]: **a reduction in smoking by 50% may slightly reduce the risk of lung cancer in
people who were smoking 15 or more cigarettes each day; however, there is no risk-free level of exposure to
tobacco smoke.** **A decrease in the number of cigarettes smoked per day (e.g. to less than 10 per day) does
not reduce the risk** of the outcomes listed in the source.

## Nicotine vaping products

**The goal for those who smoke should always be to stop smoking altogether in order to reduce or eliminate the
harms from smoking** [S1].

**Terminology** [S1]: **nicotine vaping products (NVPs)** are products containing nicotine in salt or base
form in a solution designed to be inhaled using a vaping device — vape liquids, e-liquids, e-juices, and the
nicotine solution in nicotine e-cigarettes and pods. A **vaping device** heats them to release an inhaled
aerosol. **"Heated tobacco products" are not nicotine vaping products** [S1].

**The nicotine content of e-cigarettes can vary from zero to up to over 50 mg/mL** [S1].

> **The use of e-cigarettes is controversial as its long-term safety profile is still largely unknown** [S1].

**NVPs are not approved therapeutic products and the constituents of the vapour produced by e-cigarettes has
not been rigorously tested and standardised** [S1].

The source sets out the disagreement rather than resolving it: **the RANZCP supports the legalisation and
regulation of nicotine e-cigarettes and other vaporised nicotine products to facilitate their use as harm
reduction tools**, in recognition of **disproportionately high smoking prevalence and low quit rates among
people living with mental illness — however, other organisations oppose use of e-cigarettes for this
purpose** [S1].

**Youth uptake** [S1]: **the Australian National Drug Household Survey conducted in 2019 found that 22.3% of
people aged 15–24 years reported ever using e-cigarettes while 4.5% reported current or recent use, up from
2.3% in 2016.** **An association has been observed in young people between e-cigarette use and future
experimentation with smoking.** US data showed high-school current e-cigarette use rising from **1.5% in 2011
to 20.8% in 2018**.

**Comparisons between countries need to be made with caution**, because **many contextual factors, including
the strength and maturity of tobacco control policies, influence the prevalence of tobacco use** [S1].

### ⚠️ The regulatory statement, as at 2021

> **In Australia, a precautionary approach to the use of e-cigarettes has been taken. From 1 October 2021
> nicotine has been re-scheduled to a Schedule 4 medicine, available by prescription only. Consumers require a
> prescription for all purchases of NVPs regardless of where they are sourced from. There are currently no
> Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) approved NVPs in the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG).
> Nicotine vaping products are unapproved medicines** [S1].

**This is the 2021 position.** Australia's vaping regulations changed from **1 October 2024** [S2].
**Do not rely on the paragraph above as current law** — confirm the present scheme before advising a patient.

## Where this connects in the compendium

**Recommendation 14 makes nortriptyline second-line** here on its adverse effect profile — and the
**low back pain** standard avoids antidepressants entirely, while **major depressive disorder** uses them as
treatment. Same class, three positions, each with its own reason.

**Bupropion** appears in the **ADHD** guideline as a further option for adults at very low certainty, and
here as a **Strong · high certainty** smoking cessation agent that is nonetheless **less effective than the
alternatives**.

The **alcohol** guideline notes that **varenicline may help support reduction of both tobacco and alcohol
consumption** *(Level C)* — a single agent addressing two of this compendium's substance domains.

And **recommendation 8** ties directly to the cardiovascular cluster: **NRT is safe in stable cardiovascular
disease** (Strong, high certainty), with caution after **recent MI, unstable angina, severe arrhythmias or
recent cerebrovascular events** — conditions covered by the **heart failure**, **atrial fibrillation** and
**stroke and TIA** guidelines here.
