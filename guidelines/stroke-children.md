# Stroke in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognition, imaging, stabilisation, reperfusion (alteplase, endovascular) and early antithrombotic therapy for suspected childhood stroke. The adult counterparts are `stroke` and `stroke-and-tia`.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 18 of 18 claims quoted verbatim from the **RCH Melbourne Clinical Practice Guideline *Stroke*** (Last Updated July 2024); **24 fragments re-checked by machine. 4 quoted doses.**

> ⚠️ **Time critical: alteplase within 4.5 hours, endovascular clot retrieval within 6 hours of symptom onset.** Urgent brain imaging and early paediatric Neurology involvement are essential.

## Key points

- ⚠️ **Urgent brain imaging confirms the diagnosis and guides management; involve paediatric Neurology early.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Reperfusion windows: alteplase within 4.5 hours; endovascular clot retrieval within 6 hours** of symptom onset. [S1]
- **Childhood stroke carries 5–10% mortality**; over half of survivors have long-term impairment and 10–20% have recurrent strokes. **Arterial ischaemic stroke is the commonest subtype**, just over half. [S1]

## Recognise it

- ⚠️ **Red flags — sudden onset of any of these needs immediate neurological assessment and consideration of urgent neuroimaging:** focal limb or facial weakness · visual or speech disturbance · limb incoordination or ataxia · headache with other neurological signs · altered mental state · raised ICP · new seizures with persistent neurological signs. [S1]
- **Weakness, visual or speech disturbance and ataxia point to arterial ischaemic stroke; headache, altered mental state and raised ICP point to haemorrhage.** [S1]
- **History:** congenital heart disease, sickle cell disease, recent head/neck injury or chiropractic neck manipulation (dissection risk), methamphetamine or cocaine use. **Examine for** raised ICP, hypertension, cardiac murmur, carotid or cranial bruit. [S1]

## Imaging and bloods

- ⚠️ **Suspected haemorrhage: urgent non-contrast CT. Suspected arterial ischaemic stroke: urgent MRI and MRA** — or CT with CT angiography if MRI is not possible within 30 minutes. [S1]
- **Immediately, for reperfusion eligibility:** FBE, coagulation profile, UEC, glucose, LFTs, group and hold. **Later, for cause:** ECG, echocardiography, prothrombotic markers and serum homocysteine. [S1]

## Stabilise

- **Oxygen if hypoxic. If hypertensive, discuss with Neurology before treating.** Treat recurrent or prolonged symptomatic seizures. [S1]
- **Avoid hyperthermia — keep temperature at or below 37.5 °C.** [S1]

## Reperfusion

- **Neurology coordinates acute treatment decisions** with Emergency and Haematology, and **a Pediatric NIH Stroke Scale score must be done with Neurology before reperfusion starts.** [S1]
- **Alteplase may suit specific children. Potential eligibility:** age 2 to 17 years · radiologically confirmed arterial stroke without haemorrhage · PedNIHSS 4 or more and under 24, not improving · **under 4.5 hours from onset.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Alteplase dose: total 0.9 mg/kg (max 90 mg) IV — 0.09 mg/kg bolus, then 0.81 mg/kg over 60 minutes.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Absolute contraindications to alteplase:** known cerebral AVM, aneurysm or CNS neoplasm; known alteplase allergy. [S1]
- **Endovascular therapy may suit some children** with radiologically diagnosed ischaemic stroke — discuss with Neurology. [S1]

## Antithrombotics

- **Arterial ischaemic stroke without reperfusion, or for secondary prevention: anticoagulation and antiplatelet therapy after haemorrhage is excluded on imaging.** ⚠️ **Not within 24 hours of alteplase.** [S1]

## Escalate and admit

- ⚠️ **Urgent neurosurgical consultation for:** raised ICP · intracranial haemorrhage · malignant MCA infarction · posterior circulation infarction. [S1]
- **Involve the local paediatric team for every suspected stroke; admit every child with stroke to a primary paediatric stroke centre.** [S1]
