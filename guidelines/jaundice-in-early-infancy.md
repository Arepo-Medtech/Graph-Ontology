# Jaundice in early infancy

## Recognise it

- **Measure, don't eyeball:** if significant jaundice is suspected, do a transcutaneous bilirubinometer (TcB) and/or serum bilirubin. Visual assessment alone is unreliable. [S1]
- **Most jaundice is physiological and benign**, but ⚠️ severe unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia can cause acute bilirubin encephalopathy and kernicterus, with permanent neurological injury. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Pathological features:** onset under 24 hours · rapidly rising bilirubin · unwell appearance · prolonged jaundice · conjugated hyperbilirubinaemia · evidence of haemolysis. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Pale or acholic stools at any age need urgent evaluation** for cholestatic liver disease, including biliary atresia. [S1]
- **Risk factors for severe hyperbilirubinaemia:** prematurity · poor feeding · dehydration · excessive weight loss · haemolysis · bruising/cephalhaematoma. [S1]
- **In the history:** onset under 24 hours; **over 2 weeks is prolonged**; poor weight gain or weight loss over 10% in the first week of life. [S1]
- **Jaundice may be less obvious in infants with darker skin.** [S1]

## Measure and investigate

- **TcB can screen well infants ≥35 weeks' gestation and ≥24 hours old.** Confirm with SBR if within 50 micromol/L of the treatment threshold, ≥250 micromol/L, rapidly rising, or phototherapy is being considered or has started. TcB is less reliable after phototherapy. [S1]
- **SBR is the gold standard** for diagnosis and treatment decisions; interpret it by gestation, postnatal age and risk factors on a gestation-specific nomogram. [S1]
- **Request fractionated bilirubin** in prolonged jaundice, unwell infants, suspected pathological jaundice, pale stools or dark urine. [S1]
- **Most well infants with unconjugated jaundice need no further investigation.** Physiological jaundice starts after 24 hours, peaks on day 3–5 and lasts 1–2 weeks. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Early onset (under 24 hours) is pathological:** every infant needs fractionated SBR, FBE and a direct antiglobulin test (DAT). [S1]
- **Prolonged jaundice (over 2 weeks):** fractionated SBR, FBE with film and reticulocytes, TFTs, blood group, DAT; LFTs if conjugated bilirubin is over 10%. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Conjugated hyperbilirubinaemia (any age) — over 20 µmol/L or over 20% of total: refer to gastroenterology.** A normal ultrasound does not exclude biliary atresia. [S1]
- **Breast milk jaundice** occurs in healthy, thriving breast-fed infants: onset 5–7 days, peaks at 2–3 weeks, can last up to 12 weeks — a **diagnosis of exclusion**. [S1]

## Treat

- **Plot TcB/SBR on a gestation-specific nomogram and use local treatment thresholds.** Judge severity by gestation, postnatal age, bilirubin, clinical condition, hydration and risk factors. [S1]
- **Support hydration and feeding**, especially in breast-fed infants and those with excessive weight loss or poor intake; consider a lactation consultant and maternal and child health nurse. [S1]
- **Phototherapy follows local protocol** (intensity, biliblanket, monitoring), with close monitoring of weight, hydration and bilirubin and serial SBR until resolution. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Exchange transfusion only in, or with, a Neonatal Intensive Care Unit** — contact the paediatric retrieval service. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Breast milk jaundice: do NOT stop breastfeeding.** It may last up to 12 weeks; no further bilirubin levels are needed unless the jaundice is worsening. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Sepsis: treat immediately with IV antibiotics** as per the sepsis guideline. [S1]
- **Extrahepatic obstruction or neonatal hepatitis is uncommon, but early diagnosis improves outcome** — discuss with paediatric gastroenterology if suspected. [S1]

## Escalate and discharge

- **Involve the paediatric team** if the child is unwell, the cause is unclear, or conjugated bilirubin is over 20% of total. [S1]
- **Consider transfer** if the level is rising despite adequate local treatment, or the child needs exchange transfusion. [S1]
- **Discharge when** SBR/TcB is below the treatment threshold with follow-up planned, causes needing treatment are excluded, and the infant is well and feeding well. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Sunlight exposure is not a treatment for jaundice.** [S1]
- **Arrange early MCH nurse and/or GP follow-up**, especially if under 7 days old, exclusively breastfeeding or still establishing feeds, or the level is borderline. **Re-check bilirubin in 24–48 hours** if borderline or still rising. [S1]
- **Tell parents to seek review** if jaundice persists 2–3 weeks without an established cause, they think it is worsening, or they have any other concern. [S1]
