# Cervical lymphadenopathy in children

## Recognise it

- **Common: found in more than a third of healthy children.** A well-appearing child usually needs observation and reassurance, without investigation. [S1]
- **Lymphadenopathy means abnormally enlarged head and neck nodes, usually over 1 cm.** Viral URTI is the commonest cause, and reactive nodes may persist for weeks to months. [S1]
- **Ask about** travel and possible TB exposure, animals such as cats, immunisation status, dental disease, response to antibiotics, and features of deep neck infection (e.g. trismus, muffled voice). [S1]

## Acute causes

- **Acute bacterial lymphadenitis:** usually unilateral, anterior neck, with fever; firm and tender with overlying erythema; may be fluctuant. Causes: *S. aureus*, group B strep (neonates), group A strep, anaerobes with dental disease. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Rarely linked to a deep neck infection such as retropharyngeal abscess.** Kawasaki disease can present with unilateral tender cervical nodes. [S1]

## Persistent causes

- **Subacute (2–6 weeks): usually viral. Chronic (over 6 weeks): several possible causes.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Consider malignancy if nodes are prolonged, painless and firm**, especially with weight loss, night sweats, easy bruising, hepatosplenomegaly or a mediastinal mass. [S1]
- **TB:** non-tender nodes, exposure history, fever, malaise, weight loss. **Atypical mycobacteria:** indolent, chronic unilateral nodes with a violaceous hue, usually under 5 years. [S1]
- **Cat scratch disease:** usually tender nodes in the axilla. **Head and neck eczema** can cause prominent posterior cervical nodes. [S1]
- **If investigating:** TB skin test or QuantiFERON if TB is suspected; chest X-ray if malignancy is suspected; neck ultrasound; CT or MRI may be needed before surgery. **Excisional biopsy is the gold standard; FNA is less helpful.** [S1]

## Treat

- **Most cases are self-limited and need no treatment.** [S1]
- **Bacterial lymphadenitis (unilateral, tender, fluctuant), well child: cefalexin 20 mg/kg (max 750 mg) orally tds for 7 days, reviewed at 48 hours.** [S1]
- **Unwell, or failed oral treatment: cefazolin 50 mg/kg (max 2 g) IV 8-hourly.** Follow local susceptibility patterns. [S1]
- **A fluctuant node not responding to IV antibiotics: consult surgeons about incision and drainage.** ⚠️ **Suspected mycobacterial infection: excise the whole node**, since incision and drainage may leave a sinus. [S1]

## Escalation

- **Involve the paediatric team for:** a neonate, an unwell child, nodes persisting despite oral antibiotics, or features of systemic disease (Kawasaki, malignancy, rheumatological). [S1]
- **Transfer** a child needing care beyond the hospital's comfort level. [S1]
