## Key points

- **Most straddle injuries are minor.** Consider analgesia, with or without procedural sedation, early for the examination. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Non-accidental injury is uncommon but must be considered** — if suspected, consult a senior doctor and consider advice from the forensic paediatric medical service. [S1]
- **Pre-pubertal female genital tissue is friable and well perfused**, so even minor trauma can bleed in a way that looks extensive. [S1]
- **Check the history matches the findings; document thoroughly and refer any case suspicious for abuse.** [S1]

## Assess

- **History:** mechanism, timing and setting, first aid given, **inability to pass urine or faeces**, other injuries, witnesses — and whether the injury fits the history. [S1]
- **Examine only once — involve a senior clinician early.** If non-accidental injury is suspected, contact the local forensic paediatric medical service. [S1]
- **Supine frog-leg position, with explanation and gentle handling**; consider analgesia or procedural sedation. Document in detail — a clock face helps describe location. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Can you see the posterior or upper limit of the wound? If not, consider examination under anaesthesia.** Look also for an expanding haematoma and any anal or rectal involvement. [S1]

## Treat

- **Compress bleeding with a clean dressing pad.** In older adolescents with significant vaginal bleeding, the vagina can be packed with a tampon or gauze. [S1]
- **Ice packs** reduce bleeding and swelling (not directly over the clitoris). **Irrigate with warm water. Review tetanus status.** [S1]
- **Minor** (bleeding minor or stopped, voiding spontaneously): salt-water baths, topical anaesthetic or barrier cream for pain on passing urine, **less strenuous activity for 24 hours**, simple analgesia; **GP follow-up.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Non-minor** (ongoing bleeding, laceration borders not seen, labia minora tear, unable to void, clinician concern): as above, **consider a urethral catheter if unable to void** (e.g. large vulval haematoma); **local paediatric team and gynaecology or surgical service.** [S1]

## Consult and discharge

- **Involve the local paediatric team for** a non-minor injury, a child unable to pass urine, or suspected non-accidental injury needing forensic examination. [S1]
- **Discharge when the injury is minor and the child can pass urine.** [S1]
- **Victorian Forensic Paediatric Medical Service (VFPMS): 1300 66 11 42.** [S1]
