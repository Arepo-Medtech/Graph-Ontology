# Asthma in adolescents (12 years and over)

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** starting, stepping and reviewing preventer and reliever treatment for stable asthma in adolescents aged 12 and over. Counterparts: `asthma` (Australian Asthma Handbook, adults and adolescents, stable disease) and `acute-asthma-children` (RCH, the acute exacerbation).

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 21 of 21 claims quoted verbatim from the **RCH Melbourne Clinical Practice Guideline *Asthma in adolescents (12 years and over)*** (Last updated September 2022); **38 fragments re-checked by machine. 5 quoted doses.**

> ⚠️ **Every adolescent with asthma needs an ICS-containing regimen — SABA alone is no longer recommended, and ICS is never stopped completely.** In hospital, an exacerbation is treated with SABA.

---

## Principles

- ⚠️ **Every adolescent with asthma should be on a regimen that includes an ICS; SABA (salbutamol) alone is no longer recommended.** High SABA use (more than 3 MDI canisters a year) with low preventer use is associated with more severe exacerbations and death. [S1]
- **Most adolescents can start on an as-needed ICS/LABA combination as reliever.** **Budesonide/formoterol is currently the only combination that can be prescribed as a reliever** — other combination inhalers (eg Seretide) should not be used. [S1]
- **Traditional daily ICS preventer plus as-needed SABA is also effective**, and may be preferred if the current regimen is working or DPI/Turbuhaler technique is difficult. **If already established on it, discuss with the long-term care provider** before changing. [S1]
- ⚠️ **The risk of fatal anaphylaxis is higher in adolescents with poorly controlled asthma.** [S1]

## Assess

- **Assess control by:** daytime symptoms · reliever doses per week · activity limitation · symptoms at night or on waking. **Examine inhaler technique** and look for allergic rhinitis and eczema. [S1]
- **The diagnosis is usually clinical.** Investigate only if another diagnosis is being considered or treatment is to be stepped up beyond Step 3, and consider a respiratory physician. [S1]
- **Spirometry belongs in an accredited respiratory function laboratory**, with bronchodilator response, ± exercise or other challenge tests. [S1]

## Start treatment

- **An anti-inflammatory reliever regimen can start after an acute presentation (eg on ED discharge) or at routine outpatient review.** Most adolescents start at step one. [S1]
- **The anti-inflammatory reliever regimen uses budesonide/formoterol 200 mcg/6 mcg.** [S1]
- **Turbuhaler (200/6)** is more portable with a lower carbon footprint but needs adequate technique. **Alternative: budesonide/formoterol MDI (Rapihaler) with spacer, 100 mcg/3 mcg, 2 puffs twice daily.** [S1]
- **Common ICS:** fluticasone from 125 mcg twice daily up to a total daily dose of 500 mcg; ciclesonide from 80 mcg once daily up to 320 mcg as required. [S1]
- **Lower the MDI carbon footprint:** good control to reduce SABA need, switch to DPI when possible, and prescribe 1 puff of a stronger preventer instead of 2 puffs of a weaker one. [S1]

## Step up or down

- **Good control = all of:** daytime symptoms and reliever need ≤2 days per week, no activity limitation, no night or waking symptoms. **Partial = one or two** of the uncontrolled features; **poor = three or more:** daytime symptoms or reliever need >2 days per week, activity limitation, night or waking symptoms. Pre-exercise doses don't count. [S1]
- **Poor control → step up the preventer**, but first check technique and adherence and consider other diagnoses. **After a severe exacerbation, review and consider stepping up.** [S1]
- **Good control for about 3 months → consider stepping down.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Never cease an adolescent's ICS completely.** Stepping down to reliever only means an ICS/LABA combination reliever. [S1]

## Action plan and hospital

- ⚠️ **Every adolescent needs a written asthma action plan** saying when to seek medical attention — generally at **12 budesonide/formoterol actuations a day by DPI, or 24 inhalations a day by MDI.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Exacerbation needing hospital: treat with SABA as standard** (see acute asthma). At home, if only SABA is available, use it while awaiting the ambulance. **On discharge, resume budesonide/formoterol for symptom relief.** [S1]

## Not responding

- **Before escalating:** check the diagnosis, adherence and inhaler technique, and contributing factors — allergic rhinitis, obesity, OSA, reflux, dysfunctional breathing (including vocal cord dysfunction), depression/anxiety (chest tightness without wheeze), smoking and vaping, damp or crowded housing, aeroallergens, and insufficient income to access healthcare. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Refer severe, uncontrolled asthma** (step 3 with ongoing poor response) **to a respiratory physician or specialist asthma service**; also consult if the diagnosis is unclear. [S1]
- **Add-on options for that group:** oral prednisolone 1 mg/kg (max 50 mg) daily for 2 weeks · tiotropium 2.5 mcg 2 puffs daily · montelukast 5 mg daily. [S1]
