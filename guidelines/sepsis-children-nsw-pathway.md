# Paediatric sepsis — the NSW pathway

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-22 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** NSW Health, patients 28 days corrected age to 16 years, any clinical setting.

> **Australian primary source.** Clinical Excellence Commission (NSW Health). ***Paediatric Sepsis Pathway***,
> form **SMR060.399** [S1].

## ⚠️ Why this is a separate file

The **sepsis in children** guideline in this compendium — RCH Melbourne — opens by subordinating itself:

> **Some state and territory health departments have [sepsis pathways]; these must be followed.**

**This is one of them.** In NSW it is the operative document, not the RCH guideline, and **the two differ on
specifics.** Those differences are set out below rather than averaged away.

## ⚠️ A different instrument again

This is **not a clinical practice guideline. It is a form** — a two-page NSW Health medical record with
checkboxes, a **Print Name** and a **Signature** line, holes punched to AS2828.1:2019 [S1].

**Three instrument types now sit in this compendium**: a guideline that advises (RCH), a standard that
governs licensing (Austroads), and **a pathway that is completed, signed and filed in the chart**. The third
is the only one that becomes part of the patient's record by being used.

Its three phases are printed as headings: **RECOGNISE** · **RESPOND & ESCALATE** [S1].

## Scope

> **Use for patients from 28 days corrected age to 16 years in any clinical setting** [S1]
> **Babies up to 28 days corrected age use CEC Neonatal Sepsis Pathway** [S1]
> **Use febrile neutropenia guideline where relevant** [S1]

**The 28-day boundary matches RCH's**, which handles ≤28 days in prose and everything above it by flowchart.

## RECOGNISE

> **Sepsis is infection with organ dysfunction and is a medical emergency** [S1]

**Signs of infection, or history/evidence of fever or hypothermia, PLUS any of** [S1]:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Looks sick or toxic — grunting, rigors, pallor, poor feeding** | ⚠️ **Parental, carer or clinician concern** |
| **Change in behaviour or decreased level of consciousness** | **Immunocompromised or complex medical history** |
| **Persistent tachycardia** | ⚠️ **Re-presentation or worsening with same illness** |
| **Severe unexplained pain** | **Under 3 months of age** |
| **Non-blanching rash** | **Central line or invasive device** |
| | **Recent surgery, burn, wound** |
| | **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people** |

**The right-hand column is almost entirely context rather than physiology** — who the child is, what has
already happened to them, and who is worried. **Parental, carer or clinician concern** is a listed trigger
with the same standing as a non-blanching rash.

⚠️ **"Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people" appears again** — this is the **third** document in this
compendium to list it as a sepsis or fever risk factor, after RCH sepsis and RCH febrile child, and again
**without a stated basis or accompanying cultural safety guidance**. See *Unresolved*; the pattern is recorded
in `docs/unresolved-audit.md` as Finding 4.

**Then: commence A–G assessment and document a full set of vital sign observations including blood
pressure** [S1].

### Two zones

**Laboratory features of severe illness / organ dysfunction include acidosis, low platelets, elevated
creatinine, elevated CRP or coagulopathy** [S1].

| | **RED ZONE** | **YELLOW ZONE** |
|---|---|---|
| Lactate | **≥ 4 mmol/L** | **2.0 to 3.9 mmol/L** |
| Perfusion | — | **Central capillary refill ≥ 3 seconds** |
| Response | ⚠️ **Call a RAPID RESPONSE (as per local CERS)** | **Call for a CLINICAL REVIEW within 30 minutes (as per local CERS) AND consult with the SENIOR CLINICIAN** |

*(Respiratory rate/distress, heart rate, blood pressure and level of consciousness appear in both zones with
age-banded thresholds printed on the form; **those numeric bands did not survive extraction** — see
*Unresolved*.)*

### The senior clinician decides

> **Does the senior clinician consider the patient has sepsis?** [S1]

| | Action |
|---|---|
| **PROBABLE SEPSIS** *(with or without signs of shock)* | **Resuscitate** · ⚠️ **Treat within 60 minutes** |
| **POSSIBLE SEPSIS** *(no signs of shock)* | **Investigate** · ⚠️ **Treat within 3 hours** |
| **SEPSIS UNLIKELY** | **Consider other causes of deterioration** · **Reconsider sepsis if the patient deteriorates** |

**Two treatment clocks, and a third category that stays open.** RCH says *care delivered in the first hour
following sepsis identification is crucial*; **NSW turns that into a signed checkbox with a deadline, and adds
a slower lane for the child who is probably not septic but might be.**

## RESPOND & ESCALATE — seven numbered actions

> **Complete actions 1 to 5 within 60 minutes with ongoing A–G assessment** [S1]

| # | Action [S1] |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Get help** — **consult with Paediatrician / Emergency Physician / ICU / NETS WITHIN 5 min** |
| **2** | **Commence monitoring** — **give oxygen as required to maintain SpO₂ ≥95%** |
| **3** | **Obtain access and collect pathology** — vascular access, blood culture, blood gas, lactate, BGL |
| **4** | **Commence antibiotics** |
| **5** | **Commence fluid resuscitation** |
| **6** | **Reassess** — repeat lactate |
| **7** | **Refer** — prepare inotropic support and consider respiratory support |

**Access and pathology** [S1]:

> - **Obtain vascular access within 5 minutes (intraosseous access if no vascular access)**
> - **Take blood culture prior to antibiotics (3 mL in paediatric or 10 mL in adult bottle)**
> - **Where possible collect all relevant cultures**
> - ⚠️ **Do not wait for test results: commence fluids and antibiotics**

**Antibiotics** [S1]:

> **Use Therapeutic Guidelines; Antibiotic OR local guideline OR Australian Clinical Practice Guidelines —
> antimicrobial guidelines**
> ⚠️ **Give IM ceftriaxone if IV or intraosseous access is not obtained within 15 minutes**
> **Document source of infection if known**

**Fluid** [S1]:

> - **Administer 20 mL/kg sodium chloride 0.9% IV or intraosseous rapid bolus**
> - **Assess response**
> - ⚠️ **If BGL <3 mmol/L give 2 mL/kg glucose 10%**
> - **Consider giving a second 20 mL/kg sodium chloride 0.9% IV or intraosseous rapid bolus**

**Reassess after 40 mL/kg** for persistent signs, including **urine output <1 mL/kg/hr OR hypoglycaemia,
acidosis, low white cell count or abnormal coagulation** [S1].

**Refer** [S1]:

> **Seek advice immediately from local / regional paediatric experts AND contact Intensive Care / NETS
> Tel: 1300 36 25 00**
> **Prepare adrenaline (epinephrine) infusion as per the NETS Clinical Calculator — can be given via
> peripheral IV or intraosseous access**
> **Discuss management plan with the family / carers**

⚠️ **The adrenaline concentration is delegated to a calculator**, which sidesteps the problem the RCH
documents have — see the comparison below.

## ⚠️ Where NSW and RCH differ

**Both are current Australian paediatric sepsis documents. In NSW the pathway governs.**

| | **NSW CEC pathway** [S1] | **RCH guideline** *(this compendium)* |
|---|---|---|
| **Time to antibiotics** | ⚠️ **within 60 minutes**, as a signed checkbox; **3 hours** for possible sepsis | *"care delivered in the first hour… is crucial"* — no checkbox |
| **IM antibiotic trigger** | ⚠️ **IM ceftriaxone if no access within 15 minutes** | **"consider IM if delays in IV/IO access"** — no interval |
| **Blood culture volume** | ⚠️ **3 mL paediatric or 10 mL adult bottle** — one figure | **weight-banded, 1 mL to 10 mL across seven bands** |
| **Hypoglycaemia** | ⚠️ **BGL <3 mmol/L → 2 mL/kg glucose 10%** | **BGL <3.3 mmol/L → refer to hypoglycaemia guidelines** |
| **Oxygen target** | **SpO₂ ≥95%** | *not stated* |
| **Lactate** | **≥4 red, 2.0–3.9 yellow** | **>3 suggests poor perfusion, repeat at 2 h, aim <2** |
| **Adrenaline infusion** | **per NETS Clinical Calculator** | **6 mg in 1 L** *(and 1 mg in 1 L in RCH anaphylaxis)* |
| **Fluid** | **20 mL/kg, reassess, consider second 20 mL/kg** | **20 mL/kg then 10 mL/kg increments to 40 mL/kg** |
| **Antibiotic choice** | **deferred to Therapeutic Guidelines or local** | **specified by age band** |

**Two differences are decision-changing.** The **hypoglycaemia threshold is 3.0 in NSW and 3.3 at RCH** —
a child with a BGL of 3.1 is treated under one and not the other. And the **blood culture volume is a single
figure in NSW against a seven-band weight table at RCH**, which for a small infant is the difference between
1 mL and 3 mL.

**Neither document is wrong.** They are written for different systems, and **the RCH guideline says the state
pathway wins.** The compendium's job here is to make the divergence visible before someone assumes they agree.
