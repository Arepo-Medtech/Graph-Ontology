## ⚠️ Drugs are not the treatment

> **Psychological therapies are shown to have the greatest impact on eating disorder symptom reduction and
> other outcomes. Several outpatient psychological therapies have been identified as effective first-line
> treatments for eating disorders** [S1].

AMH says the same from the other direction: **a multidisciplinary approach to treatment is important**, and
**psychological treatments such as cognitive behaviour therapy, interpersonal psychotherapy and, in anorexia
nervosa, family psychotherapy for children and adolescents are keys in management** [A1].

**Psychological therapy is provided as part of a multidisciplinary care approach in combination with medical
and nutritional management and recovery-based supports, and as much as possible, involves the person's
family, carers, and supports** [S1].

**The treatment team at a minimum would comprise a mental health professional and a medical practitioner, and
dietitians, other health professionals, and recovery support professionals may also be involved** [S1].

⚠️ **Treatment type and the setting in which the treatment is provided should be matched according to the
type of eating disorder, the severity of the problem and the person's goals, values and readiness for
change** [S1].

## Enhanced CBT (CBT-E)

**A manualised psychological treatment that is "transdiagnostic"** — **designed to address the key features
of anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa, binge eating disorders and other specified feeding and eating
disorders** [S1]. It **focuses on addressing the characteristic disruptions in eating habits and attitudes to
shape and weight that keep an eating disorder in place** [S1].

**Four stages**, beginning with **developing a mutual understanding of the person's eating problem and
helping them modify and stabilise their pattern of eating**, and ending **with a focus on dealing with
setbacks and maintaining the changes that have been obtained** [S1].

⚠️ **Dose, in sessions** [S1]:

> **With people who are not significantly underweight, CBT-E generally involves an initial assessment
> appointment followed by 20 50-minute treatment sessions over 20 weeks. With people who are underweight
> treatment needs to be longer, often involving about 40 sessions over 40 weeks.**

**For adults (18+) with moderate to severe AN, BN, BED or OSFED** [S1]. **While there have been limited
studies on CBT-E efficacy in young people, there is some evidence for the efficacy of CBT-E with family
support for older adolescents** [S1].

## Family Based Treatment (FBT)

**Commonly known as The Maudsley Model**, used to treat **adolescents with anorexia**, and **adapted for young
people with bulimia nervosa**, where ⚠️ **bulimia-nervosa-focused family therapy (FT-BN) is recommended as the
first line treatment for bulimia nervosa in children and young people** [S1].

**It aims to empower the family, namely the parents, to bring about recovery in their child with an eating
disorder within their home environment** [S1].

**Three phases** [S1]:

| Phase | Focus |
|---|---|
| **One** | **nutritional and weight restoration.** ⚠️ **Parents are charged with the responsibility of refeeding their child and containing eating disorder behaviours.** The clinician **coaches parents to manage problematic eating disorder behaviours and brainstorm barriers to refeeding** |
| **Two** | **returning control of eating to the adolescent.** Parents **gradually hand back control** while managing lapses; family issues are addressed as focus moves away from weight and food |
| **Three** | **adolescent issues and treatment completion**, assuming the young person is **weight restored and in control of their eating behaviours** |

⚠️ **FBT is suitable for young people under the age of 19 who have been experiencing an eating disorder for
three years or less** [S1].

**FBT treatment typically occurs over a 12-month period, although treatment time varies** [S1].

## ⚠️ Who may deliver these treatments

> **Evidence-based outpatient therapies delivered by an eating disorders-informed clinician are considered
> most efficacious** [S1].

**CBT-E should be delivered by a mental health clinician** with the relevant training; **FBT should be
delivered by a mental health clinician who has completed introductory eating disorders training and approved
training in FBT with ongoing supervision in FBT** [S1].

NEDC refers to **the ANZAED eating disorder treatment principles and general clinical practice and training
standards** and **the ANZAED practice and training standards for mental health professionals providing eating
disorder treatment** [S1].

## Pharmacotherapy — a small and specific role

> ⚠️ **There is very little evidence for their effectiveness in anorexia nervosa, although there is good
> evidence of a small effect in bulimia nervosa and some evidence in binge eating disorders** [A1]
> *(of SSRIs, particularly fluoxetine)*.

⚠️ **In bulimia nervosa the dose of SSRI needed is often higher than for depression** [A1].

**Lisdexamfetamine may be a second line option in binge eating disorder** [A1].

### Dosing

> ⚠️ **Paraphrased from AMH monographs, no stored quote, flagged `dose: true`. Check before prescribing.**

| Agent | Dose [A1] |
|---|---|
| **Fluoxetine** (bulimia nervosa) | **oral 20 mg once daily, increasing as indicated to 60–80 mg daily** |
| **Lisdexamfetamine** (binge eating disorder) | adult **oral 30 mg once each morning**; **increase by 20 mg at intervals of at least a week to the usual dose of 50 or 70 mg daily**; **maximum 70 mg daily**. ⚠️ **Initial treatment period 12 weeks** |

**The fluoxetine range is the point AMH makes in words**: the bulimia target of **60–80 mg** is well above the
usual antidepressant starting dose.

## Settings

**Treatment can take place within a variety of settings across outpatient, inpatient, residential or day
programs** [S1]. **Eating disorder treatment can usually be provided on an outpatient basis** — **treatment
that takes place within a setting that does not involve an overnight stay** [S1].

**There is also evidence for the effectiveness of some of these treatments in higher levels of care, such as
in residential, day program and inpatient care** [S1].

**Treatment options within Australia include both private and public options** [S1].

## ⚠️ Admission guidelines exist in four jurisdictions and not in four others

> **A number of jurisdictions have developed state/territory-wide hospital admission guidelines for eating
> disorders. Clinicians should refer to the relevant state/territory guidelines where these exist to determine
> if a patient meets the criteria for either a medical or psychiatric admission. In the absence of
> state/territory guidelines, clinicians should refer to local service-level guidelines** [S2].

| Jurisdiction | Statewide admission guideline [S2] |
|---|---|
| **ACT** | yes — **Canberra Health Services Guideline - Adults with Eating Disorders – Medical Management of Inpatients** |
| **NSW** | yes — **Guidelines for the Admission of Children and Young People with an Eating Disorder (2025)** |
| **QLD** | yes — **Queensland Health Guideline Eating Disorders: Inpatient care on adult wards (2026)**; **Assessment and treatment of children and adolescents with eating disorders in Queensland (2020)** |
| **WA** | yes — **WAEDOCS Eating Disorders: the Management of Youth and Adults - a Quick Reference Guide (2019)** |
| ⚠️ **NT** | **No statewide admission guidelines** |
| ⚠️ **SA** | **No statewide admission guidelines** |
| ⚠️ **TAS** | **No statewide admission guidelines** |
| ⚠️ **VIC** | **No statewide admission guidelines** |

**Whether a patient with anorexia nervosa meets criteria for admission depends on which state they are in,
and in four of them there is no statewide answer** — the decision falls to local service-level guidelines.
Note also that the four that exist differ in scope: **NSW's covers children and young people only**, while
**Queensland's 2026 guideline covers adult wards**.
