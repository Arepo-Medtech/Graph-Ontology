## ⚠️ The screening interval table — the single most useful thing on these pages

| Population | Interval |
|---|---|
| **Men who have sex with men** | ⚠️ **3-monthly** if any type of sex with another man in the previous 3 months; **at least annually** otherwise [P3] |
| **MSM living with HIV** | ⚠️ **3-monthly including syphilis serology — even if only on 6-monthly viral load monitoring** [P3] |
| **Other people living with HIV** | **Annually**; syphilis **at each clinical blood draw**; hepatitis C **yearly** in MSM and people who inject drugs [P10] |
| **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people** | **Regular** testing for chlamydia, gonorrhoea, syphilis and HIV **under 30 — or 35 in remote communities** — who are not known to be in a stable, long-term monogamous relationship [P1] |
| **Regional and remote** | **Annual** for ages **15–35**; ⚠️ **6-monthly in many remote communities with higher prevalence** [P11] |
| **Young people** | **At least annually**, opportunistically [P2] |
| **Sex workers** | ⚠️ **Determined in consultation with the sex worker and guided by risk assessment** — not a fixed interval [P7] |

⚠️ **Where a person sits in more than one row, the shorter interval is the operative one — but no
retrieved source says so explicitly.** See *Unresolved* #3.

## Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people

⚠️ **Higher rates of STIs and blood borne viruses occur, particularly in remote and very remote
communities** [P1]. ⚠️ **The guideline names the causes as social: "STI rates are largely driven by social
factors including poverty, lack of access to health services, incarceration and intergenerational
trauma"** [P1]. People in **urban** settings with a connection to higher-prevalence remote communities
are **also at increased risk** [P1].

- **Offer testing opportunistically or as part of an annual MBS Item 715 Health Check**, especially for
  **people aged 15–30** — or **from the age of first sexual exposure up to 34 years if from remote and
  very remote areas** [P1].
- **Always offer examination and test when symptoms are present** — discharge, dysuria, pelvic or low
  abdominal pain, post-coital or intermenstrual bleeding, dyspareunia or genital lesions [P1].
- ⚠️ **Trichomoniasis is endemic in some rural and remote populations** — test per local guidelines [P1].
- ⚠️ **In remote areas, check for donovanosis with NAAT in anyone with an ano-genital ulcer** [P1].
- ⚠️ **Syphilis incidence remains disproportionately higher.** Offer testing to **everyone who is
  pregnant, in a declared syphilis outbreak area, has symptoms, or requests STI testing** [P1].
- ⚠️ **Interpretation of syphilis serology is complex — seek expert advice if positive**, and repeat if
  there is potential exposure within the previous **90 days** [P1].
- **If a gonorrhoea NAAT is positive, take a swab at the relevant sites for culture before treatment** [P1].
- **Self-collection is acceptable** and helpful where the person is uncomfortable with clinician
  collection or consultation times are limited [P1].

⚠️ **Although HIV rates are similar to the non-Indigenous population, a recent diverging trend, high rates
of untreated STIs enhancing HIV transmission, and greater difficulties in access to HIV treatment make
this group potentially more vulnerable to HIV** [P1].

> ⚠️ **"Cultural safety in health care is essential to achieving good health outcomes for individuals and
> requires integration of cultural safety standards within health care services"** [P1]. **The guideline
> states this as a requirement, not an aspiration.**

## Young people

⚠️ **Rates of chlamydia and gonorrhoea diagnoses in Australia are highest among people aged 15–29
years** [P2]. The **9th edition of the RACGP Guidelines for Preventive Activities in General Practice
recommends opportunistic testing for chlamydia in all sexually active people aged 15–29** [P2].
⚠️ **Chlamydia is detected in approximately 1 in 20 young Australians screened in general practice** [P2].

- **Opportunistically offer chlamydia and other STI screening at least annually** — chlamydia,
  gonorrhoea, hepatitis B, syphilis and HIV [P2].
- ⚠️ **Negotiating to see all young people alone, discussing confidentiality, minimising costs and
  routinely offering screening with self-collected samples can help overcome barriers to testing** [P2].
- **Ask the young person the best way to contact them with results and reminders.** ⚠️ **From the age of
  15, young people can apply for their own Medicare card** [P2].

> ### ⚠️ Capacity, consent and reporting
> Care for adolescents **may involve a complete psychosocial HEADSSS assessment and an assessment of the
> young person's capacity for decision making and consent, while being aware of child protection issues
> and mandatory reporting requirements** [P2].
> **Young people under 18 may be capable of giving consent provided they have sufficient understanding of
> the nature and effect of the treatment.** ⚠️ **There is specific legislation in New South Wales and
> South Australia; common law applies in other jurisdictions** [P2].

**Cervical screening:** an endocervical chlamydia NAAT **may be collected at the time of cervical cancer
screening**, which is advised **from age 25** and has changed **from 2-yearly to 5-yearly** [P2].

## Men who have sex with men

> ⚠️ **"The main barriers to STI control are insufficient frequency of testing and incomplete testing in
> asymptomatic patients"** [P3]. **Most STIs in this group are asymptomatic** [P3].

- ⚠️ **Test at all three sites — oropharyngeal swab, anorectal swab and first pass urine — and perform
  syphilis serology every time an HIV test or HIV treatment monitoring is performed** [P3].
- **All three site swabs are self-collected** [P3].
- **3-monthly** if any sex with another man in the previous 3 months; **at least annually** otherwise [P3].
- ⚠️ **All MSM living with HIV should be tested 3-monthly including a blood test for syphilis, even if
  they are only having 6-monthly viral load monitoring** [P3].
- **Confirmation of a positive *N. gonorrhoeae* result by culture is not necessary for diagnosis and
  should not delay treatment** — but **collect culture before antibiotics to assist resistance
  surveillance** [P3].

⚠️ **PrEP effectively prevents HIV infection and all patients eligible under the Australian guidelines
should be actively offered it** [P3].
⚠️ **Undetectable equals untransmittable: people who take antiretroviral therapy daily as prescribed and
achieve and maintain an undetectable viral load cannot sexually transmit the virus to an HIV-negative
partner** [P3].

**This guideline is intended for all men who have sex with men, including trans men who have sex with
other men** [P3].

> ### ⚠️ What NOT to test for in asymptomatic MSM [P3]
> **Lymphogranuloma venereum** · **herpes simplex serology — not recommended in any group, due to unclear
> benefit and difficult interpretation** · ***Mycoplasma genitalium*, because the benefits of screening
> have not been established** · ***Trichomonas vaginalis***.

## Women who have sex with women

> ⚠️ **"Women who have sex with women are often presumed to be at low or no risk for STIs based on sexual
> orientation, including being refused screening in some cases, which can cause distrust of health care
> and poor health outcomes"** [P4].

- **Current evidence indicates cisgender women who have sex with women have the same rate of STIs as
  heterosexual women**, though the prevalence of particular STIs differs [P4].
- ⚠️ **Up to 90% of this group have had or continue to have sexual partners who are men** [P4].
- ⚠️ **Prevalence of bacterial vaginosis is significantly higher (20–50%) than in exclusively heterosexual
  women** [P4].
- ⚠️ **It is important not to assume that "women who have sex with women" means 2 people with vaginas and
  uteruses** — they may be cisgender or transgender, with internal or external genitals, used in a variety
  of ways [P4].
- ⚠️ **Few data are available on the risk for STIs transmitted by sex between women** [P4].

| Infection | Consideration [P4] |
|---|---|
| Chlamydia, gonorrhoea | **Woman-to-woman transmission uncommon** |
| Trichomoniasis, bacterial vaginosis | **Test if symptomatic** |
| Syphilis | **No data available on women-to-women transmission.** All sex partners tested if positive |
| HIV | Consider the **45-day** window period. All sex partners tested if positive |

## Trans and gender diverse people

- ⚠️ **Ask patients their preferred name, pronouns and preferred names for anatomical sites — which could
  be "front hole" rather than vagina or genitals** — and **avoid assumptions about sexual practices and
  partners** [P5].
- **Offer screening based on anatomy, sexual practices and patient preference, and explain why you are
  asking**: people with a vagina may be offered a self-collected vaginal swab **or a first pass urine,
  which may be considered less invasive if someone has genital dysphoria** [P5].
- ⚠️ **People with a neovagina — a surgically created vagina — should be offered first pass urine
  testing** [P5].

> **Suggested wording from the guideline:** *"People can get chlamydia and gonorrhoea in the urine,
> throat, bum and cervix. You can do your own swabs of these sites if needed. Would you like testing for
> any of these sites today?"* [P5]

⚠️ **Rates of STIs among trans and gender diverse people in Australia are largely unknown**; limited data
suggest they are higher than the general population [P5]. ⚠️ **Worldwide, trans women are at higher risk
of HIV and people at risk should be offered PrEP** [P5]. ⚠️ **Trans and gender diverse people experience
high rates of sexual assault, therefore sensitive history taking is imperative** [P5].

## Pregnant people

⚠️ **STIs and blood borne viruses in pregnancy are associated with significant morbidity and mortality,
including spontaneous abortion, fetal demise, premature labour, low birth weight and neonatal
infection** [P6].
⚠️ **PID can occur in pregnancy and may be misdiagnosed — do STI tests in a pregnant person with new or
abnormal genital discharge, bleeding, or pelvic pain** [P6].

> ### ⚠️ THE ANTENATAL SYPHILIS SCHEDULE
> **First antenatal visit · 26–28 weeks · 36 weeks or birth, whichever is earlier**, per **CDNA National
> Guidelines**. **For a person at high risk, a further test at 6 weeks postpartum** [P6].
>
> ⚠️ **Untreated syphilis in pregnancy is associated with preterm birth, neonatal death and congenital
> syphilis, and early treatment improves neonatal outcomes** [P6].
> ⚠️ **Recommendations for repeat testing vary — see local guidelines, particularly in an outbreak
> declared area** [P6].

| Infection | At the first antenatal visit [P6] |
|---|---|
| **Chlamydia and gonorrhoea** | **Routinely offer to all pregnant people under 30.** Regardless of age **where STI prevalence is high**. **Consider for adverse outcomes such as preterm rupture of membranes and miscarriage.** **Treatment during pregnancy is recommended** |
| **Hepatitis B** | **If not high-risk, HBsAg only**, with HBeAg and hepatitis B DNA **if HBsAg positive**. ⚠️ **Discuss the infant receiving hepatitis B immunoglobulin and vaccination at birth.** Assessment by an experienced clinician, **including consideration of treatment in the third trimester** |
| **HIV** | Routinely offer; **repeat if exposed within the previous 45 days** or if ongoing risk |
| **Hepatitis C** | Antibody test offered; ⚠️ **if antibody positive, test HCV RNA — the small risk of perinatal transmission is conditional on the presence of parental HCV RNA** |
| **HSV** | ⚠️ **Screening is NOT recommended in pregnancy, with either serology or swabs** |

## Sex workers

> ⚠️ **"Currently, there is no evidence that sex workers in Australia have higher rates of sexually
> transmitted infections than the general population"** [P7]. **There has been widespread adoption of
> safer sex practice in the Australian sex industry through sex worker advocacy, peer-based education and
> support and outreach services** [P7].

- ⚠️ **Frequency of testing should be determined in consultation with the sex worker and guided by risk
  assessment.** Sex workers **may request more frequent testing to comply with jurisdictional-based legal
  frameworks and workplace requirements** [P7].
- **Offer testing more often if condom and dental dam use is <100%**, or at the person's request [P7].
- **HIV:** repeat if exposed within the previous **45 days**; **consider PEP if exposed in the last 72
  hours**, and **PrEP if there is ongoing risk** [P7].
- **Syphilis:** repeat if exposed within the previous **12 weeks** [P7].
- **Hepatitis B vaccination is free in most states and territories for sex workers**, with **surface
  antibody testing 4–8 weeks after the primary course** for people at risk [P7].

> ### Condom breakage or slippage — regardless of whether there has been ejaculation [P7]
> - **Testing regularly and using condoms in personal and professional life:** **test 7 days after the
>   breakage**, or earlier if symptoms.
> - **No recent test:** **baseline testing immediately, then follow-up 7 days after.**
> - ⚠️ **Inform the sex worker that STIs detected on baseline testing are not a result of the breakage.**
> - **Consider emergency contraception, HIV PEP and hepatitis B PEP.** **Offer repeat swabs in 2 weeks
>   and repeat serology in 3 months.**

## People who use drugs

⚠️ **Injecting and non-injecting use of methamphetamines and gamma hydroxybutyrate (GHB) have been shown
to confer a higher risk of STIs and HIV, due to an association with condomless sex and group sex** [P8].
**Sexualised drug use among men who have sex with men has been associated with HIV, syphilis,
lymphogranuloma venereum and hepatitis C** [P8].

> ⚠️ **CURRENT INJECTING DRUG USE DOES NOT EXCLUDE A PATIENT FROM HEPATITIS C TREATMENT INITIATION.**
> **Curative direct-acting antivirals are available as a highly effective and well-tolerated treatment
> and can be prescribed by any registered medical practitioner in Australia** [P8].

- **Offer annual HCV testing to patients who continue to inject drugs** [P8].
- ⚠️ **People who share needles may also need HIV PrEP or PEP** [P8].

## People in custodial settings

⚠️ **Injecting drug use and unsafe tattooing occur in custodial settings, and given the lack of
appropriate and accessible harm reduction measures, incarceration is a risk factor for blood borne virus
and STI transmission** [P9].
⚠️ **Unwanted sex, including sexual assault, can occur in custodial settings**, and people may engage in
different sexual practices from when they are in the general community [P9].
**There is a high prevalence of BBVs, especially hepatitis C** [P9].

- ⚠️ **Syphilis is increasingly prevalent among Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people, and as this
  population makes up a disproportionately high percentage of prison entrants, syphilis screening should
  be considered** [P9].
- **Confirm HCV status for all people reporting a history of incarceration or injecting drug use** [P9].
- **Serological testing after HBV vaccination should be considered in custodial settings** [P9].

## People living with HIV

⚠️ **Undetectable equals untransmittable**, and **treatment should be initiated as soon as possible after
diagnosis** [P10]. **At diagnosis of HIV, testing for other STIs and blood borne viruses should be
offered** [P10]. **Hepatitis B, hepatitis C and herpes co-infection are common** [P10].

**Intervals:** MSM **every 3 months**; other people living with HIV **annually**. **Syphilis at each
clinical blood draw**, 3-monthly in MSM. **Hepatitis C yearly** in MSM and people who inject drugs [P10].

⚠️ **Women with HIV should be offered 3-yearly cervical screening** [P10].

## Regional and remote populations

- **Annual STI and BBV testing for people 15–35**; ⚠️ **6-monthly in many remote communities with higher
  prevalence** [P11].
- ⚠️ **Due to less frequent attendance, consider opportunistic STI testing at every clinical
  encounter** [P11].
- ⚠️ **Higher rates of syphilis occur in regional and remote areas — clinicians should have a low
  threshold for testing** [P11].
- ⚠️ **Congenital syphilis is a serious adverse event resulting from untreated syphilis in pregnancy. It
  can be prevented by appropriate testing and treatment** for people who are pregnant or planning
  pregnancy [P11].

**Named barriers** [P11]: health professionals **knowing patients in a social context**, or **being locums
with little opportunity to build a relationship**; and **GLBTI communities may be less visible in rural
and remote areas, with people reluctant to identify as GLBTI**.

## Refugees and migrants to Australia

⚠️ **Pre-migration STI and BBV screening varies according to entry visa status** [P12].

- **HIV screening** is done as part of the health assessment **when applying for a permanent visa for
  people aged 15 and over**, unaccompanied humanitarian minors, international adoptees, clinical symptoms,
  or if another BBV/STI is diagnosed. ⚠️ **This screening may have occurred before arrival and no further
  testing post arrival may have occurred** [P12].
- **Syphilis screening** is conducted for **people aged 15 and over applying for offshore or onshore
  protection visas** [P12].
- ⚠️ **Applicants for temporary visas are not normally required to undergo HIV testing or other STI
  screening**, except for certain groups such as applicants intending to work as, or studying to be, a
  doctor, dentist, nurse or paramedic [P12].
- ⚠️ **Published data do not support universal STI screening for newly arrived refugees** — defined as
  less than 6 months in Australia [P12].

> ⚠️ **"Screening should follow standard recommendations and be based on accurate history taking and risk
> assessment, carefully avoiding assumptions about risk based on gender, ethnicity, religious beliefs or
> cultural background, visa status or prior entry testing requirements"** [P12].

## Adult sexual assault

⚠️ **Always ask the client whether they want to involve the local Police Service or Sexual Assault
Response Team** [P13]. The guideline is written for a person who **may or may not want to make a complaint
to the police at the time of the consultation**, and can be used **before or after a forensic
examination** [P13]. ⚠️ **Ensure the client is offered the option of forensic evidence collection —
availability of medical-forensic services and timeframes vary from state to state** [P13].

⚠️ **Anyone disclosing recent or past sexual assault should be offered crisis counselling. This can be
provided by 1800 RESPECT** [P13]. **Assess psychosocial wellbeing and the need for further support at each
visit** [P13].

### Up to 72 hours [P13]

- **Assess the need for immediate care — first aid or injuries that need attention, especially head
  injury.**
- **Offer forensic evidence collection.** ⚠️ **In most states this can occur without police involvement,
  and in some states up to 120 hours post assault.**
- ⚠️ **Ask about strangulation** — *"Was any pressure put on your neck during the assault?"* **If the
  patient has experienced non-fatal strangulation they should be assessed and referred to an emergency
  care provider.**
- **HIV PEP:** ⚠️ **if indicated, 30 days of antiretroviral medication is required.** **A starter kit is
  available from most emergency departments and sexual health services.**
- **Hepatitis B:** check **surface antibody, core antibody and surface antigen**; offer vaccination to
  anyone not immune and vulnerable. ⚠️ **Hepatitis B immunoglobulin if the assailant is known to be HBV Ag
  positive — if indicated, can be administered up to 14 days post assault.**
- **Emergency contraception:** undertake a pregnancy risk assessment and offer the most suitable and
  accessible method.

### 72–120 hours [P13]

Assess immediate safety and wellbeing including the need for psychosocial support · offer emergency
contraception after pregnancy risk assessment · ⚠️ **test for chlamydia and gonorrhoea from each orifice
that had penile penetration** · **consider *Trichomonas vaginalis* testing if the patient or the offender
was from an at-risk community** · ⚠️ **STI testing and screening are not required for digital penetration
or mouth-to-vagina assault.**

### 4 weeks [P13]

Assess psychosocial wellbeing and need for further support · **STI screen if not done already** ·
**pregnancy test (urine HCG)**.

> ### ⚠️ Documentation
> **"Medical notes including an account of the assault, injury documentation, sexually transmitted
> infection tests and their results may be subpoenaed. You may be called to give evidence in court;
> therefore, your notes need to be precise"** [P13].

## ⚠️ Two numbers this source states two ways

**Recorded, not reconciled. Nothing retrieved resolves either.**

| Window period | Stated as | And as |
|---|---|---|
| **HIV** | **6 weeks** — Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander page [P1], Standard Asymptomatic Check-up | **45 days** — sex workers [P7], people who use drugs [P8], custodial [P9], women who have sex with women [P4], pregnant people [P6] |
| **Syphilis** | **90 days** — Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander page [P1] | **12 weeks** (84 days) — sex workers [P7] |

⚠️ **45 days is about 6.4 weeks; 12 weeks is 84 days, not 90.** The differences are small and unlikely to
change management in most cases — **but they are real discrepancies inside one guideline set**, and a
clinician applying the shorter figure would retest sooner than a colleague reading a different page.
