# Hidradenitis suppurativa

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults and adolescents with hidradenitis suppurativa: diagnosis, severity scoring, comorbidity screening, mild–moderate and moderate–severe treatment, flares, loss of response, and pregnancy. This is a 13-expert Delphi consensus, not a systematic GRADE guideline. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Recognise and assess

- **Diagnose with the modified Dessau criteria:** typical lesions (nodules, abscesses, tunnels, pseudo-comedones) in typical sites (axillae, groin, sub-mammary, buttocks), in 2 discrete areas within 6 months. [S1]
- **No blood or biopsy test confirms HS.** Consider mimics such as cutaneous tuberculosis, donovanosis and folliculitis. [S1]
- **Score severity with the modified Hurley stage or IHS4.** Original Hurley staging and the Sartorius score are no longer recommended for routine use. [S1]
- **Judge response with HiSCR75 or HiSCR90** (lower placebo response than HiSCR50). Allow up to 24 weeks of therapy before stopping for non-response. [S1]
- **Screen for comorbidities when signs suggest them**, including metabolic syndrome. Investigate suspected IBD endoscopically: faecal calprotectin and ASCA can be raised by HS itself. [S1]

## Overall strategy

- **Treat on four fronts:** general measures, medical therapy, surgery and adjuvant therapy. Raise smoking cessation and weight loss sensitively; both improve response to systemic therapy. [S1]
- **Combine medical and surgical treatment**, and manage pain as a core part of care. Surgery targets persistent tunnels that medical therapy alone is unlikely to clear. [S1]

## Mild to moderate disease

- **Mild–moderate disease (Hurley 1,2a/2b):** topical or oral antibiotics, the OCP or spironolactone, with laser hair removal, local surgery and intralesional botulinum toxin or corticosteroid as extra options. [S1]
- **Topical antibacterials and oral tetracyclines work about equally.** Clindamycin with rifampicin (or clindamycin alone) or other antibiotic regimens can help selected patients. An isolated recurrent lesion may be cured by simple excision with primary closure. [S1]
- **The combined OCP and spironolactone reduce peri-menstrual flares;** spironolactone can help even without them. [S1]
- **Apremilast is not recommended:** placebo-controlled studies showed no meaningful benefit. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Escalate promptly** to moderate–severe treatment when disease spreads or epithelialised tunnels develop. [S1]

## Moderate to severe disease

- **Moderate–severe disease is Hurley 2B or above, or any tunnels.** Start a biologic and manage comorbidities such as PCOS and insulin resistance. [S1]
- ⚠️ **First-line biologics: adalimumab, secukinumab or bimekizumab.** Choose by comorbidity: IBD is a relative contraindication to IL-17 inhibitors; psoriasis or axial spondyloarthritis favours them. [S1]
- **Primary non-response after 24 weeks:** escalate the dose (adalimumab 80 mg weekly, secukinumab 300 mg fortnightly, bimekizumab 320 mg 4-weekly), add intralesional steroid or deroofing, and optimise comorbidities. [S1]
- **Second line (phase 2 evidence):** infliximab, upadacitinib, anakinra, and IL-23 inhibitors (risankizumab, guselkumab), though the IL-23 inhibitors missed their primary endpoint. [S1]
- **Third line (low-level evidence):** IV ertapenem as a bridge to wide excision, combined biologics, tacrolimus, ciclosporin, methotrexate or radiotherapy. [S1]
- **Biosimilars are acceptable,** but avoid switching between originator and biosimilar, which may lose efficacy. [S1]

## Flares and loss of response

- **Plan flare treatment in advance:** incision and drainage, deroofing, intralesional steroid, oral antibiotics or oral prednisolone. Flares becoming more frequent suggest the base therapy is failing; consider changing it. [S1]
- **48% lose response to a biologic within 12 months.** First look for a reversible trigger: recent viral infection, rapid weight gain, medication changes or psychological stress. [S1]
- **On a failing first-line biologic, test for anti-drug antibodies** and new comorbidities. Escalate the dose if no antibodies are found; otherwise switch agent or move to second- or third-line therapy. [S1]

## Pregnancy and breastfeeding

- **Optimise before conception.** In mild–moderate disease stop the OCP, metformin and spironolactone; erythromycin and cephalosporins can continue. [S1]
- **Continuing a monoclonal antibody in the 1st and 2nd trimesters is a shared risk–benefit decision.** Certolizumab pegol does not cross the placenta and is an option in the third trimester. [S1]
- **HS does not preclude vaginal birth or breastfeeding.** Treat aggressively straight after delivery to prevent flares; biologics are not active in breast milk after day 3. [S1]

## Australian access

- ⚠️ **PBS applications still need a Hurley stage** even though the guideline no longer recommends it; the source lists only adalimumab and secukinumab as PBS-reimbursed (bimekizumab is now also listed). No Medicare item exists for deroofing. [S1]
