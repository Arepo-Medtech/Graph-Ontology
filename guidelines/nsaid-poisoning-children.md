# NSAID poisoning in children

## Key points

- **Most NSAID overdoses are benign at lower doses.** ⚠️ **Large overdoses can cause shock, coma, seizures and acute renal failure.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Mefenamic acid overdose is associated with seizures — over 3.5 g can cause them.** [S1]
- **Covers** celecoxib, diclofenac, etoricoxib, ibuprofen, indomethacin, ketoprofen, ketorolac, mefenamic acid, meloxicam, naproxen, parecoxib, piroxicam, sulindac, tiaprofenic acid — **not aspirin** (see salicylate poisoning). [S1]

## Assessment

- **Assess:** any deliberate self-poisoning or significant accidental ingestion · any symptomatic child. **Consider non-accidental poisoning** if developmental age doesn't fit. [S1]
- **Examine for:** epigastric pain, nausea, vomiting · drowsiness, lethargy, seizures, coma · acute renal failure · shock. [S1]
- **Ibuprofen severity:** under 200 mg/kg asymptomatic · 200–400 mg/kg mild GI and CNS effects · ⚠️ **over 400 mg/kg risk of multi-organ dysfunction.** [S1]
- **Symptomatic or concurrently ill children: FBC, UEC, LFTs.** Consider paracetamol in all intentional overdoses. [S1]

## Management

- **Supportive care. Ensure adequate hydration after large overdoses** to reduce the risk of acute renal failure. [S1]
- **Activated charcoal is not indicated.** [S1]
- **Discuss with a toxicologist**, particularly for symptomatic children and abnormal pathology. [S1]
- **Proton pump inhibitor for epigastric pain; treat seizures as for afebrile seizures.** [S1]

## Disposition

- **Admit for observation:** all symptomatic children · ⚠️ **ibuprofen 200 mg/kg or more** · all intentional overdoses. Poisons Information Centre 131126. [S1]
- **Transfer** when care exceeds the local centre's capacity. PIPER: 1300 137 650. [S1]
- **Discharge asymptomatic children with normal vital signs 4 hours after ingestion.** [S1]
- **Adolescents with deliberate ingestion need a self-harm risk assessment and drug and alcohol assessment before discharge**, with links to services if needs are found. [S1]
