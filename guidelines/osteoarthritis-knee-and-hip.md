# Osteoarthritis — knee and hip

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-21 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults, primary care.

> **Australian primary source.** RACGP. *Guideline for the management of knee and hip osteoarthritis*,
> 2nd edition [S1]. Last revised **1 July 2018**. GRADE methodology.

> ⚠️ **The RACGP flags this guideline itself**, on every page:
> **"This clinical guideline was published in 2018 and is currently being updated. The information provided
> may not reflect the most current evidence or best practice"** [S1].
>
> That notice is reproduced here rather than omitted. It is the **second** instance in this compendium of a
> guideline body declaring its own currency problem — after COPD-X pausing its evidence surveillance cycle.
> **Confirm currency before clinical use.**

## The shape of the evidence

| Strength | Count [S1] |
|---|---|
| **Strong FOR the intervention** | **3** |
| **Strong AGAINST the intervention** | **9** |
| Conditional FOR | 11 |
| Conditional AGAINST | 23 |
| Conditional (neutral) — cannot recommend either way | 16 |

> **All three strong recommendations *for* an intervention are non-pharmacological and non-surgical.**

**No drug, injection or operation earns a strong recommendation for** in this guideline. The three things the
working group is very confident about are **exercise (knee)**, **exercise (hip)** and **weight management**.
Everything a prescription or a referral can deliver is either conditional, neutral, or strongly recommended
against.

That is the finding. It is visible only when the recommendations are read as a set, and it disappears
entirely in a formulary.

## The three strong recommendations FOR

### Land-based exercise — knee
> **We strongly recommend offering land-based exercise for all people with knee OA to improve pain and
> function, regardless of their age, structural disease severity, functional status or pain levels** [S1].

> **We strongly recommend walking, muscle-strengthening exercise, and specifically, Tai Chi** [S1].

*Strong for · Quality of evidence: Low (all land-based, Tai Chi); Very low (walking, muscle-strengthening).*

**"Regardless of age, structural disease severity, functional status or pain levels"** removes every
exclusion a clinician might reach for. **Exercise has also been found to be beneficial for other
comorbidities and overall health** [S1].

Prescribe an **individualised** program accounting for preference, capability and locally available
facilities; **set realistic goals**; progress dosage with attention to frequency, duration, intensity,
number of sessions and the period over which they occur; **pay attention to strategies to optimise
adherence**; referral to an exercise professional, **in person or remotely**, may be appropriate [S1].

### Land-based exercise — hip
> **We strongly recommend offering land-based exercise for all people with hip OA... The type of exercise
> that is most beneficial is not yet known** [S1].

*Strong for · Quality of evidence: **Moderate*** — the highest-quality evidence behind any strong
recommendation here.

**Monitor the person's response, and try a different form of land-based exercise if improvements are not
evident** [S1].

### Weight management — knee and/or hip
> **We strongly recommend weight management for people with knee and/or hip OA. For those who are overweight
> (BMI ≥ 25 kg/m²) or obese (BMI ≥ 30 kg/m²), a minimum weight loss target of 5–7.5% of body weight is
> recommended** [S1].

*Strong for · Quality of evidence: **Very low***.

**It is beneficial to achieve a greater amount of weight loss, given that a relationship exists between
weight loss and symptomatic benefits. Weight loss should be combined with exercise for greater benefits. For
people of healthy body weight, education about the importance of maintaining healthy body weight is
essential** [S1].

**A strong recommendation on *very low* quality evidence** is GRADE working exactly as designed: the working
group is very confident the benefits outweigh the harms, even though the trial evidence is thin. Strength of
recommendation and quality of evidence are separate axes, and this guideline demonstrates it in both
directions — see therapeutic ultrasound and anti-NGF below, which are only *conditionally* against on
**Moderate** evidence.

## ⚠️ The nine strong recommendations AGAINST

> **We do not recommend offering...** [S1]

| Intervention | Quality of evidence [S1] |
|---|---|
| **Oral opioids** — knee and/or hip | Low (knee), Very low (hip) |
| **Transdermal opioids** — knee and/or hip | Low |
| **Doxycycline** — knee and/or hip | Low (knee), Very low (hip) |
| **Strontium ranelate** — knee and/or hip | Moderate |
| **IL-1 inhibitors** — knee and/or hip | Low |
| **FGF** — knee and/or hip | Very low |
| **Viscosupplementation injection** — **hip** | Low |
| **Stem cell therapy** — knee and/or hip | Very low |
| **Arthroscopy, lavage and debridement, meniscectomy and cartilage repair** — **knee** | Very low (lavage/debridement), Low (meniscectomy), Very low (cartilage repair) |

**Both routes of opioid are strongly against.** Not "use cautiously", not "second line" — **do not offer**.

The arthroscopy recommendation carries one exception, and it is precise:

> **...unless the person also has mechanical symptoms of a clinically locked knee, as per the Australian Knee
> Society's 'Arthroscopy position statement'** [S1].

**Stem cell therapy is strongly against on Very low evidence** — a directly marketed intervention that the
guideline places in the same tier as opioids.

## Drugs: what is actually left

| Intervention | Recommendation [S1] | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Oral NSAIDs, including COX-2 inhibitors** | **Conditional FOR** — "may be appropriate for some people" | **Moderate** |
| **Duloxetine** | **Conditional FOR** | Moderate (knee), Low (hip) |
| **Intra-articular corticosteroid injection** | **Conditional FOR**, for **short-term** pain relief | Very low |
| **Paracetamol** | **Conditional (neutral) — unable to recommend either for or against** | **Very low** |
| **Topical NSAIDs** | **Conditional (neutral)** | **Moderate** |
| **Topical capsaicin** | **Conditional AGAINST (knee)**; neutral (hip) | Low |

> **Paracetamol: we are unable to recommend either for or against... However, it might be reasonable to trial
> paracetamol for a short period and then discontinue use if it is not effective** [S1].

The drug most people start with sits in the *cannot determine* tier. The same "trial, then stop if
ineffective" framing is applied to oral NSAIDs and topical NSAIDs. **Three of the most-used agents in this
condition are all framed as time-limited trials rather than treatments.**

**Oral NSAIDs** — *"trial at the lowest effective dose for a short period, then discontinue use if not
effective. Clinicians also need to inform people, monitor and capture adverse events, especially
gastrointestinal, renal and cardiovascular"* [S1].

**Duloxetine carries an Australian regulatory fact**:

> **Duloxetine currently does not have an indication via the TGA for OA, and should be considered as an
> investigational medication only. It could be considered for some people... when other forms of pain relief
> are inadequate** [S1].

**Corticosteroid injection**: *"for short-term pain relief. Clinicians need to be cautious of the potential
harms of repeated use"* [S1].

## Other interventions, in full

**Conditional FOR** [S1]: **cognitive behavioural therapy** (combine with exercise; consider psychological
comorbidity, preference, cost and access; face-to-face or online); **stationary cycling and/or Hatha yoga**
(knee); **aquatic exercise / hydrotherapy**; **a short course of massage therapy** — *"only as an adjunctive
treatment to enable engagement with active management strategies, and only for short term"*; **weight
management plus exercise**; **local heat therapy** as a self-management home strategy, adjunctive only;
**an assistive walking device such as a cane**; **TENS for home use** — *"clinicians need to provide
sufficient instructions on self-use, and consider individual accessibility and affordability"*.

**Conditional AGAINST** [S1]: **local cold application (e.g. ice packs)**; **valgus unloading/realignment
braces** for medial tibiofemoral knee OA; **realigning patellofemoral braces**; **lateral wedge insoles**;
**unloading shoes, minimalist footwear or rocker-sole shoes** — *"however, clinicians may consider advising
people with OA to wear footwear with shock-absorbing properties and avoid high-heeled shoes"*; **kinesio
taping**; **shockwave, interferential and laser electrotherapy**; **therapeutic ultrasound** (**Moderate**
evidence, knee); **acupuncture** (traditional, laser and electrical); **bisphosphonates**; **calcitonin**;
**anti-NGF** (**Moderate** evidence); **colchicine**; **methotrexate**; **viscosupplementation** (knee);
**dextrose prolotherapy**.

**Conditional (neutral)** [S1]: **formal face-to-face self-management education programs** — *"however,
clinicians should provide information to enhance understanding about OA, its prognosis and its optimal
management"*; **varus unloading braces** (no RCT data); **medial wedged insoles**; **shock-absorbing insoles
and arch supports** (no RCT data); **patellar taping**; **pulsed electromagnetic/shortwave therapy**;
**PRP injection**.

Note the neutral verdict on **cold therapy versus heat therapy**: **heat is conditionally *for*, cold is
conditionally *against*** — a distinction rarely made in practice, and both on Very low evidence.

## How to read the two axes

The guideline states the framework explicitly [S1]:

| Recommendation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Strong for** | the working group is **very confident that the benefits clearly outweigh the harms** |
| **Strong against** | very confident that the **harms clearly outweigh the benefits** |
| **Conditional for / against** | **uncertainty over the balance**, such as when evidence quality is low or very low, or when personal preferences or costs are expected to impact the decision — **consideration of personal preferences is essential** |
| **Conditional (neutral)** | the working group **cannot determine the direction** of the recommendation |

Recommendations were formulated using the **GRADE evidence-to-decision framework**, considering quality of
evidence, balance of benefits and harms, **values and preferences, resource use**, and other relevant
considerations [S1]. Standardised wording: **"recommend offering"** for strong, **"suggest offering"** for
conditional [S1].

## Where this connects in the compendium

The strong-against list includes **bisphosphonates, calcitonin and strontium ranelate** — all osteoporosis
drugs, and **osteoporosis has its own guideline in this set**. Effective for bone, not for this joint.

**Colchicine is strongly relevant to gout**, also written here, and is **conditionally against** in OA — the
same drug, opposite verdicts in two musculoskeletal conditions that present with a painful joint.

And the opioid position sits against the broader Australian prescribing picture: **both routes strongly
against, in the most common chronic painful condition in general practice.**
