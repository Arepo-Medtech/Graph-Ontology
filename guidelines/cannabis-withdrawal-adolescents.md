# Cannabis withdrawal in adolescents

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognition, supportive care, short-term symptomatic medication and post-withdrawal linkage for cannabis withdrawal in young people. Acute cannabis toxicity is in `recreational-drug-overdose-children`; the general intoxication/withdrawal approach is `substance-use-adolescents`; nicotine replacement is in `smoking-cessation`. No adult cannabis-withdrawal counterpart exists in this compendium.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 22 of 22 claims quoted verbatim from the **RCH Melbourne Clinical Practice Guideline *Cannabis withdrawal syndrome*** (Last updated December 2024); **29 fragments re-checked by machine. 5 quoted doses.**

> **Most young people need no medication** — coping strategies, information and reassurance. ⚠️ **Treat concurrent nicotine withdrawal**, and use benzodiazepines only with caution and senior review.

---

## Recognise it

- **Occurs in heavy users** (daily or almost daily over months) after abrupt cessation or a large reduction. It may not be the presenting complaint — it can arise during any admission. [S1]
- **Time course:** starts 1–2 days after last use, peaks on days 2–6, usually settles in 2–3 weeks; sleep disturbance may persist for months. [S1]
- **Common features:** irritability, insomnia, anxiety, low mood, aggression, decreased appetite, nausea, headache. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Predictors of severe withdrawal:** previous complex withdrawal (aggression, suicidal ideation or attempt, psychosis) · polysubstance dependence · co-occurring mental health or medical conditions (e.g. T1DM, IBD). [S1]
- **Nicotine co-dependence is common** (cannabis is often mixed with tobacco), and **withdrawal from both is more severe.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Distinguish cannabis hyperemesis syndrome** — recurrent severe nausea from regular use, not withdrawal — managed with IV fluids, antiemetics, dopamine antagonists and topical capsaicin cream. [S1]

## Assess and investigate

- **Examine vital signs, look for intoxication or withdrawal, and do a mental state examination.** The Cannabis Withdrawal Assessment Scale helps grade severity but **is not validated for guiding medication.** [S1]
- **Offer opportunistic sexual health, pregnancy and blood-borne virus screening**, and catch-up vaccines (hepatitis B, HPV; consider hepatitis A). [S1]
- **Urine drug screens should not be routinely performed** — cannabinoids can persist in urine for several weeks. [S1]

## First line: non-drug care

- **Management is supportive and non-pharmacological for the vast majority; there is no standard medication regimen** — only short-term PRN symptom relief. [S1]
- **Psychoeducation** on symptoms, onset, duration and coping; **cravings** — the 3 D's (delay, distract, deep breathing), urge surfing, mindfulness, and check HALTs (hunger, anger, loneliness, tiredness). [S1]
- **Keep the environment quiet, calm, safe and private**; encourage small regular light meals and hydration to avoid dehydration. [S1]

## Second line: short-term medication

- **Pain/headache:** paracetamol 15 mg/kg (max 1 g) qid prn; ibuprofen 10 mg/kg (max 400 mg) tds prn; hyoscine butylbromide for abdominal cramps. [S1]
- **Nausea and vomiting:** ondansetron 0.15 mg/kg (max 8 mg) orally tds prn; cyclizine 1 mg/kg (max 50 mg) 8-hourly orally/IV/IM; metoclopramide 10 mg tds prn — ⚠️ **risk of extrapyramidal effects with metoclopramide.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Severe withdrawal/agitation:** diazepam 5 mg orally qid prn (**max 20 mg in 24 hours**); higher doses only under appropriate supervision; **senior clinician review; wean over 5–7 days.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not prescribe benzodiazepines in the outpatient setting without specialist consultation.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Severe withdrawal not responding to diazepam:** olanzapine 2.5–5 mg orally bd prn — **diazepam first; seek specialist advice.** [S1]
- **Insomnia:** melatonin 2–5 mg orally nocte. [S1]
- **Concurrent nicotine dependence:** nicotine replacement — **a long-acting patch plus a short-acting form is most effective**; doses per AMH Children's Dosing Companion. [S1]

## Escalation and discharge

- **Consult the paediatric team** for severe or complex withdrawal (polysubstance use, mental illness, other comorbidities), help choosing the treatment setting or titrating sedatives, or safety concerns. [S1]
- **Transfer if tertiary mental health care is needed.** **Discharge with a clear follow-up plan covering drug and alcohol and psychosocial needs**, and link to post-withdrawal services (associated with better outcomes). [S1]
- ☎️ **Addiction-medicine advice for clinicians, Victoria: 1800 812 804 (24 hours, 7 days).** [S1]
