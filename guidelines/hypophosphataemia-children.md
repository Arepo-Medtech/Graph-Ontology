# Hypophosphataemia in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** definition, causes, assessment, and oral and IV phosphate replacement for hypophosphataemia in neonates and children. No adult hypophosphataemia guideline is in this corpus. Related RCH pages: `hyperphosphataemia-children`, `diabetic-ketoacidosis-children` and `hyperosmolar-hyperglycaemic-state-children` (phosphate in DKA/HHS), `hypokalaemia-children` (potassium in IV phosphate products), `hypocalcaemia-children`.

> ⚠️ **Serum phosphate below 0.3 mmol/L is a life-threatening emergency.** Oral/enteral is preferred; **IV phosphate needs specialist advice**, and several products carry **potassium** — consider cardiac monitoring.

## Key points and definition

- **Oral/enteral is preferred.** Hypophosphataemia may be asymptomatic — **monitor phosphate in high-risk scenarios.** ⚠️ **Acute severe hypophosphataemia can be life-threatening.** Check other electrolytes and renal function. [S1]
- **The lower limit of normal falls with age** — about 1.5 mmol/L in early infancy to 0.8 mmol/L by late adolescence; ranges vary by age and assay. [S1]
- **Severe acute (under 0.5 mmol/L):** weakness, lethargy, paraesthesia. ⚠️ **Below 0.3 mmol/L is a life-threatening emergency.** [S1]
- **Requirements are higher in childhood, especially in premature infants;** phosphate balance is closely tied to calcium. [S1]

## Causes and assessment

- **Redistribution causes to watch for:** refeeding syndrome, DKA, insulin/glucose therapy, sepsis, respiratory alkalosis, hungry bone syndrome. **IV iron infusion** reduces renal reabsorption. [S1]
- **Assess renal function** (urine output, UEC) and **baseline calcium and albumin** — severe hypophosphataemia may cause hypercalcaemia. [S1]
- **Lethargy may be the only symptom.** If the cause is unclear, consider PTH, urine phosphate excretion, and CK (if severe). [S1]
- **Features:** respiratory muscle dysfunction · arrhythmias · weakness, bone pain, altered mental state, seizure, coma · **haemolytic anaemia** · rickets. [S1]

## Treatment principles

- **Treat the cause and aim for the normal range.** ⚠️ **Reduce the dose in renal impairment and seek specialist advice;** adjust to response and tolerance. [S1]

## Oral / enteral dosing

- **Oral for mild to moderate asymptomatic hypophosphataemia.** **Neonate: 1 mmol/kg/day in 2–4 divided doses** (up to 3 mmol/kg/day for osteopenia of prematurity). **Child: 2–3 mmol/kg/day in 2–4 divided doses.** [S1]
- **Usual maximum:** under 5 years 3 tablets daily · over 5 years 6 tablets daily · adolescent/adult 1–2 tablets 2–3 times a day. [S1]
- **Phosphate effervescent tablet (500 mg phosphorus) = 16.1 mmol phosphate**, plus sodium 20.4 mmol and potassium 3.1 mmol. **Part doses:** disperse one tablet in 20 mL water (0.8 mmol/mL) and measure immediately. [S1]
- **Oral adverse effects:** GI upset, over-correction, hypocalcaemia, hypernatraemia, and ⚠️ **acute phosphate nephropathy leading to acute renal failure.** [S1]

## IV dosing and administration

- **Consider IV for symptomatic or severe hypophosphataemia**, or when oral is contraindicated (severe malabsorption, nil by mouth). ⚠️ **Seek specialist advice.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Consider cardiac monitoring** (potassium content; rapid IV or overdose risks arrhythmia or arrest). **Check phosphate and potassium before and about 2 hours after the infusion.** ⚠️ **Not through the same access or lumen as magnesium- or calcium-containing fluids, including PN.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Dose: 0.36 mmol/kg by slow infusion.** Doses over 10 mmol need senior approval. **Repeat if required to max 2 mmol/kg/day (max 70 mmol/day).** [S1]
- **Mind the potassium:** if K is near the top of normal or above, use a potassium-free formulation where available; **reserve potassium dihydrogen phosphate for children who also need IV potassium.** [S1]
- **IV products:** potassium dihydrogen phosphate **1 mmol/mL phosphate + 1 mmol/mL potassium** · sodium and potassium phosphate **0.67 mmol/mL** (+ potassium 0.13, sodium 1.07 mmol/mL) · sodium dihydrogen phosphate **1 mmol/mL** (+ sodium 1 mmol/mL). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Dilute before use: peripheral 0.05 mmol/mL or weaker, central 0.12 mmol/mL or weaker. Infuse slowly over 6 hours; never faster than 0.2 mmol/kg/hour or 10 mmol/hour.** Mix thoroughly to prevent pooling. [S1]
- **IV adverse effects:** over-correction, bradycardia, hypotension, arrhythmia, **hyperkalaemia, hypocalcaemia**, AKI, dyspnoea, oedema, extravasation. [S1]

## Escalation and discharge

- **Paediatric team** if symptomatic or significantly abnormal; **renal team** if renal dysfunction; **consider transfer** if symptomatic or needing monitoring your centre cannot provide. [S1]
- **Discharge** when stable with a management plan and any acute abnormality has resolved. [S1]
