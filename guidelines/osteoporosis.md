# Osteoporosis and fragility fracture prevention

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-21 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** postmenopausal women and men aged 50 and over [S1]. A separate section covers
glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis, which has its own guideline and its own thresholds [S2].

> Every clinical sentence carries a source tag. Claims no retrieved source supports are listed under
> *Unresolved*. Australian coverage is **partial** — the RACGP/Healthy Bones material was not retrieved —
> so sources here are UK and US, and the Australian layer is PBS listing only.

## Treatment aims

Prevent fragility fracture. The target is fracture risk, not a bone density number, and the distinction runs
through everything below: assessment is risk assessment, and intervention thresholds are risk thresholds
[S1].

## Assessment

The UK guideline covers diagnosis, **fracture-risk assessment and intervention thresholds**, management of
vertebral fractures, non-pharmacological and pharmacological treatment, **duration and monitoring of
anti-resorptive therapy**, glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis, and models of care for fracture prevention,
with recommendations systematically graded by strength of evidence [S1].

Two of those deserve naming because they are commonly skipped: **intervention thresholds** (who to treat,
not merely who has low density) and **duration and monitoring of anti-resorptive therapy** (when to stop or
pause, which is the question that arises years after starting).

## Glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis

Different thresholds, and they trigger early. For adults beginning or continuing **more than 3 months of
glucocorticoids at ≥2.5 mg daily** [S2]:

**Assess as soon as possible after initiation** — clinical fracture assessment, bone mineral density with
vertebral fracture assessment or spinal x-ray, and FRAX if aged 40 or over. *Strong* recommendation [S2].

**Treat pharmacologically at medium, high or very high fracture risk.** *Strong* recommendation [S2].

Choice among oral or intravenous bisphosphonates, denosumab, or parathyroid hormone analogues is made by
**shared decision-making** [S2] — the guideline does not rank them.

**Anabolic agents are *conditionally* recommended as initial therapy** in high and very high fracture risk
[S2]. Conditional, not strong, and the distinction should survive into local adaptation.

## Australian context

PBS Schedule 4333, checked directly [S3]:

| Agent | PBS | Benefit type |
|---|---|---|
| Alendronate | listed | restricted / authority (also A, S) |
| Risedronate | listed | restricted / authority |
| Zoledronic acid | listed | authority required (streamlined) |
| Denosumab | listed | authority required (streamlined) |
| Teriparatide (PTH analogue) | listed | authority required (streamlined) |
| Romosozumab (anabolic) | listed | **authority required** |
| Raloxifene | listed | authority required (streamlined) |
| **Colecalciferol** | **not on Schedule 4333** | — |

Unlike migraine, insomnia and GAD, **nothing here is unrestricted** — every agent carries at least a
restriction. The anabolic agents that S2 conditionally recommends as *initial* therapy in high risk
(romosozumab, teriparatide) sit at the most constrained end, so initial anabolic therapy is an authority
decision in Australia before it is a clinical one.

Colecalciferol's absence is a listing fact, not a clinical one: it is widely available over the counter.
