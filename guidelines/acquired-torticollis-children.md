# Acquired torticollis in children

## Key points

- ⚠️ **Torticollis after trauma: manage as cervical spine assessment.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Fever, signs of infection or abnormal neurology → appropriate imaging** to establish a cause. [S1]
- **Most children have muscular torticollis** and can be managed with simple analgesia. [S1]

## Causes

- **Torticollis is a non-specific sign.** Causes: muscle spasm (wry neck); trauma (fracture/dislocation, spinal haematoma); atlantoaxial rotary subluxation/fixation; infection of head and neck, spine, CNS or upper lobe of the chest; juvenile idiopathic arthritis; posterior fossa and bone tumours; dystonia (including drug reactions); ocular dysfunction; benign paroxysmal torticollis. [S1]

## Assess

- **Uncomplicated acute torticollis should resolve within 7–10 days.** Ask about an awkward position (recent flight, new sleeping arrangement) and trauma. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Ask about infection and neurology:** fever, increased drooling, sore throat, dysphagia; headache, strabismus, diplopia, photophobia, ataxia. [S1]
- **Ask about drugs that cause acute dystonic reactions**, e.g. metoclopramide. [S1]
- **Examine:** midline tenderness, neck palpation and active range of motion; neurological, eye, ENT (including dentition and nodes) and chest examination. ⚠️ **Deep pathology such as infection may have no external signs.** [S1]

## Investigate

- **Consider cervical spine X-ray**, particularly for cervical spine tenderness, severe pain, **symptoms persisting ≥1 week**, or risk of atlantoaxial instability (e.g. Down, Morquio, Larsen, Marfan syndromes). [S1]
- ⚠️ **CT of the neck and/or brain if:** neurological symptoms · severe pain not relieved by analgesia or relaxants · suspected bone anomaly or abnormal X-ray · **suspected retropharyngeal abscess.** [S1]
- **Consult** general medicine, orthopaedics, ENT, ophthalmology or neurology, depending on presentation, to guide imaging. [S1]

## Treat

- **Most children: heat pack, massage and basic analgesia.** Diazepam can help in some cases of sternocleidomastoid spasm. [S1]
- **Otherwise treat the cause; stabilisation may be required.** Infection: appropriate antibiotics. ⚠️ **Refer to ENT if a retropharyngeal or parapharyngeal abscess is suspected.** [S1]
- **Atlantoaxial rotatory fixation:** rest and a soft collar. **Injury or congenital bony cause:** refer to orthopaedics. **Dystonic reaction:** benztropine. [S1]

## Consult, transfer, discharge

- **Consult the local paediatric team** for trauma, suspected deep neck space infection, or unknown cause or prolonged symptoms. [S1]
- **Consider transfer** when care is beyond the comfort level of the local provider. [S1]
- **Discharge** when nothing on history or examination needs further investigation and follow-up is arranged; **GP or paediatric follow-up is advisable** after an ED diagnosis of torticollis. [S1]
