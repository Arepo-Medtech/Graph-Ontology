# Anticancer drugs — general principles

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-22 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** ⚠️ **ANTICANCER PRESCRIBING IS NOT A GENERAL-PRACTICE ACTIVITY AND THIS GUIDELINE DOES NOT
PRETEND OTHERWISE.** It is scoped to what a **non-oncologist encounters**: recognising the toxicities in
front of them, the safety rules that have killed people when broken, and the counselling.
⚠️ **NO ANTICANCER DRUG IS DOSED.**

## ⚠️ Two rules that have killed people when broken [C1]

> ⚠️ **ONLY METHOTREXATE AND CYTARABINE CAN BE GIVEN BY THE INTRATHECAL ROUTE.** They should be labelled
> **"For intrathecal use"**, with preservative-free diluents.
> **"INADVERTENT INTRATHECAL ADMINISTRATION OF OTHER ANTICANCER DRUGS HAS CAUSED DEATH."**
> ⚠️ **"In particular, PARENTERAL VINCA ALKALOIDS SHOULD BE LABELLED WITH THE WARNING 'FOR INTRAVENOUS
> USE ONLY — FATAL IF GIVEN BY OTHER ROUTES'"** [C1].

> ⚠️ **HANDLING.** **"ONLY TRAINED PERSONNEL, USING APPROVED EQUIPMENT AND CLOTHING, SHOULD HANDLE
> HAZARDOUS DRUGS."** **These agents must not contact skin, mucosal and serosal surfaces during
> preparation and administration**, and **oral products must be handled to avoid skin contact, liberation
> of aerosols or powder, and cross-contamination with other medicines** [C1].

## Before treatment [C1]

| | [C1] |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ **Hepatitis B** | **SCREEN ALL PATIENTS** — risk of reactivation with myelo- or immunosuppressive drugs. Specialist advice if positive; antiviral prophylaxis may be required. ⚠️ **MONITOR FOR HEPATITIS DURING, AND FOR UP TO 12 MONTHS AFTER, TREATMENT** |
| **Infection** | **check for presence of infection** |
| **Dental** | **perform a dental examination to manage sources of infection or irritation** |
| **Vaccines** | **inactivated vaccine response may be REDUCED;** ⚠️ **MOST LIVE VACCINES CONTRAINDICATED in severe immunosuppression — DUE TO TREATMENT OR THE MALIGNANCY** |

**Treatment is guided by STANDARD PROTOCOLS; treatment that aims to CURE is usually MORE INTENSIVE than
treatment aiming to control or palliate.** **AMH's indications are not comprehensive — PROTOCOLS SHOULD
ALWAYS BE CONSULTED** [C1].

## ⚠️ Fertility, contraception and pregnancy [C1]

> ⚠️ **"ALTHOUGH SOME ANTICANCER DRUGS ARE ASSOCIATED WITH INFERTILITY, IT IS STILL IMPORTANT TO ENSURE
> EFFECTIVE CONTRACEPTION (INCLUDING THE PARTNERS OF TREATED MALES)"** during, and for a period after
> stopping, treatment [C1]. **Infertility is not contraception.**

| Pregnancy [C1] | |
|---|---|
| **First trimester** | ⚠️ **GENERALLY AVOIDED — estimated risk of MAJOR CONGENITAL MALFORMATIONS 10–20%**, plus miscarriage and fetal death. **In some cases (e.g. ACUTE LEUKAEMIA) it may not be possible to delay** |
| **Second and third trimesters** | **generally considered acceptable — malformation risk does not appear increased.** ⚠️ **But FETAL GROWTH RESTRICTION, LOW BIRTH WEIGHT AND STILLBIRTH may be increased** |
| **Breastfeeding** | **generally NOT recommended** |

**INFERTILITY may be transient or permanent.** ⚠️ **ALKYLATING AGENTS HAVE THE HIGHEST RISK** — reduced
sperm count and ovarian failure. **Pretreatment counselling about fertility preservation (sperm or embryo
cryopreservation) may be appropriate**, and **effects on developing gonads may lead to infertility later
in life** [C1].

## Toxicities a non-oncologist will meet

### Fatigue [C1]
> **MONITOR REGULARLY — "IT OCCURS IN NEARLY EVERYONE treated with anticancer drugs or radiotherapy."**
> **Consider TREATABLE causes: ANAEMIA, PAIN, DEPRESSION.** Management may include exercise and
> psychosocial programs [C1].

### ⚠️ Myelosuppression [C1]

**Why the counts fall in that order:** **red cells live 120 DAYS, so anaemia does not usually occur until
after repeated doses; NEUTROPHILS remain in the circulation UP TO 12 HOURS then survive 1–3 DAYS in
tissues; PLATELETS live 7–10 DAYS.** ⚠️ **So white cells and platelets are affected FIRST — but generally
recover rapidly** [C1].

| Watch for [C1] | |
|---|---|
| **Anaemia** | **fatigue, dizziness, feeling faint, shortness of breath** |
| **Thrombocytopenia** | **bleeding or bruising** |
| ⚠️ **Neutropenia** | **infection or FEVER ABOVE 38.5 °C** |

> ⚠️ **MYELOSUPPRESSION IS THE MOST COMMON REASON TO DELAY CHEMOTHERAPY AND REDUCE THE DOSAGE** [C1].
>
> **BROAD-SPECTRUM ANTIBACTERIALS (empirical initially) are indicated in FEBRILE NEUTROPENIA — refer to
> local protocols** [C1].

**G-CSFs** may be used for **primary prophylaxis** in high risk of febrile neutropenia, **secondary
prophylaxis** after a neutropenic complication where dose reduction would compromise treatment, and
**rarely** as adjunctive treatment in febrile neutropenia [C1].

> ⚠️ **ERYTHROPOIETIN AGONISTS carry a warning:** they may relieve chemotherapy-associated anaemia, **"however,
> these drugs have been associated with REDUCED OVERALL SURVIVAL AND INCREASED RISK OF TUMOUR PROGRESSION
> in some studies. TRANSFUSION MAY BE PREFERRED, ESPECIALLY IN PATIENTS WITH A POTENTIALLY CURABLE
> CANCER"** [C1].

**PJP prophylaxis may be indicated in haematological malignancies, some solid tumours, stem cell
transplantation, and with selected regimens or PROLONGED, HIGH DOSES OF CORTICOSTEROIDS** [C1].

### Nausea and vomiting [C1]

| Type | Timing |
|---|---|
| **Acute** | **first 24 hours; peaks after 5–6 HOURS** |
| **Delayed** | **>24 hours; common with CISPLATIN, CARBOPLATIN, CYCLOPHOSPHAMIDE, DOXORUBICIN.** ⚠️ **Cisplatin delayed vomiting usually peaks after 48–72 HOURS and can last 6–7 DAYS** |
| **Anticipatory** | **in patients with poor control previously** — ⚠️ **PREVENTION BY OPTIMAL ANTIEMETIC THERAPY IS IMPORTANT**; behavioural therapy, anxiolytics (lorazepam) |
| **Breakthrough** | **despite prophylaxis — consider an antiemetic FROM A DIFFERENT CLASS** |

| Emetogenic risk, IV drugs [C1] | Antiemetic management |
|---|---|
| **High, >90%** | **5HT3 antagonist + DEXAMETHASONE + substance P antagonist; olanzapine may be added.** **Delayed prophylaxis required** |
| **Moderate, 30–90%** | **dexamethasone + 5HT3 antagonist**; may require delayed prophylaxis |
| **Low, 10–30%** | **dexamethasone, metoclopramide OR a 5HT3 antagonist**; no delayed prophylaxis |
| **Minimal, <10%** | **none unless there is a history of nausea and vomiting** |

**ORAL anticancer drugs: moderate-to-high risk >30% requires a 5HT3 antagonist; minimal-to-low <30%
requires none unless there is a history** [C1].

### Diarrhoea [C1]
**Persistent or severe diarrhoea causes dehydration, electrolyte disturbance and renal insufficiency.**
**Fluid and electrolyte replacement; LOPERAMIDE, or OCTREOTIDE if severe or unresponsive.** **Consider
withholding therapy until symptoms resolve; dose reduction or stopping may be required** [C1].
⚠️ **This compendium's `diarrhoea` guideline carries AMH's loperamide doses for chemotherapy-induced
diarrhoea, which are higher than the usual maximum.**

### Mucositis [C1]
**Anywhere in the GI tract — mild atrophy to severe ulceration with extreme pain, dry mouth, inability to
swallow or speak, infection including bacteraemia, GI bleeding.** **Severe or repeated episodes require
dose reduction or stopping** [C1].

> ⚠️ **WHAT THE EVIDENCE ACTUALLY SUPPORTS:** **basic oral care (soft toothbrush, flossing, salt or
> sodium bicarbonate mouthwashes) is important "ALTHOUGH NOT PROVEN TO PREVENT OR TREAT" mucositis.**
> **Chlorhexidine's evidence for prevention is CONFLICTING.** **Evidence for topical anaesthetics such as
> lidocaine 2% oral liquid is LACKING** [C1].
>
> ✅ **ORAL COOLING WITH ICE CHIPS during administration PREVENTS OR REDUCES oral mucositis in patients
> receiving BOLUS FLUOROURACIL OR HIGH-DOSE MELPHALAN** [C1]. ✅ **PALIFERMIN, via the SAS, reduces
> severity and duration in haematological cancer patients having high-dose chemotherapy and total body
> irradiation before stem cell transplant** [C1].

### Skin, nails and hair [C1]

**ALOPECIA** affects scalp, facial and body hair, is **usually reversible**, and ⚠️ **regrowth may occur
with hair of DIFFERENT COLOUR AND TEXTURE**. **Scalp cooling may be used with some drugs** [C1].

**HAND-FOOT SYNDROME** (palmar-plantar erythrodysaesthesia): most frequently **CAPECITABINE, DOXORUBICIN
(continuous infusion or liposomal), CYTARABINE, FLUOROURACIL.** **Dose-dependent; PALMS MORE THAN SOLES.**
Dysaesthesia → swelling, pain, erythema, possible desquamation. ⚠️ **Usually resolves within 2 WEEKS if the
drug is stopped.** ⚠️ **LOSS OF FINGERPRINTS HAS BEEN REPORTED WITH CAPECITABINE** [C1].
**With some KINASE INHIBITORS (sorafenib, sunitinib) the lesions affect SOLES MORE THAN PALMS and are more
localised and hyperkeratotic** [C1]. **The most effective treatments are DOSE REDUCTION, INCREASING THE
DOSING INTERVAL, OR STOPPING** [C1].

**EGFR INHIBITORS** (cetuximab, erlotinib, gefitinib) **COMMONLY cause a PAPULOPUSTULAR RASH on the face
and upper body WITHIN THE FIRST 2–4 WEEKS.** ⚠️ **"For some EGFR inhibitors, the occurrence and/or
severity of the rash MAY BE ASSOCIATED WITH IMPROVED RESPONSE OR SURVIVAL"** — the rash may be a good sign
[C1]. **Paronychia, nail disorders and abnormal hair growth appear after 1–2 months.** **Treatment: topical
corticosteroids, topical clindamycin, or oral antibacterials; prophylactic doxycycline has been tried but
evidence is CONFLICTING** [C1].

### ⚠️ Tumour lysis syndrome [C1]

**Massive release of breakdown products → HYPERKALAEMIA, HYPERURICAEMIA, HYPERPHOSPHATAEMIA,
HYPOCALCAEMIA.** **Uric acid may precipitate in the distal renal tubule and is the MOST COMMON CAUSE OF
URAEMIA** [C1].

**More common with: acute leukaemias and non-Hodgkin lymphomas · high proliferative rate, large tumour
burden or high drug sensitivity · ⚠️ THE FIRST CYCLE, usually WITHIN 12–72 HOURS after administration
(and sometimes BEFORE chemotherapy) · decreased urinary flow, pre-existing hyperuricaemia, renal
impairment, dehydration or acidic urine** [C1].

> **PREVENTION IS IMPORTANT: increased hydration, sometimes with diuretics to maintain urine output.**
> **ALLOPURINOL STARTED BEFORE CHEMOTHERAPY** in at-risk patients; **RASBURICASE** for those unable to
> take allopurinol or at high risk. **Adequate hydration alone may suffice if risk is low** [C1].
>
> ⚠️ **URINARY ALKALINISATION to promote urate excretion IS CONTROVERSIAL** — risk of metabolic alkalosis,
> calcium phosphate precipitation and urinary xanthine crystals [C1]. **Urinary alkalinisation is still an
> open AMH topic in this compendium.**

### Secondary malignancies [C1]
> ⚠️ **"Some anticancer drugs and radiotherapy are CARCINOGENIC; ALKYLATING AGENTS, PODOPHYLLOTOXINS AND
> ANTHRACYCLINES have been most frequently implicated."** **Secondary malignancies include MYELODYSPLASIA,
> ACUTE MYELOID LEUKAEMIA and some solid tumours** [C1].

## ⚠️ Dosing and administration [C1]

> **AMH's doses "are NOT COMPREHENSIVE and have been included ONLY WHERE THE DOSE IS WELL ESTABLISHED."**
> **"Treatment protocols should be consulted, as drug doses used in single agent therapy or in combination
> treatment are OFTEN QUITE DIFFERENT and protocols MAY CHANGE FREQUENTLY"** [C1].

> ⚠️ **"For many anticancer drugs, ADJUSTING DOSES ACCORDING TO BSA DOES NOT REDUCE INTER-INDIVIDUAL
> PHARMACOKINETIC VARIABILITY IN ADULTS"** [C1] — the convention is not doing what it is assumed to do.

**When calculating body surface area** [C1]:
- **USE THE SAME CALCULATION METHOD (e.g. MOSTELLER) WITHIN A HOSPITAL**
- ⚠️ **DO NOT USE PRINTED TABLES OR SLIDE-RULES**
- ⚠️ **DO NOT CAP BSA (e.g. at 2 m²)**
- ⚠️ **USE ACTUAL WEIGHT IN OBESE PATIENTS — alternative estimates MAY RESULT IN UNDERDOSING**

**EXTRAVASATION** — leakage from vein into surrounding tissue — ranges from no damage to **SEVERE
ULCERATIVE NECROSIS.** Damaging agents are usually given **via a CENTRAL VENOUS CATHETER**, or by slow IV
push or short infusion into the side-arm of a free-flowing infusion [C1].

## Counselling [C1]

**Most anticancer drugs cause side effects, sometimes severe; other medicines may be prescribed to reduce
or treat them.** ⚠️ **"CHECK WITH YOUR CANCER SPECIALIST OR PHARMACIST BEFORE TAKING ANY OTHER MEDICINES
INCLUDING OVER-THE-COUNTER, COMPLEMENTARY OR ALTERNATIVE MEDICINES."** **Cancer Council: 13 11 20** [C1].
