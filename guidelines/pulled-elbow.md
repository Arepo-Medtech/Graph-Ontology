# Pulled elbow

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** clinical diagnosis, reduction (hyperpronation or supination/flexion), failed reduction and discharge for pulled elbow (radial head subluxation) in toddlers. > **A clinical diagnosis — no X-ray needed unless there is significant tenderness, swelling, bruising or deformity, or reduction fails.**

---

## Recognise it

- **Diagnosed clinically** from history and examination; **common in toddlers aged 1–4 years**, and treated with a reduction manoeuvre. [S1]
- **Half have no history of a pull** on the arm (50%). [S1]
- **Typical posture:** the child is not using the arm, which is held with the elbow extended and the forearm pronated. [S1]
- **On examination:** distressed only on moving the elbow, **no swelling, deformity or bruising** of elbow or wrist, usually no tenderness (check the clavicle), and marked resistance and pain on supination. [S1]
- ⚠️ **X-ray when another diagnosis is suspected:** significant tenderness, swelling, bruising or deformity, or failed reduction. [S1]

## Reduce

- **Give analgesia** — especially if assessment or management is delayed or the diagnosis is unclear — but **reduce promptly**, as that usually relieves the pain. [S1]
- **Perform a reduction manoeuvre once the clinical diagnosis is made.** Expect distress and pain; a click may be felt over the radial head. [S1]
- **Either manoeuvre may work.** **Hyperpronation:** child on the parent's lap, support the elbow with your thumb over the radial head, **fully pronate the forearm.** [S1]
- **Supination/flexion:** child on the parent's lap, thumb over the radial head, **supinate the forearm, then flex the elbow.** [S1]

## After reduction

- **Review after ten minutes** — most children will be using the arm again. [S1]
- **Success = pain-free, normal arm use within 5 to 30 minutes**, including reaching for an object above the head. [S1]
- **Failed first attempt:** consult senior staff and consider a repeat after 15 minutes. **Still unreduced after two attempts → consider X-ray.** [S1]
- **Injury more than 12 hours old:** even after an apparently successful reduction, the child may take 1–2 days to use the arm; the child can be discharged with a **broad arm sling and ED review in 48 hours.** [S1]

## Consult and discharge

- **Involve the local paediatric team if the pulled elbow can't be reduced in the acute care setting.** [S1]
- **Discharge once the pulled elbow is successfully reduced.** [S1]
