## What MS treatment is [M1]

**An inflammatory demyelinating disorder of the CNS, thought to be immune-mediated. It may follow a
RELAPSING-REMITTING course or be PROGRESSIVE — gradual deterioration of neurologic function — with or
without relapses** [M1].

| | [M1] |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ **Who runs it** | **TREATMENT SHOULD BE INITIATED AND SUPERVISED BY A NEUROLOGIST** |
| **Acute relapses** | **corticosteroids, e.g. methylprednisolone** |
| **Preventing relapses** | **immunomodulators; some may delay disability progression** |
| **Walking speed** | **may SLIGHTLY improve with FAMPRIDINE in some patients** |
| **Other symptoms** | **SPASTICITY, FATIGUE, BLADDER DYSFUNCTION, CONSTIPATION may also require treatment** |

**Consensus is to consider an immunomodulator EARLY, particularly with clinical or MRI evidence of active
inflammatory disease** [M1]. **OCRELIZUMAB may also be used in PRIMARY PROGRESSIVE MS to slow disability
progression** [M1].

> ⚠️ **"THERE ARE LIMITED DIRECT COMPARATIVE TRIALS, particularly between the newer drugs, and as clinical
> trial populations differ, COMPARATIVE EFFICACY CAN BE DIFFICULT TO DETERMINE"** [M1].
>
> **AMH's broad split: HIGH EFFICACY — alemtuzumab, cladribine, natalizumab, ocrelizumab, ofatumumab,
> ublituximab, sphingosine 1-phosphate receptor modulators. MODERATE EFFICACY — the rest.** **Choice
> depends on history and severity of MS, route of administration and adverse effects** [M1].

## ⚠️ Vaccination: the part a GP is most likely to get wrong

> **COVID-19 AND ANNUAL INFLUENZA VACCINATIONS ARE RECOMMENDED IN ALL PEOPLE WITH MS** [M1].
>
> ⚠️ **LIVE VACCINES ARE NOT RECOMMENDED during, and for a period before and after, treatment with
> immunomodulators — OTHER THAN interferon beta and glatiramer — because of the RISK OF DISSEMINATED
> INFECTION** [M1].
>
> ⚠️ **INACTIVATED VACCINES ARE SAFE BUT MAY NOT WORK.** **The humoral immune response may be INADEQUATE
> during treatment with immunomodulators other than interferon beta or glatiramer — in particular
> SPHINGOSINE 1-PHOSPHATE RECEPTOR MODULATORS AND ANTI-CD20 AGENTS APPEAR TO IMPAIR EFFICACY** [M1].
>
> ⚠️ **DELAY ANY VACCINATION IF THE PATIENT IS HAVING AN MS RELAPSE** [M1].

**Before starting an immunomodulator other than interferon beta or glatiramer: consider active or latent
infection (hepatitis B, TB, HIV) and immunisation requirements, especially live vaccines.** ⚠️ **Specific
pretreatment screening and vaccination requirements VARY FOR EACH DRUG** [M1].

| **Interferon beta-1b** | **FBC and LFT at baseline, 1, 3 and 6 months, then periodically** |

# ⚠️ Cognitive impairment — the symptom AMH does not mention [S1]

> **COGNITIVE IMPAIRMENT AFFECTS AT LEAST 65% OF PEOPLE WITH MS, and occurs in ALL disease phenotypes
> INCLUDING THE PRODROMAL PHASES** [S1].
>
> ⚠️ **It is an EARLY MARKER OF INCREASED DISEASE ACTIVITY and indicates the need to OPTIMISE
> DISEASE-MODIFYING THERAPIES to slow progression and preserve brain functioning** [S1].
>
> **It is not only a quality-of-life issue — it is a treatment-escalation signal.**

**Up to 58% of Australians with MS self-report cognitive difficulties: memory and concentration (58%),
planning (36%), judgement (41%)** [S1]. **Recent research has added SOCIAL COGNITION to the impaired
domains** [S1].

### The phenotypes, and the number that stands out [S1]

| Phenotype | Share |
|---|---|
| **Preserved cognition** | **19.4%** |
| **Mild verbal memory and semantic fluency difficulties** | **29.9%** |
| **Mild multidomain difficulties** | **19.5%** |
| **Severe executive/attention difficulties** | **13.8%** |
| **Severe multidomain difficulties** | **17.5%** |

> ⚠️ **"APPROXIMATELY 30% OF PEOPLE WITH MS MAY HAVE SEVERE ENOUGH IMPAIRMENTS TO BE CLASSIFIED AS A FORM
> OF DEMENTIA"** — and **"the unique vulnerability of this severely impaired subgroup remains LARGELY
> OVERLOOKED"** [S1].

**Trajectory: initially mild difficulties confined largely to PROCESSING SPEED, worsening over years to
include complex attention, learning, memory and executive function. More frequent in PROGRESSIVE
phenotypes and with advancing age** [S1].

### ⚠️ Why it is missed [S1]

> **"It is difficult to detect on clinical interview alone, and PATIENT SELF-REPORT IS UNRELIABLE"** [S1].
>
> ⚠️ **"Even when the overall level of cognitive impairment is severe, there is usually preservation of
> abilities in simple attention, orientation, routine language and communication skills and well-learned
> general knowledge. AS A RESULT, PEOPLE CAN PRESENT AS MORE COGNITIVELY CAPABLE THAN THEY ARE IN ACTUAL
> DAILY LIFE"** [S1].

**What it costs: household activities, family and community relationships, managing finances, MAKING SOUND
MEDICAL AND HEALTH DECISIONS, DRIVING SAFELY and sustaining paid employment — "arduous if not
impossible"** [S1].

⚠️ **RATES OF MOOD DYSFUNCTION AND PSYCHOLOGICAL DISTRESS, INCLUDING SUICIDALITY, ARE HIGH IN MS** and
often co-occur with cognitive impairment and MS fatigue [S1].

### ⚠️ Which tool — and the one a GP can actually use [S1]

> **International consensus favours the BRIEF INTERNATIONAL COGNITIVE ASSESSMENT FOR MS (BICAMS) and the
> SYMBOL DIGIT MODALITIES TEST (SDMT).**
> ⚠️ **"There is no reason why GPs could not use the SDMT in particular, as it is UNRESTRICTED FOR GENERAL
> CLINICAL USE and easy to access, administer, score and interpret"** [S1].

> ⚠️ **THE FAMILIAR TOOLS ARE THE WRONG TOOLS.** **The most frequently used are the MoCA and Addenbrooke's
> Cognitive Assessment-III.** **The MoCA is reasonably sensitive for early change in MS — but BOTH WERE
> DESIGNED TO DETECT COMMON TYPES OF DEMENTIA SUCH AS ALZHEIMER'S DISEASE, AND ARE THEREFORE LESS
> EFFICIENT AND SENSITIVE THAN SPECIFICALLY DEVELOPED MS TOOLS** [S1].

**Interpreting change:** **a clinically meaningful change differs for each tool — for example a FOUR-POINT
deterioration on the SDMT for predicting risk of loss of employment.** ⚠️ **Psychological symptoms such as
MAJOR DEPRESSION OR ANXIETY CAN ALSO AFFECT PERFORMANCE** [S1].
