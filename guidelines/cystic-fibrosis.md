# Cystic fibrosis — standards of care

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** children and adults with cystic fibrosis in Australian CF centres: diagnosis (sweat test, newborn screening), CFTR modulator access and monitoring, routine and annual review, airway microbiology and physiotherapy, exacerbations, nutrition, diabetes and bone screening, and pregnancy. Lung transplantation and service staffing are not covered here. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

> ✅ **PARAPHRASED, HASH-ANCHORED.** 24 claims paraphrased from **Cystic Fibrosis Australia (endorsed by TSANZ) — *Standards of Care for Cystic Fibrosis, Australia 2023 (2026 design refresh)*** (2023 content; PDF refreshed 28 July 2026; origin: AU). **48 anchors re-checkable by machine; 0 doses.** The source's words are not reproduced: its licence is *© 2023 Cystic Fibrosis Australia (page footer 'Standard of Care for Cystic Fibrosis © 2023'; no reuse terms stated)*.

> ⚠️ **This is a standard of care, not a drug-treatment guideline.** It sets what CF teams should provide; it does not give modulator doses, genotype eligibility lists, or clinical guidance on dornase alfa, mannitol or hypertonic saline (see Unresolved).

> ⚠️ **Australian PBS rule:** people on PBS-subsidised CFTR modulators must be reviewed by the CF specialist clinic **every 6 months** to keep subsidised supply.

## Diagnosis

- **A sweat chloride above 60 mmol/L makes CF most likely; an intermediate 30–59 mmol/L result still makes it likely,** and in a few people CF occurs even with a normal value. Always repeat the sweat test, in an accredited laboratory with experienced staff. [S1]
- **Diagnostic criteria:** CF-type organ damage or a family history, together with any one of: two disease-causing CFTR mutations, a sweat chloride above 60 mmol/L, or one such mutation with strongly suggestive features and a sweat chloride of at least 30–59 mmol/L. [S1]
- **Newborn screen positive with one mutation:** a sweat chloride below 30 mmol/L means an unaffected carrier. An equivocal 30–59 mmol/L needs an urgent repeat test; if it stays equivocal, keep the child under a respiratory paediatrician and repeat the sweat test at 6–12 months and at 2, 5, 10 and 15 years. [S1]
- **A formal CF diagnosis matters for access:** PBS-funded treatments such as dornase alfa and the modulators depend on it. [S1]

## CFTR modulator therapy

- **Triple therapy (elexacaftor/tezacaftor/ivacaftor) has made a modulator available to roughly 90% of people with CF**, infants and children included. About 10% of children have a genotype with no modulator, and they depend on close surveillance and prompt treatment. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Treat modulator therapy as standard care for every eligible child with a suitable genotype,** in line with TGA approval and prescribing rules; give eligible patients of all ages access through the PBAC listings, and check adherence at every visit. [S1]
- **Counsel before starting:** what to expect, known and unknown side effects, how to take it, interactions, and sticking with both the modulator and the rest of the regimen. Decide case by case, with the family, whether any existing treatment can be stopped. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Keep PBS eligibility: review at the CF specialist clinic every 6 months,** with a clinical assessment and FEV1 % predicted. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Monitor for modulator harms:** in children starting a modulator, check liver function quarterly through the first year (drug-induced liver injury); watch for neuropsychiatric effects; consider yearly cataract screening; and check blood pressure and urine as indicated. [S1]
- **Visit frequency on modulators is not yet evidence-based.** The baseline is at least four routine reviews a year; any reduction should be tailored and agreed with the person and the team. [S1]
- **Fertility and pregnancy:** triple therapy may raise fertility, so discuss and provide contraception. Weigh stopping any CF medicine in pregnancy against the mother's health, and discuss continuing or stopping the modulator openly on current evidence. [S1]

## Airway clearance and respiratory monitoring

- **Involve a physiotherapist from diagnosis** to teach airway clearance suited to age and development, nebulised medicines and sputum collection, and to build self-management as the child grows. [S1]
- **In hospital, a physiotherapist should see the patient within 24 hours** and provide input every day, including evenings, weekends and public holidays; treatment covers inhalation therapy, airway clearance, exercise and musculoskeletal care. [S1]
- **Teach nebulised antibiotics and run a first-dose tolerability test during the admission.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Collect airway samples at least 4 times a year** and culture them on media selective for *P. aeruginosa*, *Burkholderia cepacia*, *S. aureus* and MRSA. Send sputum for nontuberculous mycobacteria at least once a year, and sooner with unexplained decline. [S1]
- **Do spirometry at every clinic visit from age 5** (it can be tried from about 3-4 years in experienced labs) and track the trend. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Treat a pulmonary exacerbation promptly** with oral, inhaled or IV antibiotics by severity, guided by earlier cultures. Up to 20% of children do not regain baseline lung function within three months, so document recovery and review within 4-6 weeks. [S1]
- **Chest imaging:** a yearly chest X-ray is recommended; in children and young people a chest CT at least every five years is typical, with protocols that keep radiation low. [S1]

## Nutrition and extra-pulmonary screening

- **Dietitian review at every visit** follows the Australian CF nutrition guidelines: growth on charts (head circumference up to age 2), diet, glucose control, salt, pancreatic enzyme dosing and micronutrients. [S1]
- **Modulators bring a new risk of excess weight gain and obesity** in children; advice has shifted toward healthy, nutrient-dense eating tailored to the child. [S1]
- **Annual bloods:** vitamin A, D, E and K levels (plus retinol binding protein to help interpret them), FBC, electrolytes, liver function and clotting, iron, calcium and glucose. Routine yearly selenium or zinc is not supported. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Screen for CF-related diabetes with a yearly OGTT from age 10** (earlier with suggestive symptoms), done when stable. Refer diabetes to endocrinology, with HbA1c every 3 months. [S1]
- **Bone density by DEXA:** the outpatient chapter starts yearly scans at age 8, the children's chapter routine screening at age 10; later intervals depend on the result. [S1]
- **Screen mental health yearly with PHQ-9 and GAD-7** in patients over 12 years and in parents or carers of all children. [S1]
