# Nutrition in adults with head and neck cancer — key points

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** dietetic screening, referral, prescription and follow-up for adults with head and neck cancer, mainly during curative (chemo)radiotherapy and surgery. A COSA guideline hosted by Cancer Council. No corpus counterpart covers cancer nutrition or enteral feeding.

## GRADE recommendations (updated May 2024)

- **Dietitian review weekly during (chemo)radiotherapy, then at minimum fortnightly for at least 6 weeks after treatment.** [S1]
- **After that, dietitian review as clinically indicated, for as long as malnutrition, weight loss, chronic toxicities or tube feeding need managing.** [S1]
- **Prophylactic versus reactive nutrition support:** decide with dietitians, other clinicians, patients and caregivers. There may be little to no difference in long-term outcomes, **but the prophylactic approach improves short-term quality of life.** [S1]

## Screening and referral (evidence summaries)

- **Screen at diagnosis and at frequent intervals across the treatment continuum.** [S1]
- **The evidence summary states that all head and neck patients receiving radiotherapy are at high nutritional risk and should be referred to the dietitian.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **One study found weight loss of more than 5% during radiotherapy was associated with reduced disease-specific survival.** [S1]

## Prescription and feeding route (evidence summaries)

- **During radiotherapy, the cited radiation-therapy nutrition guidelines recommend aiming for 125 kJ/kg/day (30 kcal/kg/day) and 1.2 g protein/kg/day.** [S1]
- **The cited NICE nutrition support guidelines recommend considering a gastrostomy over a nasogastric tube if feeding is needed for longer than 4 weeks.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **A cited review concludes that supplemental antioxidants during chemotherapy and radiotherapy should be discouraged**, because of possible tumour protection and reduced survival. [S1]
