# Hip fracture care

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-21 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults **50 years and older** — and people under 50 whose suspected hip fracture is judged to be
caused by osteoporosis or osteopenia. All hospital settings, public and private, including subacute [S1].

> **Australian primary source.** Australian Commission on Safety and Quality in Health Care (ACSQHC).
> ***Hip Fracture Care Clinical Care Standard*** [S1]. **Seven quality statements** and a set of indicators,
> referenced to the **Australian and New Zealand Guideline for Hip Fracture Care** and the **ANZHFR**
> registry.

**This closes the gap named in the delirium guideline of this compendium**, where current hip fracture is a
key risk factor for delirium — and the relationship is reciprocal:

> **Put in place interventions to prevent or manage delirium, in accordance with the Delirium Clinical Care
> Standard** *(Quality statement 1)* [S1].

Two national standards citing each other. The delirium standard names hip fracture as a risk; the hip
fracture standard sends you back to the delirium standard.

## ⚠️ Two of the three overall indicators are not survival

| Overall indicator [S1] | Measures |
|---|---|
| **8a** | **Proportion of patients who returned to pre-fracture walking ability within 120 days following surgery** |
| **8b** | **Proportion of patients who returned to live in a private residence within 120 days following surgery** |
| **8c** | **Survival 30 days from presentation to hospital for a hip fracture** |

**Walking, and going home.** In forty guidelines this is the clearest case of a standard defining success as
**the life the patient gets back**, not the event they survived — and it puts two of them ahead of mortality
in the list.

## The time targets

> **A patient with a hip fracture receives surgery within 36 hours of first presentation to hospital**
> *(Quality statement 4)* [S1].

> **There is an expectation that all patients will receive surgery within 36 hours, no matter the hospital
> where they first present** [S1].

**Transfer does not reset the clock.** For hospitals that do not provide hip fracture surgery: **organise
prompt transfer and initiate the care pathway locally to optimise the patient's pre-operative
condition** [S1]. **If a patient sustains a fracture in hospital, surgery should be performed within 36 hours
of the fracture occurring** [S1]. Systems should include **transfer protocols, including consideration of
straight-to-ward** pathways [S1].

> **A patient with a hip fracture is mobilised without restrictions on weight bearing starting the day of, or
> the day after surgery, and at least once a day** *(Quality statement 5)* [S1].

And **"mobilised" is defined**, so the indicator cannot be satisfied loosely:

> **Mobilised means the patient manages to stand and step transfer out of bed onto a chair/commode or
> walk** [S1].

**Allow patients to bear weight as tolerated, but avoid weight-bearing if there is a clinical concern about
the fracture, the fixation or the likelihood of healing** [S1]. **Additional exercises, such as training of
gait, balance and functional tasks, can further improve patient outcomes** [S1].

## Pain — and the nerve block

**Quality statement 2** [S1]: assessed for pain **at the time of presentation to the emergency department and
regularly throughout the acute admission**, with **appropriate multimodal analgesia**, **incorporating
peripheral nerve blocks unless contraindicated**.

**Many patients will have received analgesics in the ambulance** [S1]. **Assess and document pain** [S1]:

- **immediately upon presentation to hospital**
- **within 30 minutes of administering initial analgesia**
- **hourly until the patient is settled and pain is managed**
- **regularly as part of routine nursing and other clinicians' observations throughout the admission**

> **A nerve block has the potential to reduce opioid dose requirements and their unwanted side effects (such
> as sedation, respiratory complications and delirium)** [S1].

That single sentence ties three guidelines in this compendium together: the **opioid analgesic stewardship**
standard (sedation and respiratory depression), the **delirium** standard, and this one. **The block is not
only analgesia — it is opioid-sparing, and the harm it spares is named.**

**For hospitals that do not provide hip fracture surgery**, the standard expects a nerve block **before
transfer** — it is a separate indicator [S1].

## Care at presentation

**Quality statement 1** [S1]: care guided by **timely assessment and management of medical conditions,
including cognition, pain, nutritional status and frailty**, with arrangements made **according to a locally
endorsed hip fracture pathway**, which should include:

- **screening for malnutrition, frailty, cognitive impairment and risk factors for delirium**
- **identifying comorbidity and medicines that may impact on time to surgery or readiness for surgery**
- **providing pain relief**
- **assessing for underlying acute and chronic medical conditions precipitating the fall**
- **excluding other injuries**
- **conducting relevant diagnostic imaging and pathology**

**Document the frailty assessment and use the results to guide care planning** [S1].

> **The Clinical Frailty Scale can predict adverse outcomes in older people in hospital, including
> hospital-based harm, emergency department and inpatient length of stay, the need for placement in an aged
> care facility, and death** [S1].

**Cognitive screening on presentation** is the first indicator in the standard [S1] — and the reasoning is
the same as the delirium standard's: without a baseline, an acute change cannot be detected.

## Orthogeriatric model of care

**Quality statement 3** [S1]: treatment based on an **orthogeriatric model of care as defined in the
Australian and New Zealand Guideline for Hip Fracture Care**, with **coordinated orthogeriatric and
multidisciplinary review** covering **malnutrition, frailty, cognitive impairment and delirium**.

**From the time of admission**, the model includes [S1]:

- **regular orthogeriatrician assessment including medication review**
- **managing patient comorbidities**
- **optimisation for surgery**
- **early identification of goals of care, in discussion with the patient and their family or support people,
  documented in the comprehensive care plan**
- **early assessment of nutritional status using a validated assessment tool, reassessed during the admission,
  with individualised interventions when required — including offering oral nutritional supplements
  postoperatively**
- **management based on initial assessment for frailty and cognitive impairment**

**Protein and energy oral nutritional supplements during admission** are an indicator in their own right [S1].

## ⚠️ Surgery as palliation

> **If a hip fracture complicates or precipitates a terminal illness, consider the role of surgery as part of
> a palliative care approach to alleviate symptoms and minimise suffering** [S1].

An operation framed explicitly as a **comfort measure**. It sits immediately after the instruction to
**discuss treatment options, explaining goals, benefits, risks and limitations, taking into account the
patient's medical conditions, goals of care and prior level of function** [S1] — and before the 36-hour
target, which applies **"if clinically indicated and in accordance with patient preferences."**

**Prescribe surgical antibiotic prophylaxis and thromboprophylaxis according to current guidelines** [S1].

## Preventing the next fracture

**Quality statement 6** [S1]: before leaving hospital, a **falls and bone health assessment and management
plan**, with **appropriate referral for secondary fracture prevention**.

- **Assess patients for their risk of another fracture** [S1].
- **Educate them by discussing risk factors for falls and providing written information on specific exercises
  to improve muscle strength and balance** [S1].
- > **Where clinically indicated, discuss the need for bone protection medicines and initiate treatment prior
  > to discharge** [S1].
- **Where appropriate, refer to an established fracture liaison service (FLS)** [S1].
- **Ensure the management plan is included in the patient's discharge summary and care plan** [S1].

The indicator is specific: **bone protection medicine given in hospital, or a prescription issued prior to
separation** [S1]. **Not a recommendation to the GP — a medicine started, or a script in hand.** This is the
operational end of the **osteoporosis** guideline in this compendium.

## Transition from hospital

**Quality statement 7** [S1]: an **individualised care plan developed in discussion with the patient and their
family or support person**, describing **goals of care and ongoing care needs**, and including **mobilisation
activities and expected function post-injury, wound care, pain management, nutrition, fracture prevention
strategies, changed or new medicines, and specific rehabilitation services and equipment**. **Provided to the
patient, and communicated with the patient's general practice and other ongoing clinicians and care providers
on discharge.**

> **The individualised care plan is separate to a clinical discharge summary** [S1].

Two documents with two audiences. The plan should **identify any changes in medicines, any new medicines or
ongoing pain management**, and **identify equipment and contact details for rehabilitation services and refer
as required** [S1]. Patient-facing booklets are named — **ANZHFR *My Hip Fracture*** and **NZHQSC *Recovering
from a Hip Fracture*** [S1].

## The indicators in full

| Indicator [S1] | |
|---|---|
| **1a** | **Screened for cognitive impairment using a validated tool on presentation** |
| **2a** | **Received analgesia within 30 minutes of presentation, or did not require it according to an assessment of their pain** |
| **2b** | **Received a peripheral nerve block prior to surgery** |
| **2c** | **Transferred from another hospital and received a peripheral nerve block prior to transfer** |
| **3a** | **Had a clinical frailty assessment using a validated tool** |
| **3b** | **Assessed for delirium following surgery** |
| **3c** | **Received protein and energy oral nutritional supplements during admission** |
| **4a** | **Received surgery within 36 hours of first presentation to a hospital** |
| **5a** | **Mobilised the day of, or the day after, hip fracture surgery** |
| **5b** | **Experienced a new Stage II (or higher) pressure injury** |
| **6a** | **Received bone protection medicine while in hospital, or a prescription prior to separation** |
| **7a** | **Evidence of local arrangements for an individualised care plan prior to separation** |
| **8a–c** | **Overall: pre-fracture walking at 120 days · private residence at 120 days · survival at 30 days** |

**No benchmarks are set for these indicators by the Commission.** Facilities can compare their performance
**to other facilities and to the national average in published ANZHFR annual reports and real-time
dashboards** [S1]. Definitions are specified at **METEOR** [S1].

## Where this connects in the compendium

- **Delirium** — reciprocal citation, and **assessment for delirium after surgery is an indicator here**.
- **Opioid analgesic stewardship** — the nerve block is justified by the opioid side effects it avoids, which
  that standard describes in detail.
- **Osteoporosis** — quality statement 6 is where bone protection actually gets started, and the indicator
  requires a medicine or a script before the patient leaves.
- **Dementia** — cognitive impairment screening on presentation, and the cognitive assessment tools problem.
