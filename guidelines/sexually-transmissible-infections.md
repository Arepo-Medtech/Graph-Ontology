## ⚠️ Gonorrhoea — the row AMH does not have

**Always collect samples for gonococcal culture BEFORE treating**, to determine antimicrobial sensitivity
and **contribute to antimicrobial resistance surveillance**. ⚠️ **However, do not delay treatment to wait
for these culture results** [S2].

⚠️ **Clinicians must specifically request "gonococcal culture" rather than general "culture"**, as
gonococci require specific culture conditions, and the sample should reach the laboratory **within 24
hours** [S2].

| Situation | Regimen |
|---|---|
| **Uncomplicated genital and anorectal** | **Ceftriaxone 500 mg IMI stat** in 2 mL 1% lignocaine **PLUS azithromycin 1 g PO stat** [S2] |
| **Uncomplicated pharyngeal** | **Ceftriaxone 500 mg IMI stat** in 2 mL 1% lignocaine **PLUS azithromycin 2 g PO stat** — ⚠️ **double the genital dose**. 1 g followed by 1 g six hours later may reduce GI side-effects [S2] |
| **Adult gonococcal conjunctivitis** | **Ceftriaxone 1 g IMI stat** in 2 mL 1% lignocaine **PLUS azithromycin 1 g PO stat** [S2] — conjunctivitis is listed as **purulent, sight-threatening** [S2] |
| **Rectal co-infection with chlamydia** | **Ceftriaxone 500 mg IMI stat PLUS doxycycline 100 mg PO BD — 7 days if asymptomatic, 21 days if symptomatic** [S2] |

**Treatment advice** [S2]:

- ⚠️ **Alternative treatments are not recommended because of high levels of resistance**, except for some
  remote Australian locations and severe allergic reactions — **seek local specialist advice**.
- ⚠️ **Reduced susceptibility to the first-line ceftriaxone-and-azithromycin regimen is emerging in urban
  Australia.**
- **Dual antibiotic treatment is recommended to create a pharmacological barrier** to more widespread
  resistance. ⚠️ **Ceftriaxone monotherapy should not be used outside specialist centres** monitoring
  culture-based susceptibility.
- **Why the pharyngeal dose is doubled:** ⚠️ **sharing of antimicrobial resistance genetic material
  between bacteria and reduced drug penetration to pharyngeal mucosa makes it the most likely site of
  treatment failure.**
- **Ciprofloxacin 500 mg orally immediately** can be used — ⚠️ **but only if susceptibility has been
  confirmed on NAAT or culture, and this should not delay treatment.**
- If the patient has an **IUD, leave it in place** and treat as recommended.

**Presentation** [S2]: anal and pharyngeal infection is asymptomatic in most people; **vaginal gonorrhoea
is often asymptomatic (80%)**; **penile urethral gonorrhoea is almost always symptomatic (85–90%)**.
⚠️ **Previous infection does not provide immunity to new infection.**

**After treatment** [S2]: no sexual contact for **7 days** after treatment starts, or until the course is
complete and symptoms resolved, whichever is later; **no sex with partners from the last 2 months** until
they are tested and treated; **notify the state or territory health department**; and **consider HIV PrEP
for anyone diagnosed with gonorrhoea**.

## Chlamydia

| Situation | Recommended | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| **Uncomplicated genital or pharyngeal** | **Doxycycline 100 mg PO BD 7 days** | **Azithromycin 1 g PO stat** — consider where adherence to daily treatment is likely to be poor, especially where anorectal infection is less likely [S1] |
| **Anorectal** | **Doxycycline 100 mg PO BD — 7 days if asymptomatic, 21 days if symptomatic** | **Azithromycin 1 g PO stat and repeat in 12–24 hours** [S1] |
| **Pregnancy** | **Azithromycin 1 g PO stat** [S1] | — |

⚠️ **Immediate treatment is NOT recommended for all sexual contacts** — offer testing of exposed
anatomical sites and **await results**. If contact treatment is initiated, use the recommended treatment;
**only use azithromycin if adherence is likely to be poor** or it matches the index case [S1]. This was a
named stewardship change in the 2020–2022 review, alongside **doxycycline as the recommended treatment for
*C. trachomatis* in all anatomical sites** [S9].

**Contact tracing** [S1]: **trace all partners back 6 months.** ⚠️ **The diagnosing doctor is responsible
for initiating and documenting a discussion about contact tracing.** **Consider presumptive treatment if
there has been sexual contact within the past 2 weeks**, or where circumstances mean later treatment may
not occur.

**Patient delivered partner therapy** — the index patient delivers the prescription or treatment to their
partners — ⚠️ **is approved in some jurisdictions only**, for heterosexual index patients with anogenital
or oropharyngeal chlamydia whose partners are unlikely to seek testing. **Guidance is available in
Victoria, NSW and the NT** [S1].

**Test of cure is NOT routine**, except for **pregnant people** and **anorectal infection treated with
azithromycin** — and then ⚠️ **no earlier than 4 weeks after treatment is completed, to prevent a false
positive from persistent chlamydia DNA** [S1]. Separately, **re-infection is common and retesting at 3
months is recommended** [S1].

## *Mycoplasma genitalium*

⚠️ **Macrolide-resistance testing is recommended where available**, and the regimens below assume you know
whether the infection is susceptible or resistant. **Without resistance testing it is reasonable to assume
macrolide resistance** in infections persisting after azithromycin failure and in men who have sex with
men, ⚠️ **where macrolide resistance exceeds 80% in most urban settings in Australia** [S3].

| Situation | Regimen |
|---|---|
| **Macrolide-susceptible** | **Doxycycline 100 mg BD 7 days, THEN azithromycin 1 g immediately then 500 mg daily for 3 days (total 2.5 g)** [S3] |
| **Macrolide-resistant** | **Doxycycline 100 mg BD 7 days, THEN moxifloxacin 400 mg daily 7 days** [S3] |
| **PID due to *M. genitalium*** | **Moxifloxacin 400 mg daily 14 days** [S3] |

⚠️ **Moxifloxacin requires a private prescription, is not recommended in pregnancy or breastfeeding, and
is expensive.** It can cause diarrhoea and, uncommonly, **tendinopathy, neurological and cardiac events**.
**Assess every patient for contraindications** and review concurrent medications [S3].

⚠️ **Fluoroquinolone resistance mutations are increasing and detected in 15–20% of *M. genitalium*
infections in urban centres** — though not all mutations are strongly associated with failure.
⚠️ **Speak to your laboratory about the predictive value of their assay** [S3].

**After moxifloxacin fails** — seek specialist advice. From **published case series** [S3]:

- **Pristinamycin 1 g three times daily with doxycycline 100 mg BD for 10 days — cures 75%** of
  macrolide-resistant infections. **TGA Special Access Scheme**, hospital pharmacies.
- **Minocycline 100 mg BD for 14 days — cures 70%.** Private script.
- **Doxycycline 100 mg BD with sitafloxacin 100 mg BD for 7 days** in cases that have failed all other
  therapies — **>90% efficacy**. **TGA Special Access Scheme.**

**In pregnancy** [S3]: decisions are **case by case and involve the patient**. If macrolide-susceptible,
**azithromycin is category B1 and can be prescribed**. ⚠️ **Moxifloxacin is category B3 and
contraindicated** — discuss macrolide-resistant cases with a specialist. **Pristinamycin 1 g four times
daily for 10 days** may be recommended, on **limited data**.

**After treatment** [S3]: **no condomless sex until tested for cure, 14–21 days after completion**.
⚠️ ***M. genitalium* is not a notifiable condition.**

## Pelvic inflammatory disease

| Severity | Regimen |
|---|---|
| **Mild–moderate, outpatient** | **Ceftriaxone 500 mg in 2 mL 1% lignocaine IMI (or 500 mg IV) stat PLUS metronidazole 400 mg PO BD 14 days PLUS doxycycline 100 mg PO BD 14 days** [S5] |
| **Severe, inpatient** | **Ceftriaxone 2 g IV daily OR cefotaxime 2 g IV TDS, PLUS azithromycin 500 mg IV daily, PLUS metronidazole 500 mg IV BD** [S5] |
| **Pregnancy or breastfeeding** | ⚠️ **Avoid doxycycline.** Ceftriaxone 500 mg IMI/IV stat **PLUS metronidazole 400 mg PO BD 14 days PLUS azithromycin 1 g PO stat PLUS azithromycin 1 g PO stat one week later** [S5] |

⚠️ **Begin treatment immediately on a provisional diagnosis, without waiting for test results. Rapid
response to appropriate antibiotic treatment is highly predictive of PID** [S5].

**Consider removal of an IUD if there is no response within 48–72 hours** — balancing the risk of
pregnancy and considering oral emergency contraception [S5].

**Consider admission if** [S5]: clinically unwell including unstable vital signs, or pain not manageable
as an outpatient · **diagnosis uncertain** · **a surgical emergency cannot be excluded** · suspected or
definite **pelvic abscess** · severe illness or **no response to outpatient management in 48–72 hours** ·
intolerance to oral therapy · **pregnancy** · **homeless or unstable accommodation**.

**Follow-up** [S5]: **review at 48–72 hours**; **if no clinical improvement by 72 hours, consider
admission**; **further review at 1–2 weeks**, repeating a pregnancy test if indicated.

### ⚠️ The guideline states its own evidence limit on *M. genitalium*-associated PID

*M. genitalium* results are **often received about a week after presumptive PID treatment has begun**.
Empirical doxycycline and metronidazole **has recently shown some efficacy**. If the patient is
responding, **complete the 14-day empiric regimen and undertake a test of cure**; **if still symptomatic,
recall and switch to 14 days of moxifloxacin**. ⚠️ **"This regimen was evaluated in a randomised
controlled trial of all-cause PID and has been used in patients with *M. genitalium*-associated PID but is
not supported by randomised data"** [S3]. **That last sentence is the guideline's own, and it is the
reason this section is not stated more confidently.**

## Epididymo-orchitis

⚠️ **Treat sexually active men with epididymo-orchitis presumptively for gonorrhoea and chlamydia** [S6].

**Chlamydia or gonorrhoea suspected:** **ceftriaxone 500 mg in 2 mL 1% lignocaine IMI stat PLUS EITHER
doxycycline 100 mg PO BD for 14 days OR azithromycin 1 g PO stat, repeated one week later** [S6].

- **For men who engage in insertive anal sex**, treat empirically as above, but ⚠️ **if response is poor,
  alternative treatment may be required to treat enteric organisms** — seek specialist advice [S6].
- **In severe cases treatment may need to continue for up to 3 weeks** [S6].
- **Bed rest, scrotal support and analgesia are commonly required.** **Complete resolution of swelling may
  take several weeks, but a substantial response should occur in 4–5 days** — and follow-up is at 4–5
  days [S6].
- **Test of cure is not recommended**, but men with chlamydia and gonorrhoea **should be retested for
  re-infection after 3 months**. ⚠️ **Confirmed UTIs in men often require further investigation or
  urological referral** [S6].
- **Contact tracing: if STI-related, all partners from the past 6 months are tested and treated; if
  urinary tract infection, contact tracing is not required** [S6].

## Genital herpes

| Situation | Recommended | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| **Initial episode** | **Valaciclovir 500 mg PO BD 5–10 days** | **Aciclovir 400 mg PO TDS 5–10 days** [S4] |
| **Recurrence — episodic**, ⚠️ **self-initiated at the first hint of symptoms** | **Valaciclovir 500 mg PO BD 3 days** | **Famciclovir 1 g orally twice a day for 1 day**, or **aciclovir 800 mg TDS 2 days** [S4] |
| **Recurrence — suppressive** | **Valaciclovir 500 mg PO daily 6 months** | **Famciclovir 250 mg PO BD 6 months**, or **aciclovir 400 mg BD** [S4] |
| **Suppression in pregnancy** | **Valaciclovir 500 mg PO BD from 36 weeks until birth** | **Aciclovir 400 mg TDS from 36 weeks until birth** [S4] |

- **Initial episodes may require a 10-day course if symptoms are slow to resolve**, and ⚠️ **treatment
  should not be delayed** in moderate-to-severe episodes, particularly initial ones [S4].
- ⚠️ **Review the need for suppressive therapy 6-monthly**, as recurrences usually become less frequent
  and less severe with time [S4].
- **In pregnancy: routinely commence suppression from 36 weeks in people who know they have the
  infection, with or without lesions**, and earlier with multiple recurrent lesions. ⚠️ **The greatest
  risk of sequelae for the baby is when HSV is acquired in the third trimester or close to delivery** [S4].
- ⚠️ **PBS streamline authority scripts required.** Those with **frequent recurrences (eg 2 or 3 times per
  month)** or immunosuppression **may require higher doses — PBS authority required for an increased
  amount** [S4].
- **Symptom relief:** regular analgesia · **topical lignocaine** for erosions, fissures and ulcers ·
  **urinating in a bath or shower relieves superficial dysuria**. ⚠️ **Neuropathic bladder requires urgent
  catheterisation and referral** [S4].
- ⚠️ **HSV is not a notifiable disease and contact tracing is not recommended** — but patients may need
  support if they wish to disclose [S4].

### The psychosocial section, which is part of the treatment

⚠️ **The impact of a diagnosis "can be profound but is often based on misinformation."** Providing facts
about **high community prevalence (70–80% HSV1, 12–15% HSV2)** and largely mild clinical effect **is
important for reducing stigma**. **Transmission can occur from someone unaware they have the infection**,
and symptoms can first occur **days or years after acquisition**. **Refer to a psychologist** where the
patient cannot accept the diagnosis, is significantly distressed, or is **anxious about having the
infection when tested negative** [S4].

## Penile urethritis and cervicitis

### Urethritis [S8]

| Situation | Recommended | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| **NGU likely** | **Doxycycline 100 mg PO BD 7 days** | — |
| **Gonorrhoea likely** | **Ceftriaxone 500 mg in 2 mL 1% lignocaine IMI stat PLUS azithromycin 1 g PO stat** | **Ceftriaxone 500 mg IMI stat PLUS doxycycline 100 mg PO BD 7 days** |

**Ceftriaxone is the most effective treatment for gonorrhoea, but azithromycin is added to reduce the
chance of resistance emerging.** **Doxycycline is preferred for NGU and chlamydia and also initiates
treatment for *M. genitalium*.** When NGU is likely but you also want to cover gonorrhoea, **it is
reasonable to add doxycycline instead of azithromycin to ceftriaxone** [S8].

If symptoms do not resolve, **seek specialist advice for persistent NGU, including *M. genitalium* (often
resistant), herpes simplex virus and adenovirus** [S8].

### Cervicitis [S7]

⚠️ **It is important to know which organism is causing the cervicitis**, and **if possible wait for the
test results before treating, as targeted treatment is more successful**. **If same-day treatment is
given, treat for *C. trachomatis* and *N. gonorrhoeae* at a minimum.**

| Organism | Recommended | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| **Chlamydia** | **Doxycycline 100 mg PO BD 7 days** | **Azithromycin 500 mg × 2 PO stat** — safe in pregnancy |
| **Gonorrhoea** | **Ceftriaxone 500 mg in 2 mL 1% lignocaine IMI stat PLUS azithromycin 500 mg × 2 PO stat** | Alternatives not recommended because of resistance — seek local specialist advice |

⚠️ **Use azithromycin 1 g stat as first-line *C. trachomatis* treatment in pregnancy; *N. gonorrhoeae*
treatment is the same as in non-pregnant people** [S7].

**Patients at increased risk of being lost to follow-up should be treated at initial assessment whether a
pathogen is identified or not** [S7].

## ⚠️ Where this source and AMH disagree

**Recorded, not averaged. Neither is resolved here.**

### 1. Azithromycin for chlamydia

**AMH** states flatly, in its genital infections table, that for chlamydial and other non-gonococcal
urethritis **"azithromycin is no longer recommended as *M. genitalium* is increasingly resistant"** [A1].

**The national STI guidelines do not say this of chlamydia.** They retain **azithromycin 1 g stat as a
named alternative** for uncomplicated chlamydia where adherence is likely to be poor [S1], and as the
**recommended treatment in pregnancy** [S1] [S7].

⚠️ **A prescriber reading AMH alone would conclude azithromycin is off the table for chlamydia, including
in pregnancy. The national guideline does not support that.**

### 2. The *M. genitalium* azithromycin schedule

**AMH**'s cervicitis row says "doxycycline followed by either azithromycin or moxifloxacin if
macrolide-resistant" — **without specifying the azithromycin schedule** [A1].

**The national guideline's schedule is not a single dose.** It is **1 g immediately then 500 mg daily for
3 days — total 2.5 g** — after 7 days of doxycycline [S3].

⚠️ **A single 1 g dose would be under-treatment.**

### 3. HSV suppression duration — partial

**AMH** says treat suppression for **6 to 12 months then review** [A1]. The national guideline specifies
**valaciclovir 500 mg daily for 6 months** with the need for suppression **reviewed 6-monthly** [S4].
Compatible in practice, but **AMH's upper bound of 12 months before review is longer**.

### 4. Where they agree

Both give **doxycycline plus metronidazole for 14 days plus a single IM ceftriaxone dose** for
mild-to-moderate PID, and both allow the **breastfeeding substitution of two azithromycin doses a week
apart** [A1] [S5]. ⚠️ **AMH has no severe/inpatient PID regimen; the national guideline does** [S5].

## ⚠️ *Clostridioides difficile* — the source that would not open

AMH's gastrointestinal table cites Australasian guidelines at *Intern Med J* 2025; 55(3): 503–13. **The
citation is confirmed** against the public PubMed record: Longhitano, Roder, Blackmore, Campbell, May and
Athan, *Australasian Society of Infectious Diseases updated guidelines for the management of
Clostridioides difficile infection in adults and children in Australia and New Zealand*, **PMID 40035163,
[doi:10.1111/imj.16638](https://doi.org/10.1111/imj.16638)**, published 2025-03-04, indexed as a
**Practice Guideline** [C1]. *(Citation and abstract retrieved from PubMed.)*

**The abstract independently corroborates AMH's headline change**: the updated guidelines explore changes
**"including the replacement of oral metronidazole with vancomycin for initial CDI and the emerging role
for fidaxomicin and faecal-microbiota transplant"** [C1]. The previous Australasian guidelines were
published in **2016** [C1]. The abstract also records that CDI **"is associated with significant morbidity
and mortality within the Australian population"** and that treatment **poses challenges at both community
and hospital-based levels due to the recurrent, refractory and potentially severe nature of the
disease** [C1].
