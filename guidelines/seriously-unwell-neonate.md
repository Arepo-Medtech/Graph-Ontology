# Recognising the seriously unwell neonate and young infant

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** history, examination red flags, differential diagnosis, first investigations and initial treatment for neonates (<28 days corrected) and young infants (<3 months) who may be seriously unwell. > ⚠️ **Any fever ≥38°C in a neonate → investigate and give empiric IV antibiotics.** Unwell infants may have no fever — or a low temperature (<36.5°C).

> ⚠️ **Any neonate or young infant who looks unwell: assess promptly and discuss with a senior doctor.**

---

## Key points

- **Infection is the likeliest cause** in neonates (<28 days corrected age) and young infants (<3 months), but other serious conditions can look the same at first. [S1]
- ⚠️ **A fever in any neonate (≥38°C) warrants investigation and empiric IV antibiotics.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Fever is not always present** — temperature may be normal or low (<36.5°C). Observation, serial examination and investigations often help. **Early senior review is essential.** [S1]
- **UTI is the most common bacterial infection.** Highest-risk babies: low birth weight or premature, known medical condition (e.g. congenital anomaly), socially disadvantaged families. [S1]

## History

- **Ask about:** irritability, fever, lethargy or sleepiness, **poor feeding (<50% of normal over the previous 24 hours)**, **urine output (<4 wet nappies in 24 hours)**, vomiting (especially bilious), apnoea, BRUE or seizures. [S1]
- **Birth history:** prematurity, maternal GBS colonisation, perinatal stress, prolonged rupture of membranes, maternal fever, Apgars and resuscitation, postnatal antibiotics, vitamin K. [S1]
- **Also:** previous child with early onset sepsis, immunodeficiency, sick contacts, **HSV exposure**, and **high parental concern**. [S1]

## Examination red flags

- **Behaviour and appearance are the best guide** to whether serious illness is likely. [S1]
- **Breathing:** grunting, tachypnoea, increased work of breathing, hypoxia, apnoea, chest deformity. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Circulation:** pallor, mottling, cyanosis; tachy- or bradycardia; **central capillary refill ≥3 seconds**; murmur, weak or reduced femoral pulses, enlarged liver; weight gain from fluid overload; hypotension. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Disability/neuro:** lethargy, poor feeding, irritability, not responding to social cues, difficult to wake, weak/high-pitched/continuous cry, reduced tone, abnormal movements, focal signs, focal/complex/prolonged seizures. [S1]
- **Haematological:** non-blanching rash, bruising, bleeding. **Infection:** fever or hypothermia, bulging fontanelle, neck stiffness, **vesicular rash or encephalopathy (HSV)**. [S1]

## Don't miss

- ⚠️ **Primary HSV in the first month:** skin vesicles (absent in 1/3 of neonates, who can be afebrile), seizures or encephalopathy. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Malrotation with volvulus:** bile-stained vomit, abdominal distension. [S1]
- **Pyloric stenosis:** progressive, non-bilious projectile vomiting, olive mass, hypochloraemic hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis. **Intussusception:** intermittent severe pain, vomiting, pallor, lethargy, rectal bleeding (red currant stool is late). [S1]
- **NEC:** abdominal distension, tenderness, vomiting, blood in stool, abdominal wall discolouration or erythema. [S1]
- **Congenital heart disease:** cyanosis, murmur (not always), sweating with feeds, cardiac failure, poor or absent peripheral pulses. **SVT:** persistent marked tachycardia, pallor, poor feeding. [S1]
- **Congenital adrenal hyperplasia:** ambiguous genitalia, hypotension, dehydration, hyponatraemia, hyperkalaemia, hypoglycaemia. **Non-accidental injury:** bruising, unexplained injury, intracranial injury, neglect. [S1]

## Investigate

- **Unwell neonate or young infant:** BGL, VBG if available, FBE, UEC, LFT, CRP, blood culture, urine MCS (SPA). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Lumbar puncture in neonates <28 days unless contraindicated; consider it in young infants.** [S1]
- **By likely cause, consider** ammonia, viral swabs (e.g. HSV), ECG, and CXR, abdominal or neuroimaging. [S1]

## Treat

- ⚠️ **Every seriously unwell neonate or young infant:** prompt sepsis management with **early empiric antibiotics (IV/IM/intraosseous)**, aciclovir if indicated, adequate analgesia and sedation. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Fluids:** resuscitate as required; maintenance accounting for oral intake; **restrict to two-thirds of maintenance in meningitis or respiratory infection**; judicious in cardiac causes. [S1]
- **Suspected bowel obstruction: nasogastric tube on free drainage.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Suspected duct-dependent congenital heart disease in a neonate: discuss early with cardiology/ICU/retrieval and consider IV prostaglandin.** [S1]
- **Involve a senior clinician early and refer early** to paediatric, surgical or sub-specialist teams as indicated. [S1]
