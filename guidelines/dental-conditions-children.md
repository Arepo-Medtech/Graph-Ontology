# Dental conditions (non-traumatic) in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** caries prevention, dental abscess antibiotics, dental socket bleeding, natal teeth and oral habits in children. > ⚠️ **Antibiotics do not replace dental treatment:** once an abscess has formed, extraction or root canal therapy is usually needed.

## Prevention

- **Opportunistic education on oral hygiene and dental access** can prevent caries and odontogenic infection. [S1]
- **Caries affects more than 40% of Australian children** and can start as soon as teeth erupt. Early childhood caries mainly affects the upper front teeth, in breast- and bottle-fed children who keep feeding or comfort-sucking at night. [S1]
- **Wipe infants' teeth and gums; from over 12 months, brush twice daily with a smear of paediatric fluoridated toothpaste**, done or supervised by a parent. **Annual dental review.** [S1]
- **Persistent thumb-sucking or dummy use after 3 years: dental consultation and cessation counselling.** [S1]

## Assess

- **Ask about** cardiac conditions that may need endocarditis prophylaxis for dental procedures, and medicines that reduce saliva (e.g. antihistamines, anticonvulsants, antidepressants) or cause gum hypertrophy (e.g. phenytoin). [S1]
- **'Lift the lip'** to examine the upper front teeth and gums; look for white or brown spots or lines at the gum line that don't brush off. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Abscess signs:** tender gingival swelling or erythema, a draining gingival sinus, facial cellulitis over the tooth, submandibular or periorbital areas, trismus. **Fever and systemic symptoms may be absent.** [S1]

## Dental abscess

- **Once an abscess has formed, extraction or root canal therapy is usually required.** Definitive treatment, i.e. extraction, is still needed after pain and infection are treated. [S1]
- **Give antibiotics for spreading infection, systemic signs, or when definitive dental treatment will be delayed more than 24 hours.** [S1]
- **Localised infection:** e.g. **amoxicillin 25 mg/kg (max 500 mg) orally tds.** [S1]
- **Spreading, no systemic features:** e.g. **amoxicillin/clavulanate 22.5/3.2 mg/kg (max 875/125 mg) orally bd for 5 days.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Spreading infection with severe or systemic features:** e.g. **IV amoxicillin/clavulanate 6-hourly.** The page's dose figure is ambiguous and is not restated here — see Unresolved. [S1]
- **Follow local susceptibility patterns.** [S1]

## Dental socket bleeding

- **Bleeding after treatment** comes from a disturbed clot or a bleeding disorder. **Assess haemodynamic status**, rinse the mouth with cold water, and **bite on gauze for 30 minutes**; if bleeding continues, consider tranexamic-acid-soaked gauze. Treat any bleeding disorder; surgical dressing or sutures may be needed. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Severe bleeding:** IV access, FBE, coagulation studies, cross-match, IV fluids, firm pressure; **discuss with maxillofacial surgery and haematology.** [S1]

## Natal teeth

- **Usually no intervention.** Extract if very loose, an inhalation risk, interfering with breastfeeding, or ulcerating the tongue, frenulum or lip. [S1]

## Escalation and discharge

- **Involve the specialist paediatric dental team** for an abscess with severe or systemic features needing admission, or a child whose medical, developmental or behavioural needs call for specialist dental, anaesthetic or haematology input. [S1]
- **Transfer** if specialist dental or maxillofacial care is not available locally. [S1]
- **Discharge when** suitable for oral therapy, with cellulitis improving and extraction planned or done. [S1]
- **Public dental access** is set by each state; some children qualify for the Child Dental Benefits Schedule. [S1]
