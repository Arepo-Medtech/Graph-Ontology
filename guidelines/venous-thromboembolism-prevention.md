## What is being prevented

> **Venous thromboembolism is a major cause of morbidity and mortality for patients admitted to hospital, and
> a common preventable cause of in-hospital death** [S1].

- **Deep vein thrombosis (DVT)**: **blood clots form in veins, usually deep inside the legs or in the pelvis,
  where they may cause symptoms like pain, tenderness, redness, or swelling of the leg** [S1].
- **Pulmonary embolism (PE)**: **a blood clot breaks off, moves through the veins and blocks blood vessels in
  the lungs.** Symptoms include **shortness of breath, coughing up blood, chest pain, faintness, and loss of
  consciousness**. **If enough blood vessels are blocked in the lungs, the person can die** [S1].

**The goal of the Standard** is to **reduce avoidable death or disability caused by hospital-acquired VTE**
through improved identification of at-risk patients, **assessment of VTE *and* bleeding risk**, and
appropriate use of prevention methods — with patients receiving information so they can **share in decisions
with their clinicians both in and out of hospital** [S1].

## ⚠️ Two risks, assessed together, and reassessed every seven days

The Standard is built around a **balance**, not a protocol. Every statement that touches prophylaxis touches
both sides of it.

> **A patient assessed to be at risk of VTE has a prevention plan developed that balances the risk of
> thrombosis against the risk and consequences of bleeding (as an adverse effect of VTE prevention medicines).
> Other contraindications to VTE prevention methods are also considered before offering any to the patient**
> *(Quality statement 2)* [S1].

> **During hospitalisation, a patient's thrombosis and bleeding risk is reassessed and documented at intervals
> no longer than every seven days, whenever the patient's clinical condition or goals of care change, and on
> discharge from hospital. The patient is also monitored for VTE-related complications each time risk is
> reassessed** *(Quality statement 6)* [S1].

**A hard ceiling of seven days**, plus two event triggers — **a change in clinical condition**, and **a change
in goals of care**. That second trigger is easy to miss: a shift toward comfort care changes the
thrombosis–bleeding balance as much as a fall in platelets does.

**Reassessment must also include** [S1]: **a review of any VTE-related complications that may have occurred
(such as a clot or bleed)**, and **any medicine-related problems related to the use of medicines to prevent
VTE, such as erroneous concomitant prescribing**. **If VTE risk or bleeding risk changes during the admission,
review prophylaxis and adjust in line with a current, locally endorsed, evidence-based guideline and the
patient's preferences** [S1].

## Assessing risk

**Quality statement 1** [S1]: a **timely assessment of VTE risk using a locally endorsed evidence-based tool**,
with the result **documented at the time of the assessment, in a place that is easily accessible to all
clinicians involved in the patient's care**.

- **Use a standardised, locally endorsed, evidence-based tool or checklist** — the **NSW Clinical Excellence
  Commission Adult VTE Risk Assessment Tool** is given as an example [S1].
- **Ensure the tool includes an assessment of patient and admission-related factors that are known to increase
  VTE risk** [S1].
- > **Use the best possible medication history (BPMH) obtained as part of the admission process to identify
  > medicines the patient might be taking (including complementary and over-the-counter medicines)** [S1].

**For bleeding risk** *(QS2)* [S1]: **a standardised, locally endorsed evidence-based tool or checklist**
assessing **the reason for admission, the patient's comorbidities, bleeding history, and medication history** —
again using the **BPMH** to identify **complementary and over-the-counter medicines that increase the risk of
bleeding**. **Appendix 1 of the Standard lists medicines that can affect bleeding risk.**

**The same medication history feeds both assessments**, and in both cases the source specifically names
**complementary and over-the-counter** medicines — the ones least likely to be volunteered.

## Prevention methods

**Quality statement 5** [S1]: **medicines and/or mechanical methods of VTE prevention according to a current,
locally endorsed, evidence-based guideline**, taking into consideration **the patient's clinical condition and
their preferences**.

> **Provide information and education about the recommended prophylaxis at the time it is initiated to reduce
> the likelihood of harm associated with its use** — **the risks and benefits, how to use it, how long to use
> it for, possible side effects and how they should be managed, and any associated precautions** [S1].

> **Invite the patient to ask questions, and use methods such as teach-back to confirm the patient understands
> how to use their** prophylaxis [S1].

**Teach-back at initiation**, because much of this prophylaxis continues after the patient goes home and is
self-administered.

## Informing the patient

**Quality statement 3** [S1]: information and education **tailored to their risks and needs**, and the patient
**shares in decisions** regarding the plan. It covers:

- **VTE and ways to potentially prevent it, in a format they can understand**
- > **the importance of mobilising as soon as possible and remaining hydrated, unless mobility or fluid
  > restrictions are in place**
- **the findings of their VTE risk assessment and their individual risk factors**
- **the risks and benefits of prophylaxis — how it works, correct use, monitoring requirements, available
  options to manage potential adverse events such as bleeding, precautions, and the potential outcomes if
  prophylaxis is not used correctly**
- **the symptoms of VTE and bleeding, how to minimise the risk of these occurring, and what to do if they
  occur**

**Mobilisation and hydration are the two things the patient can do themselves**, and they are listed second —
ahead of the drug.

## Documenting and communicating the plan

**Quality statement 4** [S1]: the plan is **documented and communicated to all clinicians involved in the
patient's care** — on **a national standard medication chart (paper or electronic)**, or the medical record,
approved risk assessment form, or other place per local policy.

> **Ensure other clinicians involved in the patient's care are aware that a prevention plan has been documented
> so it can be actioned** [S1].

**Documented is not the same as actioned**, and the Standard separates the two.

## Transition from hospital

**Quality statement 7** [S1]: a **written discharge plan or care plan before they leave hospital**, describing
**ongoing, individualised care to prevent VTE following discharge**; **discussed with the patient before they
leave** to ensure they understand the recommended care and follow-up; and **communicated to the patient's GP or
ongoing clinical provider within 48 hours of discharge**.

**Before a patient leaves hospital, reassess their risk of VTE and bleeding to determine the need for
prophylaxis after discharge** [S1]. The plan should **summarise the reason for admission, risk factors for
VTE, any prophylaxis used while in hospital, and any ongoing prophylaxis needed** [S1].

## The three indicators

| Indicator [S1] | |
|---|---|
| **1a** | **Proportion of patients admitted to hospital who were assessed for VTE risk within 24 hours of admission, using a locally endorsed risk assessment tool, and had the outcome documented in their medical record** |
| **5a** | **Proportion of patients prescribed VTE prophylaxis appropriate to their VTE and bleeding risks in accordance with the locally approved guideline/policy** |
| **7a** | **Proportion of patients discharged on VTE prophylaxis that had a documented care plan that included the prescribed medicine(s), dose and duration of treatment** |

**Seven quality statements, three indicators** — the fewest indicators per statement of any standard in this
compendium. What is measured is: **assessed within 24 hours**, **prescribed appropriately to *both* risks**,
and **discharged with medicine, dose and duration written down**.

**Indicator 1a supplies the 24-hour clock** that the quality statement itself only calls "timely".

**No benchmarks are set for these indicators by the Commission**; definitions are at **METEOR** [S1].

## Cultural safety and equity

This Standard carries the Commission's cultural safety and equity section, reproduced in full in the
**acute coronary syndromes** guideline of this compendium. Its core statement applies here unchanged:

> **Health outcomes for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples can be improved by addressing systemic
> racism and other root causes that reduce access to care** ... **it is recognised that cultural safety is
> determined by the Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander individuals, families and communities experiencing
> the care** [S1].

## Where this connects in the compendium

| Guideline here | Connection |
|---|---|
| **Hip fracture care** | *"prescribe... thromboprophylaxis according to current guidelines"* — **this is the standard governing that instruction**; hip fracture patients are a high-VTE-risk surgical population |
| **Atrial fibrillation** | the same thrombosis-versus-bleeding balance, with the AF guideline's caution that **bleeding risk scores should not be used to avoid anticoagulation** |
| **Acute coronary syndromes** | patients discharged on aspirin or dual antiplatelet therapy arrive here with an altered bleeding risk |
| **Stroke and TIA** | shared antithrombotic decisions |
| **Delirium, dementia, hip fracture** | **immobility** is the common thread; mobilisation is prophylaxis in all four |
