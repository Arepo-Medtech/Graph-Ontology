# Kawasaki disease

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** diagnosis of complete and incomplete Kawasaki disease, risk stratification, IVIg, aspirin and corticosteroid treatment, and disposition. The prolonged-fever entry point is `febrile-child`.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 23 of 23 claims quoted verbatim from the **RCH Melbourne Clinical Practice Guideline *Kawasaki disease*** (Last updated January 2026); **43 fragments re-checked by machine. 4 quoted doses.**

> ⚠️ **Timely IVIg under senior guidance reduces coronary artery aneurysm risk. Do not wait for echocardiography to treat.**

> ⚠️ **Infants under 12 months are at higher risk of incomplete KD and aneurysms** and may present with prolonged fever and few features.

---

## Recognise it

- **KD is an acute medium-vessel vasculitis with a predilection for the coronary arteries**; untreated, about 25% develop coronary artery aneurysms. [S1]
- **Complete KD = fever ≥4 days (day 1 = fever onset) AND at least 4 principal features at any point** (not necessarily together) **AND no better alternative explanation.** [S1]
- **Features appear sequentially — ask about ones that have already resolved.** [S1]
- **Principal features:** bilateral non-exudative painless conjunctival injection, often limbal-sparing; polymorphous erythematous rash (not bullous, vesicular or haemorrhagic); palmar/plantar erythema with painful oedema and/or periungual desquamation at 2–3 weeks; unilateral tender anterior cervical nodes ≥1.5 cm; lip, tongue ('strawberry') or oropharyngeal erythema (not exudative or ulcerated). [S1]
- **Incomplete KD:** suggestive presentation with fewer than 4 principal features; needs abnormal investigations to support it. **Infants and adolescents often present incompletely and have higher cardiac risk.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Infants under 12 months with prolonged unexplained fever — even with normal inflammatory markers — should have an echocardiogram.** [S1]
- **Differentials:** a positive respiratory virus PCR does not exclude KD (about 1/3 are positive); consider KD when GAS infection fails to improve after 24–48 hours of antibiotics, and in an infant with prolonged fever and unexplained irritability or aseptic meningitis. [S1]

## Risk stratification

- **High risk of CAA:** age ≤6 months, or right coronary or LAD Z-score ≥2.5. [S1]
- **Other factors that may increase IVIg resistance or CAA risk:** age 6–12 months; coronary Z-score ≥2; IVIg ≥10 days after fever onset; IVIg resistance; KD shock syndrome or MAS; CRP ≥130 mg/L; East/South/mixed Asian ethnicity; anaemia; hypoalbuminaemia; hyponatraemia. [S1]

## Investigate

- **No single test diagnoses KD.** In all children consider FBE, CRP, ESR, UEC, LFTs, blood culture, urine microscopy and culture, ECG, and serum stored before IVIg. **Do not repeat ESR after IVIg** — it is falsely raised. [S1]
- ⚠️ **A normal echocardiogram does not exclude KD, and waiting for one should not delay treatment.** Suggested schedule: baseline at presentation, follow-up at 4–6 weeks. [S1]

## Treat

- ⚠️ **All children with KD: IVIg 2 g/kg as a single infusion**, usually at incrementing rates over 8–12 hours. Early treatment improves coronary outcomes; timing per senior advice and local protocol. [S1]
- **Give IVIg without delay when clinically safe; still give it beyond 10 days of fever if inflammation is active** (fever, raised markers, abnormal coronaries). [S1]
- **Aspirin 5 mg/kg orally daily** to prevent thrombosis, until follow-up cardiology review (~6 weeks post onset). Higher doses do not improve coronary outcomes. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Avoid NSAIDs while on aspirin** — ibuprofen may reduce its cardioprotective effect. [S1]
- **High CAA risk (or clinician discretion): add corticosteroids** — example regimen 2 mg/kg once daily IV methylprednisolone or oral prednisolone (max 50 mg), tapered over 2–4 weeks by response; follow local protocols or subspecialty advice. [S1]
- **IVIg resistance** (fever ≥38°C 36 hours or more after the first IVIg finishes): consider other causes and consult local KD experts; options include a **second IVIg dose** and adding **infliximab or corticosteroids**. [S1]
- **Antithrombotic therapy for CAAs is managed with cardiology.** [S1]
- **Resolving KD** (convalescent, no fever, normal markers): start aspirin and refer for echocardiography. [S1]
- **KD shock syndrome** (hypotension, poor perfusion or myocardial dysfunction): consider a slower IVIg infusion. **Fever during infusion may be KD activity** — consider paracetamol 30 minutes before and regularly during. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Delay live vaccines (MMR, MMRV, varicella, rotavirus) for 11 months after IVIg**; if measles exposure risk is high, vaccinate and repeat after 11 months. [S1]

## Disposition

- **Consult the paediatric team whenever KD is suspected. Transfer** if there is cardiac involvement or timely echocardiography is unavailable locally. [S1]
- **Discharge when afebrile and well ≥36 hours after treatment**, with daily aspirin, a follow-up plan including repeat echo, and education to re-present with fever or returning symptoms. [S1]
