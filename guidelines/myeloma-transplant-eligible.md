# Newly diagnosed multiple myeloma — transplant-eligible patients

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** induction, stem-cell mobilisation, high-dose melphalan with autologous transplant, consolidation, maintenance and follow-up for adults with newly diagnosed myeloma judged fit for transplant. Transplant-ineligible patients are covered by a separate MSAG statement (not yet authored). PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

> ✅ **PARAPHRASED, HASH-ANCHORED.** 20 claims paraphrased from **Medical and Scientific Advisory Group (MSAG) of Myeloma Australia, Internal Medicine Journal — *Management of patients with newly diagnosed multiple myeloma who are eligible for autologous stem cell transplantation: position statement of the Medical and Scientific Advisory Group of Myeloma Australia*** (published 4 August 2025; origin: AU). **32 anchors re-checkable by machine; 2 doses.** The source's words are not reproduced: its licence is *CC BY-NC 4.0*.

> ⚠️ **Australia differs from the international standard here, because of PBS funding.** A CD38 antibody (daratumumab) added to induction is the global standard, but it is **not PBS-reimbursed upfront**. So RVD remains the Australian standard induction.

## Who is eligible

- **Transplant eligibility is an individual judgement:** age, comorbidity, frailty, disability and the patient's wishes all count. Frailty and comorbidity indices can help, but none is validated for this decision. [S1]
- **Selected fit patients up to age 75 can be transplanted.** Trials mostly capped age at 65, yet data show transplant can be done safely in chosen older patients without a significant rise in transplant-related harm. [S1]

## Induction

- **Give 3 to 6 induction cycles before transplant, aiming for at least a partial response.** (Grade A, Level II) [S1]
- ⚠️ **RVD (lenalidomide, bortezomib, dexamethasone) is the recommended induction for most Australian patients**, because a CD38 antibody is not funded upfront. (Grade A, Level II) [S1]
- **Bortezomib by subcutaneous injection where possible.** A modified **weekly** schedule reduces neuropathy; keep **twice-weekly** dosing for when fast control matters, such as renal failure from cast nephropathy. (Grade B, Level III-3) [S1]
- **In acute severe renal impairment**, another bortezomib-based regimen (VD, VCD or VTD) can be used for induction instead. (Grade B, Level II) [S1]
- **With systemic AL amyloidosis**, where full-dose lenalidomide is poorly tolerated, use **daratumumab with cyclophosphamide, bortezomib and dexamethasone**, which the PBS funds for this. (Grade A, Level II) [S1]
- **Without a partial response to RVD, the best next step is uncertain.** Proceeding to transplant is reasonable; trials or salvage can be considered, but the PBS funds a new regimen only on proven progression. [S1]

## Stem cell mobilisation

- **Collect stem cells after 3–4 cycles of RVD, whether transplant is planned now or deferred.** (Grade A, Level II) [S1]
- **Three ways to mobilise:** G-CSF (10 mcg/kg) on its own; G-CSF plus plerixafor; or G-CSF plus cyclophosphamide (1.5–2 g/m²). When chemotherapy is part of it, choose the **intermediate** cyclophosphamide range over high doses (>2 g/m²): yields are much the same. (Grade B, Level III-3) [S1]
- **Use plerixafor when G-CSF alone or cyclophosphamide with G-CSF fails;** the PBS funds it only after failed chemo-mobilisation. (Grade B, Level II) [S1]

## High-dose melphalan

- ⚠️ **Melphalan 200 mg/m² for patients with normal kidney function.** Consider 140 mg/m² when eGFR is below 30 mL/min/1.73m². If a patient is judged unfit for 200 mg/m², reconsider whether transplant is appropriate at all. [S1]
- **Upfront transplant after induction is still the standard of care.** Deferral is reasonable in standard-risk disease, because overall survival was equal in both major trials. [S1]
- **Tandem (second) transplant may still suit eligible high-risk patients, particularly with del17p;** consider it in young high-risk patients, then give maintenance. [S1]

## After transplant

- **Consolidation has uncertain benefit.** Discuss it; offering it is reasonable if induction was well tolerated and there is no peripheral neuropathy. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Lenalidomide maintenance improves survival and is standard after transplant.** Continue until progression or unacceptable toxicity. [S1]
- **Warn patients about second cancers**, especially after 2–3 years from transplant, and keep up age-appropriate cancer screening. [S1]
- **In Australia, dual maintenance is not funded,** so lenalidomide stays the standard even in high-risk disease. [S1]
- **Assess response 3 months after transplant.** Bone marrow biopsy is needed to confirm complete response. MRD testing is possible but not funded in Australia. [S1]
- **Allogeneic transplant has a limited role outside trials;** if considered, it belongs in an experienced centre. [S1]
