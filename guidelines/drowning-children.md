# Drowning in children

## Background

- **Most drowning causes minimal or no respiratory impairment** and needs no admission beyond a period of observation. [S1]
- **'Near-drowning', 'secondary drowning' and 'wet drowning' are no longer used** and do not influence management. [S1]

## Assess

- **Timeline:** immersion time; time to and type of basic life support; time to first respiratory effort. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Consider supervision and safety:** look for inconsistencies in the story, or a story incompatible with developmental age. [S1]
- ⚠️ **In a competent swimmer, look for a medical cause:** seizures, hypoglycaemia, arrhythmias including Long QT syndrome, intoxication. [S1]
- **Cervical spine injury is uncommon** but consider it with a history of trauma such as diving. [S1]

## Investigate

- **Asymptomatic, alert children need no investigations**; otherwise be guided by history and condition. **Chest X-ray findings do not correlate with outcome.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Altered mental state without significant hypoxia → look for another cause:** hypothermia, traumatic brain injury, co-existing medical condition, hypoglycaemia. [S1]
- **ECG** may help diagnose arrhythmias and Long QT syndrome. [S1]

## Treat

- **Principles: maintain oxygenation, prevent aspiration, stabilise body temperature.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Vomiting and aspiration of gastric contents are common and a major complication — place spontaneously breathing children in the recovery (lateral decubitus) position.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **No prophylactic antibiotics.** Reserve antibiotics for signs of infection or sepsis, or the rare submersion in grossly contaminated water. [S1]
- **Hypothermia is common:** remove wet clothes, dry the child, minimise exposure. [S1]
- **Actively warm to normothermia if core temperature is below 34 °C and there is no evidence of brain injury** — warmed IV fluids, humidified oxygen, forced-air warming blankets. [S1]
- **Hypoxic brain injury:** there is some evidence for therapeutic hypothermia — **discuss with a paediatric intensivist.** [S1]

## Escalation and discharge

- **Consult the paediatric team** for increased respiratory effort, **SpO2 under 95%**, abnormal lung examination, or a suspected underlying medical cause. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Consider transfer** for persisting altered conscious state, respiratory compromise needing assisted ventilation, or ongoing hypoxia. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Discharge when:** observed **8 hours from the time of drowning**, asymptomatic, normal respiratory examination, **SpO2 ≥95%**, water-safety education given, no ongoing safety concerns, and social work referral made if appropriate. [S1]
- **Adverse prognostic indicators:** submersion over 5 minutes · effective basic life support after more than 10 minutes · arrival with CPR in progress · GCS under 5 · rectal temperature under 30 °C · arterial pH under 7.1 on arrival. [S1]
