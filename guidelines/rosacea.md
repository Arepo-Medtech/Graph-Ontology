## The disease

**A chronic and relapsing inflammatory skin disorder characterised by central facial erythema, flushing and
telangiectasia; inflammatory papules and pustules may be present** [A1].

⚠️ **The aim of treatment is to reduce the symptoms** [A1] — there is no claim of cure or remission here,
unlike the psoriasis topic which states remission as a goal.

## ⚠️ Antibiotics are used as anti-inflammatories

> **Antibiotics are used for their anti-inflammatory effects in papulopustular rosacea** [A1].

That single practice point reframes the whole treatment: doxycycline here is **not** treating infection, which
is why the dose is lower than an antibacterial dose and why courses run for months.

## Treatment by severity

| Presentation | Treatment [A1] |
|---|---|
| **Mild inflammatory papules or pustules** | **topical metronidazole or azelaic acid** — ⚠️ **metronidazole is preferred as it is less irritating than azelaic acid**. **Topical ivermectin may be used as an alternative** |
| **Moderate-to-severe papules/pustules, or topical therapy inadequate** | **oral antibacterials** — **doxycycline is the drug of choice**. **Erythromycin may be used if doxycycline is unsuccessful or contraindicated, but evidence of its efficacy is limited** |
| **More severe, or topical alone inadequate** | **combination oral doxycycline with topical metronidazole or azelaic acid has been used, but evidence is limited** |
| **Persistent facial erythema** | **topical brimonidine, applied daily as required** |
| **Non-response or recurrence** | ⚠️ **refer to a dermatologist; other treatments may be suitable, eg isotretinoin** |

## ⚠️ Timeframes — the reason patients stop too early

> **Improvement may occur after 2–4 weeks but the full effect often takes up to 6–12 weeks; long-term
> metronidazole treatment may be needed** [A1].

**Oral antibacterials**: **a 4–12-week course is often used and repeated as required; in some cases long-term
treatment is needed** [A1].

## Dosing

> ⚠️ **Paraphrased from AMH monographs, no stored quote, flagged `dose: true`. Check before prescribing.**

| Agent | Dose [A1] |
|---|---|
| **Metronidazole, topical** | **gel or cream, rub a thin film into affected areas twice a day** |
| **Azelaic acid** | **apply morning and night** |
| **Ivermectin, topical** | **apply once a day for up to 4 months; repeat course if needed** |
| ⚠️ **Brimonidine, topical** | **once a day or less often if desired**. **Start with a small amount of gel, then increase gradually after 1 week to no more than a small, pea-sized amount to each area of the face** |
| **Doxycycline** | **adult, oral 50 mg once daily** |
| **Erythromycin** | **adult and child >12 years, oral 250–500 mg twice daily** |
| **Isotretinoin** (specialist) | **usual initial 0.25–0.3 mg/kg daily in 1 or 2 doses**, adjusted to response; **maximum 1 mg/kg daily**, which **may be poorly tolerated**. ⚠️ **Use the lowest effective dose, which may be very low, eg 10 mg 3 times a week** |

⚠️ **Note the doxycycline dose: 50 mg once daily** — half the acne starting point's eventual step-up, and
given for anti-inflammatory effect.

**Isotretinoin in rosacea**: **specialists may continue treatment, eg for 4–8 weeks, after the inflammatory
lesions are well controlled**, and ⚠️ **papulopustular rosacea often relapses after stopping isotretinoin;
repeat courses or long-term treatment may be needed** [A1].

## Counselling — trigger avoidance

**Minimise or avoid factors that cause flushing or skin irritation** [A1]: **emotional stress**, **extremes of
temperature**, **alcohol**, **hot drinks**, **spicy foods**, **sun or wind exposure**, **chemical exfoliating
agents**.

**Use a soap substitute with a pH of around 6** [A1].
