# Hydrofluoric acid exposure in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** skin, eye, ingestion and inhalation exposure to hydrofluoric acid (HF) in children: fluorosis risk, calcium gluconate gel, IV calcium, bicarbonate and magnesium in arrest, and disposition. > ⚠️ **HF is systemically toxic by any route: fluorosis causes hypocalcaemia and arrhythmia.** Every at-risk child (**all ingestions**) goes to resus with continuous cardiac monitoring. **Never use calcium chloride for local or regional infiltration.**

---

## Key points

- ⚠️ **Skin exposure alone can cause systemic hypocalcaemia.** Pain may be delayed and out of proportion to what is visible. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not use local anaesthetic techniques for pain** — they can mask ongoing symptoms. [S1]
- **HF is corrosive and systemically toxic by any route through fluorosis, which shows as hypocalcaemia.** Sources: rust cleaners, car wheel cleaners (aluminium brightener), glass etching, industry. Concentrations run up to 70%; domestic products are likely under 5%. [S1]

## Assessment

- **All exposures need medical assessment.** ⚠️ **Hypocalcaemia risk:** over 1% BSA with over 50% HF · over 5% BSA at any concentration · **any ingestion.** [S1]
- **Fluorosis (any route) → hypocalcaemia and hypomagnesaemia:** perioral numbness, paraesthesiae, cramps · irritability, seizures · tetany · Chovostek's and Trosseau's signs · ⚠️ arrhythmias (long QT) · heart failure. [S1]
- **Skin pain is immediate with over 50% HF but can be delayed up to 24 hours with weaker solutions**, and may precede any blistering or erythema as a marker of tissue destruction. [S1]
- **Ingestion:** under 20% irritates the oesophagus; higher concentrations may burn. **Inhalation:** immediate irritation, but cough, wheeze and breathlessness may be delayed. [S1]
- **Investigations:** electrolytes and blood gas (calcium, magnesium, potassium) · **ECG — prolonged QT marks hypocalcaemia** · consider urgent endoscopy after ingestion. [S1]

## First aid and decontamination

- **Apply calcium gluconate 2.5% gel to contaminated skin; repeat every 15 minutes until pain stops.** [S1]
- **No gel available?** Make one by adding 10 mL of calcium gluconate 10% injection to 30 mL of sterile surgical lubricant. [S1]
- **Remove clothing and jewellery into sealed bags out of patient areas; wash off, scrubbing under nails.** ⚠️ **Staff wear PPE including chemical-resistant gloves.** [S1]
- **Flush eyes with 0.9% saline.** ⚠️ **Ingestion: activated charcoal is not indicated.** [S1]

## Resuscitation

- ⚠️ **Everyone at risk of fluorosis — including all ingestions — is managed in a resuscitation area on continuous cardiac monitoring.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Cardiac arrest or ventricular arrhythmia: calcium gluconate 10% 0.5 mL/kg, max 60 mL** — higher than the standard maximum. [S1]
- **Plus IV sodium bicarbonate (1 mmol/mL) 1 mL/kg (1 mmol/kg).** [S1]
- **Plus IV magnesium sulfate 50% (2 mmol/mL):** VF **0.05–0.1 mL/kg (0.1–0.2 mmol/kg) slow IV**; deficiency **0.2 mL/kg (0.4 mmol/kg) over 4 hours.** [S1]

## Ongoing care

- **Correct hypocalcaemia with calcium gluconate 10% 0.5 mL/kg**; repeated and higher doses may be needed — discuss with a toxicologist. Correct other electrolytes too. [S1]
- **Skin:** calcium gluconate 2.5% gel topically, repeated every 15 minutes. **Inhalation: nebulised 1 mL calcium gluconate 10% in 3 mL 0.9% sodium chloride.** [S1]
- **Refractory pain:** discuss with Poisons Information (131126) — subcutaneous calcium gluconate 0.5 mL/cm², regional IV or intra-arterial infusion. ⚠️ **Never use calcium chloride for local or regional infiltration: it causes tissue damage.** [S1]
- **Monitor for 12 hours if at risk of systemic fluorosis.** [S1]

## Disposition

- **Consider admission for all adolescents with intentional exposure**; assess self-harm risk before ward transfer, but **never delay urgent care such as ICU admission.** [S1]
- **Transfer** if care is beyond the local hospital's comfort. **PIPER: 1300 137 650.** [S1]
- **Discharge:** pain controlled with simple analgesia only · normal GCS · normal ECG (if required) · observation period complete · after deliberate ingestion, low assessed risk of further self-harm. [S1]
