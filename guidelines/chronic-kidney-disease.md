## Why detection is the whole problem

CKD is **silent until as much as 90% of kidney function is lost**, and **fewer than 10% of people with CKD
are aware they have it** [S1]. It is twice as common as diabetes: **more than 2 million Australians live
with CKD**, and **1 in 10 Australians — 1 in 5 First Nations Australians — aged 18 and over are living with
signs of it** [S1].

The consequence that reframes CKD as a cardiovascular problem: **people with CKD are up to 20 times more
likely to die from a heart attack or stroke than to progress to kidney failure** [S1].

Detected early and managed appropriately, deterioration in kidney function **can be reduced by as much as
50%**, and new treatments can slow progression **by up to 15 years** [S1]. **Late referral is common — 17%
of people commence dialysis within 90 days of referral to a kidney service** [S1].

## Definition

CKD is [S1]:

- an estimated or measured **GFR <60 mL/min/1.73m² present for ≥3 months**, with or without evidence of
  kidney damage; **or**
- **evidence of kidney damage present for ≥3 months**, with or without decreased GFR — albuminuria;
  haematuria after exclusion of urological causes; structural abnormalities (e.g. on imaging); or
  pathological abnormalities (e.g. biopsy).

The ≥3 months applies to both limbs. A single abnormal result is not CKD.

## Who to test, and how often

**Three out of four Australian adults have at least one factor increasing their risk** [S1], so detection is
targeted rather than universal.

| Risk factor | Kidney Health Check frequency [S1] |
|---|---|
| Diabetes | **annually** |
| Hypertension | **annually** |
| **First Nations Australians aged ≥18** | **annually** |
| Established cardiovascular disease* | every 2 years |
| Family history of kidney failure | every 2 years |
| Obesity (BMI ≥30 kg/m²) | every 2 years |
| Current or past smoker/vaper | every 2 years |
| History of acute kidney injury | **yearly for the first 3 years post-AKI**, then every 2 years |
| Aged ≥60 | once off, unless other indications develop |

\* previous diagnosis of coronary heart disease, cerebrovascular or peripheral vascular disease [S1].

**The Kidney Health Check is three things: blood pressure, uACR, eGFR** [S1]. First morning void is preferred
for uACR. Consider ambulatory or home BP monitoring in addition to in-clinic readings.

Also recommended after **hypertensive disorders of pregnancy** including pre-eclampsia and
pregnancy-induced hypertension, and for people who had **AKI as a complication of COVID-19, or long
COVID** [S1].

**First Nations Australians experience a higher burden, earlier onset and faster progression of kidney
disease, due to ongoing impacts of colonisation** [S1] — which is why the screening interval differs and the
age threshold is 18.

## Treatment goals

| Parameter | Goal [S1] |
|---|---|
| **Blood pressure** | **consistently below 130/80 mmHg for all people with CKD** |
| **Albuminuria** | **a reduction in uACR of at least 30%** |
| Glycaemic control | BGL 6–8 mmol/L fasting, 8–10 mmol/L postprandial; HbA1c ≤53 mmol/mol (range 48–58), ≤7% (range 6.5–7.5), individualised |
| **Potassium** | **K⁺ ≤6.0 mmol/L** |
| **Lipids** | **no target serum cholesterol level recommended.** Use statin ± ezetimibe to reduce CVD events and death in people with CKD (eGFR ≥15) and **CVD risk ≥10%**; **First Nations Australians with CKD and CVD risk ≥5%** |
| Salt | <5 g per day |
| Weight | BMI <25 kg/m² (<23 Asian population); waist <94 cm men (<90 cm Asian men), <80 cm women |
| Physical activity | 2.5–5 hours moderate intensity weekly; muscle strengthening ≥2× weekly; some activity better than none |
| Alcohol | ≤10 standard drinks per week, ≤4 on any one day. **No specific safe level is recommended in CKD** |
| Immunisation | influenza, pneumococcal, COVID-19, herpes zoster, plus NIP schedule |

Two of these deserve emphasis because they differ from general practice habits:

**Lipids are managed by risk, not by a cholesterol number.** The handbook recommends *no target serum
cholesterol level* [S1] — the decision is statin ± ezetimibe based on CVD risk, with a **lower threshold
(≥5%) for First Nations Australians**.

**Albuminuria has a target of its own** — at least a 30% reduction in uACR [S1] — making it a treatment
endpoint rather than only a staging marker.

## What is new in the 5th edition

The handbook lists [S1]: new medications to slow progression and reduce CVD risk (**SGLT-2 inhibitors and
non-steroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists**), a revised detection and diagnosis algorithm,
**guidelines for culturally safe kidney care for First Nations Australians**, how the updated **Aus CVD risk
calculator** applies at varying CKD stages, expanded nutrition guidance, a new nephrology referral algorithm,
a **stepwise approach to albuminuria reduction**, and new sections on genetic kidney disease, pharmacological
management, heart failure and CKD, pain management, contraception and pregnancy, and hyperuricaemia.
