# Androgenetic alopecia

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-22 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults. ⚠️ **Female pattern hair loss is covered only where the sources address it** — see
*Unresolved*. **Paediatric alopecia is out of scope.**

> **Mixed sources.** **S1** is the **open** RACGP *AJGP* case study (**November 2025**), **quoted verbatim**
> — 14 claims, 6 of them doses. **A1** is the **Australian Medicines Handbook** across three pages,
> **paraphrased** — 17 claims, **no passage stored**.

**AMH topic closed by this guideline:** *Alopecia, androgenetic*.

---

## ⚠️ Diagnose before treating [S1]

| Diagnosis | Presentation |
|---|---|
| **Androgenic alopecia** | **gradual onset, recession over the temples and loss on the vertex**; **no scarring, inflammation or scaling**; ⚠️ **NEGATIVE hair pull test**; **may have family history** |
| **Telogen effluvium** | **acute onset DIFFUSE loss**, **often preceded by a trigger (stress, surgery, illness, medication)**; ⚠️ **POSITIVE hair pull test** |
| **Alopecia areata** | **focal patches of more severe loss exposing smooth skin**; **spontaneously regrows**; ⚠️ **POSITIVE hair pull test**; **associated with other autoimmune diseases** |
| **Hypothyroidism** | **slow-growing, brittle, dry and coarse hair**, with **hypothyroid symptoms** |
| **Trichotillomania** | **a psychiatric condition involving hair pulling** |

⚠️ **The hair pull test separates the commonest three.**

## ⚠️ Who responds

> **Response is best when many thin, miniature hairs still remain before starting treatment.** ⚠️ **NO
> BENEFIT IS SEEN WHERE THERE IS NO VISIBLE HAIR** [A1].

**Hair growth may be marginally stimulated or stabilised — hair loss stopped or slowed — in mild-to-moderate
thinning** by **topical minoxidil in both men and women, or low-dose oral finasteride in men** [A1].

⚠️ **Unlikely to respond if over 60 years** [A1], and **safety and efficacy data in men over 41 years are
limited** [A1].

## What the trial showed

> **A randomised controlled trial of 428 men compared the three most common regimens, observing hair
> regrowth in 80.5% taking oral finasteride, 59.0% applying topical minoxidil and 94.1% taking combined
> treatment at 12-month follow-up** [S1].

**Topical minoxidil or oral finasteride monotherapy is first line in men with mild-to-moderate disease.**
⚠️ **Combination might be considered, although the added efficacy may not be enough to justify the increased
risk of adverse effects.** **In severe cases, combination therapy is recommended from the outset** [S1].

⚠️ **Although there are no known physiological sequelae, the cosmetic impact may generate considerable
social anxiety — so early combination therapy might be warranted in particularly concerned patients** [S1].

## Doses

| | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Topical minoxidil** | **1 mL of 5% solution per day** [S1]; **liquid 1 mL twice a day**, or **foam ~half a capful (1 g) twice a day for men, once a day for women** [A1] | ⚠️ **poorer adherence than oral**; **over the counter**; **follow-up at 6 months** [S1] |
| ⚠️ **Oral minoxidil** | ⚠️ **optimal dosing not yet established** — **most commonly 2.5 mg/day, escalating to 5 mg if response is inadequate; other common starting doses 1 mg or 1.25 mg** [S1] | ⚠️ **doses under 2.5 mg must be compounded**; **often compounded with 1 mg finasteride**; ⚠️ **poorer side-effect profile than topical** [S1] |
| **Oral finasteride** | **1 mg once daily** [S1][A1] | ⚠️ **dose not commonly escalated** [S1]; ⚠️ **a dose increase does not provide additional benefit** [A1]; **follow-up at 6–12 months** [S1] |

> ⚠️ **NONE of the three is covered under the PBS for androgenetic alopecia** [S1]. **Minoxidil is over the
> counter; both orals need a prescription** [S1].

⚠️ **A1 covers ONLY topical minoxidil.** Its androgenetic alopecia topic names **"topical minoxidil"** and
its monograph is **Minoxidil (skin)**. ⚠️ **Oral minoxidil appears in S1 alone — a coverage gap in A1, not a
disagreement.**

### Timing and stopping

| | [A1] | [S1] |
|---|---|---|
| **First visible benefit** | **3–4 months** | ⚠️ **six months of continuous use is needed to recognise benefit** |
| **Maximal response** | **after 12 months of continuous treatment**; **maximum improvement with finasteride within 1–2 years, maintained above baseline for 5 years while hair loss was progressive with placebo** | — |
| ⚠️ **Stop if no benefit** | ⚠️ **minoxidil at 6 months; finasteride at 12 months** | — |
| ⚠️ **On stopping** | ⚠️ **benefit is lost within 6–12 months** | — |

⚠️ **Warn about the initial shed:** **a temporary increase in hair loss may occur during the first months**
[A1] — **as minoxidil moves follicles into the anagen (growth) phase** [S1]. **Encourage progress
photographs** [S1].

**Continuous treatment is required to maintain response**, and ⚠️ **generally not all hair loss is
recovered** [A1].

### Using topical minoxidil [A1]

**Apply to dry skin and hair**; **wash hands well after application**; **allow the product to dry before
going to bed**. ⚠️ **Do not wear a wig, scarf or hat for at least 1 hour after application, as this may
increase absorption.** ⚠️ **Wait at least 4 hours before washing the hair.** **Avoid contact with eyes.**

⚠️ **Systemic absorption is normally minimal, but excess use, occlusion or application to inflamed or
atrophied skin may result in absorption sufficient to cause systemic effects** [A1].

## Adverse effects

### Minoxidil

| | Topical 5% [S1] | Oral 1 mg [S1] |
|---|---|---|
| **Scalp pruritus / contact dermatitis** | ⚠️ **19% at 24 weeks** | **not associated** |
| ⚠️ **Hypertrichosis** | **4%** | ⚠️ **27%** |
| **Limb oedema** | — | **4%** |

**A1 adds:** **itch, erythema, dry skin, mild dermatitis, allergic contact dermatitis from either propylene
glycol in the liquid product or minoxidil**, and ⚠️ **hypertrichosis appears more common in women,
particularly if the higher strength is used, usually on the face** [A1].

⚠️ **The higher concentration is slightly more effective in men but there is no difference in women** —
and **increasing the dose volume does not provide additional benefit** [A1].

**Pregnancy: avoid (limited data). Breastfeeding: appears safe, low topical absorption** [A1].

### Finasteride

**S1's quantified risks** [S1]: ⚠️ **a 2.22-fold increased risk of erectile dysfunction (95% CI 1.03–4.78)**;
**a 1.08-fold risk of decreased libido and 1.75-fold risk of ejaculatory dysfunction**, ⚠️ **although these
findings are not statistically significant.**

**A1** lists **impotence, decreased libido and ejaculation disorder as infrequent**, with **breast tenderness
and enlargement, allergic reaction, poor semen quality, infertility and depression as rare** [A1].

> ⚠️ **On the psychiatric question, S1 is careful:** **finasteride has been associated with depression and
> suicidality, HOWEVER these findings stem from cohort studies that are not protected from confounding, and
> it remains possible that treatment-seeking hair loss patients are more predisposed to adverse mental
> health outcomes than those who do not seek treatment** [S1].

### ⚠️ Fertility

> **Finasteride leads to mildly impaired sperm parameters, which improve when medication is ceased in 57%
> of cases.** ⚠️ **Patients must be asked if they are hoping to conceive in the future, and should be
> started on MINOXIDIL INSTEAD if this is the case** [S1].

**A1 lists poor semen quality and infertility as rare adverse effects** [A1].

### ⚠️ Handling the tablets — A1 softens the product information

> **Although the product information recommends that women who are or may become pregnant should not handle
> broken or crushed tablets without gloves, IT IS HIGHLY UNLIKELY THAT ENOUGH FINASTERIDE WOULD BE ABSORBED
> TO CAUSE FETAL HARM** [A1].

⚠️ **Note the contrast with this compendium's BPH guideline**, where A1 states the glove warning **without
that qualifier** for the 5 mg products. **Same drug, two monographs, two framings.**

## ⚠️ The PSA problem, and the two sources give different numbers

| Source | Figure |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ **A1, finasteride (dermatology)** | **after 48 weeks of finasteride 1 mg daily, PSA is reduced by an average of 42%** |
| ⚠️ **S1** | **finasteride is known to reduce PSA by roughly 50%** — stated for the **1 mg** alopecia indication |
| **A1, 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors** (see **BPH and prostatitis**) | **after 1 year of finasteride 5 mg daily, PSA is reduced by an average of 50%** |

⚠️ **A1 is dose-specific; S1 is not.** **The 50% figure belongs to the 5 mg prostate dose in A1's own
account, and S1 applies it to the 1 mg hair dose.** ⚠️ **Both figures are stated here with their doses
attached, because a correction factor applied at the wrong dose changes whether a PSA looks reassuring.**

**A1 adds:** ⚠️ **it is not clear how long after stopping treatment PSA returns to baseline, though it
should be within 6 months** [A1].

**S1's practical point stands either way:** ⚠️ **as PSA remains an important marker for prostate cancer, it
is important that patients are aware that their threshold for urology referral should be lower** [S1].

## Women

**Topical minoxidil works in both men and women** [A1]. ⚠️ **Trials have shown NO BENEFIT from finasteride
in postmenopausal women** [A1]. **Estrogen and spironolactone have been used in women but efficacy is
unknown** [A1].

⚠️ **S1's case and trial evidence are in men only.**
