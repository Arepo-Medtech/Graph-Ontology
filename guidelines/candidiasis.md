# Candidiasis

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-21 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults, primary care.

> **Australian primary source.** ASHM *Australian STI Management Guidelines for use in primary care* —
> *Candidiasis* [S1], modified 30 June 2026.

## It is not an STI, and it is often not a disease

> ***Candida* species can be normal flora and therefore not need treatment if asymptomatic** [S1].

> **Can be sexually transmitted but is not considered a sexually transmissible infection** [S1].

Both sentences sit in an STI guideline, which is where they are most needed. The topic is included
**because it is the differential**, not because it belongs to the category. It **can arise spontaneously or
secondary to disturbance of vaginal flora — e.g. antibiotic therapy — or increased oestrogen levels, e.g.
pregnancy, postmenopausal oestrogen therapy** [S1].

A positive culture in an asymptomatic person is a finding, not an indication.

## ⚠️ Boric acid can be fatal if ingested

> ***Candida glabrata*: boric acid 600 mg vaginal pessaries (from a compounding pharmacy), one nocte for
> 14 days — boric acid can be fatal if ingested, avoid in pregnancy** [S1].

The only warning of **lethality** attached to a recommended treatment anywhere in twenty-six guidelines. It
is a compounded pessary that goes home in a bag with other medicines, and the source puts the warning in
the treatment cell itself rather than in a footnote.

## Species changes the drug entirely

| Situation | Recommended [S1] | Alternative [S1] |
|---|---|---|
| **Uncomplicated** | **Vaginal azole creams** — e.g. **clotrimazole 10% vaginal cream, 1 applicator intravaginally at night, as a stat dose or a 3–7 day course** — **or clotrimazole 500 mg vaginal pessary stat**.<br>**For balanitis: clotrimazole 1% twice daily until symptoms settle, then for a further week** | **Fluconazole 150 mg PO stat** |
| **Recurrent *Candida albicans*** | **Treat each episode with a longer course of azole cream (rather than a stat dose)** and/or **induction with fluconazole 150 mg PO for 3 doses, 3 days apart**, followed by **maintenance fluconazole 150 mg PO weekly for 6 months** | **Clotrimazole 10% vaginal cream (1 applicator) nightly for 10–14 days, then 500 mg vaginal pessary weekly after induction** |
| ***Candida glabrata*** | **Boric acid 600 mg vaginal pessaries** (compounding pharmacy), **one nocte for 14 days** — **fatal if ingested; avoid in pregnancy** | **Nystatin 100 000 IU inserted per vaginam for 14 days** |

**Consider non-*albicans* species in cases of confirmed recurrent candidiasis** [S1]. That is the whole
reason to culture: *glabrata* does not get an azole at all.

## When to test

**Usually diagnosed on the basis of symptoms and signs** [S1]. Tests are for **recurrent disease**:

| Site/specimen | Test | Note [S1] |
|---|---|---|
| **High vaginal swab or self-collected vaginal swab** | **Microscopy and culture** | **Culture for yeast enables species differentiation and sensitivities**, and differentiation from **bacterial vaginosis or trichomoniasis** |
| **Penis** | **Microscopy and culture** | Culture for yeast |

**Recurrent candidiasis is defined as 4 or more episodes in a 12-month period, and may occur in nearly 10%
of women. It should be confirmed by culture on at least one occasion** [S1].

The auditable outcome applies only to that group, and it audits **two** things:

> **100% of patients with recurrent *Candida* have had yeast cultures and genital examination performed to
> inform further treatment** [S1].

**Consider PCR testing for herpes only if there are suggestive clinical signs and symptoms** [S1].

## ⚠️ What else it might be

> **Other causes for symptoms must be excluded by genital examination — e.g. dermatitis, lichen sclerosis,
> herpes simplex viruses** [S1].

> **Consider diabetes mellitus, HIV infection or other causes of immunosuppression if poorly controlled
> disease** [S1].

**Reconsider the diagnosis if there is no response to therapy** [S1]. Recurrent thrush that will not settle
is one of the few presentations in primary care that may be the first sign of undiagnosed diabetes or HIV,
and the source says so directly.

## Treatment advice

- **Intravaginal and oral azoles have similar efficacy — topical therapy provides quicker symptom relief,
  but women generally prefer oral therapy** [S1]. A rare explicit statement that **patient preference, not
  efficacy, is the deciding factor.**
- > **Vulvar treatment alone is inadequate due to a vaginal reservoir — both sites should be treated** [S1].

  The single most common treatment failure on this page, and it is a coverage error rather than a drug one.
- **The addition of hydrocortisone 1% cream may provide symptomatic relief** [S1].
- **No evidence that specific diets or the use of probiotics influence recurrence** [S1] — the second flat
  statement about probiotics in this source, after bacterial vaginosis.
- **Oral azoles cannot be used in pregnancy** [S1].
- > **No hepatic monitoring is required for fluconazole use at the above doses** [S1].

  A deliberate **de-escalation** — the source removing a test rather than adding one, including for the
  6-month weekly maintenance regimen.
- **More severe disease and symptoms may need longer courses of oral or topical treatment** [S1].
- **Seek specialist advice before treating complicated presentations, or recurrent disease not responding to
  therapy** [S1].

## Irritants and barriers

- **Avoid local irritants** — soap, bath oil, body wash, bubble bath, spermicide, vaginal lubricant and
  vaginal hygiene products [S1].
- **Latex barrier contraception such as condoms can be damaged by antifungal vaginal creams or oil-based
  products** [S1] — the same hazard flagged for BV treatments, from a different drug class.

## Partners

> **Partners do not usually require treatment** [S1].

Worth reading against bacterial vaginosis in the same source, where partner treatment is **now
recommended**. Two conditions presenting with vaginal discharge, opposite partner advice, both current — the
kind of distinction lost whenever "vaginal discharge" is treated as one problem.

The exception is specific:

> **Post-coital penile hypersensitivity to vaginal *Candida* colonisation is possible and responds to
> partner treatment. Hydrocortisone 1% cream may provide symptomatic relief** [S1].

**Contact tracing is not required** [S1].

## Presentation

| Penile [S1] | Vulvar/vaginal [S1] |
|---|---|
| **Red rash on genitals, especially under the foreskin**, may or may not be itchy | **Thick, white, clumping vaginal discharge — although discharge can appear normal or be absent** |
| **Swelling of foreskin if severe** | Genital/vulvar **itch, burning, soreness** |
| **Fissures** | **Superficial dyspareunia**; **external dysuria** |
| **Superficial erosions** | **Excoriation, erythema, fissures, swelling** |

The parenthesis matters: **discharge can appear normal or be absent**. Absence of discharge does not exclude
it, and presence of discharge does not confirm it.

**Majority *Candida albicans*, rarely other *Candida* species** [S1].

## Follow-up

**Not indicated for uncomplicated infection.** **Test of cure not required.** **Retesting not indicated
unless symptoms fail to resolve — also consider alternative diagnoses** [S1].

## Pregnancy

**May need a longer course of topical treatment — e.g. 7 days minimum. Fluconazole and boric acid are
contraindicated** [S1].
