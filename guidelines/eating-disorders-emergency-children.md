# Eating disorders in the emergency department (children and adolescents)

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** ED history, examination, investigations, risk stratification for admission, refeeding precautions, cardiac monitoring, mental health assessment and disposition for eating disorders in children and adolescents up to 18 years. The general (all-ages, outpatient-focused) counterpart is `eating-disorders`, which carries no admission thresholds.

## Key points

- ⚠️ **Children and adolescents with eating disorders can appear well despite being medically unstable** — cardiovascular complications, electrolyte imbalance, dehydration. [S1]
- ⚠️ **They may present with suicidal ideation, escalating compensatory behaviours or comorbid psychiatric disorders needing urgent mental health assessment.** [S1]
- **Anorexia nervosa has the highest mortality of any psychiatric disorder.** ARFID can also cause medical instability; seek specialist input, but admission criteria are similar. [S1]
- **Applies up to 18 years**; age limits for specialist services vary by location. [S1]

## Assess

- **History in all patients:** current, pre-morbid and highest weight and speed of loss · fainting, dizziness, palpitations, cold intolerance, constipation, abdominal symptoms · menstrual history · **mood, suicide and self-harm risk** · HEEADSSS. [S1]
- **Examine:** weight, height, BMI, temperature, and **postural HR and BP lying to standing, 2–3 minutes apart**; look for undernutrition, purging signs (enamel erosion, Russell's sign, enlarged salivary glands) and acrocyanosis. [S1]
- **Bloods:** glucose, VBG, UEC, Ca, Mg, PO4, FBE, LFTs, ketones; **if new diagnosis** add ESR, thyroid function, coeliac screen. **12-lead ECG**; urinalysis; βhCG if amenorrhoeic. [S1]

## Admission — risk stratification

- ⚠️ **Strongly consider admission for medical instability: any red-flag feature or multiple amber-flag features.** Discuss with the paediatric or adolescent medicine on-call clinician for unit placement or if unsure. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Heart rate awake — consider admission:** under 50 bpm or postural rise over 50 bpm · **concern:** 50–60 bpm or postural rise over 20 bpm · **moderate risk:** over 60 bpm with normal postural change. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Blood pressure — consider admission:** SBP under 80 or postural drop over 20 mmHg · **concern:** SBP under 90 or postural drop over 15 mmHg · **moderate risk:** normal resting SBP and postural drop under 15 mmHg. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Temperature — consider admission:** under 35.5°C · **concern:** under 36.0°C · **moderate risk:** over 36.0°C. **Syncope:** recurrent · occasional · none or pre-syncope. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Hydration — consider admission:** fluid refusal over 48 hours or severe dehydration (over 10%) · **concern:** severe fluid restriction or moderate dehydration (5–9%) · **moderate risk:** minimal restriction, no more than mild dehydration (under 5%). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Weight loss — consider admission:** over 1 kg/week or over 15% in 3–6 months · **concern:** 0.5 to 1 kg/week · **moderate risk:** under 0.5 kg/week or fluctuating. **Percent of median BMI:** under 70% · 70–80% · over 80%. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Also listed as signs of medical instability** (column not recoverable): **QTc over 450 msec or other significant ECG abnormality**; hypophosphataemia under 0.9 mmol/L, hypokalaemia under 3.0 mmol/L, hypoglycaemia under 3.0 mmol/L, hyponatraemia under 130 mmol/L; transaminases over 3 times normal; Hb under 100 g/L. [S1]

## Nutrition, hydration and monitoring

- ⚠️ **Refeeding syndrome:** potentially fatal fluid and electrolyte shifts on re-feeding an undernourished child; **its hallmark is hypophosphataemia.** Large oral or IV fluid volumes risk fluid overload and cardiac compromise. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Start nutrition and hydration only after investigations are reviewed and a plan is agreed with the admitting team** — then as soon as possible, ideally in the ED. [S1]
- **If feeding starts in the ED, consider first: thiamine 100 mg orally and phosphate 500 mg (elemental) orally.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Consider ongoing cardiac monitoring** (after discussing with the admitting consultant) for severe electrolyte disturbance such as K under 2.5 mmol/L, **HR under 45 bpm, or HR under 40 bpm asleep.** [S1]

## Disposition

- **Medically stable, no urgent mental health need:** new diagnosis — discuss outpatient management with the on-call consultant; known diagnosis — refer to the local service or the patient's known ED clinician. [S1]
- **If there are mental health concerns, discuss with the local mental health team** — urgent assessment may be needed. [S1]
- **Involve the paediatric or adolescent team** if medically unstable or needing admission, at first presentation, or to plan follow-up. **Transfer** if care exceeds the local facility. [S1]
- **Discharge only when medically stable, mental health assessment is complete, and follow-up is arranged.** [S1]
