# Neuroendocrine neoplasms (NENs) — practice points

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** specialist-led care of adults with neuroendocrine neoplasms (mainly gastroenteropancreatic), MEN 1 and phaeochromocytoma/paraganglioma: biomarkers, carcinoid heart disease, systemic therapy, surveillance and supportive care. No corpus counterpart covers NENs; `anticancer-drugs-general-principles` covers anticancer drugs generally.

## Who decides

- **Make individualised treatment decisions for NENs routinely in a multidisciplinary setting.** [S1]

## Biomarkers

- ⚠️ **Serum chromogranin A can be falsely elevated**, for example by proton pump inhibitors, **and assays differ, so use the same assay for the one patient.** [S1]

## Carcinoid heart disease

- **Screen patients with functional NENs every 6-12 months for carcinoid heart disease, including an annual echocardiogram.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Valvular abnormalities on echocardiogram should prompt early cardiology referral, even if the patient is asymptomatic.** [S1]

## Surgery

- **Surgical metastasectomy is not recommended for Grade 3 NET or neuroendocrine carcinoma.** [S1]

## Systemic therapy

- **Recommended starting dose for an anti-proliferative effect: octreotide LAR 30 mg or lanreotide 120 mg every 4 weeks.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Withhold long-acting SSA therapy at least four weeks before somatostatin scintigraphy.** [S1]
- **Consider platinum/etoposide chemotherapy first-line for advanced Grade 3 poorly-differentiated neuroendocrine carcinomas.** [S1]

## Hereditary syndromes and phaeochromocytoma

- **Offer routine MEN 1 surveillance screening to everyone diagnosed with MEN 1, starting by age 12 years.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Functioning phaeochromocytomas and paragangliomas require pre-operative alpha-blockade to control blood pressure.** [S1]

## Supportive care

- **Refer patients with unplanned weight loss or at risk of malnutrition immediately to an accredited practising dietitian.** [S1]
- **Prophylactic niacin 40-80 mg/day may be considered in carcinoid syndrome.** [S1]
