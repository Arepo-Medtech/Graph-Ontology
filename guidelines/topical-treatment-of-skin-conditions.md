# Topical treatment of skin conditions — general principles

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-22 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** all ages. **Underpins every topical skin guideline in this compendium** — eczema, psoriasis,
acne, rosacea, tinea, ectoparasites.

> **Mixed sources.** **S1** is the **open** RACGP *AJGP* review (**2021**), **quoted verbatim** — 18 claims.
> **A1** is the **Australian Medicines Handbook**, **paraphrased** — 14 claims, **no passage stored**.
> ⚠️ **AMH's vehicle and weekly-quantity tables are summarised, not reproduced row by row.**

**AMH topics closed by this guideline (2):** *General principles: topical treatment of skin conditions*,
*Skin conditions, general principles of treatment*.

## ⚠️ The finding: the usual advice is backwards

> ⚠️ **The advice of applying TCS agents 'sparsely' is no longer applicable; instead, 'LIBERALLY' is
> encouraged** [S1].
>
> ⚠️ **Undertreated conditions are more widespread than overtreatment** [S1].

**Steroid phobia exists among non-dermatologist professionals (eg general practitioners and pharmacists) as
well as patients and parents**, with **evidence of non-adherence and unresolved outcomes of skin conditions
affecting quality of life and healthcare costs** [S1].

⚠️ **The benefits significantly outweigh any unwanted effects if TCS agents are correctly prescribed** —
so **it is prudent for practitioners to properly educate the users to avoid non-adherence from steroid
phobia** [S1].

**And a related misconception:** ⚠️ **diluting the topical steroid with moisturiser DOES NOT CHANGE the
potency of the medicine** [S1].

## How much — and the two sources size it differently

| | **A1 (AMH)** | **S1 (AJGP)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Method** | **suggested WEEKLY quantities in grams, by body area and age** | **FINGERTIP UNITS per application** |
| **Assumes** | ⚠️ **twice daily application for 1 week** | ⚠️ **once daily is generally recommended** |

⚠️ **Neither converts to the other, and this guideline does not attempt the arithmetic — the two rest on
different application frequencies.** Both are given with their assumptions stated.

### A1 · Suggested weekly quantities

**Based on twice daily application for 1 week** [A1]:

| Area | **>10 years** | **3–12 months** |
|---|---|---|
| **Face and neck** | **20 g** | **7 g** |
| **Arm and hand** | **30 g** | **7 g** |
| **Leg and foot** | **55 g** | **10 g** |
| **Trunk (front)** | **50 g** | **7 g** |
| **Trunk (back and buttocks)** | **50 g** | **10 g** |

**Quantities increase through the 1–3, 3–6 and 6–10 year bands** [A1].

> ⚠️ **For corticosteroids, calcipotriol and pimecrolimus, do not exceed these without specialist
> recommendation** [A1].

### S1 · Fingertip units

> **1 FTU = 0.5 g** [S1].

- **A child aged six years with face and neck involvement requires 2 FTU (1 g) daily, and a 15 g tube would
  last two weeks** [S1]
- **An adult with one leg and foot involvement requires 8 FTU (4 g) daily, and two tubes of 15 g or one
  tube of 30 g would last one week** [S1]

⚠️ **Greater quantities of the 15 g tube of potent TCS can be prescribed under the PBS streamlined authority
with the repeat, depending on the percentage of body surface involvement, calculated from the rule of
nines** [S1].

⚠️ **The quantity conversation is a compliance conversation:** a 15 g tube that should last a week and lasts
three months was not applied.

## Where it goes decides how much gets in

**Topical administration is often preferred as it allows direct contact between drug and tissue, minimising
systemic adverse effects** [A1]. **Penetration and therefore effectiveness is influenced by** [A1]:

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ **Skin damage (burns, eczema)** | **increases permeability** |
| **Scale and crust (psoriasis)** | ⚠️ **may REDUCE penetration** |
| **Site** | ⚠️ **facial skin is more permeable**; **palms and soles can be resistant**; **intertriginous areas are more permeable** |
| ⚠️ **Hydration, occlusion, wet dressings, raised temperature** | **increase systemic absorption** |
| ⚠️ **Children** | **higher surface area–weight ratio, absorb a higher systemic dose by weight than adults**; ⚠️ **in preterm neonates the stratum corneum barrier is less effective** |
| **Elderly with thin/fragile skin** | **may have increased permeability** |

**S1 agrees and puts it as three rules** [S1]: ⚠️ **ointments penetrate skin slightly better than creams**;
**thin skin (face, eyelid) absorbs more readily than thick (trunk, limb, hand, foot)**; **intertriginous
areas (axilla, groin, breast folds) retain topical medications longer, with increased absorption**.

⚠️ **When applying more than one product to the same area of skin, allow sufficient time for absorption
between applications** [A1].

## Choosing a vehicle [A1]

| Vehicle | Effect | Where | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ointments** | **occlusive and lubricating** | **dry, scaly skin** | ⚠️ **better penetration than creams and lotions**, but **greasy, difficult to wash off, may cause folliculitis** |
| **Creams** | **cooling and lubricating** | **moist or dry skin; preferred for face and flexures** | ⚠️ **preservatives can cause sensitisation**; **less emollient and more irritant than ointments**; **often preferred by patients** |
| **Lotions and solutions** | **drying and cooling** | **hairy and intertriginous regions; weeping surfaces** | **can be applied without friction** |
| **Pastes** | **drying and protective** | **psoriasis, warts** | **more occlusive and absorptive, less greasy than ointments; reduce spreading of active ingredient** |
| **Aerosols and sprays** | **cooling** | **where direct application is difficult or painful** | **allow application without touching skin** |
| **Ethanol-based gels, lotions, liquids** | **drying and cooling** | **moist areas** | ⚠️ **excessive drying can cause irritation; can sting on broken skin** |

**Vehicles also deliver the drug:** **specific components can be added to increase percutaneous absorption,
eg dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), propylene glycol** [A1].

## ⚠️ Sensitisation

**Some additives may cause sensitisation: lanolin, ethylenediamine, fragrances, propylene glycol, and
preservatives such as chlorocresol and hydroxybenzoates (parabens).** ⚠️ **Creams generally contain more
additives than ointments and hence are more likely to cause sensitisation. Some drugs may also cause
sensitisation** [A1].

⚠️ **A "worsening" rash on treatment may be a reaction to the vehicle, not a failure of the drug.**

## Topical corticosteroid potency

⚠️ **TCS agents have different formulations, percentages and molecular structures DESPITE SIMILAR OR
NEAR-SIMILAR NAMES, and these variations affect potency, efficacy and effect** [S1].

**Classification is not standardised internationally:** **the WHO introduced class I (ultrahigh) to VII
(low) in 1997, but some countries established a reversed order as well as ill-defined subdivisions, leading
to confusion.** ⚠️ **Australasian guidelines favour mild, moderate, potent and very potent, with
corresponding classes I to IV** [S1].

| Potency | Agents [S1] |
|---|---|
| **Mild (I)** | **hydrocortisone 0.5–1%**, **hydrocortisone acetate 0.5–1%** |
| **Moderate (II)** | **clobetasone butyrate 0.05%**, **hydrocortisone butyrate 0.1%**, **betamethasone valerate 0.02–0.05%**, **triamcinolone acetonide 0.02–0.05%**, **methylprednisolone aceponate 0.1%** |

⚠️ **S1's table continues to potent (III) and very potent (IV); only the first two classes are carried here
because the remainder was not captured in full from the retrieved text — see *Unresolved*.**

## Applying it [S1]

- ⚠️ **Once daily is generally recommended for better compliance.** **Twice daily may be considered for the
  initial week(s) for certain severe lesions, reducing to daily or alternate-day depending on response**
- **Apply in the evening/night after bathing, to avoid incidental removal during day-to-day activities**
- ⚠️ **Use adjunct moisturisers/emollients after application** — **moisturiser alone is useful as a
  steroid-sparing agent in trivial dermatitis**
- **Occlusion** (**tubular bandage or plastic wrap; a non-irritant glove or sock for hand or foot**) is
  **favourable for severe and thick/keratotic/lichenified lesions** — ⚠️ **but needs close monitoring for
  reaction to the occlusive material and systemic absorption**
- ⚠️ **Phase out rather than stop**: **daily → alternate-day → twice-weekly or weekend use over weeks or
  months once active inflammation has subsided**, **while continuing moisturiser**

## Adverse effects [S1]

**Rare if used correctly:** **skin atrophy, striae, telangiectasia, easy bruising, hypertrichosis.**
**Uncommon:** **periorificial dermatitis, steroid rosacea, red skin syndrome, contact dermatitis, pigment
alteration.** ⚠️ **HPA axis suppression is extremely rare.**

⚠️ **Be mindful of iatrogenic Cushing's syndrome, growth retardation, cataract and glaucoma from systemic
absorption** — attributable to **prolonged misuse of potent or ultrapotent agents over large skin areas
and/or occlusion**, ⚠️ **especially in young children and elderly patients with thin skin** [S1].

**Generally reversible on cessation** [S1]. ⚠️ **Methylprednisolone aceponate and mometasone furoate are
associated with lesser local and systemic adverse effects than older formulations, because of their
lipophilic structure and pharmacokinetics** [S1].

⚠️ **Mild-to-moderate-potency steroids and short-term use of potent topical steroids are safe in pregnancy
and lactation**, per a systematic review [S1].

**Complex or recalcitrant cases require referral to a dermatologist** [S1].

## Where this connects in the compendium

| Guideline here | Connection |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ **Eczema** | the main indication; ⚠️ **"apply sparsely" is the advice this guideline reverses**, and **damaged skin absorbs more** |
| ⚠️ **Psoriasis** | ⚠️ **scale and crust REDUCE penetration** [A1], and **pastes are the vehicle A1 names for psoriasis** |
| ⚠️ **Tinea and nail infections** | ⚠️ **a steroid on undiagnosed tinea produces tinea incognito** — potency selection assumes the diagnosis is right |
| **Acne / rosacea** | **facial skin is more permeable**, and **creams are preferred for face and flexures** |
| **Glaucoma** | ⚠️ **cataract and glaucoma are named systemic risks of prolonged potent TCS misuse** [S1] |
| **Dry eye / conjunctivitis** | the same preservative-sensitisation problem in a different tissue |
