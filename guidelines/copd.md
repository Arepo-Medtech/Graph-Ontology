## Diagnosis

COPD is preventable and treatable, characterised by airflow limitation that is **not fully reversible**,
usually progressive, with significant extrapulmonary effects [S1]. Diagnosis in practice rests on three
things together [S1]:

- exertional breathlessness, cough and sputum
- a history of smoking or exposure to other noxious agents
- **FEV₁/FVC <0.7 post-bronchodilator**

| Recommendation | LoE | SoR |
|---|---|---|
| Smoking is the most important risk factor for developing COPD | I | Strong |
| **Smoking cessation reduces mortality** in people with COPD | I | Strong |
| Begin with a thorough history and examination as the first step to diagnosis | III-2 | Strong |
| **Confirm COPD with spirometry (post-bronchodilator FEV₁/FVC <0.7)** | III-2 | Strong |
| A large post-bronchodilator FEV₁ increase (greater confidence if >15% **and** >400 mL) might suggest asthma or coexisting asthma and COPD; consider history, symptom pattern and further investigation | III-2 | Strong |
| Further investigations may be needed to confirm or exclude other conditions and assess severity | III-2 | Strong |
| Consider referral to specialist respiratory services if needed | III-2 | Strong |
| Regularly assess COPD symptoms and exacerbation risk | III-2 | Strong |

**Definitions the guideline pins down** [S1]: *chronic bronchitis* is daily sputum production for at least
three months of two or more consecutive years; *emphysema* is a pathological diagnosis — alveolar dilatation
and destruction.

**Asthma overlap is common and not a technicality.** A systematic review of 19 studies found coexisting
asthma in **27%** of COPD patients in population-based studies and **28%** in hospital-based studies, and
reviews found more frequent exacerbations in those with features of both compared with COPD alone [S1].

## Optimise function (stable disease)

Principal goals of therapy: **stop smoking**, optimise function through symptom relief with medications and
pulmonary rehabilitation, and prevent or treat aggravating factors and complications [S2].

| Recommendation | LoE | SoR |
|---|---|---|
| Begin with a comprehensive assessment as the first step to optimising function | III-2 | Strong |
| Recognise that comorbid conditions are common | III-2 | Strong |
| **Regularly check inhaler technique and adherence** | I | Strong |
| Optimise pharmacotherapy using a **stepwise** approach | I | Strong |
| Recommend non-pharmacological strategies such as pulmonary rehabilitation and regular exercise **to anyone with COPD** | I | Strong |
| **Refer to pulmonary rehabilitation** to improve quality of life, exercise capacity, and reduce exacerbations | I | Strong |
| Lung volume reduction (surgical and endobronchial) can enhance lung function, exercise capacity and quality of life | I | **Weak** |
| Consider palliative care **early**, ideally multidisciplinary, for symptoms and psychosocial issues | II | **Weak** |

Adherence to inhaled regimens is associated with **reduced risk of death and of hospital admission** for
exacerbations (RR 0.56, 95% CI 0.48–0.65) [S2][S3] — which is why "check inhaler technique and adherence"
carries LoE I and Strong, above any specific drug choice.

## Manage exacerbations

**Diagnose** on changes in the patient's baseline dyspnoea, cough and/or sputum that **exceed normal
day-to-day variation**, are acute in onset, and may warrant a change in medication or admission (III-2,
Strong) [S3].

| Recommendation | LoE | SoR |
|---|---|---|
| Diagnosing and treating exacerbations early may prevent admission and delay progression | III-2 | Strong |
| **Inhaled short-acting bronchodilators first line** | I | Strong |
| Consider **systemic corticosteroids — oral where possible, 30–50 mg daily for 5 days** | I | Strong |
| Consider **antibiotics (amoxycillin or doxycycline, 5 days)** where there are clinical features of infection: increased sputum volume and colour change and/or fever | I | Strong |
| **Supplemental oxygen targeting SpO₂ 88–92%** to improve survival | II | Strong |
| **Controlled oxygen delivery 0.5–2.0 L/min** for hypoxaemia | II | Strong |
| **Non-invasive ventilation improves survival** in acute hypercapnic respiratory failure | I | Strong |
| Refer to pulmonary rehabilitation, particularly during recovery after an exacerbation | I | Strong |
| Ensure comprehensive follow-up after discharge | I | Strong |
| Coordinate multidisciplinary support for home management in the community | I | Weak |

The oxygen target is the recommendation most often got wrong, and it is explicit: **88–92%**, delivered at
**0.5–2.0 L/min**, not the higher saturations appropriate elsewhere.

**Antibiotics are conditional on infection features**, not on the exacerbation itself — increased sputum
volume *and* colour change and/or fever [S3].

### Risk and prognosis

**DECAF** is a 30-day mortality prediction score for COPD admissions, derived from 920 consecutive patients
and validated in 2,645 across 6 UK hospitals. Its five components are extended MRC Dyspnoea score,
eosinopenia, consolidation, acidaemia and atrial fibrillation. AUROC 0.86 (95% CI 0.82–0.89); a score of 3
confers **27.2% 30-day mortality** [S3].

**Pulmonary embolism should be considered** in an exacerbation when signs of respiratory infection are
absent and chest pain or cardiac failure are present. A systematic review of 880 hospitalised patients
undergoing CTPA found **16% had PE**, with wide between-study variation (3–29%) [S3].

**Statins do not prevent exacerbations.** STATCOPE found simvastatin 40 mg daily for at least 12 months did
not affect exacerbation rates or time to first exacerbation, despite favourable observational data
(evidence level II) [S3].

## Australian context — PBS

PBS Schedule 4333, checked directly [S5]:

| Class | Agents | Benefit type |
|---|---|---|
| SABA | salbutamol | **unrestricted** (also R) |
| SAMA | ipratropium | **unrestricted** (also R) |
| LAMA | tiotropium, glycopyrronium, umeclidinium, aclidinium | restricted / authority (streamlined) |
| LABA | indacaterol, formoterol, salmeterol | restricted / authority (streamlined) |
| ICS | budesonide, fluticasone | unrestricted through authority, depending on form |
| PDE-4 inhibitor | **roflumilast** | **not on Schedule 4333** |
| Exacerbation antibiotics | amoxicillin, doxycycline | **unrestricted** (also R) |
| Systemic corticosteroid | prednisolone | **unrestricted** (also R) |

The acute exacerbation kit is fully unrestricted — short-acting bronchodilators, prednisolone, amoxicillin
and doxycycline — which matches the guideline's first-line recommendations exactly. Maintenance long-acting
bronchodilators are restricted or authority-required, so the constraint sits on **maintenance**, not on
**rescue**.
