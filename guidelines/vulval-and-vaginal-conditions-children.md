# Vulval and vaginal conditions in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** assessment and management of vulval and vaginal pain, itch, discharge and bleeding in prepubertal children: vulvovaginitis, pinworms, bacterial overgrowth, labial adhesions, lichen sclerosus, vulvodynia and urethral prolapse. > ⚠️ **Do not perform an internal vaginal examination or take internal vaginal swabs in prepubescent children.** Be alert to signs of sexual abuse.

## Key points

- **Vulvovaginitis is common and usually resolves with simple measures.** [S1]
- **Consider pinworms** when a prepubertal child has distressing nocturnal vaginal or perineal pain and/or itch. [S1]
- **Recurrent UTI symptoms with negative cultures: assess for vulvodynia.** [S1]
- **Newborns** commonly have breast buds, white mucoid discharge, small vaginal blood loss and hymenal tags from maternal oestrogen; **bleeding in the first week of life needs no investigation or treatment.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Abnormal vaginal bleeding:** foreign body, excoriation (vulvovaginitis, lichen sclerosus, pinworms), trauma including straddle injury or **sexual abuse**, **precocious puberty** (secondary sexual characteristics under 8 years), urethral prolapse, rarely tumour. [S1]

## Examine

- **Consider whether a perineal examination is needed** and discuss with a senior clinician. **Examine once, by the most suitable clinician**, with a senior clinician supervising/chaperoning alongside the parent, after explanation and consent. [S1]
- **Position:** supine or semi-supine on a parent's lap in frog-leg, or lying on the side with knees to chest. [S1]
- **Mild vulval erythema is normal before puberty.** Pale atrophic patches suggest lichen sclerosus; pain on cotton-bud touch approaching the peri-hymenal area suggests vulvodynia. [S1]
- **Document by describing, not interpreting**, findings. ⚠️ **Offensive discharge with bleeding before menarche suggests a vaginal foreign body.** [S1]

## Investigate

- **Unclear cause or suspected vaginal foreign body: discuss with a gynaecologist.** Consider urine MCS. [S1]
- **Profuse or offensive discharge: swab for MCS at the introitus only** — no internal vaginal swabs. [S1]

## Vulvovaginitis

- **Features:** vulval discomfort, itch, dysuria, discharge, redness. **Causes:** thin, alkaline prepubertal mucosa; moisture, poor hygiene, irritants; pinworms, bacterial overgrowth, STI, foreign body. **Most need no investigations.** [S1]
- **Treat:** explanation and reassurance, remove environmental causes, good hygiene and vulval care, **vinegar baths (½ cup white vinegar in a shallow bath)**; low-dose steroid cream for severe itch or excoriation. [S1]
- **Pinworms: mebendazole 50 mg (over 6 months and 10 kg or less) or 100 mg (over 10 kg) orally as a single dose, repeated after 2 weeks**, and treat all household contacts. [S1]
- **Bacterial overgrowth** (e.g. group A streptococcus, *E. coli*) with significant erythema and pain: **consider cefalexin** per local antimicrobial guidelines. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Sexual abuse occasionally presents as vulvovaginitis.** Discuss persistent, recurrent or multiple presentations with gynaecology. [S1]

## Labial adhesions

- **A normal variant** developing from 3 months and resolving spontaneously by 6 to 8 years; usually asymptomatic. [S1]
- **If the child voids easily, no treatment.** Oestrogen cream or manual separation are **not recommended** (high recurrence; separation is painful). **Outflow obstruction:** consider a short course of oestrogen cream after discussion with gynaecology. [S1]

## Lichen sclerosus, vulvodynia, urethral prolapse

- **Lichen sclerosus:** onset 5–7 years; itch, bleeding or discharge; pale atrophic patches. ⚠️ **Purpuric haemorrhage after scratching is occasionally mistaken for sexual abuse.** [S1]
- **Lichen sclerosus treatment:** reassurance, avoid irritants, treat constipation, barrier ointments short term; **a course of high- then medium-potency topical steroid is often required — seek specialist advice.** [S1]
- **Vulvodynia (pain 3 months or more without identifiable cause):** vulval care, pelvic floor physiotherapy, psychology if anxiety or depression, manage constipation; topical local anaesthetic short term only; **neuropathic agents (amitriptyline, gabapentin, pregabalin) only on specialist advice.** [S1]
- **Urethral prolapse:** chronic bleeding, pain, dysuria, straining; treat the precipitant (cough, constipation) and **refer to gynaecology** for topical oestrogen. [S1]

## Escalate and discharge

- **Consult gynaecology** if the diagnosis is unclear, advice is needed, or a foreign body needs removal. ⚠️ **Suspected sexual assault: follow local guidelines; early specialist consultation is vital.** [S1]
- **Transfer** if a vaginal foreign body is suspected and examination under anaesthetic is not available locally. **Discharge** once diagnosed with follow-up in place. [S1]
