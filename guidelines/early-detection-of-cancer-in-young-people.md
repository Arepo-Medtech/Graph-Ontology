## When to refer

- **Refer urgently if symptoms or signs are persistent and unexplained.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Refer urgently if a young person presents three or more times with the same problem and there is no clear diagnosis.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Negative results may be falsely reassuring and should not delay referral.** [S1]

## How and where to refer

- **Make the referral within 1 working day.** [S1]
- **Refer to a paediatric oncologist or a Youth Cancer Service**, depending on local arrangements. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Avoid biopsies in primary care.** [S1]

## Immediate referral or emergency admission

- ⚠️ **Unexplained petechiae or hepatosplenomegaly: refer to a specialist immediately.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **A mediastinal or hilar mass on chest x-ray: refer to a specialist immediately.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Reduced level of consciousness: emergency department admission is required.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Stridor due to thyroid swelling: immediate emergency department admission.** [S1]

## Specific cancers

- **Suspected primary bone tumour or sarcoma: the first investigation should be an x-ray.** [S1]
- **An unexplained mass deep to the fascia, non-tender or progressively enlarging warrants urgent specialist referral.** [S1]
- **A swelling or mass in the body of the testis: refer urgently to a specialist.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Melanoma: refer locally advanced melanoma without biopsy, and avoid excision in primary care.** [S1]
