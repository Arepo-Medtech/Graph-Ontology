# Human papillomavirus (HPV)

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** HPV vaccination — age, number of doses, immunocompromise — and its relation to cervical screening. Treatment of warts is covered separately.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 9 of 9 claims quoted verbatim from the **Australian Immunisation Handbook**; **11 fragments re-checked by machine. 7 quoted doses.**

> ✅ **This closes a gap the AMH-derived [`immunisation`](immunisation.md) guideline left open** — its *Unresolved* #5 records that AMH *"does not state how many doses of HPV"*. The Handbook does: **one dose** for immunocompetent people starting at 9–25.

## Who and when

- **Adolescents and young adults are recommended HPV vaccine from 9 years of age.** ⚠️ **The optimal age is around 12–13, before exposure.** Anyone not vaccinated by 14 **can still receive it up to 25**. [S1]
- **Funded through the NIP from 12 to 25**, usually given in **School Year 7** (about 12–13). [S1]
- ⚠️ **It may be considered under 9 in children at risk of exposure** — for example **a history of sexual abuse** or a diagnosed STI. [S1]

## ⚠️ How many doses

- ✅ **ONE DOSE for immunocompetent people who start at 9–25** — that is, before their 26th birthday. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Starting at 26 or older: 3 doses — 8 weeks between doses 1 and 2, 16 weeks between doses 2 and 3.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Immunocompromising conditions: 9vHPV, regardless of starting age** — a lower immune response and a higher risk of persistent infection and HPV-related disease. Includes **inborn errors of immunity, HIV, malignancy, haematopoietic stem cell or solid organ transplant, and immunosuppressive therapy**. [S1]
- **Asplenia does NOT raise the risk** — people without a spleen get the **1-dose schedule** at 9–25. [S1]

## Vaccination does not replace screening

- ⚠️ **HPV types not in the vaccines can also cause cervical cancer — so both cervical screening AND vaccination are recommended.** [S1]
- **Low-risk types 6, 11, 40, 42, 43, 44, 54, 61, 70, 72, 81 and 89** are mostly associated with **non-malignant lesions such as genital warts**. **Anogenital HPV is mainly transmitted through sex.** [S1]
