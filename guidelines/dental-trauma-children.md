# Dental trauma in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** assessment and first management of injuries to primary and permanent teeth, supporting bone, gingivae and oral mucosa in children, including the avulsed tooth. Non-traumatic dental problems are in `dental-conditions-children`; skin and lip wounds are in `lacerations-children`.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 21 of 21 claims quoted verbatim from the **RCH Melbourne Clinical Practice Guideline *Dental trauma*** (Last updated July 2024); **33 fragments re-checked by machine. 1 quoted doses.**

> ⚠️ **An avulsed permanent tooth is a dental emergency:** handle the crown only, store in milk (or saline), replant if possible. **Primary teeth are never replanted.**

## Primary versus permanent

- **Management depends on whether the tooth is primary or permanent.** Primary teeth are **never repositioned, splinted or replanted** — they are observed or extracted. [S1]
- **Replanting permanent teeth or fragments needs urgent management** to improve long-term viability. [S1]
- **Do the primary and secondary survey first** where required — dental trauma often comes with facial and head injuries. ⚠️ **In infants and young children with oral trauma, consider child abuse.** [S1]

## Assess

- **Time since injury matters** — avulsion of a permanent tooth is a dental emergency. Ask about first aid (rinsing, wet or dry storage) and tetanus status. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Account for every lost tooth and fragment**; if any are missing, examine the chest and oral soft tissues for aspiration or embedding. **Chest X-ray if aspiration is suspected.** [S1]
- **Lift the lips firmly** to find gingival or mucosal injury — degloving is missed otherwise; a swollen tender chin suggests mandibular degloving. [S1]
- **OPG for children over 3 years who can cooperate** if a mandible fracture, TMJ injury or fully intruded tooth is a concern. [S1]

## Treat

- **Discuss with the emergency dental service** and send an intraoral photograph where possible; they decide between emergency specialist care and community dental referral. [S1]
- **Prophylactic antibiotics after replantation of an avulsed tooth**, for soft-tissue injuries with debris, or when operative management is needed. Give a tetanus booster if required. [S1]
- **0.12% chlorhexidine mouthwash swabbed twice a day for 10–14 days** reduces infection risk; a soft diet lets loose teeth firm up. [S1]

## Loose or displaced teeth

- **Concussion** (tender but firm): community dentist; usually heals without intervention. **Subluxation** (tender, loose, blood at the gum): emergency dental service if very loose. [S1]
- **Lateral luxation, extrusion or intrusion:** emergency dental service. Primary teeth are monitored or extracted; permanent teeth are usually repositioned or splinted under local or general anaesthetic. [S1]
- **Avulsion:** distinguish from a fully intruded tooth (may need X-ray). Refer to the emergency dental service. **Primary: do not reinsert.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Avulsed permanent tooth:** store in **milk (first choice) or saline**; **never water, never scrub it**; handle the crown only, not the root. [S1]
- **If possible, return it to the socket** and bite on gauze, supported with alfoil. **Best prognosis if dry time is under 20 minutes.** [S1]

## Fractured teeth, bone and mucosa

- **Enamel or enamel-dentine fracture:** community dentist. **Pulp exposed (pink and painful):** emergency dental service — primary for likely extraction; permanent, keep fragments in milk for rebonding. [S1]
- **Crown-root fracture** (may be an aspiration risk) **and root fracture:** emergency dental service. [S1]
- **Supporting bone:** check the bite, OPG, refer to dental and maxillofacial; **follow up every mandibular or maxillary fracture, even undisplaced.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Degloving injuries and deep tears need operative cleaning, debridement and suturing** to reduce osteomyelitis risk. [S1]
- **Crown discolouration is common and may resolve**; extraction only for pulp infection or abscess. [S1]
- **Transfer** if specialist dental or maxillofacial care is not available locally; **discharge** once intervention is complete and follow-up is arranged. [S1]
