## Anovulatory infertility

- **Usually treated first line with letrozole or clomifene.** Other aromatase inhibitors or selective
  estrogen receptor modulators are **occasionally** used [M1].
- **Gonadotrophins (follicle stimulating hormones) may be used, eg if oral therapies are
  unsuccessful** [M1].
- **In polycystic ovary syndrome, metformin may be added to clomifene or letrozole.** ⚠️ **Metformin alone
  may occasionally be used but is less effective than letrozole, clomifene, or clomifene with
  metformin** [M1].
- ⚠️ **Lifestyle changes, eg weight loss if obese, may also help restore fertility** [M1].

## Other causes

- **Women with hypopituitarism require FSH (eg follitropin) and LH (eg lutropin alfa).** **HCG and/or FSH
  may be used in infertile males with hypopituitarism** [M1].
- **Bromocriptine or cabergoline** may be used to normalise prolactin in **hyperprolactinaemia that
  prevents ovulation, when surgery is not indicated** [M1].

## Assisted reproduction

**May include down regulation with GnRH agonists (nafarelin, triptorelin) or GnRH antagonists (ganirelix,
cetrorelix), and artificial stimulation of ovarian follicle growth by gonadotrophins** [M1].

**HCG is used to time (trigger) release of eggs from the ovarian follicles**, and rarely for luteal phase
support [M1].

> ### ⚠️ Ovarian hyperstimulation — the substitution that reduces it
> **The combination of GnRH agonists, gonadotrophins and HCG is associated with a risk of serious ovarian
> hyperstimulation.** ⚠️ **In protocols using GnRH antagonists, GnRH agonists (eg triptorelin) may be used
> instead of HCG to trigger egg release, with little risk of ovarian hyperstimulation** [M1].

**Progesterone is used for luteal phase support.** ⚠️ **It is essential when ovulation and natural
progesterone production do not occur, eg treatment with donor oocytes, and is rarely indicated in natural
conceptions** [M1].

## ⚠️ Regulatory status — three drugs used outside their marketed indications

| Drug | Status [M1] |
|---|---|
| **Letrozole** for ovulation induction in PCOS | ⚠️ **Not a marketed indication** |
| **Metformin** for ovulation induction in PCOS | ⚠️ **Not a marketed indication** |
| **Nafarelin** for ovulation induction | ⚠️ **Not marketed for ovulation induction** |
| **Choriogonadotropin alfa** | ⚠️ **Not marketed for use in men**, despite HCG being listed for stimulation of spermatogenesis |
| **HCG itself** | ⚠️ **Not marketed in Australia** — may be available via the **Special Access Scheme or Section 19A approval**. **Acts as LH** |

**PBS:** most items are **PBS-S100 IVF program**. **Choriogonadotropin alfa, follitropin alfa and
follitropin beta are also PBS-R** for infertility indications other than ART, and **clomifene is PBS-R**
for anovulatory infertility and IVF [M1].

**Other notes** [M1]: **human menopausal gonadotrophin acts as both LH and FSH** · **corifollitropin alfa
is long-acting, used with a GnRH antagonist and given as a single SC dose** · **lutropin alfa, alone or
with follitropin alfa, is for severe LH and FSH deficiency, which AMH defines as LH below 1.2 IU/L** ·
**goserelin for pituitary down regulation is rarely used**.
