# Supraventricular tachycardia in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognition, vagal manoeuvres, adenosine precautions and disposition for SVT in infants and children. The adult counterpart is `arrhythmias`, which puts paediatric dosing out of scope.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 25 of 25 claims quoted verbatim from the **RCH Melbourne Clinical Practice Guideline *Supraventricular Tachycardia SVT*** (Last updated January 2022); **45 fragments re-checked by machine. 1 quoted doses.**

> ⚠️ **A child in shock goes straight to resuscitation.** Vagal manoeuvres are for the clinically stable child only, and **never use eyeball pressure or carotid sinus massage.**

---

## Recognise it

- **SVT is common in infancy and childhood**, usually re-entrant, in otherwise well children. **More than a third of new-onset SVT occurs in the first few weeks of life**, often presenting after many hours with heart failure. [S1]
- **Younger children: usually AVRT** (including Wolff-Parkinson-White). **Adolescents: more often AVNRT.** [S1]
- **SVT has a fixed rate, usually over 220 bpm**, with abrupt onset and offset; P-waves are not visible or sit after the QRS. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Assume a broad-complex tachycardia is VT**, not SVT with aberrancy, unless there is clear evidence it is not VT. [S1]
- **Look for and address contributors**: sepsis, pain, dehydration, anxiety and fever. [S1]
- **Infants tolerate it well and present late** — irritability, poor feeding, lethargy, sweating, then congestive heart failure. **Older children feel palpitations and present earlier**, often with a normal examination. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Identify the child in shock** (pallor, poor perfusion, decreased consciousness, hypotension) **and resuscitate immediately.** Red flags: unwell appearance, hypotension, drowsiness. **BP machines and oximeters may be unreliable at SVT rates.** [S1]

## Investigate

- **12-lead ECG before and after reversion.** CXR only if heart failure signs; **blood tests are not recommended** unless considering other causes. [S1]
- **Sinus tachycardia:** under 220, normal P-waves, variable rate, unwell child. **SVT:** over 220 (commonly 250–300 in infants), P-waves absent or after the QRS, fixed rate. [S1]
- **Atrial flutter** reaches up to 500/min in neonates (300/min in children); sawtooth waves are often seen only with adenosine-induced AV block. **Atrial ectopic tachycardia** may not respond to DC cardioversion or adenosine and can cause cardiomyopathy. **Junctional ectopic tachycardia:** 170–300, post cardiac surgery. [S1]

## Vagal manoeuvres

- ⚠️ **Only in a clinically stable child, on continuous cardiac monitoring.** If unsuccessful or the child deteriorates, move to medical treatment. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do NOT use eyeball pressure or carotid sinus massage.** [S1]
- **Under 6 months: facial immersion in ice water for 5 seconds** (diving reflex) — no need to occlude the nostrils; safe and 90% effective. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Never use facial immersion in an infant with haemodynamic compromise or shock.** [S1]
- **Older infants and toddlers:** ice-cold facecloth or bag of ice slurry to the face for 15–30 seconds. [S1]
- **School-aged children: Valsalva** — supine, blow into a thumb or 10 mL syringe after full inspiration for 10–15 seconds, visibly straining with no air escape. [S1]
- **Modified Valsalva is more effective:** strain semi-recumbent, then lie flat immediately with passive leg raise to 45 degrees. [S1]

## Adenosine and verapamil

- **Adenosine's half-life is 5–10 seconds**; it briefly blocks the AV node. **Always record a rhythm strip during it** — the response helps diagnosis even if SVT doesn't terminate. [S1]
- **Flushing and chest tightness are common.** ⚠️ **It can exacerbate asthma — avoid with a history of severe asthma or active wheeze.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Lower dose with dipyridamole; avoid if possible after cardiac transplant** (asystole, bradycardia — lower dose and consult cardiology if used). **Children on theophylline need higher doses** — consult cardiology. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Adenosine can turn re-entry SVT into atrial fibrillation — dangerous with WPW, needs DC cardioversion.** Rapid re-initiation is not uncommon; discuss with cardiology. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Verapamil is contraindicated under 12 months** (irreversible hypotension, lethal ventricular dysfunction) and is not routinely used in children. [S1]

## Disposition

- **Often needs admission:** first presentation · under 3 months · heart failure · needed DC conversion. [S1]
- **Known SVT with a breakthrough episode:** discuss with the child's usual cardiology team. [S1]
- **Discharge once reverted, not meeting admission criteria, and observed for 90 minutes**, with follow-up arranged. [S1]
