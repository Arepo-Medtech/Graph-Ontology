# Antihistamine poisoning in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** risk assessment, ECG monitoring, decontamination and disposition for H1-antihistamine overdose (sedating and lesser-sedating) in children. H2 antagonists are excluded. ## Key points

- **Most antihistamine poisonings need only supportive care.** ⚠️ **Large H1-antihistamine overdoses are associated with cardiac arrhythmia** and may need close monitoring and specific treatment. [S1]
- **Check for co-ingestants in the formulation, such as paracetamol.** This page covers **H1 antagonists only**, not H2 antagonists such as cimetidine and ranitidine. [S1]

## Sedating vs lesser-sedating

- ⚠️ **Sedating antihistamines** add alpha-adrenergic, anticholinergic and serotonergic effects: overdose brings sedation and anticholinergic signs, and **at higher doses sodium-channel blockade with QT prolongation and Torsades de Pointes.** [S1]
- **Sedating examples:** cyproheptadine, dexchlorpheniramine, diphenhydramine, dimenhydrinate, doxylamine, pheniramine, promethazine, trimeprazine, brompheniramine. [S1]
- **Lesser-sedating antihistamines** (cetirizine, desloratadine, fexofenadine, loratadine) generally have less serious toxicity: dizziness, tachycardia, headache, drowsiness or agitation. They don't affect the QT at normal doses, but there is concern about some effect in overdose. [S1]

## Who needs assessment

- **Assess:** acute ingestion of **more than 3 times the maximum daily dose** · any intentional self-poisoning or significant accidental ingestion · any symptomatic child. ⚠️ **Consider non-accidental poisoning** if the developmental age doesn't fit. [S1]
- **Establish the formulation** (syrup, immediate or modified release), exact name and tablet size, and **calculate the maximum possible dose per kg.** [S1]
- **Investigations:** ECG at presentation and **repeated 4–6 hourly until normal**; **paracetamol level in all intentional overdoses.** [S1]

## Management — sedating antihistamines

- **Monitor conscious level and breathing**, and support respiratory depression or depressed conscious state. In adolescents, look for co-ingestants such as paracetamol and alcohol. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Charcoal: consider only after a massive ingestion, within 1 hour or after the airway is protected — discuss with a toxicologist.** [S1]
- **Seizures or severe agitation: benzodiazepines.** ⚠️ **Start cardiac monitoring and do a 12-lead ECG** for long QT and arrhythmias; consider repeating it at 6 hours if symptomatic. **Discuss anticholinesterases with a toxicologist.** [S1]

## Management — lesser-sedating antihistamines

- **12-lead ECG for long QT and arrhythmias**; cardiac monitoring may be needed after large overdoses. Otherwise treat symptoms. [S1]

## Disposition

- **Consider admission for every intentional poisoning.** **Transfer** for a significantly decreased or non-improving conscious state, or need for respiratory support — PIPER 1300 137 650. [S1]
- **Discharge** if no features of poisoning by **6 hours after ingestion**, with a normal GCS and normal ECG; after intentional poisoning, a risk assessment showing low risk of further self-harm. [S1]
