# Nicotine poisoning in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** nicotine ingestion or exposure in children (cigarettes, butts, gum, patches, e-cigarette liquid): dose thresholds, biphasic toxicity, charcoal, monitoring and disposition. Nicotine as a cessation treatment is in `smoking-cessation`. General principles: `poisoning-initial-management-children`; AMH decontamination counterpart: `gastrointestinal-decontamination`.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 18 of 18 claims quoted verbatim from the **RCH Melbourne Clinical Practice Guideline *Nicotine Poisoning*** (Last Updated January 2019); **20 fragments re-checked by machine. 3 quoted doses.**

> ⚠️ **ANY liquid nicotine ingestion needs urgent assessment** — e-cigarette liquids are often highly concentrated and small volumes can kill a child. **More than 0.5 mg/kg is potentially lethal.**

## Key points

- **Mild exposures may cause GI symptoms only; larger doses are rapidly absorbed** and can cause seizures, cardiovascular collapse and coma. [S1]
- ⚠️ **E-cigarette liquid nicotine is often highly concentrated: small volumes may cause life-threatening toxicity in children.** [S1]
- **Biphasic:** stimulation within minutes (agitation, sweating, vomiting, tachycardia, bronchoconstriction, seizures); ⚠️ at higher doses a **second phase of bradycardia, hypotension, respiratory failure and coma 1 to 4 hours after exposure.** [S1]

## Dose

- ⚠️ **Any ingestion may cause mild toxicity; the minimum potentially lethal dose is anything over 0.5 mg/kg.** [S1]
- **Nicotine content:** filtered cigarette 13 to 30 mg · used butt 5 to 7 mg · gum/lozenge 2 to 4 mg · patch (total) 36 to 114 mg · e-cigarette liquid cartridge 0 to 36 mg/mL. [S1]
- ⚠️ **E-cigarette products were unregulated in Australia** (as at this page): **labels are unreliable** and true concentrations vary widely. [S1]

## Assessment

- ⚠️ **Urgent assessment for:** ANY quantity of liquid nicotine · more than one whole cigarette · more than three butts · any symptomatic child · all deliberate self-poisoning or significant accidental ingestion. [S1]
- **Calculate the likely dose in mg/kg** (true concentration of imported liquid nicotine may be hard to know) and consider co-ingestants such as paracetamol. [S1]
- **Early signs (under 1 hour):** nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, salivation · tachycardia, hypertension · breathlessness, bronchorrhoea, wheeze · agitation, fasciculation, seizures. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Late signs in larger exposures (1 to 4 hours):** bradycardia, arrhythmia, hypotension, shock · hypopnoea, apnoea · lethargy, muscle paralysis, stupor, coma. [S1]
- **Symptomatic: ECG initially and repeated at 4 hours until normal.** No specific pathology. [S1]

## Management

- **Most exposures are low-risk and management is supportive.** Higher-risk: cardiorespiratory monitoring, and treat hypotension, symptomatic bradycardia and respiratory failure by standard resuscitation guidelines. [S1]
- **Activated charcoal is very rarely indicated: 1 g/kg may be recommended in high-risk cases presenting early — discuss with a toxicologist.** [S1]
- **Significant skin exposure: wash with soap and water.** [S1]

## Disposition

- **Asymptomatic, or recovering from GI symptoms only: monitor 4 hours before discharge.** [S1]
- **Consider admission** for any child with signs or symptoms of a larger exposure and for adolescents with an intentional overdose. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Transfer all significant ingestions and anyone who needed initial resuscitation.** **PIPER: 1300 137 650.** [S1]
- **Discharge:** normal GCS · normal ECG · observation complete · after deliberate ingestion, low assessed risk of further self-harm. [S1]
