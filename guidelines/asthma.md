# Asthma — adults and adolescents (12 years and over)

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-21 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults and adolescents ≥12 years, stable disease. Children and acute asthma are separate topics.

> **Australian primary source throughout.** *Australian Asthma Handbook*, National Asthma Council Australia,
> **Version 3.0 (2025)** [S1] — the national guidelines for health professionals. Unusually, the handbook
> carries PBS codes and authority codes inline, so the Australian layer is the source's own.

## The single most important statement

> **All treatment levels include ICS. Treatment solely with as-needed SABA is not recommended for adults or
> adolescents with asthma, even if symptoms are infrequent** [S1].

The handbook flags this as an alert. Reliever-only asthma management is not a lighter version of treatment;
it is outside the guideline at every level of severity.

## Treatment levels

Four levels, least to most intensive. The optimal level for an individual may change over time [S1].

| Level | **Recommended** | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Low-dose **budesonide–formoterol, taken as needed** (anti-inflammatory reliever only) | maintenance low-dose ICS + as-needed SABA |
| **2** | **Low-dose MART** — maintenance low-dose ICS-formoterol, plus extra doses from the *same inhaler* as needed | maintenance low-dose ICS-LABA + as-needed SABA |
| **3** | **Medium-dose MART** — maintenance medium-dose ICS-formoterol (more inhalations of the low-dose inhaler), plus extra doses from the same inhaler as needed | maintenance medium-dose ICS-LABA + as-needed SABA |
| **4** | **Targeted intensive treatment** — regimens based on ICS-LABA, sometimes with add-ons such as LAMAs or monoclonal antibodies | — |

**MART is one inhaler doing two jobs.** The patient uses their regular maintenance inhaler *also* as the
reliever. **Other ICS-LABA combinations cannot be used this way** [S1] — only the ICS-formoterol products the
TGA has approved for it.

### Why ICS-formoterol rather than SABA for relief

The handbook's stated rationale [S1]:

- At levels 1–3, ICS-formoterol reliever regimens **reduce severe exacerbations requiring oral
  corticosteroids** compared with as-needed SABA. Low-dose budesonide–formoterol as needed *markedly*
  reduces that risk versus SABA alone (Crossingham 2021); MART reduces it versus the same or higher dose of
  ICS or ICS-LABA (Sobieraj 2018).
- **Preventing exacerbations is the point, because the exacerbation treatment itself carries harm.** Short
  courses of oral corticosteroids are associated with increased lifetime risk of osteoporosis, pneumonia,
  cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disease, cataract, sleep apnoea, renal impairment, depression/anxiety,
  type 2 diabetes and weight gain (Price 2018).
- **Poor ICS adherence is common.** Combining ICS with the reliever ensures patients take ICS at least
  whenever they have symptoms (Murphy 2021).
- When stepping down, an ICS-formoterol reliever means **both the ICS dose and the bronchodilator rise
  automatically** if symptoms increase (Rabe 2006).

## ICS dose bands (total daily dose, micrograms)

These are dose *options*, **not equivalences** — the handbook states the table does not indicate dose
equivalence [S1].

| Active ingredient | Low | Medium | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beclometasone dipropionate (extra-fine) | 100–200 | 250–400 | >400 |
| Budesonide | 200–400 | 500–800 | >800 |
| Ciclesonide | 80–160 | 240–320 | >320 |
| Fluticasone furoate | – | 100 | 200 |
| Fluticasone propionate | 100–200 | 250–500 | >500 |
| Mometasone furoate with indacaterol (capsule inhaler)* | 62.5 | 127.5 | 260 |
| Mometasone furoate with indacaterol and glycopyrronium* | – | 68 | 136 |

\* delivered dose, not metered dose, in line with inhaler labels; all others are metered dose [S1].

## Level 4 — targeted intensive treatment

| Strategy | Role [S1] |
|---|---|
| **Monoclonal antibodies (biologics)** | targeted anti-inflammatory treatment by allergic status and inflammatory phenotype, **specialist care** |
| **High-dose ICS-LABA + as-needed SABA** | short-term (3–6 month) trial while investigating causes, or pending biologic eligibility; specialist care when medium-dose ICS-LABA fails |
| **ICS-LABA-LAMA + as-needed SABA** (medium or high ICS) | trial where blood eosinophils/FeNO are normal; long-term for selected patients with demonstrated benefit, including those ineligible for biologics |
| **Montelukast** | may be considered as add-on in aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease; limited use in severe asthma. ⚠ **TGA boxed warning for neuropsychiatric adverse effects** — counsel accordingly |
| **Azithromycin** | add-on in specialist care for persistent exacerbations despite medium-dose ICS-LABA. **Screening required**, cautions apply |

## Prescribing cautions

**A patient on an anti-inflammatory reliever must not be on a concomitant single-agent long-acting
beta₂-agonist** — this is in the TGA-approved indication [S1].

**Three days of deterioration is a reassessment trigger.** If a patient experiences a 3-day period of
deteriorating symptoms after taking additional as-needed inhalations, reassess for alternative explanations
of persisting symptoms [S1].

**Maximum doses** stated in TGA-approved product information are rarely needed in practice [S1]. For
budesonide–formoterol 200/6 DPI: 6 inhalations per occasion, 8 per day, 12 in one day temporarily.

## Australian context — PBS

The handbook carries PBS and authority codes for each product directly [S1]. Anti-inflammatory reliever
products (budesonide–formoterol without maintenance) are PBS-listed with authority codes and **2 repeats**;
MART products carry **5 repeats**. Several MART listings are marked *"Condition must be stable for the
prescriber to consider the listed maximum quantity"* — the **60-day prescribing** flag [S1].

Age restrictions differ by device and are a prescribing constraint, not a clinical one: most budesonide–
formoterol products are ≥12 years, while *DuoResp Spiromax* and *Fostair* are **≥18 years** [S1].
