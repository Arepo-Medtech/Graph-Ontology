# Acute behavioural disturbance in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation

## Key points

- **Focus on verbal and non-verbal de-escalation**, emphasising the child's safety and using existing behaviour or communication plans. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Exclude organic causes — pain, encephalopathy, delirium** — and consider an underlying neurodevelopmental condition or adverse childhood experiences. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Physical restraint is a last resort**, used only to facilitate rapidly effective pharmacological treatment by appropriately trained staff. [S1]
- **The first job is to reduce the child's distress**; assess and treat the underlying cause once distress is addressed. [S1]

## Assess

- **History:** previous episodes and what worked · neurodevelopmental, intellectual, communication or mental health issues · existing behaviour plans or aids · adverse childhood experiences — **a calm child may be frozen or dissociated** · triggers · pain · medications and reactions. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Consider intoxication** with alcohol, illicit drugs or prescribed medication. **Briefly exclude** focal neurology, acute pain, altered conscious state or a toxidrome; examine fully once calmer. [S1]
- **Investigations are not routine** — only as needed to exclude organic causes of altered conscious state. [S1]

## De-escalate

- **De-escalation is first line.** Treat the cause, **involve senior staff early and consider a Code Response**, and debrief child, family and staff. [S1]
- **Environment:** a private, calm, quiet room with soft lighting; remove triggers and dangerous items; know the exits. [S1]
- **Child:** respect personal space; consider their needs (language, neurodevelopment, trauma); offer analgesia, food and drink; involve mental health early; **offer planned 'collaborative' sedation, eg oral medication.** [S1]
- **Staff:** agree one plan before approaching; **one person talks** to the child and family; concise non-judgemental language in a calm voice; clear limits and choices, no bargaining; ignore insults. [S1]

## Sedation and consent

- ⚠️ **Oral or IM sedation only if de-escalation fails or safety is at risk**; a safe environment without physical restraint is the priority. Use a **stepwise** approach, escalating faster if safety demands. [S1]
- **Seek consent wherever possible**; a young person with capacity can consent. **If the child is a danger to self or others, no consent is needed**, but know your duty of care and your jurisdiction's Mental Health Act. [S1]
- **A child who needs no acute medical or psychiatric care should be discharged to a safe environment rather than restrained** chemically or physically. [S1]

## Adverse reactions

- **Benzodiazepines:** airway support; consider flumazenil. ⚠️ **No flumazenil for a child on frequent or long-term benzodiazepines, or who may have taken seizure-causing substances**; always involve senior staff. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Paradoxical reactions** (more agitation and anxiety) may occur in children with autism or developmental delay. [S1]
- **Droperidol (typical antipsychotic) — listed management: benzatropine 0.02 mg/kg (max 1 mg) IV or IM, repeat in 15 min if required.** [S1]
- **Olanzapine-associated dystonic reactions: benzatropine 0.02 mg/kg (max 1 mg) IV or IM, repeat in 15 min if required.** [S1]
- **Ketamine:** airway support — the row is marked 'with rapid administration'. [S1]

## After sedation

- **Monitor in a safe clinical area without waking the child unnecessarily**, using a local sedation scale. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Alert: observations 30-minutely for 2 hours. Agitated: continuous observation and regular vital signs. Altered conscious state: 1:1 support, continuous pulse oximetry and regular medical review.** [S1]
- **After medication, every child needs a medical and mental health assessment.** Document reasons, drugs, doses and routes, and which interventions worked. [S1]

## Admit, transfer, discharge

- **Involve the paediatric team** if admission is needed to treat a medical cause or observe until drug toxicity resolves. **Transfer** for tertiary inpatient mental health care, sedation complications, or care beyond the hospital's comfort level. [S1]
- **Discharge when** distress has settled, significant medical or psychiatric illness is excluded or addressed, the cause is treated, carers are able and willing (consider crisis care), and **follow-up is planned.** [S1]
