# Iron deficiency in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** diagnosis, dietary management, oral iron dosing and formulations, and IV iron indications for iron deficiency with or without anaemia in infants (over 1 month) and children. Workup of anaemia generally is in `anaemia-children`; the adult counterpart is `iron-deficiency`.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 23 of 23 claims quoted verbatim from the **RCH Melbourne Clinical Practice Guideline *Iron deficiency*** (Last updated Aug 2023); **25 fragments re-checked by machine. 13 quoted doses.**

> **Serum ferritin is the screening test; ferritin <20 μg/L = borderline/low stores.** Most children do well on oral iron plus dietary change.

---

## Recognise it

- **Iron deficiency is the most common cause of anaemia in children.** In infants and toddlers it is mainly nutritional (too few iron-rich foods or too much cow milk), rarely malabsorption or GI bleeding. **Transfusion is rarely required.** [S1]
- **Risk factors** (the page tabulates them across infants, children and adolescents; column split not restated here): maternal iron deficiency, prematurity/low birth weight, multiple pregnancy, exclusive breast-feeding after 6 months, late or insufficient iron-rich solids, excess cow milk, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander, vegetarian/vegan diet, GI disorders (e.g. Meckel diverticulum, coeliac, IBD), other chronic blood loss, **heavy menstrual bleeding**, extreme athletes. [S1]
- **Symptoms:** impaired cognition, memory, learning and concentration; behavioural disturbance; fatigue; pica. [S1]

## Investigate

- **Ferritin <20 μg/L = borderline/low iron stores**; local thresholds may vary (e.g. 10-30 μg/L). [S1]
- ⚠️ **A normal ferritin does not exclude iron deficiency** with coexisting infection, inflammation or liver disease (acute phase reactant). [S1]
- **Do not request iron studies or serum iron to diagnose iron deficiency** — serum iron reflects recent intake, not stores. [S1]
- **An FBE is needed to diagnose iron deficiency anaemia** — typically microcytic, hypochromic (low MCV and MCH). [S1]

## Diet

- **Treat low ferritin, with or without anaemia, with iron plus dietary change:** more iron-rich foods, less cow milk. [S1]
- ⚠️ **No cow milk under 12 months; limit to <500 mL/day over 12 months.** Consider dietitian referral. [S1]

## Oral iron

- **Doses are elemental iron. Prevention: 1-2 mg/kg/day (max 30 mg/day). Treatment of iron deficiency and IDA: 3-6 mg/kg/day**; consider higher doses in severe anaemia (Hb <80 g/L). [S1]
- **These doses are for infants and children >1 month**; use neonatal guidelines for neonates. [S1]
- **Warn parents:** black stools, constipation and teeth staining — drink through a straw and brush teeth afterwards. Vitamin C (e.g. orange juice) improves absorption. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Never give oral and parenteral iron together; withhold oral iron for at least one week after an infusion.** [S1]
- **Severe anaemia: follow up within a week** to check compliance and response (reticulocytosis, rising Hb). [S1]
- **Continue iron for at least 3 months after the anaemia is corrected**, then recheck Hb and ferritin. **Poor compliance is the leading cause of treatment failure.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Over-the-counter multivitamin/mineral supplements don't contain enough iron to treat IDA — don't use them.** [S1]

## Formulations and quick doses

- **Ferrous sulfate oral mixture (Ferro-liquid®) = 6 mg/mL elemental iron.** Iron polymaltose mixture (Maltofer®) = 10 mg/mL, for children who don't tolerate Ferro-liquid; off-label under 12 years. [S1]
- **Fefol® spansules (ferrous sulfate 270 mg) = 87.4 mg (elemental iron column)**; can be opened and sprinkled on food, not crushed or chewed. **Ferro-grad® (ferrous sulfate 325 mg) = 105 mg** (same column), modified release, swallowed whole. [S1]
- **Mild to moderate IDA (3 mg/kg/day), Ferro-Liquid:** <10 kg 0.5 mL/kg/day · 10 kg 5 mL/day · 20 kg 10 mL/day · 30 kg 15 mL/day · >40 kg 20 mL/day. [S1]
- **Severe IDA (Hb 80 g/L or less; 6 mg/kg/day), Ferro-Liquid:** <10 kg 1 mL/kg/day · 10 kg 10 mL/day · 20 kg 20 mL/day · 30 kg 30 mL/day · >40 kg 35 mL/day. [S1]
- **Over 40 kg,** doses are usually limited to one spansule/tablet per day unless Hb and reticulocytes fail to improve. [S1]

## IV iron and referral

- **Consider IV iron for:** contraindication, intolerance or serious compliance problems with oral iron · malabsorption (e.g. GI disease) · erythropoietin-stimulating agents · blood loss exceeding absorptive capacity · rapid repletion (e.g. before non-deferrable surgery) · persistent deficiency despite a 3-month oral trial. [S1]
- **Consult the local paediatric team** if the cause is unclear or deficiency persists despite a 3-month adequate oral trial. [S1]
