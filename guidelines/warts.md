## ⚠️ Refer, don't treat, if

> **Specialist referral is required if warts are large, extensive or in certain locations (eg urethral,
> intra-anal, cervical), for PREGNANT WOMEN WITH ANOGENITAL WARTS, if the diagnosis is in doubt, or if
> standard treatments fail** [A1].

## Classification

**Caused by human papillomavirus, with different genotypes infecting particular locations** [A1]:

- **cutaneous** — **common, flat, mosaic, plantar and palmar**
- **anogenital**
- **other** — **oral, laryngeal, conjunctival and nasal**

---

## Cutaneous warts

> ⚠️ **In immunocompetent individuals, cutaneous warts may disappear without treatment within a few months
> or years** [A1].

**Consider treatment when warts are a cosmetic problem or cause unwanted effects — pain, functional
impairment, psychological distress** [A1]. **Choice depends on location and number of warts, previous
treatments, and patient preference** [A1].

### Salicylic acid — first choice

> **The treatment of choice, as it is relatively safe, inexpensive and may be used at home** [A1].

⚠️ **Irritant and not suitable for application to the face or broken skin; reduce frequency of application
if discomfort occurs** [A1].

> ⚠️ **If warts remain after 3 months of treatment, consider cryotherapy** [A1].

⚠️ **This guideline gives no strength, frequency or duration for salicylic acid — see *Unresolved*.**

### Podophyllum resin

⚠️ **Other treatments are preferred, as evidence of efficacy is lacking and close supervision is required
due to risk of systemic toxicity. Contraindicated in pregnancy** [A1].

### Preparation and technique [A1]

- **Before applying treatment, remove any excess skin from warts with a blade, pumice stone or emery board**
- ⚠️ **Treat plantar warts at pressure points with non-surgical methods to reduce risk of painful
  scarring; consider referral to a podiatrist**
- **Advise on reducing spread: avoid skin maceration or trauma to warts; wear thongs in public showers and
  change rooms**

## Anogenital warts

> **Some anogenital warts may disappear without treatment within a year** [A1].

**Consider treatment when warts cause unwanted effects — itch, pain, bleeding, psychological
distress** [A1].

| Line | Agent | [A1] |
|---|---|---|
| **First** | **Imiquimod** | **for SELF-treatment of external anogenital warts**; **works by modifying the immune response**; **advantage of less frequent application**. ⚠️ **Other treatments preferred in pregnancy** |
| **Second** | **Podophyllotoxin** | ⚠️ **available via the Special Access Scheme**; **irritant but low systemic toxicity**. ⚠️ **Contraindicated in pregnancy** |
| **Specialist** | **Trichloroacetic acid** | **used by some specialists for internal and external warts**; ⚠️ **highly irritant and NOT suitable for self-treatment**; ⚠️ **mainly used in pregnancy** |

### Imiquimod

**Enhances the immune response to viral infections and tumours by inducing immune system cells as well as
interferon and other cytokines** [A1].

| | [A1] |
|---|---|
| **Dose** | **apply to the affected area 3 times a week (eg Monday, Wednesday, Friday)**, **leave on for 6–10 hours** |
| **Duration** | **until total clearance or a maximum of 16 weeks** |
| ⚠️ **Expectation** | ⚠️ **the median time to total wart clearance is 10 WEEKS** |
| **How** | **apply before bedtime as a thin layer after washing and drying the area; wash off with mild soap and water 6–10 hours later; wash hands before and after; avoid bathing or showering after application** |

> ⚠️ **Imiquimod cream may WEAKEN CONDOMS AND DIAPHRAGMS and should be washed off before they are used.**
> **If the patient decides to have sex, apply the cream afterwards, or wash it off if already
> applied** [A1].

⚠️ **That is a contraceptive failure risk attached to a wart cream, in exactly the population using
barrier methods to limit HPV transmission.** See **contraception**.

⚠️ **NOT approved for urethral, intravaginal, cervical, rectal or intra-anal warts** [A1] — the same sites
AMH lists for specialist referral.

**Cautions:** ⚠️ **efficacy may be reduced in HIV and immunocompromised patients**; **theoretical risk of
reduced immunosuppressant activity with immunosuppressants**; ⚠️ **autoimmune and inflammatory skin
disorders such as psoriasis and eczema may be exacerbated**; **broken skin raises potential for increased
systemic absorption** [A1].

**Adverse effects:** **local skin reactions — erythema, itch, burning, erosion, ulceration, blisters,
exfoliation, skin hardening, oedema, scabbing, and** ⚠️ **hypo- or hyperpigmentation which MAY BE
PERMANENT.** ⚠️ **Rarely Stevens–Johnson syndrome and erythema multiforme** [A1].

**Pregnancy:** **limited data; although it should be safe to use (minimal or poor absorption), other
treatments for anogenital warts are preferred** [A1].

### Podophyllotoxin

⚠️ **Not marketed in Australia; may be available through the Special Access Scheme** [A1]. **Binds to
tubulin, arresting mitosis in metaphase and leading to epithelial cell death** [A1].

> **A 7-day treatment cycle: apply the liquid twice a day for 3 days, followed by no treatment for 4 days.
> The cycle may be repeated as necessary for up to 4 or 5 cycles** [A1].

⚠️ **Direct medical supervision is required for lesions in children and for lesion areas >4 cm².**
⚠️ **Prescribers should apply the first treatment and instruct the patient in proper use** [A1].

⚠️ **Do not use on broken skin.** ⚠️ **Contraindicated in pregnancy — data lacking, but podophyllum resin,
the parent compound, may be teratogenic. Avoid in breastfeeding** [A1].

**Apply to the wart only, avoiding normal skin. Avoid contact with eyes.** **Common: local irritation
including burning, inflammation, pain, erosion, itch** [A1].

## Physical treatments

**Cryotherapy (usually liquid nitrogen) may be used for both cutaneous and anogenital warts.** ⚠️ **It
requires multiple treatments, is painful — ESSENTIALLY PREVENTING USE IN YOUNG CHILDREN — and may cause
scarring, dyspigmentation, infection and, rarely, damage to underlying nerves** [A1].

**Electrosurgery, excision or laser are further options depending on location; some specialists may consider
these when topical agents or cryotherapy fail or are not suitable** [A1].

## ⚠️ After treatment

> ⚠️ **HPV may persist after treatment and recurrence is common** [A1].
>
> ⚠️ **As HPV may persist after treatment, it can be transmitted EVEN WHEN NO WARTS ARE VISIBLE; using a
> condom may reduce the risk of HPV transmission** [A1].

**HPV vaccination protects against the HPV types that commonly cause anogenital warts, reducing their
incidence** [A1].

⚠️ **Clearing the warts is not clearing the virus, and the guideline says so because the patient will
assume otherwise.**
