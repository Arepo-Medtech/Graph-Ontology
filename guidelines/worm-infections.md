## What is actually seen in Australia

- ⚠️ **Australia is free of many worm infections, but threadworm (*Enterobius vermicularis*) is common
  throughout** [M1].
- **Hookworms (*Ancylostoma duodenale*, *Necator americanus*), the common roundworm (*Ascaris
  lumbricoides*), the whipworm (*Trichuris trichiura*) and *Strongyloides stercoralis* are not uncommon in
  northern Australia** [M1].
- **Hydatid (*Echinococcus granulosus*) disease is occasionally seen, particularly in residents and former
  residents of farming communities** [M1].
- **A traveller may acquire these or other worm infections, eg schistosomiasis, while overseas** [M1].

**Whether worms cause disease depends particularly on the intensity of infection** — with most intestinal
worms, **disease develops only if the worm burden is sufficiently high**. In some infections, **particularly
hydatids, the location of the worm is important**. ⚠️ **With the exception of *S. stercoralis*, worms cannot
multiply in the human host**, so the burden depends on duration and intensity of exposure [M1].

> ### ⚠️ AMH's stated treatment threshold is social, not clinical
> **"Most people do not like the idea of harbouring worms and it is reasonable in Australia to treat any
> person with a helminth infection"** [M1].

## Treatment

| Infection | Treatment [M1] |
|---|---|
| **Hookworm** | **Single dose albendazole**, or a **3-day course of pyrantel or mebendazole** |
| **Roundworm** | **Single dose albendazole or pyrantel**, or a **3-day course of mebendazole** |
| **Whipworm** | **3-day course of albendazole or mebendazole** |
| **Threadworm** | **Single dose albendazole, mebendazole or pyrantel** |
| **Strongyloidiasis** | **Single dose ivermectin** preferred; **3-day course of albendazole** if unable to take ivermectin, eg **children under 15 kg** |
| ***Fasciola hepatica*** (liver fluke) | ⚠️ **Usually resistant to the available anthelmintics**, but often responds to **triclabendazole — Special Access Scheme, seek specialist advice** |

> ### ⚠️ Threadworm: the two instructions that decide whether it works
> **Repeat drug treatment after 2 weeks if infection is not eradicated** [M1].
> **Treat all members of the family, irrespective of whether they have been diagnosed with the
> infection** [M1].

⚠️ **Immunocompromised people with strongyloidiasis need longer treatment — seek specialist advice** [M1].

## AMH's comparative table

| Drug | Pregnancy | Breastfeeding | Children | Adverse effects |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Albendazole** | ⚠️ **Avoid use** | Appears safe | **May use if >6 months** | Well tolerated |
| **Mebendazole** | ⚠️ **Avoid in first trimester** | May use | **May use if >6 months** | Well tolerated |
| **Ivermectin** | ⚠️ **Avoid use** | May use | ⚠️ **May use if >5 years and/or >15 kg** | Mild adverse effects when used for strongyloidiasis |
| **Praziquantel** | Appears safe | Safe | May use | Well tolerated in short courses |
| **Pyrantel** | **Safe** | **Safe** | May use | Well tolerated |

*(All from [M1].)*

⚠️ **Pyrantel is the only drug in the table rated safe in both pregnancy and breastfeeding** — which
matters because it is an option for hookworm, roundworm and threadworm.
