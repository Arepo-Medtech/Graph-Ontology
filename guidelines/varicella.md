# Varicella (chickenpox)

## Schedule

- **All children under 14: 2 doses of varicella-containing vaccine, the first at 18 months, as MMRV.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **14 and over: 2 doses at least 4 weeks apart**, monovalent — **MMRV is not recommended in this age group.** A longer interval is acceptable. [S1]
- **Non-immunity is judged on a history of no previous varicella**, which can be supplemented by serology. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Varicella is more severe in adults, especially pregnant women and the immunocompromised.** [S1]

## Post-exposure

- ⚠️ **Post-exposure vaccination: within 5 days, preferably within 3**, for non-immune age-eligible people with a significant exposure to varicella **or herpes zoster**. [S1]

## Transmission

- ⚠️ **Infectious from 48 hours before the rash until all lesions have crusted.** Spread by respiratory secretions and **from the vesicle fluid of varicella or zoster lesions**. [S1]

## Pregnancy

- ⚠️ **The vaccine is contraindicated in pregnancy; avoid pregnancy for 28 days after vaccination** — a theoretical risk only: **there are no reports of vaccine-induced congenital varicella syndrome.** [S1]
- **Infection in pregnancy can cause congenital varicella syndrome** — skin scarring, limb defects, ocular anomalies, neurological malformations. Risk is **higher with 2nd-trimester than 1st-trimester infection (1.4% vs 0.55%)**. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Maternal varicella near delivery can cause severe neonatal varicella**; the fetus is also at risk of **zoster in infancy (0.8–1.7%)**, highest after 3rd-trimester infection. [S1]
