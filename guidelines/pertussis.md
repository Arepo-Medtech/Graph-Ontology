## ⚠️ Pregnancy — the single most effective intervention

> **dTpa as a single dose in each pregnancy.** It **reduces the risk of pertussis in young infants aged
> 3–4 months by 90%**, by transplacental transfer of antibodies [S1].

- **Each pregnancy, including closely spaced ones** — antibody levels wane, and the level a mother needs
  to pass on immunity is unknown [S1].
- ⚠️ **Optimal window: 20–32 weeks gestation.** Maternal antibody transfer starts from 13 weeks and is
  maximal from 30 weeks; ⚠️ **antibody levels do not peak until about 2 weeks after vaccination** [S1].

## Schedule

| age | vaccine |
|---|---|
| **2, 4, 6 months** | DTPa-hepB-IPV-Hib |
| **18 months** | DTPa |
| **4 years** | DTPa-IPV |
| **11–13 years** | dTpa — **1 dose regardless of how many previous doses** |
| **≥65 years** | dTpa **if none in the past 10 years** |

*All from [S1].*

- ⚠️ **The first dose can be given as early as 6 weeks** — and the next doses stay at **4 and 6 months** [S1].
- The 18-month and 4-year doses exist **because immunity wanes after the infant doses** [S1].
- **No preference** between Vaxelis and Infanrix hexa [S1].

⚠️ **Adult household contacts and carers of infants under 6 months: dTpa at least 2 weeks before close
contact**, if their last dose was over 10 years ago [S1].

## Who gives it to infants

⚠️ **Parents are the source in more than 50% of infant cases.** Household contacts and carers are often
the source; adolescents and adults with waning immunity are **a significant reservoir** [S1].

**Notification rates are higher among Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children** than
non-Indigenous children across all age groups under 5 [S1].

## Recognition

- Spread by **aerosols**; **incubation 7–20 days**; infects **90% of susceptible household contacts** [S1].
- ⚠️ **Natural infection does not give long-term protection — reinfection occurs** [S1].
- ⚠️ **The whoop is the exception.** It is **less common in anyone with immunity** from vaccination or
  infection [S1].
- ⚠️ **In very young infants the cough may be absent — the only signs may be apnoea or cyanosis** [S1].
- **More than 25% of adults have a cough lasting 5 days or more each year, and *B. pertussis* may cause up
  to 7% of them** [S1] — the case for a low threshold to test.

**Diagnosis:** culture or **nucleic acid testing** of a nasal or throat swab or nasopharyngeal aspirate, or
serology. **Nucleic acid testing is usually the method of choice**; the right test depends on age,
vaccination history and duration of symptoms [S1].

## Notification

> ⚠️ **Suspected and confirmed pertussis are notifiable in every state and territory.**
> **Investigate suspected cases regardless of vaccination status** — immunisation is not 100% effective
> and immunity wanes [S1].

## Safety

- ⚠️ **The only absolute contraindications:** anaphylaxis after a previous dose of any acellular
  pertussis vaccine, or after any component of one [S1].
- ⚠️ **Adacel's syringe tip cap contains latex** — use an alternative product in latex allergy [S1].
- Acellular vaccines cause **fever in ~20%** (whole-cell ~45%) and **local reactions in ~10%** (whole-cell
  ~40%). **Whole-cell vaccines are no longer available in Australia** [S1].
- **Extensive limb swelling in under 2% after DTPa boosters** — swelling or redness over at least half the
  limb circumference and the joints above and below [S1].

**dTpa (Adacel):** 0.5 mL, **intramuscular**; registered as a booster **from age 10** [S1].
