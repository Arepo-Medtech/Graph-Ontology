## Part 1 — Is this angina? [S1]

⚠️ **Language first.** **The preferred terms are 'cardiac', 'possibly cardiac' or 'non-cardiac'.**
⚠️ **'Atypical chest pain' is commonly used to mean non-cardiac pain but LACKS SPECIFICITY** [S1].

| | **Suggestive of angina** | **Argues against** |
|---|---|---|
| **Quality** | **pain, discomfort, heaviness, tightness, pressure, constriction, squeezing** | ⚠️ **sharp pain increasing with inspiration and lying supine → think PERICARDITIS** |
| **Onset** | **builds gradually in intensity over a few minutes** | ⚠️ **lasting only a few seconds → unlikely ischaemic** |
| **Location** | **often perceived as RETROSTERNAL** | ⚠️ **localised to a fingertip, over the LV apex or a costochondral junction, or reproduced by movement or palpation of the chest wall or arms** |
| **Radiation** | **left arm, neck or jaw** | ⚠️ **into the lower extremities** |
| **Provocation** | **exercise brings it on; it dissipates over a few minutes with rest** | — |
| **At rest** | ⚠️ **at rest or on minimal exertion USUALLY INDICATES ACUTE CORONARY SYNDROME** | — |
| **Associated** | **dyspnoea, palpitations, diaphoresis, light-headedness, syncope or pre-syncope, upper abdominal symptoms or heartburn unrelated to meals, nausea or vomiting** | — |

All rows quoted from [S1].

### ⚠️ Two traps

> ⚠️ **RELIEF BY NITRATE IS NOT A DIAGNOSTIC CRITERION.** **Relief of pain with nitroglycerin is not
> necessarily diagnostic of myocardial ischaemia and should not be used as a diagnostic criterion** [S1].
> **Oesophageal spasm responds too.**

> ⚠️ **SEVERITY DOES NOT TRACK DANGER.** **The severity of chest discomfort is not always a reliable
> indicator of how serious the underlying condition is** [S1].

⚠️ **Women, elderly patients, those with dementia and people with diabetes may not exhibit typical symptoms
of ischaemic chest pain** [S1].

## What chest pain in the community actually turns out to be [S1]

> **Among those presenting to their primary care doctor with chest pain, the final diagnosis was NOT
> ischaemic heart disease in 83%** [S1].

| Cause | Share |
|---|---|
| **Musculoskeletal, including costochondritis** | **36%** |
| **Gastrointestinal** | **19%** |
| **Cardiac (total)** | **16%** |
| — **stable angina** | **10.5%** |
| — ⚠️ **unstable angina or MI** | **1.5%** |
| — **other cardiac** | **3.8%** |
| **Psychiatric** | **8%** |
| **Pulmonary** | **5%** |
| **Other/unknown** | **16%** |

All figures quoted from [S1], which reports them from a single primary-care series.

**Non-cardiac chest pain frequently originates from gastrointestinal sources, with erosive and non-erosive
gastro-oesophageal reflux disease the most common factors** [S1].

⚠️ **Dual aetiologies occur — respiratory, musculoskeletal, gastrointestinal and cardiac causes may
co-exist simultaneously and must not be overlooked** [S1].

## Investigation in the community [S1]

> **The ECG remains the best initial test for evaluating chest pain: rapid, inexpensive and diagnostically
> and prognostically informative** [S1].

⚠️ **In NSTEMI the ECG may be NORMAL in approximately one-third of cases** [S1]. A normal ECG does not
end the assessment.

**Troponin is elevated in many conditions other than MI, including pulmonary embolism and acute aortic
dissection** [S1].

### ⚠️ When ONE troponin is enough — and when it is not

| | [S1] |
|---|---|
| **A single serum troponin may be used** | only where the patient **has been symptom free for at least 24 hours** AND has **NONE of: ongoing chest pain, syncope, heart failure, abnormal ECG** |
| ⚠️ **Refer immediately to an emergency department** | **symptoms within the preceding 24 hours**, OR **the troponin is positive** |

**High-sensitivity troponin has a limited role in the community setting**, but **enables safe rule-out of MI
within one to two hours for most patients** [S1].

**For acute or stable chest pain assessed as low risk, urgent diagnostic testing for suspected coronary
artery disease is NOT needed** [S1].

### Stable-patient testing, with the limits stated [S1]

| Test | ⚠️ Limit quoted in the source |
|---|---|
| **Coronary artery calcium scoring** | ⚠️ **NOT useful in high-risk patients, in patients with documented CAD, or in symptomatic patients** |
| **CT coronary angiography** | ⚠️ **difficult to interpret with coronary calcification, obesity, atrial fibrillation, or heart rate >65 bpm** |

## ⚠️ Not angina — the ones that kill [S1]

| | Quoted from [S1] |
|---|---|
| **Aortic dissection** | **abrupt, intense TEARING pain spreading through the back**; **~20% present with NEUROLOGICAL DEFICITS**; ⚠️ **BP difference >20 mmHg between the arms raises suspicion** |
| **Pulmonary embolism** | **pleuritic chest pain 66%, dyspnoea 73%, cough 37%, haemoptysis 13%**; **D-dimer rules PE OUT in low- or intermediate-risk patients — high sensitivity, low false-negative rate — but has LOW specificity and a high false-positive rate** |
| **Tension pneumothorax** | **reduced or absent breath sounds on the affected side with hyper-resonance on percussion** |
| **Oesophageal rupture** | **severe vomiting or retching FOLLOWED BY acute, severe chest or epigastric pain** |

# Part 2 — Treating stable angina [AMH]

⚠️ **Everything from here is paraphrased from AMH and stored without quotes. None of it is machine
re-checkable, and every dose is in the attestation queue.**

## Before any drug [A1]

**The rationale for drug treatment is to provide symptom relief AND to reduce the risk of death or
myocardial infarction** [A1].

- **Evaluate and manage risk factors** (smoking, diabetes). ⚠️ **Low-dose aspirin and treatment of
  dyslipidaemia and hypertension are what reduce the risk of MI** [A1] — the antianginals below are
  largely symptomatic.
- **Consider causes needing specific treatment** (e.g. **aortic stenosis**) and the **need for
  revascularisation** [A1].
- **Investigate specific precipitants: exertion, ANAEMIA, THYROTOXICOSIS** [A1].

## The ladder [A1]

| Step | Drug |
|---|---|
| **Acute attack / before a precipitating activity** | **short-acting nitrate — sublingual glyceryl trinitrate or isosorbide dinitrate** |
| **1** | ⚠️ **BETA-BLOCKER — recommended as initial therapy** |
| **1 (alternative)** | **calcium channel blocker, in particular VERAPAMIL or DILTIAZEM** — recommended **when beta-blockers are contraindicated** |
| **2** | **add a DIHYDROPYRIDINE calcium channel blocker** if the beta-blocker does not control symptoms |
| **3** | ⚠️ **a third agent only if revascularisation is not appropriate or while waiting for it — seek specialist advice; LIMITED EVIDENCE of improvement with 3 drugs** |
| **Neither beta-blocker nor CCB usable** | **long-acting nitrate, ivabradine, nicorandil or perhexiline** |

> ⚠️ **VASOSPASTIC ANGINA INVERTS THE LADDER.** **Use calcium channel blockers or long-acting nitrates;
> BETA-BLOCKERS MAY EXACERBATE SYMPTOMS** — alpha-mediated vasoconstriction is left unopposed by
> beta-mediated vasodilation [A1]. AMH's beta-blocker page states the same risk as **worsening coronary
> artery spasm** [A9].

### What each class actually buys

- **Beta-blockers reduce frequency of angina, prolong exercise capacity and decrease the risk of adverse
  cardiac events and mortality.** **Carvedilol, bisoprolol or controlled-release metoprolol are
  particularly useful where heart failure coexists** [A1].
- **Symptomatic efficacy of calcium channel blockers is SIMILAR to that of beta-blockers** [A1].
- ⚠️ **ONLY CONTROLLED-RELEASE VERAPAMIL has been shown to reduce the incidence of cardiovascular events in
  stable angina**, and may decrease the risk of reinfarction and death after MI — **but it is
  contraindicated in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction** [A1].
- **Diltiazem has less negative inotropic effect and less effect on impulse generation and conduction than
  verapamil, causes less reflex tachycardia than the dihydropyridines, and can be used safely with nitrates
  and cautiously with beta-blockers (monitor for bradycardia)** [A1].

### ⚠️ Two combination rules

> ⚠️ **AVOID a beta-blocker with VERAPAMIL** — risk of **severe bradycardia and heart block** [A1];
> the verapamil page adds **left ventricular failure** and says avoid **unless under specialist
> supervision** [A11].
> **Combine a beta-blocker with DILTIAZEM cautiously** [A1].

> ⚠️ **NEVER STOP A BETA-BLOCKER ABRUPTLY.** **Abrupt withdrawal may cause increased myocardial ischaemia,
> risk of infarction and sudden death.** **Reduce the dose gradually over 2 WEEKS — or over 4–6 WEEKS if
> the patient has been treated for many years** [A1].

## Doses

subscription before use.**

### Acute relief and pre-activity prophylaxis

| | Dose [AMH] |
|---|---|
| **GTN sublingual SPRAY — acute** | **400–800 micrograms (1 or 2 sprays); repeat after 5 minutes if necessary to a maximum of 3 sprays** [A3] |
| **GTN sublingual SPRAY — before activity** | **400 micrograms (1 spray) 5–10 minutes before** [A3] |
| **GTN sublingual TABLET — acute** | **300–600 micrograms every 3–4 minutes until pain resolves, maximum 1800 micrograms** [A3] ⚠️ **see discontinuation below** |
| **GTN sublingual TABLET — before activity** | **300–600 micrograms 5–10 minutes before** [A3] |
| **Isosorbide dinitrate sublingual — acute** | **5–10 mg** [A5] |
| **Isosorbide dinitrate sublingual — before activity** | **5–10 mg taken 10 minutes before** [A5] |

> ⚠️ **GLYCERYL TRINITRATE SUBLINGUAL TABLETS HAVE BEEN DISCONTINUED IN AUSTRALIA** — an alternative may be
> available via **Section 19A approval** [A3]. The PBS-listed GTN products are the **400 microgram/dose
> spray** and the **5 mg and 10 mg per 24 hours patches** [A3].
> ⚠️ **So the remaining PBS-listed sublingual TABLET for an acute attack is ISOSORBIDE DINITRATE 5 mg
> (Isordil)** [A5] — AMH still prints the GTN tablet dose, and a prescriber working from the dose table
> alone would reach for a product that is no longer marketed.

**Counsel every patient given a short-acting nitrate:** **sit or lie down before use**, and ⚠️ **CALL AN
AMBULANCE if symptoms are severe, get worse quickly, or last 10 minutes** [A3][A5].

**Spray priming** [A3]: **5 sprays into the air before first use**; **1 spray if unused for 7 days**;
**5 sprays if unused for more than 4 months**.
**Tablet storage** [A3]: **original glass bottle, away from moisture, heat and light, lid closed tightly;
do NOT carry close to the body** — otherwise they may not work.

## Nitrates: the tolerance rule [A2]

> ⚠️ **TOLERANCE, WITH LOSS OF EFFECT, OCCURS WITH CONTINUOUS EXPOSURE.** **Avoid it by ensuring a
> nitrate-free interval each day** [A1][A2]. **Short-acting sublingual nitrates can still be used during
> that interval** [A2].

| | Nitrate-free interval [AMH] |
|---|---|
| **GTN patch** | ⚠️ **patch-free ≥8 hours, ideally 10–12 hours, in each 24 hours — usually overnight unless the angina is nocturnal** [A3] |
| **Isosorbide mononitrate** | ⚠️ **once daily only; twice daily leaves NO nitrate-free interval and tolerance is more likely** [A4] |

**Take the long-acting nitrate at the time of day when angina is most frequent** [A4]. **Isosorbide
mononitrate is NOT for acute episodes — its onset is too slow** [A4].

### Onset and duration [A2]

| | Route | Onset | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Glyceryl trinitrate** | **sublingual** | **<5 minutes** | **<1 hour** |
| | **patch** | **30–60 minutes** | **as long as applied** |
| **Isosorbide dinitrate** | **sublingual** | **<10 minutes** | **1–2 hours** |
| **Isosorbide mononitrate** | **controlled release oral** | **1–2 hours** | **prolonged** |

**Oral bioavailability when swallowed: GTN poor, isosorbide dinitrate 20–25%, isosorbide mononitrate
100%** [A2].

### ⚠️ Nitrate contraindications [A2]

⚠️ **CONTRAINDICATED with a PDE5 inhibitor (e.g. sildenafil) or riociguat.** Also contraindicated in
**hypovolaemia, raised intracranial pressure, significant anaemia, hypotension, hypertrophic obstructive
cardiomyopathy, cardiac tamponade, aortic or mitral stenosis, and cor pulmonale** [A2].

**Common adverse effects, mostly vasodilator: headache, flushing, palpitations, orthostatic hypotension,
fainting, peripheral oedema. Infrequent: contact dermatitis (topical) and ⚠️ REBOUND ANGINA** [A2].

**Remove GTN patches before diathermy, defibrillation or cardioversion** [A3]. **GTN is adsorbed onto some
plastics such as PVC — an infusion needs a glass bottle and a polyethylene giving set** [A3].

## ⚠️ The three reserve drugs, and what is wrong with each

### Nicorandil [A6]

**Venous and arterial dilation via its nitrate moiety and an effect on potassium channels. Similar efficacy
to beta-blockers, nitrates and calcium channel blockers in reducing angina frequency, and it reduces the
incidence of cardiovascular events in stable angina** [A6].

> ⚠️ **NICORANDIL CAUSES LARGE PAINFUL ULCERS — particularly ORAL and PERIANAL — and FISTULAE of the GI and
> genital tracts**, as well as **ocular, cutaneous and peristomal ulcers and lesions at wounds or surgical
> sites**. ⚠️ **MISDIAGNOSIS IS COMMON, and healing occurs ONLY after stopping nicorandil** [A6].

**Associated with fistula formation in diverticular disease.** **Contraindicated by the manufacturer with a
PDE5 inhibitor or riociguat** [A6].

### Ivabradine [A8]

**Inhibits a current regulating the interval between sinoatrial node depolarisations, reducing heart rate by
about 10 beats/minute** [A8]. **Indicated in stable angina only with NORMAL SINUS RHYTHM and heart rate
>70 bpm; contraindicated if heart rate is <70 bpm before treatment** [A8].

> ⚠️ **RESERVE IT.** **There is NO EVIDENCE that cardiovascular events or mortality improve, and there may
> be risks with long-term use such as atrial fibrillation** [A8].
> ⚠️ **Adding ivabradine to standard treatment for coronary artery disease WITHOUT heart failure did not
> improve cardiovascular outcomes overall, and INCREASED the combined risk of cardiovascular death and
> non-fatal MI in a subgroup with symptomatic angina** — at a maximum dose higher than recommended [A8].

**Contraindicated with potent CYP3A4 inhibitors (itraconazole, clarithromycin) and with diltiazem or
verapamil** [A8]. **Phosphenes — transient areas of enhanced brightness in the visual field — are COMMON,
especially in the first 2 months, and usually resolve** [A8]. **Stop if resting heart rate remains <50 bpm
at the lowest dose** [A8].

> ⚠️ **The PBS listing for ivabradine is CHRONIC HEART FAILURE, not angina** [A8]. A patient started on it
> for angina pays privately.

### Perhexiline [A7]

**For angina refractory to other drugs or surgical procedures; may be used with other antianginals and in
patients with heart failure** [A7].

> ⚠️ **METABOLISM IS SATURABLE AND THE THERAPEUTIC RANGE IS NARROW.** **Poor metabolisers may need as
> little as 50 mg ONCE A WEEK; ultrarapid metabolisers may need up to 600 mg DAILY** [A7] — a
> **twelve-fold-per-week to eighty-four-fold-per-week span between two patients on the same drug.**
> **Dose adjustments during chronic treatment: increase by NO MORE THAN 50 mg daily** [A7].

| Monitoring [A7] | |
|---|---|
| **Therapeutic range** | **0.15–0.6 mg/L (0.5–2 micromol/L)** |
| **First sample** | **within 3–5 days of starting, to identify poor metabolisers** |
| **Then** | **monthly until stable in range, then every 3 months** |
| **Also** | **liver function at baseline and regular intervals; weight; blood glucose; signs of hepatotoxicity or neuropathy** |

⚠️ **Perhexiline can cause HYPOGLYCAEMIA**, most likely in those on **insulin or a sulfonylurea** — monitor
blood glucose carefully **especially in the first few days** and adjust the antidiabetic dose [A7].
**Hepatotoxicity (cirrhosis, hepatitis) and peripheral neuropathy are the infrequent serious effects** [A7].

## Beta-blocker safety carried from [A9]

**Contraindicated in bradycardia of 45–50 bpm, second- or third-degree AV block, sick sinus syndrome
without a pacemaker, severe hypotension, uncontrolled heart failure, cardiogenic or hypovolaemic shock, and
severe or poorly controlled asthma** [A9].

- ⚠️ **Well-controlled asthma or COPD is NOT an automatic bar:** **beta1-selective agents such as metoprolol
  may be considered — on specialist advice for asthma — because of their morbidity and mortality benefits
  in heart failure and acute coronary syndromes** [A9].
- **Beta-blockers may mask signs of acute hypoglycaemia such as tachycardia and tremor**; **beta1-selective
  agents and those with alpha1-blocking activity are preferred in type 2 diabetes** [A9].
- **Continue through the perioperative period**, aware of **increased risk of bradycardia and hypotension
  during surgery** [A9].

**Calcium channel blocker bar:** ⚠️ **diltiazem and verapamil are CONTRAINDICATED in heart failure with
reduced ejection fraction** unless under specialist supervision, and in **severe bradycardia, sick sinus
syndrome, second- or third-degree AV block without a pacemaker, systolic BP <90 mmHg, and AF or atrial
flutter with an accessory pathway** [A10]. **Constipation is COMMON with verapamil; ileus is rare** [A11].
