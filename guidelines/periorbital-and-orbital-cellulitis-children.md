# Periorbital and orbital cellulitis in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** distinguishing periorbital (preseptal) from orbital (postseptal) cellulitis, severity, imaging, antibiotics and disposition in children. ## Recognise it

- ⚠️ **Orbital cellulitis is an emergency** with complications including intracranial infection, cavernous sinus thrombosis and vision loss. [S1]
- **Orbital = postseptal** (behind the orbital septum), and over 80% relate to local sinus disease. **Periorbital = preseptal** — eyelids and surrounding skin, not the orbit. **The globe is not involved in either.** [S1]
- **The two overlap clinically and can be hard to tell apart.** Typical presentation: unilateral eyelid swelling and erythema, with unilateral eye pain or tenderness. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Red flags for orbital cellulitis:** painful or restricted eye movements · reduced acuity, relative afferent pupil defect or diplopia · proptosis · severe headache or other signs of intracranial involvement. [S1]
- **Neonates: consider gonorrhoea and *Chlamydia*** and send PCR swabs. [S1]
- **Bilateral or painless (non-tender) swelling in a well child is more likely allergic.** [S1]

## Orbital cellulitis: investigate and manage

- ⚠️ **Admit, keep fasted until the need for surgery is clear, and seek ENT and ophthalmology advice urgently.** Consider urgent contrast CT of orbits and sinuses ± brain. [S1]
- **FBE and blood culture.** ⚠️ **Lumbar puncture is contraindicated** — risk of raised intracranial pressure from possible intracranial extension. [S1]
- **Treat underlying sinus disease** (e.g. nasal decongestants, steroids), often guided by ENT. [S1]
- **Orbital cellulitis IV: cefotaxime 50 mg/kg (max 2 g) 6-hourly or ceftriaxone 100 mg/kg (max 4 g) daily**; add vancomycin if MRSA is suspected. [S1]
- **IV usually for at least 3–4 days, then oral amoxicillin/clavulanate 22.5 mg/kg (amoxicillin component, max 875 mg) twice daily — 10–14 days total.** [S1]

## Periorbital cellulitis: manage by severity

- ⚠️ **Inadequate Hib vaccination: treat as severe periorbital cellulitis.** [S1]
- **Severe:** investigate and manage as an inpatient, as for orbital cellulitis. [S1]
- **Moderate:** inpatient or hospital-in-the-home; blood culture if febrile and unwell. Change to oral once improving; **if not improving or worse within 24–48 hours, manage as severe.** [S1]
- **Moderate or severe, IV:** cefazolin 50 mg/kg (max 2 g) 8-hourly **or** ceftriaxone 50 mg/kg (max 2 g) daily (HITH) **or**, if MRSA suspected, clindamycin 15 mg/kg (max 600 mg) IV 8-hourly or oral. IV usually 1–2 days, then oral. [S1]
- **Moderate or severe, oral step-down:** as for mild periorbital cellulitis; if MRSA suspected, clindamycin 15 mg/kg (max 600 mg) three times daily. **Total 7–10 days.** [S1]
- **Mild periorbital cellulitis in a well child can often be treated orally if follow-up is assured: cefalexin 20 mg/kg (max 750 mg) three times daily for 7–10 days.** [S1]
- **Follow local susceptibility guidance**, including on community-acquired MRSA. [S1]

## Disposition and follow-up

- **Discuss with the paediatric team** for suspected orbital cellulitis, moderate–severe periorbital cellulitis, or no improvement or deterioration after 24–48 hours. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Consider transfer** for severe periorbital or orbital cellulitis, or suspected intracranial involvement (altered conscious state, seizures, focal neurological signs). [S1]
- **Discharge — mild:** oral course prescribed and follow-up assured. **Moderate:** after 24–48 hours of IV antibiotics with improvement, tolerating oral, follow-up assured. [S1]
