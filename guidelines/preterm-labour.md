## Tocolytics

**Used to delay delivery for 24–48 hours, allowing time for transfer to a hospital with appropriate
neonatal facilities and/or administration of corticosteroids** [M1].

> ⚠️ **Tocolysis is generally not indicated before the fetus is viable, when gestation is over 34 weeks, or
> when prolonging the pregnancy is hazardous — eg placental abruption, intrauterine infection** [M1].

**Nifedipine is preferred** [M1]:

- **Appears more effective than salbutamol — fewer births within 7 days of treatment.**
- **Fewer maternal adverse effects.**
- **Associated with reduced neonatal morbidity.**
- **Given orally**, and **may be considered for maintenance treatment** — ⚠️ **however such treatment is
  controversial as evidence for efficacy is lacking**.

⚠️ **There is insufficient evidence to support routine use of glyceryl trinitrate patches or indometacin
for treatment of preterm labour** [M1].

## Antenatal corticosteroids

**Give a single course to women at risk of preterm delivery within 7 days who are at 24–34 weeks
gestation** — ⚠️ **see local protocols as they may differ** [M1].

**Betamethasone or dexamethasone given to the mother accelerates fetal lung maturation and reduces
neonatal death, respiratory distress syndrome and cerebroventricular haemorrhage.** ⚠️ **There is
insufficient evidence to recommend one rather than the other** [M1].

> ### ⚠️ Repeat courses — the trade-off stated in full
> Refer to local protocols or seek specialist advice. **According to a systematic review, repeat courses
> have some short-term benefits — reduced RDS and morbidity — compared with a single course**, ⚠️ **but
> weight, head circumference and length are reduced at birth.** **Follow-up at 2–3 years of age suggested
> no harms, nor any benefits** [M1].

---

## Magnesium sulfate

**Given to women at risk of preterm birth, magnesium sulfate is neuroprotective and reduces the risk of
cerebral palsy in the infant** [M1].

> ⚠️ **Give to women under 30 weeks gestation who are likely to deliver within 24 hours** [M1].
>
> **This is the narrowest window of the three, and the one most easily missed** — a woman at 31 weeks
> qualifies for corticosteroids and tocolysis but not for magnesium sulfate.
