# Emergency airway management in children

## Key points

- **Optimise physiology before every emergency intubation.** [S1]
- **Plan early:** consider calling for help, allocate team roles, agree a plan for failed intubation, and use strategies for situational awareness. **Use an emergency intubation checklist** and other cognitive aids. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Use continuous waveform ETCO2 to confirm ETT position.** If the waveform is lost or the child arrests, reassess, start resuscitation and check the ETT position, including by direct visualisation. [S1]
- **Emergency airway problems are rarely anatomical** — usually physiological or situational. **The strongest predictors of adverse events are multiple attempts, and respiratory or cardiovascular failure as the indication.** [S1]

## Optimise physiology

- **Hypoxia:** elevate the head of the bed up to 40 degrees and **pre-oxygenate with positive airway pressure/PEEP** (BVM with PEEP valve, T-piece, CPAP, BiPAP or HFNC). [S1]
- **Consider apnoeic oxygenation** with nasal cannula oxygen 2 L/kg/min (max 15 L/min). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Abort the attempt if saturations fall below 93% or drop 10% from baseline.** [S1]
- **Hypotension:** fluid resuscitation 10–20 mL/kg; titrate the induction agent dose. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Consider push-dose adrenaline 1 microg/kg (max 50 microg) in the non-arrested child**, repeated as required, including after induction. **This is NOT the cardiac-arrest adrenaline dose.** [S1]

## Assess the airway and the situation

- **Consult anaesthetics if a difficult airway is anticipated.** Congenital syndromes with micrognathia, macroglossia or midface hypoplasia, and some acquired conditions, are often associated with difficult airways. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Higher risk of airway obstruction:** head injury or intoxication, trauma, airway debris, facial or neck burns, and **inhaled smoke or liquids even without facial burns**. [S1]
- ⚠️ **If the airway, physiology or team skillset raises concern, maintain oxygenation and call for help before giving any medication.** [S1]

## Equipment, monitoring and drugs

- **Lay equipment out on an airway template before intubating**; items not on the template (T-piece, suction, video laryngoscope) must be at the bedside and checked. [S1]
- **Monitor saturations, heart rate, respiratory rate, BP (2-minute cycle) and ETCO2.** Draw up induction agent and muscle relaxant, and prepare post-intubation sedation. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Every induction agent can cause acute hypotension at 'normal' doses in unwell children — reduce the dose significantly.** **Ketamine 0.5–2 mg/kg is the preferred default induction agent.** [S1]
- **Rocuronium 1.2–1.6 mg/kg is the preferred default muscle relaxant**; unwell children may need higher doses for normal onset. [S1]
- **The Team Leader reads the checklist aloud, challenge-response, before every emergency intubation**, and reassesses after any failed attempt. [S1]

## Intubation

- ⚠️ **Cricoid force is not routinely used.** In children it compresses the trachea, displaces rather than compresses the oesophagus, and lowers sphincter tone, making regurgitation more likely. [S1]
- ⚠️ **If you cannot see the cords, do not pass the tube.** Remove the laryngoscope, reoxygenate, and replan with a different intubator, position or technique. [S1]

## Confirm the tube

- ⚠️ **Unrecognised oesophageal intubation can be rapidly fatal**, and no single method is 100% reliable. [S1]
- **Waveform capnography is the best objective confirmation.** A zero reading means oesophageal placement or no cardiac output; in arrest or poor perfusion it is less accurate, so use other methods if inconclusive. [S1]
- ⚠️ **If placement is still uncertain, remove the tube and reoxygenate.** Auscultation, chest rise, misting, oximetry and CXR are less reliable; **hypoxia is a late, ominous sign**, and CXR only assesses depth. [S1]

## After intubation

- **Secure the tube, reconfirm its position, start ongoing sedation.** Debrief the team routinely — hot or cold, short (5–10 mins). [S1]
- **Consider transfer** if the local hospital cannot manage intubated children. [S1]
