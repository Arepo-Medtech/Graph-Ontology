**Scope:** adults, **general practice**, including **acute severe ulcerative colitis** as a recognition-and-

---

## Why this sits in general practice at all

> **Australia has one of the highest rates of IBD in the world, with a prevalence of more than one in
> 300.** **More than 50% of patients are diagnosed before 35 years of age** [S2].
>
> ⚠️ **UP TO ONE-THIRD OF PATIENTS WITH IBD ARE MANAGED EXCLUSIVELY BY A PRIMARY CARE PHYSICIAN** [S2].
>
> **In 2021, ulcerative colitis patients accounted for an estimated 334 of every 100,000 patients
> presenting to GPs in Australia** [S1].

## Part 1 — The drug ladder [AMH]

**Shared rationale: relieve symptoms and improve quality of life, induce and maintain remission, prevent
complications and nutritional deficiencies** [C1].

**Before starting either disease's treatment, consider:** **active or latent infection (hepatitis B, TB,
HIV)** · **immunisation requirements, ESPECIALLY LIVE VACCINES** · **hepatitis B vaccination if at risk** ·
**history of malignant disease** [C1].

### ⚠️ Crohn's disease: the first prescription is stopping smoking

> ⚠️ **ADVISE ALL PATIENTS WITH CROHN'S DISEASE WHO SMOKE TO STOP.** **Smoking is associated with a
> worsening clinical course and INCREASES THE RISK OF REQUIRING SURGERY** [C1].
> **This is not on AMH's ulcerative colitis page.**

| Step [C1] | |
|---|---|
| **Induce, mild localised ileocaecal** | **oral BUDESONIDE — fewer adverse effects than oral prednisolone** |
| **Induce, moderate-to-severe** | **oral prednisolone or IV hydrocortisone**; **response to IV usually within 3–5 days**, then switch to oral and taper over a few weeks |
| **Proctitis** | **rectal corticosteroids** |
| **Maintain** | **azathioprine or mercaptopurine** — ⚠️ **less effective than TNF-alpha antagonists**; **started WITH corticosteroids in acute disease because full effect takes up to 3 MONTHS** |
| **Maintain, thiopurine-refractory or intolerant** | **methotrexate** — usually **SC (preferred) or IM first**, then switched to oral |
| **Moderate-to-severe, inadequate conventional response** | **infliximab, adalimumab** — ⚠️ **infliximab FIRST LINE in acute disease if IV corticosteroid inadequate or prognosis poor (e.g. fistulising)** |
| **Next** | **vedolizumab, ustekinumab, upadacitinib** |

⚠️ **Corticosteroids induce but do not maintain remission — avoid long-term use** [C1].
**TNF-alpha antagonists are often used WITH a thiopurine to reduce treatment failure; if response is lost,
switching to ANOTHER TNF-alpha antagonist is usually tried before another class** [C1].

> ⚠️ **5-AMINOSALICYLATES HAVE LIMITED BENEFIT IN CROHN'S DISEASE AND ARE NOT EFFECTIVE AT MAINTAINING
> MEDICALLY-INDUCED REMISSION** [C1][C3][C4]. **AMH says this three times, on three separate pages.**

**Other:** **metronidazole or ciprofloxacin for complications such as simple perianal fistulae or
infection** · ⚠️ **COLESTYRAMINE reduces diarrhoea from terminal ileal disease or resection by binding bile
salts** · **EXCLUSIVE ENTERAL NUTRITION first line to induce remission in CHILDREN with mild-to-moderate
disease — it benefits growth and lacks adverse effects** · ⚠️ **PROBIOTICS ARE NOT USEFUL for maintaining
remission** [C1].

### Ulcerative colitis: 5-ASA is the mainstay

| Step [C2] | |
|---|---|
| **Mild-to-moderate, induce and maintain** | ⚠️ **5-AMINOSALICYLATES — the mainstay** |
| **Left-sided or extensive** | ⚠️ **RECTAL ENEMA PLUS ORAL mesalazine is MORE EFFECTIVE THAN EITHER ALONE** |
| **Mild-to-moderate active proctitis** | ⚠️ **MESALAZINE SUPPOSITORIES are preferred initial treatment and are MORE EFFECTIVE THAN A RECTAL CORTICOSTEROID**; add oral mesalazine if inadequate |
| **Unresponsive to oral + rectal 5-ASA** | **oral prednisolone or budesonide (Cortiment)** |
| **Acute severe** | **IV corticosteroids**; ⚠️ **rescue therapy (e.g. infliximab) if inadequate response AFTER 3 DAYS** |
| **Maintain / steroid-sparing** | **azathioprine or mercaptopurine** — started in acute disease, full effect takes 3 months |
| **Moderate-to-severe** | **infliximab, adalimumab, rarely golimumab**; then **vedolizumab, tofacitinib, upadacitinib, ustekinumab, ozanimod or etrasimod** |

**Suppositories or enemas may be appropriate for disease confined to the rectum or distal colon** [C2].

> **Vedolizumab appears slower in onset than TNF-alpha antagonists, but early evidence suggests its
> SELECTIVE ACTION ON THE GUT leads to LESS SYSTEMIC IMMUNOSUPPRESSION AND FEWER SERIOUS INFECTIONS** [C2].

⚠️ **For tofacitinib, upadacitinib, ustekinumab, ozanimod and etrasimod, AMH says the same thing about each
one: COMPARATIVE TRIALS ARE NEEDED** [C2]. **Five agents, no head-to-head evidence.**

### ⚠️ Three things to avoid in severe ulcerative colitis [C2]

> ⚠️ **OPIOIDS — INCLUDING ANTIDIARRHOEALS SUCH AS LOPERAMIDE — AND ANTICHOLINERGICS: AVOID IN SEVERE
> DISEASE BECAUSE OF THE RISK OF TOXIC MEGACOLON** [C2].
> **S1 says the same from the surgical side: medications that risk inducing colonic dilatation —
> anticholinergics, antidiarrhoeals and opiates — should be used cautiously** [S1].
>
> ⚠️ **NSAIDs: avoid in severe disease** — associated with disease-related hospitalisation and relapse.
> **DIET: there is NO EVIDENCE that dietary intervention, including bowel rest and parenteral nutrition,
> has any specific therapeutic effect.**

**PROBIOTICS: limited data suggest they are NOT MORE EFFECTIVE THAN PLACEBO for inducing or maintaining
remission in mild-to-moderate ulcerative colitis** [C2].

## Doses [AMH]

⚠️ **All doses in this section are licensed and unquoted.**

### ⚠️ The mesalazine brand trap

> ⚠️ **DIFFERENT BRANDS OF MESALAZINE ARE NOT INTERCHANGEABLE** because of differences in formulation and
> release characteristics [C3]. **The dose table below is why that matters: the regimens are not
> variations on one number — they are different drugs' worth of difference.**

| Ulcerative colitis, adult, ACUTE [C3] | Dose |
|---|---|
| **Asacol tablet** | **2.4 g once daily, or up to 4.8 g daily in divided doses** |
| **Mesasal tablet** | **500 mg three times daily** |
| **Mezavant tablet or generics** | **2.4–4.8 g once daily** |
| **Pentasa tablet/granules** | **up to 4 g daily in 1–4 doses** |
| **Salofalk tablet/granules** | **1.5–3 g daily in 1–3 doses** |

| Ulcerative colitis, adult, MAINTENANCE [C3] | Dose |
|---|---|
| **Asacol** | **1.6–2.4 g once daily or divided** |
| **Mesasal** | **250 mg three times daily** |
| **Mezavant** | **2.4 g once daily** |
| **Pentasa** | **initially 1.5–2 g daily in 2–3 doses, or 2 g once daily** |
| **Salofalk** | **1.5 g daily in 1–3 doses** |

| Rectal [C3] | Dose |
|---|---|
| **Pentasa enema or suppository; Salofalk suppository** | **1 g once daily** |
| **Salofalk enema or rectal foam** | **2–4 g once daily** |

**Crohn's disease, adult** [C3]: **acute** — Mesasal 500 mg tds · Pentasa up to 4 g daily divided ·
Salofalk 3–4.5 g daily in 1–3 doses. **Maintenance** — Mesasal 250 mg tds · Pentasa up to 4 g daily
divided · Salofalk 1.5–3 g daily in 1–3 doses. ⚠️ **Noting AMH's own view that 5-ASA has limited benefit
here.**

⚠️ **Even the food instructions differ by brand** [C3]: **MESASAL at least half an hour before food** ·
**SALOFALK tablets at least one hour before food (granules unaffected)** · **PENTASA unaffected, and
tablets may be dispersed in 50 mL cold water, stirred and taken immediately.** **All swallowed whole, not
chewed or crushed.**

### Sulfasalazine [C4]

| Indication | Dose |
|---|---|
| **Ulcerative colitis, adult, acute** | **2–4 g daily in 3 or 4 doses**; ⚠️ **up to 8 g daily is sometimes used but risk of toxicity is increased** |
| **Ulcerative colitis, adult, maintenance** | **500 mg four times daily** |
| **Colonic Crohn's, mild active, adult** | **3–6 g daily in divided doses** |
| **Ulcerative colitis, child >2 years, acute** | **10–15 mg/kg (max 1 g) 4–6 times daily, max 60 mg/kg daily** |
| **Ulcerative colitis, child >2 years, maintenance** | **5–7.5 mg/kg (max 500 mg) four times daily** |

> ⚠️ **REVERSIBLE MALE INFERTILITY FROM OLIGOSPERMIA IS A COMMON ADVERSE EFFECT OF SULFASALAZINE — not a
> rare one** [C4]. **S2 agrees from the other side: for male fertility, SWITCH TO AN ALTERNATIVE THREE
> MONTHS PRIOR to conception attempts** [S2]. ⚠️ **Two independent sources, the same warning. This belongs
> in the conversation at the time of prescribing, not at the time of trying to conceive.**

⚠️ **Contraindicated in sulfonamide allergy.** **Increased myelosuppression risk in blood dyscrasias;
increased haemolysis risk in G6PD DEFICIENCY; asthma and severe allergy may be exacerbated** [C4].

**Warn the patient:** **urine may become dark orange; skin and tears yellow-orange;** ⚠️ **SOFT CONTACT
LENSES MAY BE PERMANENTLY STAINED** [C4]. **Take with food to reduce stomach upset** [C4].

> **SULFASALAZINE IMPAIRS FOLIC ACID ABSORPTION — consider 5 mg daily, especially before and during
> pregnancy** [C4]. **S2 puts a number on who needs more: 2–5 mg daily in extensive ileal disease, on a
> fibre-restricted diet, and on sulfasalazine, for three months before conception and the first 12 weeks**
> [S2].

### Budesonide [C5]

| Indication | Dose |
|---|---|
| **Crohn's disease** | **Entocort or Budenofalk capsule, >12 years, 9 mg once daily in the morning JUST BEFORE FOOD, for up to 8 weeks, then taper over 2–4 weeks** |
| **Ulcerative colitis** | **Budenofalk rectal foam, >18 years, 1 applicatorful (2 mg) once daily for up to 6–8 weeks** · **Cortiment tablet, >18 years, 9 mg once daily in the morning for up to 8 weeks** |

**Budesonide has a favourable topical-to-systemic ratio (high first-pass metabolism).** ⚠️ **BUT ADRENAL
SUPPRESSION MAY OCCUR EVEN WITH SHORT COURSES OF ORAL THERAPY**, and ⚠️ **switching from a corticosteroid
with significant systemic effects to budesonide requires CAUTIOUS TAILORING OF DOSE TO AVOID ADRENAL
CRISIS** [C5].

> ⚠️ **CORTIMENT — the 9 mg tablet for ulcerative colitis — is listed by AMH with NO PBS listing**, while
> the Crohn's capsules and the rectal foam are PBS-listed [C5]. **The ulcerative colitis oral option is the
> one the patient pays for.**

### Monitoring [C3][C4]

| | Schedule |
|---|---|
| **Any 5-aminosalicylate** | **renal and liver function at baseline; every 3 months in the first year; then every 6 months** |
| ⚠️ **Sulfasalazine — more intensive** | **FBC plus liver and renal function at baseline; EVERY 2–4 WEEKS FOR THE FIRST 3 MONTHS; then every 3 months in the first year; then every 6 months; AND 1 MONTH AFTER ANY DOSE CHANGE** |

**5-ASA adverse effects are MORE COMMON WITH HIGHER DOSES: nausea, rash, headache, diarrhoea; infrequent
INTERSTITIAL NEPHRITIS; rare blood dyscrasias, reversible pancreatitis, hepatitis** [C3].
**Contraindicated in salicylate allergy; caution in renal impairment, monitor especially if CrCl <20
mL/minute** [C3].

# Part 2 — Acute severe ulcerative colitis [S1]

> ⚠️ **ASUC IS SEEN IN UP TO 25% OF PATIENTS WITH ULCERATIVE COLITIS** [S1]. **One in four.**

## The criteria [S1]

> **Truelove and Witts — the most sensitive and prevalently used from a surgical perspective.**
> **BLOODY STOOLS >6 PER DAY, PLUS ONE OF:**

| Sign | Threshold [S1] |
|---|---|
| **Tachycardia** | **heart rate >90 beats/min** |
| **Pyrexia** | **core temperature >37.8 °C** |
| **Raised inflammatory markers** | **ESR >30 mm/h** or **CRP >30 mg/L** |
| **Low haemoglobin** | **Hb <10.5 g/dL** |

**Australian guidelines, based on the Montreal classification, stratify risk into mild (≤4 stools without
blood), moderate (>4 with or without blood) and severe (≥6 stools, with blood and signs of toxicity)** [S1].

⚠️ **Whichever criteria are used, THE CLASSIFICATION OF SEVERITY REQUIRES PERIODIC RE-EVALUATION** [S1].

## What a GP can start [S1]

- ⚠️ **STOOL TESTING TO EXCLUDE AN INFECTIVE CAUSE IS INDICATED** [S1].
- **Faecal calprotectin can assess severity — a level >50 mcg/g might represent active inflammation** [S1].
- ⚠️ **Screen for opportunistic infections — HIV, hepatitis B and C, varicella zoster — WHICH WOULD
  OTHERWISE BE MASKED, in preparation for rescue pharmacotherapy (infliximab or cyclosporin)** [S1].
- **If prior TB exposure is likely, an interferon-gamma release assay is recommended** [S1].
- **Serum magnesium and lipid profiles may be arranged in preparation for cyclosporin** [S1].
- **Abdominal radiography is used to identify TOXIC MEGACOLON** [S1].

## In hospital [S1]

> **Initial management is IV glucocorticoids with potential escalation to a biologic, usually infliximab,
> AIMING TO REDUCE COLECTOMY RATES BY UP TO 80%** [S1].
> **Rescue therapy is typically second line WHEN IV STEROID THERAPY FAILS OVER THREE TO FIVE DAYS** [S1]
> — ⚠️ **AMH says rescue therapy is considered after an INADEQUATE RESPONSE AT 3 DAYS** [C2]. **The two are
> consistent; AMH sets the earlier end of S1's window.**

**Sigmoidoscopy should be performed at the earliest opportunity — studies show improved outcomes with
endoscopy WITHIN 72 HOURS of hospitalisation** [S1].

**ANTIBIOTICS: a recent randomised trial concluded they DID NOT IMPROVE OUTCOMES outside a clinical context
of severe sepsis** [S1].

> ⚠️ **THROMBOEMBOLISM: patients with ASUC have an UP TO EIGHT-FOLD INCREASED RISK.** **Compression
> stockings and chemical thromboprophylaxis with low-molecular-weight or unfractionated heparin are
> usually prescribed** [S1].
> **Increased POST-DISCHARGE VTE risk: age 45 or older, hospital stay of seven days or longer, and previous
> C. difficile infection on initial admission** [S1] — **three flags a GP can check on the discharge
> summary.**

## ⚠️ When surgery cannot wait [S1]

> **Immediate surgical intervention is indicated for: TOXIC MEGACOLON · HAEMATOCHEZIA WITH HAEMODYNAMIC
> INSTABILITY · PERFORATION** [S1].

**The Travis criteria predict surgical need: MORE THAN EIGHT DAILY STOOLS, or THREE TO EIGHT STOOLS WITH A
CRP ABOVE 45 mg/L, AFTER THREE DAYS ON STEROIDS** [S1].

**Elective surgery indications: INABILITY TO WEAN OFF STEROIDS, or DYSPLASIA** [S1].

**Urgent and emergency patients typically undergo total abdominal colectomy and end ileostomy; elective
patients are usually offered restorative techniques, most commonly ileal pouch anal anastomosis** [S1].

## After surgery — what walks back into the GP's room [S1]

| Complication | Rate [S1] |
|---|---|
| **Anastomotic stricture** — identifiable **on per rectal examination** | **4–16%** |
| **Bowel obstruction** — prioritise cross-sectional imaging, refer promptly | **2–4%** |
| **Pelvic sepsis and pouch failure** | **8.5–9.5%** |
| **Faecal and overnight incontinence after pouch surgery** | ⚠️ **53–76%**, with **an average of six daily stools or an overnight stool** |
| **Impotence (male)** | **1.5%** |
| **Retrograde ejaculation (male)** | **4%** |
| **Dyspareunia (female, temporary)** | **7%** — **frequency of intercourse and capacity to achieve orgasm usually unchanged** |
| **Have given birth, among those who had surgery and hoped to conceive** | **approximately 88%** |

> ⚠️ **IMMEDIATE emergency department referral for infective symptoms, faecal frequency of MORE THAN FOUR
> TO SIX BOWEL MOTIONS A DAY, loose stools, or symptoms of obstruction** [S1].

# Part 3 — Pregnancy [S2]

⚠️ **AMH's whole contribution here is: "seek specialist advice", maintain disease control, corticosteroids
are suitable for active disease, continue maintenance medications, and stop methotrexate at least 3 months
before conception** [C1][C2]. **Everything else in this Part is from S2, an open source that is four years
old — stated so it can be weighed.**

## The number that drives everything [S2]

> **Of those in clinical remission, approximately ONE-THIRD will flare in pregnancy — compared with 55% OF
> THOSE WITH ACTIVE DISEASE AT CONCEPTION** [S2]. **Flares occur in between 20% and 55% of patients
> antenatally** [S2].
>
> ⚠️ **Compared with remission, ACTIVE IBD in pregnancy carries a GREATER THAN THREE-FOLD INCREASED RISK
> of spontaneous abortion, low birth weight and preterm delivery, and an increased risk of stillbirth**
> [S2].
>
> ⚠️ **THEREFORE: THREE MONTHS OF CORTICOSTEROID-FREE remission — clinical and biochemical, with
> cross-sectional imaging or endoscopy — IS RECOMMENDED BEFORE ATTEMPTING CONCEPTION** [S2].

## Preconception [S2]

| | [S2] |
|---|---|
| **Folate** | **2–5 mg daily** in **extensive ileal disease**, on a **fibre-restricted diet**, and on **SULFASALAZINE** — **for 3 months before conception and the first 12 weeks** |
| **Weight** | **BMI >30: a 5–10% body weight reduction before conception** decreases the risk of adverse obstetric outcomes such as stillbirth |
| **Contraception** | ⚠️ **prefer LONG-ACTING, NON-OESTROGEN containing methods in ACTIVE IBD** (theoretical VTE risk) · **oral contraceptive absorption is MAINTAINED in mild UC or short ileal resections** · ⚠️ **non-oral methods after extensive small bowel resection or with active small bowel disease** |
| **Vaccination** | ⚠️ **LIVE (MMR) vaccination in patients on thiopurines, biologics or long-term corticosteroids must be tailored to individual risk WITH INFECTIOUS DISEASE SPECIALIST INPUT** |
| **Fertility** | **not adversely affected with WELL-CONTROLLED IBD**; may be decreased with **severely active or complex penetrating disease, extensive prior resections, or low BMI**. **Refer for fertility review within 6–12 months of unsuccessful attempts**, particularly after prior surgery |

### Hereditability — the reassurance patients ask for [S2]

> **The risk of IBD in a child born to a mother with IBD is approximately 1.6–4.1% for ulcerative colitis
> and 2.7–4.8% for Crohn's disease**, varying by ethnicity. ⚠️ **But as high as 30% WHERE BOTH PARENTS ARE
> AFFECTED** [S2].

## ⚠️ Medications across the perinatal period [S2]

| Drug | Before conception | Pregnancy | Breastfeeding |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Aminosalicylates** | ⚠️ **MALE: switch to an alternative 3 MONTHS BEFORE conception attempts** | **continue — with 5 mg daily folic acid if on sulfasalazine** | **continue —** ⚠️ **AVOID SULFASALAZINE** |
| **Thiopurines** | **continue** — ⚠️ **check 6-thioguanine and 6-methylmercaptopurine before conception and monitor through pregnancy (risk of INTRAHEPATIC CHOLESTASIS OF PREGNANCY)** | **continue** | **continue** |
| ⚠️ **Methotrexate** | **CEASE AT LEAST 3 MONTHS before conception** | ⚠️ **CONTRAINDICATED** | ⚠️ **CONTRAINDICATED** |
| ⚠️ **Tofacitinib** | **CEASE AT LEAST 4 WEEKS before trying to conceive** | ⚠️ **CONTRAINDICATED** | ⚠️ **CONTRAINDICATED** |
| **Biologics (anti-TNF, ustekinumab, vedolizumab)** | **continue** | ⚠️ **CONTINUE THROUGHOUT CONCEPTION AND PREGNANCY** | **continue** |
| **Corticosteroids** | **aim for 3 months corticosteroid-free remission** | ⚠️ **avoid prolonged (>6 week) courses or use as maintenance** | **continue at doses <40 mg prednisolone daily** |

⚠️ **AMH and S2 agree on methotrexate: at least 3 months before conception** [C1][S2]. **Two independent
sources, the same interval.**

**For a moderate-to-severe flare in pregnancy, or inadequate response to aminosalicylates:** **commence
PREDNISOLONE 40 mg DAILY, WEANING BY 5 mg WEEKLY, with early gastroenterology assessment** [S2].
**Budesonide controlled release may be trialled** — ⚠️ **it has been used in small bowel Crohn's in
pregnancy with no consequent adverse events, BUT OVERALL SAFETY DATA ARE EXTREMELY LIMITED** [S2].

## Antenatal monitoring [S2]

> **Assessment in EACH TRIMESTER, INCLUDING IN THOSE IN REMISSION** [S2].

⚠️ **ROUTINE INFLAMMATORY MARKERS ARE AFFECTED BY PREGNANCY** and must be interpreted against pregnancy
reference ranges [S2].

> ⚠️ **FAECAL CALPROTECTIN REMAINS ACCURATE IN PREGNANCY** [S2] — and can evaluate symptoms possibly
> attributable to new-onset IBD, such as rectal bleeding and diarrhoea.

| Faecal calprotectin in pregnancy [S2] | Action |
|---|---|
| **≥50 μg/g** | **expedient gastroenterological referral for consideration of endoscopy or imaging** |
| ⚠️ **>250 μg/g** | **associated with increased risks of adverse obstetric outcomes IRRESPECTIVE OF CLINICAL SYMPTOMS**; **consider therapeutic escalation and early gastroenterological review** |
| **Frequency** | **at least once in each trimester, including in clinical remission** |

⚠️ **Note that S1 puts the general active-inflammation threshold at >50 mcg/g [S1] and S2 uses ≥50 μg/g as
a referral trigger in pregnancy [S2] — the same number, used for different decisions.**
