# Paediatric sepsis — the Queensland guideline

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-22 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** children presenting to a Queensland emergency department with suspected or confirmed sepsis.

> **Australian primary source.** Children's Health Queensland. ***Sepsis — Recognition and emergency
> management in children***, Queensland Paediatric Clinical Guidelines. **Last updated May 2026** [S1].

**The second of the three state pathways** the RCH guideline says **must be followed** — and **four months
old**, against RCH's March 2020.

## ⚠️ Three documents, three answers, on two questions that matter

**This compendium now holds three current Australian paediatric sepsis documents.** Two of their disagreements
are not stylistic.

### Blood culture volume

| | |
|---|---|
| **RCH** | **weight-banded, seven rows**, 1 mL (<1.5 kg) to 10 mL (>25 kg); **adult anaerobic bottle specified from 5 kg up** |
| **NSW CEC** | **3 mL in paediatric or 10 mL in adult bottle** — one figure |
| ⚠️ **Queensland** | **"Aiming 2–6 mL, ideally 4 mL; for neonates, 1 mL is the minimum."** **"Collection of anaerobic blood culture is not needed."** [S1] |

**Queensland says don't take the anaerobic bottle. RCH specifies its volume.** That is a direct
contradiction, not a difference of emphasis — and **culture sensitivity increases with blood volume** [S1] is
the shared premise underneath all three.

### IM ceftriaxone when there is no access

| | Trigger | Dose |
|---|---|---|
| **RCH** | **"If IV access unavailable"** | **100 mg/kg (max 4 g) daily** |
| **NSW CEC** | ⚠️ **no access within 15 minutes** | *not stated* |
| ⚠️ **Queensland** | ⚠️ **no IV or IO access within 60 minutes** | **Ceftriaxone 50 mg/kg IM (maximum 2 grams)** [S1] |

**A twofold dose difference and a fourfold difference in when to give it.** And only Queensland states the
caveat:

> **IM dosing of antibiotics often fails to deliver adequate coverage due to poor perfusion and so should only
> be used when other methods have failed** [S1].

**Neither document is wrong for its own system.** RCH says the state pathway governs; in Queensland these are
the numbers.

## ⚠️ The intubation warning, with the drugs named

The RCH sepsis flowchart carries one line — *there is a high risk of cardiac arrest associated with use of
induction agents in children with septic shock* — and nothing else. **Queensland says what to do about it** [S1]:

> ⚠️ **Child may arrest from cardiovascular collapse on RSI/intubation.**
>
> **Avoid drugs with negative inotropy (such as Midazolam or Propofol) and have arrest dose of Adrenaline IV
> ready. Avoid pure alpha agonists such as metaraminol as myocardial depression can be significant.**
>
> - **Adequately pre-oxygenate child and have haemodynamics optimised with concomitant fluid resuscitation and
>   Adrenaline infusion prior to intubation**
> - ⚠️ **Reduce the induction drug dose in a child with significant cardiovascular compromise (i.e. 50% of
>   weight-based dose)**
> - **Ketamine (0.5–1 mg/kg) and/or Fentanyl (1–2 microgram/kg) for induction (less cardiodepressant) and
>   Rocuronium (1.2 mg/kg) for muscle relaxation are generally a suitable combination for rapid sequence
>   induction**

**Half-dose induction, named agents to avoid, adrenaline drawn up before you start.** This is the single
largest expansion any state document has made on the RCH text in this compendium.

## ⚠️ A third position on lumbar puncture

> **Not indicated in the initial work up and contraindicated in established sepsis until haemodynamically
> stable. Once stable, only perform in alert child with no signs of raised ICP, coagulopathy or haemodynamic
> compromise.** **Can do WCC and PCR for meningitis diagnosis on CSF from delayed LP** [S1].

| Document | Position |
|---|---|
| **Febrile child (RCH)** | **without delay and ideally before antibiotics** |
| **Sepsis (RCH)** | **should be considered once the child has been stabilised** |
| ⚠️ **Sepsis (Queensland)** | **not indicated initially and contraindicated until haemodynamically stable** |

**All three are defensible for their patient** — the well febrile child, the stabilising child, the shocked
child. **The point is that "do an LP" means three different things depending on which document is open**, and
the delayed-LP note that **WCC and PCR still work on later CSF** is the practical resolution none of the
others offer.

## Recognition

**Key points** [S1]:

> **Sepsis is a medical emergency: early recognition and treatment is imperative for survival** ·
> **Consider sepsis in every child with acute illness or new onset of organ dysfunction** ·
> **Diagnosis is based on clinical judgement supported by laboratory findings** ·
> **Initial treatment bundle… ideally within 60 minutes of presentation** ·
> **Early escalation to paediatric critical care (onsite or via Retrieval Services Queensland) is essential**

⚠️ **The mortality rate for untreated septic shock is more than 80% and even with treatment is estimated at
15–20% in children** [S1]. **Failure to recognise sepsis and the delay in appropriate treatment are common
themes in reviews of sepsis related mortality in children** [S1].

**High-risk groups** [S1]: **neonates and premature infants** · **children of Aboriginal / Torres Strait
Islander / Pacific Islander / Māori origin** · **unimmunised or incomplete immunisation status** · **asplenia
(surgical or functional e.g. sickle cell disease)** · **long-term steroid use** · **recent surgical procedure
(within 6 weeks)** · **intravenous recreational drug use** · **indwelling lines or catheters (e.g. VP shunt or
CVAD)** · **recent inpatient episode of sepsis (within 6–12 weeks)**.

⚠️ **Fourth document in this compendium** to list Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander origin as a sepsis or
fever risk factor without a stated basis — see *Unresolved* and Finding 4 of `docs/unresolved-audit.md`.

**Features of severe illness** [S1]: **altered mental state · severe tachypnoea, increased work of breathing,
grunt, weak cry or apnoea · marked and persistent tachycardia (including high normal in the absence of drivers
i.e. fever, pain) · hypotension · poor skin perfusion · indicators of moderate to severe dehydration**.

> **It is important to pay attention to concerns expressed by the caregiver, particularly changes in usual
> behaviour of the child** [S1].

**Fourth Australian paediatric document to give the caregiver's account formal standing.**

### ⚠️ Use the system score, not the total

> **The Paediatric sepsis pathway features of severe illness refer to the CEWT scores for the system examined
> e.g. the respiratory rate or heart rate; NOT the overall CEWT score which can be misleading** [S1].

**An early warning score that warns against its own aggregate.** A child can be very abnormal in one system
and unremarkable overall.

**Normal vital signs by age, Childhood Early Warning Tool** [S1]:

| Age | Heart rate (bpm) | Minimum systolic BP (mmHg) | Respiratory rate (bpm) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **<1 year** | **100–159** | **>75** | **21–45** |
| **1–4 years** | **90–139** | **>80** | **16–35** |
| **5–11 years** | **80–129** | **>85** | **16–30** |
| **12–17 years** | **60–119** | **>90** | **16–25** |

**Laboratory features of severe illness**: **lactate >2 concerning, >4** · **glucose <3 (note can be high with
stress)** · **low platelets** · **elevated creatinine** · **elevated INR** · **elevated bilirubin or
ALT** [S1].

## Septic shock

> **Children with septic shock may have normal blood pressure.** ⚠️ **The way in which the child presents does
> not change management nor determine type of inotropic support** [S1].

**That sentence retires a long-taught distinction.** Cold shock and warm shock are described in full — and
then explicitly declared **not** to alter what you give.

| **Shock with vasoconstriction** [S1] | **Shock with vasodilation** [S1] |
|---|---|
| **common in infants and young children** | **more common in older children (and adults)** |
| **constricted peripheral systemic vasculature: cold peripheries and prolonged capillary refill time** | **vasoplegia… low systemic vascular resistance with low diastolic blood pressure: brisk capillary refill time ('flash' capillary refill) and pulses… full or bounding** |
| **tachycardia is usually present; relative bradycardia is a pre-terminal sign** | same |

⚠️ > **Hypotension is a late, and often terminal, sign in paediatric septic shock** [S1].

## Investigations

> ⚠️ **In septic shock, do not delay antibiotic administration for specimen collection or testing beyond ONE
> hour** [S1].

**Blood culture: prioritise over other blood tests. Ideally collected prior to antibiotics but do not delay
treatment for collection.** **If the child has a CVAD, blood cultures should be taken from each lumen as per
protocol** [S1].

> ⚠️ **Do NOT attribute increased lactate to difficult venepuncture** [S1].

**High lactate >4 has been associated with increased mortality, PICU admission and hospital length of stay; >2
is potentially concerning but >4 requires urgent action** [S1].

## Management — the first 60 minutes

> **In septic shock, antimicrobials should be administered as soon as possible within 1 hour of recognition…
> In sepsis without shock, antimicrobials should still be administered as soon as possible, suggested within
> up to 3 hours of recognition** [S1].

**Fluid** [S1]:

> - **Provide immediate fluid resuscitation starting with 10–20 mL/kg of balanced solution (like Plasmalyte)
>   or sodium chloride 0.9%** — ⚠️ **to be pushed in less than five minutes using 50 mL syringe with a staff
>   member dedicated to pushing fluids**
> - **Titrate to response: decrease in heart rate and the improvement of end-organ perfusion. Repeat as
>   necessary, evaluating for signs of fluid overload**
> - ⚠️ **Hypotonic fluids should never be used as bolus therapy**
> - **Commence inotropic support of Adrenaline (as first line) if normal physiological parameters are not
>   restored after giving more than 40–60 mL/kg of fluids, or anytime if hypotension is present**

**"A staff member dedicated to pushing fluids" is a staffing instruction inside a drug order.** It is the only
place in this compendium where a guideline allocates a person to a task.

**Inotropes** [S1]:

> - **First-line: Adrenaline starting at 0.1 microgram/kg/min** — ⚠️ **dosing >0.5 microgram/kg/min is
>   concerning and should be done only in conjunction with PICU/RSQ** — **can be administered temporarily via
>   a peripheral IV or IO line before central access is gained**
> - **Second-line: Noradrenaline starting at 0.1 microgram/kg/min**
> - **Push dose pressors: Adrenaline 1 microgram/kg (i.e. 0.1 mL/kg of a 1:100,000 Adrenaline solution) if
>   infusion is being prepared and the patient remains in shock**

**Also**: **consider umbilical line access in newborns up to 2 weeks of life** [S1]; **for suspected adrenal
insufficiency, collect blood for cortisol and ACTH then administer Hydrocortisone IV (1 mg/kg)** [S1];
**consider alternative diagnoses, especially neonates who may have a metabolic or cardiogenic cause** [S1];
and **electrolyte disturbance (e.g. hypocalcaemia) is common… and can contribute to poor cardiac
function** [S1].

## Escalation

**Escalate to paediatric critical care if the patient still has**: **hypotension (BP CEWT score ≥2)** ·
**reduced level of consciousness despite resuscitation** · **lactate ≥2 mmol/L or not reducing** ·
**persistent tachycardia (HR CEWT score ≥2)** [S1]. **If no onsite paediatric critical care service, contact
RSQ on 1300 799 127** [S1].
