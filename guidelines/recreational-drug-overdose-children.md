## Scope

- **For 24-hour advice, call the Victorian Poisons Information Centre on 13 11 26.** [S1]
- **Most adolescent recreational-drug presentations involve alcohol, often with other agents.** The page covers the acute issues only. [S1]

## Amphetamines (ice, speed, ecstasy, MDMA)

- ⚠️ **Amphetamines: a small dose may cause significant toxicity.** Sympathomimetic effects — agitation, sweating, tachycardia, hypertension. ⚠️ **Complications:** severe hyperthermia, rhabdomyolysis, seizures, intracranial haemorrhage, hyponatraemia, cerebral oedema. [S1]
- **Investigate:** 12-lead ECG, BSL, electrolytes and renal function, CK, troponin; **consider intracranial imaging** if vascular dissection is a concern. **Manage:** safe, low-stimulus environment · **continuous cardiac monitoring** · **benzodiazepines to control agitation.** [S1]

## Cannabis

- **Cannabis effects are dose related:** mild sedation, euphoria and disinhibition, progressing to CNS depression, anxiety and psychotic symptoms, postural hypotension and tachycardia. [S1]
- **Benign symptoms: supportive care until asymptomatic. Anxiety: diazepam. Cardiovascular effects: IV fluids.** [S1]

## GHB

- ⚠️ **GHB causes CNS and respiratory depression progressing to coma**, with bradycardia and myoclonic jerks. [S1]
- **Manage GHB in a resuscitation environment; expect improvement within 4–6 hours.** [S1]

## Hallucinogens

- **Hallucinogens (Datura / angel's trumpet, magic mushrooms, LSD)** listed with an **anticholinergic toxidrome**: agitation, delirium, hallucinations, mydriasis, tachycardia, dry flushed skin. [S1]
- **Supportive care; treat delirium with titrated diazepam.** ⚠️ **Antipsychotics such as haloperidol may worsen anticholinergic symptoms.** [S1]

## Inhalants (huffing, sniffing, chroming)

- **Inhalants act like alcohol** — euphoria and disinhibition, then CNS depression. ⚠️ **Complications:** sustained 'high', encephalopathy and seizures, methaemoglobinaemia, **cardiac arrhythmia and sudden death.** [S1]
- **Treat behavioural disturbance or delirium with diazepam.** ⚠️ **In cardiac arrest, limit exogenous adrenaline.** [S1]

## Opioids

- ⚠️ **Opioids: CNS and respiratory depression with miosis.** Dextropropoxyphene — seizures and arrhythmias; **tramadol — seizures and serotonin syndrome.** [S1]
- **Monitor for CNS and respiratory depression; airway protection and ventilation ensure a good outcome.** **Naloxone infusion** — note its short half-life. **Seizures: benzodiazepines and phenobarbitone.** [S1]

## Escalate

- **Emergency advice and paediatric ICU transfer: PIPER, 1300 137 650.** [S1]
