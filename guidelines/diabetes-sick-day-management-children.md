# Unwell children with established diabetes at home (sick-day and phone advice)

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** phone and home advice for children with established type 1 diabetes who are unwell: BGL and ketone checks, hypo- and hyperglycaemia, pump failure, glucagon, and when to send to ED. Related: `diabetes-new-presentation-children`, `diabetic-ketoacidosis-children`, `hypoglycaemia-children`. No adult sick-day counterpart in this corpus (`type-2-diabetes` covers adult type 2).

> ⚠️ **Never advise omitting basal insulin — even when unwell, fasting or hypoglycaemic. Always check ketones.** Ketones over 3 mmol/L at any time: go to ED.

## Key rules

- ⚠️ **Always request a ketone check in an unwell child with diabetes** — ketones can be raised even with normal glucose. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Never advise omission of basal/background insulin**, even when unwell, fasting or hypoglycaemic. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Treat nausea/vomiting as insulin deficiency until proven otherwise.** Ketones over 3, especially with vomiting, mean high DKA risk and likely admission. [S1]
- **Interpreting results (T1DM):** hypoglycaemia 3.9 mmol/L or below · normal pre-meal 4–7 · normal post-meal 4–10 with ketones under 0.6 · high over 11 with ketones 0.6 or more · very high over 15 (check ketones), with ketones over 1 needing insulin. [S1]

## Home management

- **Increase monitoring: finger-prick BGL at least 2–4-hourly including overnight (1–2-hourly if more unwell).** Confirm CGM readings by finger prick, especially under 4 or over 10 mmol/L. [S1]
- **Fluids: BGL under 14 — sugar-containing fluids** (diluted juice, electrolyte drinks); **BGL 14 or more — sugar-free fluids.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not advise omitting insulin even with hypoglycaemia.** Hypoglycaemia with ketones over 0.6 mmol/L means insulin **and** carbohydrate deficiency and needs urgent medical attention. [S1]
- **Pump users may need a temporary basal rate of 70% of normal** for hypoglycaemia while unwell (follow the child's plan first). Reduce the next insulin dose by 10% if BGL is under 4 more than once. [S1]
- **Hyperglycaemia with ketones under 0.6:** usual plan, with a correction at the next rapid-acting dose. **Ketones 0.6 or more = insulin deficiency:** give an additional insulin bolus. [S1]
- **Sick-day bolus = extra rapid-acting insulin on top of usual doses, tabulated as 10% or 20% of the total daily dose (TDD).** TDD is all rapid plus long-acting insulin in a day; if unknown, **estimate 1 unit/kg** from recent weight. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Hypoglycaemia with ketones 0.6 or more needs hospital** for fluid and insulin. [S1]

## Insulin pumps

- ⚠️ **Pumps deliver only rapid-acting insulin, so failure or disconnection carries a high DKA risk.** If BGL stays high or ketones are 0.6 or more: check the line, change catheter, tubing and reservoir; **BGL over 15 = treat as pump failure** and give supplemental insulin by subcutaneous injection. [S1]
- **Ketones may rise for 1–2 hours after a bolus** before falling if treated correctly. [S1]
- **Faulty pump:** disconnect; if BGL and ketones are normal, give **1/6 of TDD as rapid-acting insulin by injection every 4 hours** until diabetes-team review. [S1]

## Glucagon

- ⚠️ **Severe hypoglycaemia (confused, unconscious or seizing): IM glucagon — under 25 kg 0.5 mg (half vial, 0.5 mL); over 25 kg 1 mg (full vial, 1 mL).** Seizure: call 000. [S1]
- **Minidose SC glucagon** for BGL under 4 mmol/L when oral treatment fails or is not tolerated **but there is no CNS impairment.** Reconstitute 1 mg in 1 mL; 1 insulin-syringe unit = 10 microg. [S1]
- **Minidose initial dose:** under 2 years 20 microg (2-unit mark) · 2–15 years 10 microg per year of age (1 unit per year) · over 15 years 150 microg (15-unit mark). [S1]
- **Check BGL at 30 minutes.** 5.5 mmol/L or more: hourly BGL and cut the next insulin by 10%. Under 5.5: **second and then third minidose at double the initial dose**; still under 5.5 after the third, go to hospital. Discard reconstituted glucagon after 24 hours. [S1]

## Send to ED

- ⚠️ **Advise ED if:** BGL cannot be kept above 4 mmol/L · BGL under 5.5 despite 3 glucagon minidoses · ketones over 0.6 with vomiting or not tolerating oral · **ketones over 3 at any time** · cannot maintain hydration · confused, lethargic or severe abdominal pain · deteriorating or unclear condition. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Urgent DKA review:** ketones over 3 (especially with vomiting or abdominal pain), rapid breathing, drowsiness, or ketones rising (especially over 1.5) despite extra boluses. [S1]
- **Run out of insulin: never advise skipping a dose** — arrange a script. **Too much given:** extra carbohydrate and recheck BGL; **too little:** check BGL and ketones. [S1]
