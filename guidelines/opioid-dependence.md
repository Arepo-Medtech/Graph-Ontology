## ⚠️ The drug is not the main danger

AMH is unusually direct about where the harm actually comes from:

> **Providing the dose is not excessive (resulting in overdose), opioids are not particularly toxic** [A1].

The problems it lists are **contextual**, not pharmacological [A1]:

- **Opioids are often used with other substances that may cause toxic effects** — **alcohol, benzodiazepines or pregabalin**, and **aspirin, ibuprofen or paracetamol from opioid-containing combination analgesics**
- **Criminal or high-risk activity may be necessary to finance the habit**
- **It may be impossible to maintain usual activities — employment, education, social interaction**
- ⚠️ **The identity and strength of illicit drugs may be unknown; they are often diluted with chemicals (sometimes toxic) of similar appearance or taste**
- **IV use may cause phlebitis and collapsed veins**
- **Sharing needles or equipment increases risk of hepatitis B and C and HIV**
- **Non-sterile equipment and drug increases risk of abscess, septicaemia, endocarditis, chest infections**

**Long-term effects include irregular menstruation, impotence and mood changes** [A1].

**Tolerance to euphoric effects develops rapidly, causing users to repeatedly increase dosage and to use more
dangerous routes such as injecting or smoking** [A1].

## Withdrawal — unpleasant, not dangerous

> ⚠️ **Opioid withdrawal reactions, although unpleasant, are not life-threatening** [A1].

**Symptoms**: **dilated pupils, goose pimples, diarrhoea, cramps, muscle aches, extreme anxiety,
restlessness, insomnia, nausea or vomiting, sweating, tearing, tachycardia, increased BP** [A1].

**Timing follows the drug's duration of action.** For short-acting opioids such as heroin, symptoms **begin
within a few hours of the last dose, are most severe after 2–4 days, then gradually subside after a week of
abstinence** [A1]. ⚠️ **A milder, protracted withdrawal syndrome — craving, sleep disturbance — may last for
several months** [A1].

## Before starting

**Consider conditions that complicate treatment**: **pregnancy**, **misuse of other substances (alcohol,
benzodiazepines, gabapentin, pregabalin)**, **chronic pain syndromes**, **personality disorder**,
**schizophrenia**, **infections especially hepatitis B and C and HIV**, **hepatic or renal disease** [A1].

⚠️ **Psychosocial support is needed to aid motivation for lifestyle readjustment and to develop behavioural
skills to enable continued abstinence** [A1] — the drug is presented as the enabler of that support, not a
substitute for it.

## Maintenance is the preferred strategy

> **Opioid maintenance reduces fatalities and infections, and improves health and social functioning. It is
> more effective than interventions to achieve opioid abstinence, and is especially useful during pregnancy,
> for long-term users, and for those with HIV or hepatitis** [A1].

**Choice is determined with the patient**, and **after a period of time — ideally several years — the patient
may decide to stop** [A1].

| | Methadone [A1] | Buprenorphine [A1] |
|---|---|---|
| **Type** | full opioid agonist | partial opioid agonist |
| **Evidence** | **more evidence for efficacy than buprenorphine** | — |
| **Preference** | **preferred by some patients, especially those needing a greater level of opioid effects** | — |
| ⚠️ **Safety** | **more sedating**, and **overdose is more likely, especially in the first few weeks of treatment** | **appears safer, overdose is less likely** |
| **Titration** | — | **dosage is easier to titrate**, **fewer drug interactions** |
| ⚠️ **Drawbacks** | — | **sublingual buprenorphine is associated with precipitated withdrawal (in the first 2–5 days) and lower rates of retention in treatment** |

**Long-acting SC injections (weekly or monthly) may be more convenient for some patients.** ⚠️ **However,
after stopping injections patients continue to be at risk of buprenorphine's adverse effects and drug
interactions, potentially for months after treatment** [A1].

## Dosing

> ⚠️ **Paraphrased from AMH monographs, no stored quote, flagged `dose: true`. Opioid dependence treatment is
> regulated and prescriber-authorised in every Australian jurisdiction — check against AMH, the national
> guidelines and your state or territory requirements before prescribing.**

| Agent | Dose [A1] |
|---|---|
| **Methadone** | oral, **initially 20–30 mg daily**, then **increase by small increments according to local protocols**, to a **maintenance dose around 60–80 mg daily in most patients** |
| **Buprenorphine, maintenance** | **2–8 mg on the first day** in 1 or 2 doses depending on withdrawal severity; **increase by 2–8 mg every 1–3 days**; **usual maintenance 12–24 mg daily (up to 32 mg daily)** |
| **Buprenorphine, alternate-day** | once stabilised, **up to double the usual daily dose but no more than 32 mg** on alternate days |
| **Buprenorphine, withdrawal** | tailored to withdrawal severity, then **gradually reduced, eg over 5–8 days, to zero** |
| **Buprenorphine with naloxone** | sublingual, **same schedule as buprenorphine alone** (doses expressed as buprenorphine) |
| **Buprenorphine SC (Buvidal®)** | **once a week or once a month, adjusted according to response** — ⚠️ **see product information for doses**; **buprenorphine-naive patients start weekly, then change to monthly once stabilised** |
| **Naltrexone, opioid abstinence** | oral, **initially 25 mg daily**; **if no withdrawal symptoms occur, 50 mg once daily** |

⚠️ **Naltrexone three-times-weekly dosing** — **eg 100 mg Monday, 100 mg Wednesday, 150 mg Friday** — is
offered **if daily dosing is difficult**, but the source flags that **this may increase risk** [A1]; the
specific risk was not fully retrieved and is recorded below.

## Managed withdrawal

**Long-term abstinence is not easily maintained; those with a short duration of drug use and who are well
motivated to stop are most likely to succeed** [A1].

> ⚠️ **Withdrawal during pregnancy must be avoided — risk of fetal stress, inducing labour or
> miscarriage** [A1].

**Opioid agonist, short course**: **buprenorphine is titrated to requirements then gradually reduced to
zero**, and **provides better control of opioid withdrawal symptoms than using clonidine after abrupt
withdrawal** [A1]. **Methadone is rarely used for this — seek specialist advice** [A1].

**Abrupt withdrawal** may be done at home, **but often more support can be provided in the inpatient
setting** [A1]. ⚠️ **Consider specialist assessment if an unstable medical or psychiatric condition is
present**, and **contact a specialist agency if a supportive home environment is lacking or the patient is
dependent on multiple drugs** [A1].

**Symptomatic agents** — ⚠️ **prescribe limited quantities and review frequently, with a responsible adult to
supervise medication use** [A1]:

| Symptom | Agent [A1] |
|---|---|
| Pain | **paracetamol or an NSAID if needed** |
| Nausea | **antiemetics, eg metoclopramide** |
| Diarrhoea | **antidiarrhoeals, eg loperamide** |
| **Autonomic hyperactivity** | ⚠️ **clonidine** — **effective for anxiety, sweating, tachycardia and hypertension, but does not affect craving or aches and pains**. **Because of its effects on BP it is generally used in an inpatient setting. Monitor BP and taper slowly over 3 days to prevent hypertensive rebound** |
| Anxiety and agitation | ⚠️ **diazepam** — **higher doses (eg 5–10 mg 4 times daily, seek specialist advice) may be needed due to cross-tolerance of sedative effects**. **Limit treatment to a few days only to avoid secondary benzodiazepine dependence** |

## ⚠️ Relapse and the overdose risk it creates

> **After successful withdrawal some symptoms may continue, eg craving, anxiety, insomnia; resumption of
> opioid use is very common and may carry increased risk of overdose due to reduced opioid tolerance** [A1].

**That is the central paradox of abstinence-based treatment** and the strongest argument in the topic for
maintenance: successful withdrawal *lowers tolerance*, so the common relapse is more dangerous than the
dependence was.

**Strategies to avoid cues associated with situations, people and places connected with drug use may be
necessary for many years** [A1].

**Naltrexone** may assist abstinence after withdrawal. ⚠️ **It does not relieve persistent withdrawal symptoms
such as anxiety or insomnia.** **It may be suitable for those with high motivation to be opioid free, a good
support network, and a short history of opioid use** [A1].
