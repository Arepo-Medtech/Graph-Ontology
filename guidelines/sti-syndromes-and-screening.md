## Anorectal syndromes

> ### ⚠️ EMPIRICAL TREATMENT SHOULD BE INITIATED WITHOUT WAITING FOR RESULTS [Y7]
> **Doxycycline 100 mg PO BD for 21 days** (chlamydia or LGV)
> **PLUS ceftriaxone 500 mg in 2 mL of 1% lignocaine IMI stat** (gonorrhoea)
> **PLUS valaciclovir 500 mg PO BD for 5–10 days** (herpes).
>
> ⚠️ **If specific tests are negative, treatment for that STI can be discontinued** [Y7].
>
> **This regimen has no AMH counterpart.**

- ⚠️ **Patients with STI proctitis are frequently misdiagnosed with non-STI causes** — ulcerative colitis,
  trauma, radiation proctitis. **STIs should be excluded before further investigations such as flexible
  sigmoidoscopy or colonoscopy** [Y7].
- ⚠️ **Many cis-gendered women are having condomless anal sex and should be tested for STI that cause
  proctitis if they present with symptoms** [Y7].
- Any patient with anorectal symptoms **should ideally have a DARE with a proctoscope**, with swabs taken
  from the rectum under direct visualisation [Y7].
- ⚠️ **Men who have sex with men should have three-site (pharyngeal, urethral, anal) and serological
  testing including HIV — rectal infections are frequently accompanied by infections at other sites** [Y7].

**Testing caveats that change management** [Y7]:

- ⚠️ **Anorectal chlamydia presenting with proctitis should raise the suspicion of LGV, which requires
  test-of-cure or a longer course of treatment.**
- **Take gonorrhoea culture for sensitivity before empirical treatment** — but **waiting for results
  should not delay treatment**.
- ⚠️ **The absence of ulceration does not completely rule out HSV or mpox.**
- ⚠️ **A negative NAAT is not sufficient to exclude syphilis** — serology may be negative during early
  primary syphilis with proctitis.
- ***M. genitalium* testing is not routinely recommended for proctitis**, though it may be considered in
  sexual contacts or where no other cause is found. **The role of testing in clinical proctitis is not
  clear** [Y7].

**Symptom pointers** [Y7]: **tenesmus** suggests **LGV** or proctocolitis such as shigella ·
**constipation predominates in proctitis**, alternating constipation and diarrhoea in proctocolitis ·
**perianal ulcers** suggest herpes, mpox or syphilis, but **absence of ulceration does not exclude them**.

## Anogenital ulcers

**Commonest STI causes: HSV or syphilis. Uncommonly mpox or LGV. Rarely donovanosis or chancroid.**
Non-infectious causes include **fixed drug eruptions, aphthous ulcers, trauma, squamous intra-epithelial
lesions, carcinoma, Behçet disease and Crohn's disease** [Y5].

| Clue | Points to |
|---|---|
| **Painful ulcers, may commence as vesicles; inguinal nodes often tender** | HSV [Y5] |
| **Indurated lesions with non-tender lymphadenopathy** | Syphilis [Y5] |
| **Concurrent bubo, ulcer testing negative for HSV and syphilis, high STI risk** | ⚠️ **Suspect LGV** [Y5] |
| Older or immunocompromised | **Consider varicella zoster** [Y5] |

⚠️ **Syphilis is more likely with sexual exposure to men who have sex with men, Aboriginal and Torres
Strait Island populations in remote Australia, and outside Australia — however syphilis incidence is
rising among heterosexuals** [Y5].

⚠️ **Consider biopsy if the ulcer persists and other investigations are negative** [Y5].

**Diagnosis** [Y5]: swab of the **base of the ulcer or deroofed vesicle** for HSV and zoster NAAT ·
syphilis needs **both an ulcer-base NAAT and blood serology** · mpox needs a **dry sterile swab**, dry
transport preferred. ⚠️ **For donovanosis, NAAT is highly sensitive and specific but only available in
research laboratories**, and histology **requires an experienced histopathologist as classic Donovan
bodies may be sparse**. ⚠️ **If chancroid is suspected, seek specialist advice.**

**Treatment lives in the organism guidelines** — but two cross-cutting points [Y5]:
⚠️ **Seek specialist advice for all patients who are pregnant, or hypersensitive to penicillin**, when
treating syphilis. On herpes, **limited evidence indicates aciclovir, famciclovir and valaciclovir are
therapeutically equivalent**, so **the patient's ability to adhere to the dosing frequency should decide**.

## Anogenital lumps

Causes: **HPV, molluscum contagiosum, HSV, syphilis**; **haemorrhoids, folliculitis, impetigo,
intra-epithelial neoplasia or carcinoma**; and ⚠️ **normal anatomical variations — skin tags, sebaceous
glands, penile or vulval papillae, Fordyce spots** [Y6].

> ⚠️ **On the genitals, care must be taken to avoid misdiagnosing normal anatomic variants — vestibular
> papillae, sebaceous glands, angiokeratomata, pearly penile papules, Tyson glands and Fordyce spots —
> as warts.** These are **usually notable for the regularity and consistency in their appearance and
> distribution** [Y6].

⚠️ **Syphilis can cause painless irregular nodules with a wart-like appearance in moist anatomical
sites** [Y6].

**Genital warts** [Y6]: ⚠️ **diagnosis is usually based on visual appearance, and HPV DNA testing is not
used to diagnose genital warts.** ⚠️ **Consider biopsy to exclude high-grade squamous intraepithelial
lesions and cancer** if lesions are atypical — variable pigmentation, raised plaque-like lesions,
ulceration, bleeding — **or fail to respond to standard treatments**. ⚠️ **Cervical warts should be
referred for specialist assessment.**

## Pelvic inflammatory disease

| Severity | Regimen |
|---|---|
| **Mild–moderate, outpatient** | **Ceftriaxone 500 mg in 2 mL 1% lignocaine IMI (or 500 mg IV) stat PLUS metronidazole 400 mg PO BD 14 days PLUS doxycycline 100 mg PO BD 14 days** [Y3] |
| **Severe, inpatient** | **Ceftriaxone 2 g IV daily OR cefotaxime 2 g IV TDS, PLUS azithromycin 500 mg IV daily, PLUS metronidazole 500 mg IV BD** [Y3] |
| **Pregnancy or breastfeeding** | ⚠️ **Avoid doxycycline.** Ceftriaxone 500 mg IMI/IV stat **PLUS metronidazole 400 mg PO BD 14 days PLUS azithromycin 1 g PO stat PLUS azithromycin 1 g PO stat one week later** [Y3] |

⚠️ **Begin treatment immediately on a provisional diagnosis, without waiting for test results. Rapid
response to appropriate antibiotic treatment is highly predictive of PID** [Y3].

**Consider removal of an IUD if there is no response within 48–72 hours** — balancing the risk of
pregnancy and considering oral emergency contraception [Y3].

**Consider admission if** [Y3]: clinically unwell including unstable vital signs, or pain not manageable
as an outpatient · **diagnosis uncertain** · **a surgical emergency cannot be excluded** · suspected or
definite **pelvic abscess** · severe illness or **no response to outpatient management in 48–72 hours** ·
intolerance to oral therapy · **pregnancy** · **homeless or unstable accommodation**.

**Follow-up** [Y3]: **review at 48–72 hours**; **if no improvement by 72 hours, consider admission**;
**further review at 1–2 weeks**. **Auditable outcome: 100% of people diagnosed with PID have had
investigations for gonorrhoea and chlamydia** [Y3].

### ⚠️ The guideline states its own evidence limit on *M. genitalium*-associated PID

Results are **often received about a week after presumptive PID treatment has begun**. Empirical
doxycycline and metronidazole **has recently shown some efficacy**. If the patient is responding,
**complete the 14-day empiric regimen and undertake a test of cure**; **if still symptomatic, recall and
switch to 14 days of moxifloxacin**. ⚠️ **"This regimen was evaluated in a randomised controlled trial of
all-cause PID and has been used in patients with *M. genitalium*-associated PID but is not supported by
randomised data"** [X3]. **That sentence is the guideline's own.**

## Epididymo-orchitis

⚠️ **Treat sexually active men with epididymo-orchitis presumptively for gonorrhoea and chlamydia** [Y2]:

> **Ceftriaxone 500 mg in 2 mL 1% lignocaine IMI stat PLUS EITHER doxycycline 100 mg PO BD for 14 days
> OR azithromycin 1 g PO stat, repeated one week later** [Y2].

- **For men who engage in insertive anal sex**, treat empirically as above, but ⚠️ **if response is poor,
  alternative treatment may be required to treat enteric organisms** — seek specialist advice [Y2].
- **In severe cases treatment may need to continue for up to 3 weeks** [Y2].
- **Bed rest, scrotal support and analgesia are commonly required.** **Complete resolution of swelling may
  take several weeks, but a substantial response should occur in 4–5 days** — and follow-up is at 4–5
  days [Y2].
- **Test of cure is not recommended**, but men with chlamydia and gonorrhoea **should be retested for
  re-infection after 3 months**. ⚠️ **Confirmed UTIs in men often require further investigation or
  urological referral** [Y2].
- **Contact tracing: if STI-related, all partners from the past 6 months are tested and treated; if
  urinary tract infection, contact tracing is not required** [Y2].

## Penile urethritis

| Situation | Recommended | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| **NGU likely** | **Doxycycline 100 mg PO BD 7 days** | — [Y4] |
| **Gonorrhoea likely** | **Ceftriaxone 500 mg in 2 mL 1% lignocaine IMI stat PLUS azithromycin 1 g PO stat** | **Ceftriaxone 500 mg IMI stat PLUS doxycycline 100 mg PO BD 7 days** [Y4] |

**Ceftriaxone is the most effective treatment for gonorrhoea, but azithromycin is added to reduce the
chance of resistance emerging.** **Doxycycline is preferred for NGU and chlamydia and also initiates
treatment for *M. genitalium*.** When NGU is likely but you also want to cover gonorrhoea, **it is
reasonable to add doxycycline instead of azithromycin to ceftriaxone** [Y4].

If symptoms do not resolve, **seek specialist advice for persistent NGU, including *M. genitalium* (often
resistant), herpes simplex virus and adenovirus** [Y4].

## Cervicitis

⚠️ **It is important to know which organism is causing the cervicitis**, and **if possible wait for the
test results before treating, as targeted treatment is more successful**. **If same-day treatment is
given, treat for *C. trachomatis* and *N. gonorrhoeae* at a minimum** [Y1].

| Organism | Recommended | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| **Chlamydia** | **Doxycycline 100 mg PO BD 7 days** | **Azithromycin 500 mg × 2 PO stat** — safe in pregnancy [Y1] |
| **Gonorrhoea** | **Ceftriaxone 500 mg in 2 mL 1% lignocaine IMI stat PLUS azithromycin 500 mg × 2 PO stat** | Alternatives not recommended because of resistance [Y1] |

⚠️ **Use azithromycin 1 g stat as first-line *C. trachomatis* treatment in pregnancy; *N. gonorrhoeae*
treatment is the same as in non-pregnant people** [Y1].

**Patients at increased risk of being lost to follow-up should be treated at initial assessment whether a
pathogen is identified or not** [Y1].

## Vaginal discharge

⚠️ **The most common cause of vaginal discharge in people of reproductive age is normal physiological
discharge** — exclude other causes with history, examination and investigations [Y8].
⚠️ **While bacterial vaginosis is not considered an STI, it is associated with sexual activity** [Y8].

| Discharge | Suggests |
|---|---|
| White and clear, non-offensive, **varying with the menstrual cycle** | Physiological [Y8] |
| **Thin, grey-white, offensive and fishy odour** | Bacterial vaginosis [Y8] |
| **Thick, white, non-offensive** | Candidiasis [Y8] |
| **Minimal or purulent** | Chlamydia, *M. genitalium* [Y8] |
| **Purulent** | Gonorrhoea [Y8] |
| **Offensive green and yellow, scanty to profuse and frothy** | Trichomoniasis [Y8] |

⚠️ **Systemic symptoms such as fever and tachycardia indicate upper genital tract infection and PID**, and
lower abdominal pain or deep dyspareunia also suggest it [Y8].

Non-infectious causes include ⚠️ **atrophic vaginitis in lactating and postmenopausal people, and in trans
men and non-binary people using gender affirming testosterone replacement** [Y8].

## Sex-associated diarrhoea

⚠️ **Most infectious causes of diarrhoea can be transmitted sexually.** Transmission is most likely during
**oroanal sex**, but organisms using **fomite transmission will likely also transmit during other types of
sex**. For people with casual sexual partners it **may be important to consider broader differential
diagnoses**, particularly for **men who have sex with men and their partners, where outbreaks have
occurred** [Y10].

| Pattern | Sex-associated causes |
|---|---|
| **Enteritis** — watery diarrhoea, central abdominal cramping | Giardiasis, rotavirus, *Cryptosporidium*, *E. coli* [Y10] |
| **Proctocolitis** — dysentery with bloody diarrhoea, anal mucus, lower abdominal pain | *Shigella*, *Campylobacter*, *Cryptosporidium*, **CMV especially in people living with HIV**, *Entamoeba histolytica* [Y10] |
| **Distal proctitis** — constipation, pain on defecation, anal mucus | *C. trachomatis* **especially serovar L (LGV)**, *N. gonorrhoeae*, syphilis, HSV [Y10] |

> ⚠️ ***Shigella* infections among men who have sex with men are commonly resistant to multiple classes of
> antibiotics and often have a different resistance profile from those in the general community.**
> ⚠️ **Seek advice from your local public health unit or sexual health specialists on whether to initiate
> empiric treatment or delay until sensitivity results are available**, depending on symptom severity and
> the likelihood of onward sexual transmission [Y10].

**Investigate** with stool MC&S, ova/cysts/parasites and PCR **before administering antibiotics** in
mild-to-moderate diarrhoea without sepsis; for distal proctitis add an **anal swab for PCR** for
*C. trachomatis*, *N. gonorrhoeae*, *M. genitalium*, HSV and *T. pallidum*; and ⚠️ **proctitis with
confirmed chlamydia should be tested for LGV** [Y10].

## Skin rash and genital dermatology

⚠️ **Infections are often transmitted via intimate skin-to-skin contact or kissing, not only with genital
and anal penetration. Several eye, mouth and joint conditions are related to STIs** [Y11]. The page is
**explicitly not a comprehensive dermatological reference** [Y11].

- **Sexually acquired reactive arthritis** after *C. trachomatis* or *M. genitalium* may include
  **painless mouth ulcers · keratoderma blenorrhagicum (tender, thickened skin and scaly patches on the
  soles and lower legs) · circinate balanitis · erythema nodosum · nail thickening and onycholysis** [Y11].
- ⚠️ **A rash is present in most patients with disseminated gonococcal infection.** It affects the
  **trunk, limbs, palms and soles and usually spares the face, scalp and mouth**, with micro-abscesses,
  macules, papules, pustules and vesicles [Y11].
- **Syphilis:** in primary disease a papule **soon ulcerates to produce a chancre, a 1–2 cm ulcer with a
  raised, indurated margin**. In secondary disease, **rashes can take any form** and may resemble **drug
  eruption, pityriasis rosea, psoriasis or dermatitis**, with **involvement of the palms and soles** [Y11].

⚠️ **In genital dermatology, always examine from head to toe, as many genital skin conditions are not
sexually transmitted and can be found elsewhere — nails, mouth, hairline, behind ears, natal cleft,
buttocks.** **Many dermatological conditions do not require any investigation** [Y9].

The differential runs across **normal anatomical variants · fungal · viral · bacterial · parasites ·
inflammatory · autoimmune · trauma including sexual trauma, self-inflicted trauma and assault ·
malignancy · fixed drug eruptions and medication side-effects** [Y9].

## Standard asymptomatic check-up

> ⚠️ **ALL STI TESTING SHOULD INCLUDE BOTH HIV AND SYPHILIS TESTING** [Y12].

**Perform an asymptomatic check for people who** request STI testing · are at increased risk (new partner,
living in or travelling to higher-prevalence areas in Australia or overseas) · have **a known exposure to
any STI or a history of an STI within the past 12 months** · or are a partner of any of the above [Y12].

| Blood test | Consideration |
|---|---|
| **HIV antigen/antibody** | **Repeat if recent exposure — 6-week window period** [Y12] |
| **Syphilis serology** | ⚠️ **If recent exposure, repeat at 12 weeks AND presumptively treat** [Y12] |
| **Hepatitis B** — HBsAg, anti-HBs, anti-HBc | Establish status and **immunise if not previously documented** [Y12] |

> ⚠️ **READ THE HEPATITIS B RULE AS A BIRTH COHORT, NOT AN AGE.** The page says routine adolescent
> hepatitis B immunisation began in **1997** and universal infant immunisation in **May 2000**, therefore
> **"people who are 34 years old or younger in 2020 and who grew up in Australia can generally be assumed
> to have been vaccinated and do not need testing"** [Y12].
> ⚠️ **The sentence is anchored to the year 2020 on the page and has not been reworded to the current
> year.** Taken literally as an age today it would exclude the wrong people.

- ⚠️ **A vaginal swab is more sensitive than first pass urine and is the specimen of choice**; an
  endocervical swab can replace it if speculum examination is indicated [Y12].
- ⚠️ **Patients with a positive gonorrhoea NAAT should be recalled for treatment and, at the same visit,
  specimens for gonorrhoea culture for antibiotic sensitivity should be collected** [Y12].
- ⚠️ **Asymptomatic screening is not recommended for hepatitis C, trichomonas or *M. genitalium*** unless
  indicated by population group guidelines. **Hepatitis C testing** should be performed in people living
  with HIV, current PrEP use, history of injecting drug use, anal sex with a partner with HCV,
  incarceration, non-professional tattoos or piercings, or **receipt of organs or blood products before
  1990** [Y12].
- **Extragenital swabs are not routinely collected in women** but may be indicated in those at increased
  risk, including sexual contacts of gonorrhoea and chlamydia, or sex workers [Y12].

## Contact tracing

> ⚠️ **THE DIAGNOSING CLINICIAN IS RESPONSIBLE FOR INITIATING CONTACT TRACING** for STIs and blood borne
> viruses. ⚠️ **PUBLIC HEALTH UNITS IN AUSTRALIA DO NOT ROUTINELY CONTACT TRACE ALL NOTIFIABLE
> INFECTIONS** [Y13].

A clinician can **support the client to do their own**, **offer to do it on their behalf**, or in complex
situations **refer to specialist contact tracing support** [Y13].

**Contact tracing reduces transmission, prevents re-infection and allows early treatment** which may avoid
future complications. **Current** partners are high priority; **previous partners also need tracing as
they may have asymptomatic infections** [Y13].

⚠️ **Some infections pose a transmission risk to unborn babies — syphilis, gonorrhoea, HIV, chlamydia,
hepatitis B — to injecting partners (hepatitis C, HIV) and to household contacts (hepatitis B)** [Y13].

**Provider-initiated referral:** with consent the clinician can inform the partners, and ⚠️ **this can be
performed anonymously**. ⚠️ **Anonymous tracing is considered the best option for notifying partners about
HIV infection, syphilis in pregnancy, or where there are concerns around domestic violence or sexual
assault.** Ensure the patient understands how you will approach their partner, **reassure them about the
confidentiality of both parties**, and consider whether **presumptive treatment** of the contact is
needed [Y13].

⚠️ **Document your discussion about contact tracing in the patient notes** [Y13]. The page supplies
wording — *"For this STI, we need to let all partners at risk from the last [x] months know"* — and
advises **using cues such as location or events, and non-judgemental language** [Y13].

**How far back to trace is set per organism** — see the organism guidelines. Examples held elsewhere in
this compendium: **chlamydia 6 months**, **gonorrhoea 2 months**, **epididymo-orchitis 6 months if
STI-related** [Y2].

## ⚠️ Where ASHM and AMH disagree

**Recorded, not averaged. Neither is resolved here.** The AMH side of each comparison is paraphrased under
subscription and cannot be quoted; **the ASHM side of every comparison is quoted.**

### 1. Azithromycin for chlamydia

**AMH** states flatly, in its genital infections table, that for chlamydial and other non-gonococcal
urethritis **"azithromycin is no longer recommended as *M. genitalium* is increasingly resistant"** [A1].

**ASHM does not say this of chlamydia.** It retains **azithromycin 1 g stat as a named alternative** where
adherence is likely to be poor, and as the **recommended treatment in pregnancy** [X2].

⚠️ **A prescriber reading AMH alone would rule out the pregnancy option.**

### 2. The *M. genitalium* azithromycin schedule

**AMH** says "doxycycline followed by either azithromycin or moxifloxacin if macrolide-resistant" —
**without specifying the azithromycin schedule** [A1]. **ASHM's schedule is not a single dose:** it is
**1 g immediately then 500 mg daily for 3 days, total 2.5 g**, after 7 days of doxycycline [X3].

⚠️ **A single 1 g dose would be under-treatment.**

### 3. Gonorrhoea — a gap in AMH

**AMH's genital infections table contains no gonorrhoea treatment row**; gonorrhoea appears there only as
a pathogen within sexually acquired epididymo-orchitis and PID [A1]. **ASHM's regimen is
ceftriaxone 500 mg IMI stat plus azithromycin 1 g PO stat, azithromycin doubled to 2 g for pharyngeal
infection** [X1] — held in this compendium's
[`chlamydia-and-gonorrhoea`](chlamydia-and-gonorrhoea.md) guideline.

### 4. Syndromes — a bigger gap in AMH

⚠️ **AMH has no syndrome rows at all** for anorectal syndromes, anogenital ulcers, anogenital lumps,
vaginal discharge, genital dermatology, sex-associated diarrhoea or skin rash [A1]. **The anorectal
empirical triple regimen above has no AMH counterpart.**

### 5. Where they agree

Both give **doxycycline plus metronidazole for 14 days plus a single IM ceftriaxone dose** for
mild-to-moderate PID, and both allow the **breastfeeding substitution of two azithromycin doses a week
apart** [A1] [Y3]. ⚠️ **AMH has no severe/inpatient PID regimen; ASHM does** [Y3].
