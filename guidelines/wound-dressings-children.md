# Wound dressings for acute traumatic wounds in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** cleaning, irrigation, antibiotic indications, dressing choice by wound type and review interval, and scar aftercare for acute traumatic wounds in children. Closure and suture choice are in `lacerations-children`; burns dressings are in `burns-post-acute-care-children`.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 19 of 19 claims quoted verbatim from the **RCH Melbourne Clinical Practice Guideline *Wound dressings - acute traumatic wounds*** (Last updated March 2023); **30 fragments re-checked by machine. 2 quoted doses.**

> **Irrigation before dressing is the priority; most wounds do not need antibiotics** if they are cleaned and decontaminated adequately.

---

## Principles

- **Use an aseptic technique for all wound care**; irrigate to decontaminate before dressing, and give adequate analgesia. [S1]
- **Examine for nerve, vessel and tendon damage**, testing movement while exploring — especially palmar and hand wounds. [S1]
- **X-ray** for fracture or a radiopaque foreign body (glass, metal); **ultrasound** for a puncture with a radiolucent one (thorn, splinter). **Swab only if considering antibiotics.** [S1]

## Clean and irrigate

- **Antiseptic irrigation can damage tissue defences and slow healing** — except contaminated wounds, which may benefit from **chlorhexidine 0.05% or povidone-iodine 1%** irrigation. [S1]
- **Irrigate with 0.9% sodium chloride from a 20–30 mL syringe and large-bore non-bevelled needle**, held just above the wound with continuous gentle pressure; **about 50–100 mL per cm of wound.** [S1]
- **Refer to plastics or general surgery** (in ED or as outpatient) for cosmetically sensitive wounds, deep wounds or significant tissue loss, and injury to tendons, nerves or vessels. [S1]

## Antibiotics

- **Most wounds do not need antibiotics**; decontamination matters more. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Reserve antibiotics for:** animal or human bites · penetrating wounds of vital structures (e.g. eye) · contaminated wounds, especially deep · severe or contaminated water wounds · **cleaning delayed over 8 hours** · extensive tissue damage or massive contamination. [S1]

## Dressing by wound type

- **By healing stage:** hydrogels for debridement; low-adherent moisture-maintaining for granulation; low-adherent for epithelialisation. [S1]
- **Grazes and abrasions:** keep dry. Clean — film, tulle, fixation sheet or dry; soiled — dry or tulle. **Review at 2 days.** [S1]
- **Closed lacerations (sutured or glued):** protect, open or dry, paper-tape support after suture removal; **review 3–7 days.** **Puncture wounds:** keep dry and visible, open or dry; **review 1–2 days.** [S1]
- **Infected wound:** antimicrobial, moist healing; **avoid semi-occlusive dressings**, consider alginate or hydrocolloid if exudate is high; **review 1–2 days.** [S1]
- **Slough:** dry — moisture retention (hydrocolloid, semi-permeable); moist — absorbent (alginate, foam). [S1]

## Dressing cautions

- **Semi-permeable film** (OpSite, Tegaderm): superficial wounds or as a secondary dressing; **not for highly exudative wounds.** [S1]
- **Fixation sheet** (Fixomull, Hypafix, Mefix): changes can be left 5–7 days; wash with soap and water and pat dry twice daily; **oil before removal**. Not for infected wounds or adhesive allergy. [S1]
- **Calcium alginate** (Kaltostat): for moderate or high exudate and haemostasis; **not recommended in anaerobic infection**; the gel can be mistaken for slough or pus. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Antimicrobial dressings** (Acticoat, Aquacel Ag, Idosorb) for infected wounds and burns — **caution with iodine dressings in thyroid disease.** [S1]

## Aftercare and discharge

- **Educate on healing, infection signs and scars:** avoid sun; **scar massage from 2–4 weeks after closure or suture removal, 5–10 min twice daily.** [S1]
- **Consult surgery/plastics** if the dressing is too complex or the specific dressing is unavailable. **Discharge** once the dressing is on and follow-up is planned. [S1]
