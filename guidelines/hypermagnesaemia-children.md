## Key points and causes

- **Often an asymptomatic laboratory finding. Stop any magnesium-containing medicines or IV solutions, and ask about non-prescribed supplements.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Seek specialist advice for any symptomatic child.** [S1]
- **Serum level is a poor guide to body stores** (under 1% is extracellular). An asymptomatic high result is sometimes seen after IV magnesium. **Symptoms are unusual below 2 mmol/L.** [S1]
- **Causes:** iatrogenic excess from IV infusion or PN (especially with renal impairment), tumour lysis syndrome, and renal impairment. [S1]
- **After cardiac surgery the target may deliberately be the upper end of normal** to avoid arrhythmia. [S1]

## Assess

- ⚠️ **Check the sample:** timing against recent magnesium infusions, and whether it was drawn from the same IV access the infusion ran through. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Signs:** loss of deep tendon reflexes · hypotension, ECG changes · **apnoea** · decreased consciousness, somnolence, paralysis. [S1]

## Treat

- **Stop any magnesium-containing medicines or IV solutions.** ⚠️ **Symptomatic: discuss with PICU** and consider calcium chloride or gluconate, furosemide, or haemodialysis if anuric. [S1]
- **Monitor closely**, clinically and with serum magnesium. [S1]

## Escalation

- **Involve the paediatric team** if symptomatic or significantly abnormal; **consider transfer** if symptomatic or needing monitoring your centre cannot provide. [S1]
