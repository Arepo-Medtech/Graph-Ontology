## ⚠️ The two things that change outcomes

> **"REFER TO A RHEUMATOLOGIST ONCE THE DIAGNOSIS IS MADE, AS MUCH OF THE JOINT DAMAGE OCCURS EARLY IN
> THE DISEASE, and treatment with an antirheumatic agent at this stage WILL RETARD PROGRESSIVE JOINT
> DESTRUCTION AND REDUCE LONG-TERM DISABILITY"** [R1].
>
> ⚠️ **"PATIENTS WITH RHEUMATOID ARTHRITIS ARE AT INCREASED RISK OF DEATH DUE TO CARDIOVASCULAR DISEASE
> compared with the general population. MANAGE CARDIOVASCULAR RISK FACTORS AND DISCOURAGE SMOKING"**
> [R1]. **The thing most likely to kill the patient is not the joints.**

**NSAIDs, paracetamol and an antirheumatic drug can be started with the FIRST SYMPTOMS** [R1].

## ⚠️ When to refer — the triggers [S1]

**The RACGP guideline's thresholds, as reported by S1:**

> **Rheumatologist referral should occur:**
> - ⚠️ **IMMEDIATELY if rheumatoid factor and/or ANTI-CCP ANTIBODY IS POSITIVE**
> - ⚠️ **WITHIN SIX WEEKS after persistent swelling in MORE THAN ONE JOINT — EVEN IF RHEUMATOID FACTOR IS
>   NEGATIVE**
> - ⚠️ **IF NSAIDs OR STEROIDS ARE REQUIRED BEYOND SIX WEEKS after initial treatment** [S1]

**The diagnostic process and initial plan** [S1]: **history, clinical examination, and investigations —
ESR and/or CRP, RHEUMATOID FACTOR, ANTI-CYCLIC CITRULLINATED PEPTIDE (CCP) ANTIBODY** — plus **simple
analgesics and NSAIDs, having evaluated cardiovascular, gastrointestinal and renal status**. **If these
fail: low-dose corticosteroids AFTER consulting a rheumatologist, and DMARDs as early as possible for
patients with poor prognosis** [S1].

> ⚠️ **S1'S OWN CAVEAT ON THAT GUIDELINE, QUOTED:** the management actions it observed correlate with the
> RACGP recommendations — **"although these are now 10 YEARS OLD and may warrant revision."** **That was
> written in 2021.** ⚠️ **The referral triggers above therefore rest on a guideline that S1 already called
> dated five years ago. They are reported as reported; no newer Australian source was retrieved.**

### And the referral may not be available [S1]

> **The RACGP guidelines recommend early referral to improve outcomes — "HOWEVER, ACCESS TO SPECIALISTS
> OFTEN DIFFERS ACROSS GEOGRAPHIC AREAS"**, and **"COST IS ALSO A MAJOR BARRIER TO SPECIALIST CARE"** [S1].

## What Australian general practice actually does [S1]

**In Australia in 2015, RA ranked 14TH of the 20 leading causes of total disease burden. Population
prevalence is reported at 0.5–0.6%, significantly higher in females** [S1].

**Across a decade of BEACH data, proportions of RA management encounters REMAINED STATIC, were higher for
female patients, INCREASED WITH PATIENT AND GP AGE, increased with socioeconomic disadvantage in major
cities, were higher for patients from English-speaking backgrounds, and were higher in regional and remote
areas** [S1].

> **The study's conclusion: "GP BEHAVIOUR INDICATES EQUITY AND UNIFORMITY IN RA MANAGEMENT NATIONALLY"**,
> with **very few differences across rurality groups** [S1].

> ⚠️ **TWO PRESCRIBING FIGURES THE AUTHORS THEMSELVES FLAG** [S1]:
> **NSAIDs at 12% of RA contacts** — "it would be useful to determine the DURATION of use" given the
> risks. **OPIOIDS at 12.5% of RA contacts** — "should also be used prudently, given growing evidence of
> opioid-related harm."
>
> **This compendium's `chronic-pain` guideline carries the opioid review framework.**

**People living in the most disadvantaged areas are more likely to be current smokers and have obesity —
factors that EXACERBATE symptoms of RA** [S1].

## Drug treatment [R1]

**Drug choice is based on disease severity (e.g. active synovitis), response to previous therapy,
comorbidities, and the relative efficacy and toxicity of the drugs** [R1].

| Stage | [R1] |
|---|---|
| **Early RA** | **symptoms best controlled by DISEASE SUPPRESSION with an antirheumatic**; analgesics including NSAIDs may also be needed |
| **Late disease** | **inflammation may not be prominent — pain is often MECHANICAL, from established joint damage.** Physiotherapy, occupational therapy, surgery. **Paracetamol instead of, or with, an NSAID** |

### Conventional antirheumatics [R1]

> **INITIAL TREATMENT IS USUALLY METHOTREXATE ALONE.** If inadequate, a combination **that includes
> methotrexate if tolerated** [R1].
>
> **SULFASALAZINE or HYDROXYCHLOROQUINE may be chosen first if symptoms are MILD with NO signs of poor
> prognosis.** ⚠️ **SULFASALAZINE IS MORE EFFECTIVE AND ACTS SOONER THAN HYDROXYCHLOROQUINE** [R1].

### Corticosteroids [R1]

**LOW-DOSE ORAL corticosteroids are well tolerated and, USED IN EARLY DISEASE, MAY REDUCE EROSIONS.** Used
to minimise disease activity before the antirheumatic works, and during flares [R1].
**INTRA-ARTICULAR corticosteroids can be used for symptomatic joints with synovitis at ANY stage.**
⚠️ **PULSE (higher dose) oral, IM or IV corticosteroids can control disease rapidly BUT USE IS
CONTROVERSIAL** [R1].

> ⚠️ **WHEN STOPPING, REDUCE THE DOSE SLOWLY TO AVOID REBOUND FLARE.** **There is concern about long-term
> adverse effects such as OSTEOPOROSIS, and about DIFFICULTY WEANING PATIENTS FROM TREATMENT** [R1].

### Cytokine modulators [R1]

**Faster onset than conventional antirheumatics — WITHIN WEEKS RATHER THAN MONTHS.** Used **usually WITH
methotrexate** when conventional antirheumatics are inadequate [R1].

> ⚠️ **THE TRADE-OFF, IN AMH'S WORDS:** pro-inflammatory cytokines "are also important in the immune
> defence against TUMOURS AND INFECTION" [R1].
> ⚠️ **AVOID USING MORE THAN ONE CYTOKINE MODULATOR AT A TIME — THIS FURTHER INCREASES SUSCEPTIBILITY TO
> SERIOUS INFECTIONS** [R1].

## Regimens and adverse effects [R2]

⚠️ **These are DOSING FREQUENCIES, not doses.**

| Conventional | Regimen | Selected adverse effects |
|---|---|---|
| **Methotrexate** | ⚠️ **oral/SC/IM ONCE A WEEK** | myelosuppression, mucosal ulcers, infections, nausea, diarrhoea |
| **Sulfasalazine** | **oral, 2 or 3 doses daily** | myelosuppression, rash |
| **Hydroxychloroquine** | **oral once or twice daily** | ⚠️ **RETINAL DAMAGE**, rash, GI effects |
| **Leflunomide** | **oral once daily** | infections, diarrhoea, ⚠️ **PERIPHERAL NEUROPATHY**, alopecia, rash, raised liver enzymes, headache |
| **Azathioprine** | **oral once or twice daily** | myelosuppression, hepatotoxicity |
| **Ciclosporin** | **oral twice daily** | renal insufficiency, anaemia, hypertension |

| Cytokine modulators | Regimen | Selected adverse effects |
|---|---|---|
| **Abatacept** | **SC weekly or IV every 4 weeks** | infections |
| **Anakinra** | **SC DAILY** | infections, neutropenia |
| **Rituximab** | **IV 2-dose course, repeat after >6 months** | infections, infusion reactions |
| **Tocilizumab** | **SC weekly or IV every 4 weeks** | infections, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, hyperlipidaemia, infusion reactions |

| Janus kinase inhibitors | Regimen | Selected adverse effects |
|---|---|---|
| **Baricitinib** | **oral once daily** | infections, ⚠️ **VTE**, hyperlipidaemia |
| **Upadacitinib** | **oral once daily** | infections, ⚠️ **VTE**, hyperlipidaemia |
| **Tofacitinib** | **oral twice daily** | infections, ⚠️ **VTE**, neutropenia, lymphopenia, hyperlipidaemia |

| TNF-alpha antagonists | Regimen |
|---|---|
| **Adalimumab** | **SC every 2 weeks** |
| **Certolizumab** | **SC every 2 or 4 weeks** |
| **Etanercept** | **SC once or twice a week** |
| **Golimumab** | **SC once a month** |
| **Infliximab** | **IV every 8 weeks or SC every 2 weeks** |

⚠️ **Class effects for TNF-alpha antagonists: infections, WORSENING HEART FAILURE, blood dyscrasias** [R2].
⚠️ **VENOUS THROMBOEMBOLISM is listed for ALL THREE Janus kinase inhibitors** [R2].

## Before and alongside immunosuppression [R1]

**Before starting treatment that suppresses immune function, consider active or latent infection (hepatitis
B, TB), IMMUNISATION requirements ESPECIALLY LIVE VACCINES, hepatitis B vaccination if at risk, and any
history of malignant disease** [R1].

> ⚠️ **ENSURE ALL PATIENTS WITH RA RECEIVE PNEUMOCOCCAL AND ANNUAL INFLUENZA VACCINATIONS** [R1].

**This compendium's `immunomodulators-general-principles` guideline carries the full screening,
vaccination and infection checklist that governs every drug in the table above.**

**Also: assess baseline function and joint damage, consider prognosis, provide education about the disease
and joint protection, and arrange PHYSICAL AND OCCUPATIONAL THERAPY review** [R1].

### One non-drug measure with a number [R1]

> **"Adding FISH OIL supplements — AT LEAST 2.7 g OF OMEGA-3 FATTY ACIDS DAILY — reduces severity of
> symptoms, compared to placebo"** [R1].

**AMH also refers readers to the Australian evidence-based LIVING recommendations for the treatment of
inflammatory arthritis at livingguidelines.org** [R1].
