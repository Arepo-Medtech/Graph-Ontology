# Intravenous fluids in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** resuscitation boluses, maintenance fluid choice and rate, fluid restriction, deficit replacement and monitoring for children aged 1 month to 18 years who cannot take enteral fluids. > ⚠️ **Most unwell children need only 2/3 maintenance.** Hypotonic fluids can cause hyponatraemia; glucose-containing fluids are never a shock bolus.

## Principles

- **Treat IV fluid as a prescribed medication; use the enteral route whenever possible.** This guidance applies from 1 month to 18 years. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Red flags:** sodium under 135 or over 145 mmol/L, or changing by more than 0.5 mmol/L/hr — notify a senior clinician; short gut or major GI pathology; **resuscitation over 20 mL/kg.** [S1]
- **Weigh at the start and at least daily** — repeated weights are the best measure of fluid status. Record intake and losses with at least 12-hourly subtotals. [S1]
- **Check electrolytes and glucose before starting, and at least every 24 hours** while running more than 50% maintenance; recheck at 4–6 hours in sicker children or with large losses or abnormal electrolytes. [S1]

## Resuscitation

- ⚠️ **Shock: 10–20 mL/kg sodium chloride 0.9% as fast as possible, reassess after each bolus, repeat 10–20 mL/kg as needed.** Senior clinician at ≥20 mL/kg; **consider inotropes at ≥40 mL/kg.** Bolus volumes are not counted in later calculations. [S1]
- **Trauma: packed red cells 10 mL/kg first-line.** Plasma-Lyte 148, Hartmann's or albumin only on senior advice. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Never give a glucose-containing fluid as a shock bolus.** [S1]

## Choosing the fluid

- **Maintenance: sodium chloride 0.9% with glucose 5% (± potassium)** in most situations; include glucose (usually 5%) for most children, especially nil by mouth. [S1]
- **Alternatives:** Plasma-Lyte 148 with glucose 5% (5 mmol/L potassium, less chloride) for hyperchloraemia and acidosis. Hartmann's with glucose 5% is rarely used and **is incompatible with some drugs, e.g. ceftriaxone.** [S1]
- **Potassium chloride 20 mmol/L**, premixed with sodium chloride 0.9% and glucose 5%, once electrolytes and renal function are known. [S1]
- **Glucose 15–20%** only on senior advice, ideally centrally; **over 20%** only in intensive care. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Hypotonic fluids (e.g. sodium chloride 0.45%) are not for routine use** — only with a senior clinician or in intensive care; they may cause hyponatraemia. [S1]

## Maintenance rate

- **Full maintenance (well children only):** 3–10 kg: 100 mL/kg/day (4 mL/kg/hr); 10–20 kg: 1000 + 50 × (weight − 10) mL/day [40 + 2 × (weight − 10) mL/hr]; 20–60 kg: 1500 + 20 × (weight − 20) mL/day [60 + 1 × (weight − 20) mL/hr]; over 60 kg: normal maximum 2400 mL/day (100 mL/hr). Consider ideal body weight; adjust for all unwell children. [S1]
- **Infants: prescribe IV fluid on water requirement (100 mL/kg/day up to 10 kg)**, not the 150–180 mL/kg/day enteral milk volume. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Use 2/3 maintenance in most unwell children unless dehydrated** — high ADH risks water overload and hyponatraemia. [S1]
- **Further restriction may be needed (seek senior advice)** in acute CNS conditions, pulmonary conditions including bronchiolitis and ventilation, post-operatively and after trauma. [S1]
- **Hourly rates, full → 2/3 maintenance:** 5 kg 20 → 13 · 10 kg 40 → 27 · 20 kg 60 → 40 · 30 kg 70 → 47 · 40 kg 80 → 53 · 50 kg 90 → 60 · ≥60 kg 100 → 67 mL/hr. [S1]

## Replacing a deficit

- **Replace at most 5% deficit over 24 hours (on top of maintenance), then reassess.** Over 5% dehydrated — e.g. 10% — replace 5% in the first 24 hours and 5% in the next 24. [S1]
- **Clinician estimates of % dehydration are unreliable** — use a constant rate on premorbid weight and adjust only the duration. [S1]
- **Dehydrated child, maintenance + 5% deficit over 24 hours:** under 4 kg — consult a senior clinician · 5 kg 30 · 10 kg 60 · 20 kg 100 · 30 kg 130 · 40 kg 160 · 50 kg 190 mL/hr. [S1]
- **Deranged electrolytes:** consult a senior clinician and consider slower deficit replacement. [S1]
- **Replace significant ongoing losses** by the previous hour or 4-hour period (e.g. 200 mL over 4 hours → 50 mL/hr for the next 4), commonly with **sodium chloride 0.9% + potassium chloride 20 mmol/L** for GI losses. [S1]

## Safety and escalation

- **Use a burette in small children; set pumps to an hourly volume and check it each hour.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Consider transfer** for severe electrolyte or glucose abnormalities, or **shock needing ≥40 mL/kg of boluses.** [S1]
