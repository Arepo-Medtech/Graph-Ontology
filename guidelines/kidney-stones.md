## Composition

**70–80% consist of calcium oxalate and/or calcium phosphate, with 5–10% containing uric acid** [M1].
**Renal colic due to movement of a stone into the ureter is the most common symptom** [M1].

---

## Acute renal colic

| | [M1] |
|---|---|
| **Analgesia** | ⚠️ **Unless contraindicated, use an NSAID (any route) FIRST-LINE.** **Opioids cause high rates of vomiting** — consider only **if NSAIDs are inadequate** |
| **Fluids** | ⚠️ **Normal fluid intake.** High fluid intake **is likely to increase the risk of obstruction and worsen pain** |
| **Alpha-blockers** | Sometimes used in **selected patients with lower ureteral stones under 10 mm** to help stone passage. ⚠️ **Only patients with stones 5–10 mm are likely to benefit, because of the high spontaneous passage rate — up to 89% — of smaller stones** |
| **Surgery** | **May be required** |

## Prevention

⚠️ **The recurrence rate is high** [M1].

- **Increasing fluid intake — to achieve a urine output above 2 L daily if possible — is important in
  prevention** [M1].
- **Dietary measures such as restricting salt intake may also help.** ⚠️ **Normal dietary calcium intake
  is recommended — not restricted** [M1].
- **When fluid and dietary measures are ineffective, consider drug treatment** to prevent new stones or
  growth of existing stones — **seek specialist advice** [M1].

| Drug | Mechanism | Used for [M1] |
|---|---|---|
| **Allopurinol** | Decreases urinary urate | **Uric acid or calcium oxalate stones in people with hyperuricosuria** |
| **Potassium citrate** | Increases urine pH and citrate concentration, inhibits crystal formation | **Uric acid or calcium oxalate stones, especially with hypocitraturia** |
| **Thiazide diuretics** | Reduce calcium excretion | **Calcium stones in people with hypercalciuria** |

---

## ⚠️ Drugs that cause or worsen stones

- **Whether ascorbic acid increases the risk of calcium oxalate stone formation is unclear** — but
  ⚠️ **doses above 1 g daily should be avoided** [M1].
- **The effect of calcium and/or vitamin D supplementation on recurrence is unclear.** Use with caution,
  and ⚠️ **advise that calcium supplements are taken with food, to bind oxalate and decrease urinary
  oxalate excretion** [M1].
- ⚠️ **Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors — acetazolamide, topiramate — can increase the risk of calcium
  phosphate stones** [M1].
- **Probenecid can increase the risk of uric acid stones** [M1].
- ⚠️ **Renally excreted drugs with low solubility at urine pH, eg ciprofloxacin, may crystallise in the
  urinary tract forming stones, especially at high doses** [M1].
